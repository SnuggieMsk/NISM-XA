# Chapter 13: Overview of Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **The category placement, the "lower of" sponsor interest, and pass-through taxation for I and II but not III are near-certain exam marks.**

**Q1.** Which regulations govern AIFs in India, and from which year?
> **A:** The **SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012**.

**Q2.** Which earlier regulations did the AIF Regulations replace?
> **A:** The **SEBI (Venture Capital Funds) Regulations, 1996**. Funds already registered under them continue until wound up, but **all new funds must register under the AIF Regulations**.

**Q3.** Define an AIF.
> **A:** A **privately pooled investment vehicle established in India** as a **trust, company, LLP or body corporate**, which collects funds from sophisticated investors — Indian or foreign — and invests them according to a **defined investment policy** for the benefit of those investors.

**Q4.** Name four kinds of pool that are expressly NOT AIFs.
> **A:** **Mutual funds** (SEBI MF Regulations, 1996) and **collective investment schemes** (CIS Regulations, 1999), plus **family trusts, ESOP/employee-welfare and gratuity trusts, holding companies and securitisation trusts**.

**Q5.** How may an AIF raise money?
> **A:** **Only by private placement**, using a **placement memorandum** — never by public issue or public advertisement.

**Q6.** When must the placement memorandum be filed with SEBI?
> **A:** **At least 30 days before the launch** of the scheme, with SEBI's comments incorporated before launch.

**Q7.** What defines a Category I AIF?
> **A:** It invests in **start-ups, early-stage ventures, SMEs, social ventures, infrastructure** or other sectors the government or regulators consider **socially or economically desirable** — and is therefore the only category on which **incentives or concessions may be granted**.

**Q8.** Name the four sub-categories of Category I.
> **A:** **Venture Capital Funds (including Angel Funds), SME Funds, Social Venture Funds and Infrastructure Funds.**

**Q9.** What defines a Category II AIF?
> **A:** A fund that **does not fall in Category I or III** and **does not undertake leverage or borrowing** other than for day-to-day operational requirements. Examples: **private equity, debt, real estate, distressed-asset and fund-of-funds**.

**Q10.** What defines a Category III AIF?
> **A:** A fund employing **diverse or complex trading strategies**, which **may employ leverage** including through listed or unlisted **derivatives**. Examples: **hedge funds and PIPE funds**.

**Q11.** Place these in categories: venture capital, private equity, debt, infrastructure, SME, hedge, social venture, angel.
> **A:** **Category I** — venture capital, angel, SME, social venture, infrastructure. **Category II** — private equity, debt. **Category III** — hedge. *(Memory hook: "VASI in I".)*

**Q12.** Which categories must be close-ended, and which may be open-ended?
> **A:** **Categories I and II must be close-ended** with a **minimum tenure of 3 years**. **Category III may be open-ended or close-ended.**

**Q13.** By how much may the tenure of a close-ended AIF be extended, and with whose consent?
> **A:** By up to **2 years**, with the consent of **two-thirds of unit holders by value** of their investment.

**Q14.** What happens if that consent is not obtained?
> **A:** The fund must **fully liquidate its assets within one year** of the end of its tenure.

**Q15.** What is the minimum corpus of an AIF scheme?
> **A:** **₹20 crore** — and **₹10 crore** for an angel fund.

**Q16.** What is the minimum investment by an investor in an AIF?
> **A:** **₹1 crore.** *(Do not confuse this with the PMS minimum of ₹50 lakh.)*

**Q17.** Who may invest less than ₹1 crore, and how much less?
> **A:** **Employees and directors of the AIF, and employees and directors of the Manager** — for them the minimum is **₹25 lakh**.

**Q18.** Why does SEBI allow employees and directors a lower minimum?
> **A:** The ₹1 crore is a **sophistication filter** to keep uninformed investors out; insiders already understand the strategy and the risks. It also lets the investment team **invest alongside clients**.

**Q19.** What is the maximum number of investors in an AIF scheme?
> **A:** **1000** — and only **49** in an angel fund scheme.

**Q20.** Why is there a cap on investor numbers at all?
> **A:** Because an AIF may raise money **only by private placement**. The cap is what keeps the placement genuinely private rather than a disguised public issue.

**Q21.** State the sponsor/manager continuing interest for each category.
> **A:** **Category I and II** — lower of **2.5% of corpus or ₹5 crore**. **Category III** — lower of **5% of corpus or ₹10 crore**. **Angel fund** — lower of **2.5% of corpus or ₹50 lakh**.

**Q22.** Why is Category III's continuing interest double that of Categories I and II?
> **A:** Category III may use **leverage and complex strategies**, so investors' money can be lost faster. SEBI **doubles the alignment requirement** to keep more of the manager's own money at risk.

**Q23.** A Category II AIF has a corpus of ₹300 crore. What continuing interest must the sponsor hold?
> **A:** 2.5% × ₹300 crore = ₹7.5 crore, but the rule is the **lower of** that and ₹5 crore → **₹5 crore**.

**Q24.** A Category III AIF has a corpus of ₹120 crore. What continuing interest applies?
> **A:** 5% × ₹120 crore = **₹6 crore**, which is **lower than ₹10 crore** → **₹6 crore**.

**Q25.** May the sponsor meet the continuing-interest requirement by waiving management fees?
> **A:** **No.** It must be **real money invested and kept in the fund**.

**Q26.** Can AIF units be listed, and what is the minimum tradable lot?
> **A:** Units of a **close-ended** AIF **may** be listed after the final close, with a **minimum tradable lot of ₹1 crore**. **Angel funds may not be listed.**

**Q27.** Does listing make an AIF liquid?
> **A:** **No.** A ₹1 crore minimum lot with almost no buyers is a legal right to sell, not a practical one. Never describe a listed AIF as liquid.

**Q28.** Which category may use leverage, and on what conditions?
> **A:** **Category III only** — with **investor consent**, **disclosure in the placement memorandum**, and within the **maximum limit specified by SEBI**.

**Q29.** What temporary borrowing is allowed to Categories I and II?
> **A:** Only to meet temporary funding needs — for **not more than 30 days**, on **not more than 4 occasions a year**, and **not more than 10% of investable funds**.

**Q30.** What is the maximum an AIF may invest in a single investee company?
> **A:** **25% of investable funds** for **Categories I and II**; **10%** for **Category III**.

**Q31.** How often must Category I and II investments be valued?
> **A:** By an **independent valuer at least once every six months**.

**Q32.** How often must a Category III AIF disclose NAV?
> **A:** **At least quarterly** if close-ended and **at least monthly** if open-ended.

**Q33.** Why do Categories I and II value only twice a year while Category III reports monthly or quarterly?
> **A:** Categories I and II hold **unlisted assets with no market price**, so an independent valuer is needed and more frequent valuation would not be meaningful. Category III trades **listed securities and derivatives** that price daily.

**Q34.** Which AIF categories enjoy tax pass-through status?
> **A:** **Categories I and II only.** **Category III does NOT** get pass-through.

**Q35.** What does "pass-through" actually mean for the investor?
> **A:** The fund is treated as **transparent** — income is deemed to arise **directly to the investor as if he had invested himself**. There is **no tax at the fund level**, the **character of the income is preserved** (capital gain stays a capital gain), and the investor pays at **his own rate**.

**Q36.** What is the exception to pass-through for Categories I and II?
> **A:** **Business income** of the fund is taxed **at the fund level**; only the other income passes through. The fund also **deducts tax at source** on income credited or paid to resident investors.

**Q37.** How is a Category III AIF taxed, and what is the suitability consequence?
> **A:** **At the fund level**, generally at the **maximum marginal rate**, with investors receiving income already taxed. So every investor bears effectively the **same** rate — making Category III **relatively less tax-efficient for an investor in a low tax bracket**.

**Q38.** Which category may receive incentives or concessions from the government or SEBI?
> **A:** **Category I only** — because it funds sectors regarded as socially or economically desirable. Categories II and III receive **none**.

**Q39.** What is an angel fund, and which category is it in?
> **A:** A **sub-category of venture capital funds** — therefore **Category I** — that raises money from **angel investors** to fund very early-stage ventures.

**Q40.** Who qualifies as an angel investor?
> **A:** An **individual with net tangible assets of at least ₹2 crore** (excluding the principal residence) **plus** early-stage investment experience, serial-entrepreneur experience or senior-management experience; **or a body corporate**; **or a registered AIF/VCF**.

**Q41.** What must an angel fund's investee company look like?
> **A:** Incorporated **within 3 years**, turnover **below ₹25 crore**, and **not** part of an industrial group with turnover above **₹300 crore**.

**Q42.** What is the size and lock-in of an angel fund's investment in a venture?
> **A:** Between **₹50 lakh and ₹5 crore**, locked in for **3 years**.

**Q43.** What is the minimum commitment by an angel investor?
> **A:** **₹25 lakh**, which may be drawn down over a **maximum of 5 years**. The ₹1 crore AIF minimum does **not** apply to angel funds.

**Q44.** How does a private equity fund's manager get paid out of profits?
> **A:** Through **carried interest** — typically up to **20% of profits**, payable only after a **minimum rate of return (hurdle)** is achieved. The remaining roughly **80%** goes to the investors.

**Q45.** Name the financing stages a PE fund may participate in.
> **A:** **Seed**, **start-up/early**, **expansion (second and third stage)**, **mezzanine** (for companies expected to go public), and **buyouts / mergers and acquisitions**.

**Q46.** How does a PE or VC fund exit an investment?
> **A:** By an **IPO**, a **sale to a financial or strategic investor**, or a **buyback by the company or promoters** — agreed with the promoters, and vulnerable to weak equity markets at exit time.

**Q47.** What is a debt fund AIF, and why can't it simply lend?
> **A:** A **Category II** AIF investing in **debt or debt securities** of investee companies. It is a **pooled investment vehicle**, so it invests in instruments — it cannot run a lending business with investors' money. It receives **no special concessions**.

**Q48.** What is distinctive about a social venture fund?
> **A:** It is **Category I**; it may **accept grants and make grants** to social ventures; and its investors expressly accept **muted (below-market) returns** in exchange for measurable social impact.

**Q49.** What is a hedge fund in the Indian regulatory framework?
> **A:** A **Category III AIF** — complex strategies, **leverage permitted**, **open or close-ended**, and **taxed at the fund level with no pass-through**.

**Q50.** Give three reasons to add alternatives to a portfolio.
> **A:** **Diversification** from **low correlation** with listed markets; an **illiquidity premium** for accepting a lock-in; and **access to the unlisted opportunity set**, which is most of the Indian economy. Real assets also act as an **inflation hedge**.

**Q51.** What is the classic wrong reason for buying alternatives?
> **A:** "Higher return with lower risk." Alternatives are **higher risk on their own**; they improve the **portfolio's** risk-return trade-off through low correlation. They belong as a **satellite**, never the core.

**Q52.** For whom is an AIF suitable?
> **A:** An investor with a **large investible surplus**, a **7–10 year horizon**, **no liquidity need** from that money, **high risk tolerance**, an **already complete core portfolio**, and the sophistication to understand the strategy and fees.

**Q53.** Why is the ₹1 crore best described as a commitment rather than a payment?
> **A:** Because AIFs **draw down capital over time through capital calls** as deals are found — the investor must keep the committed money available, not merely have it once.

**Q54.** State the three managed-portfolio minimums in order.
> **A:** **Mutual fund ₹100–₹5,000 → PMS ₹50 lakh → AIF ₹1 crore.**

**Q55.** What are the SEBI registration fees for AIFs?
> **A:** **Application ₹1 lakh**; **Category I ₹5 lakh** (**angel fund ₹2 lakh**); **Category II ₹10 lakh**; **Category III ₹15 lakh**.

**Q56.** What experience must an AIF's key personnel have?
> **A:** At least **one key person with a minimum of 5 years' experience** in asset or portfolio management, advisory, securities trading or dealing in other financial assets — and the sponsor, manager and key personnel must all be **fit and proper persons**.

**Q57.** Name three enablers of AIF growth in India.
> **A:** A single clear **regulatory framework since 2012**; **pass-through taxation** for Categories I and II; and **rising HNI/UHNI wealth and family offices** — supported by a maturing **start-up ecosystem**, listing and demat of units, and **GIFT City/IFSC** structures.

**Q58.** Why must an investment adviser be especially careful when recommending an AIF?
> **A:** An AIF pays the IA **no commission** — the IA is paid by the client — so the recommendation must rest **entirely on suitability**. Recommending a decade-long lock-in to a client who cannot bear it is a **suitability failure**, however good the fund.
