# Chapter 13: Overview of Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) — 100 MCQ Question Bank

> Three tiers — 🟢 Easy (Recall), 🟡 Medium (Application), 🔴 Hard (Numerical & Scenario). **Category placement, the "lower of" sponsor commitment and pass-through taxation for I and II but not III carry the most marks.** Remember the **25% negative marking**.

## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Alternative Investment Funds in India are governed by:
A) SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996  B) SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020  C) SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012  D) SEBI (Collective Investment Schemes) Regulations, 1999
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The **SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012**, notified in May 2012.
A) Governs mutual funds. B) Governs portfolio managers. D) Governs collective investment schemes — a vehicle expressly excluded from the AIF definition.
</details>

**Q2.** The AIF Regulations replaced which earlier framework?
A) SEBI (Debenture Trustees) Regulations, 1993  B) SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996  C) The Companies Act, 2013  D) SEBI (Venture Capital Funds) Regulations, 1996
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — The **SEBI (Venture Capital Funds) Regulations, 1996** were repealed. Funds already registered under them continue until wound up; **all new funds register under the AIF Regulations**.
A) Concerns debenture trustees, an unrelated intermediary. B) The mutual fund regulations remain fully in force. C) Company law was never the framework for pooled funds.
</details>

**Q3.** An AIF is best defined as:
A) A publicly offered pooled scheme  B) A privately pooled investment vehicle established in India with a defined investment policy  C) An unregulated offshore fund  D) A bank deposit product for wealthy clients
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Privately pooled**, **established in India** as a trust, company, LLP or body corporate, investing per a **defined investment policy** for investors' benefit.
A) Public offering is exactly what an AIF may **not** do. C) An AIF is registered with SEBI and must be set up in India. D) An AIF is a pooled investment vehicle, not a deposit.
</details>

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** Which category may receive incentives or concessions from the government or regulators?
A) Category III  B) Category II  C) Category I  D) All categories equally
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Only **Category I**, because it funds sectors the state regards as socially or economically desirable.

**Q37.** Why is the minimum for employees and directors set at ₹25 lakh rather than ₹1 crore?
A) They earn less than clients  B) SEBI wants to shrink the fund's corpus  C) They already understand the strategy and risks, so the sophistication filter is unnecessary  D) It is a drafting inconsistency
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The ₹1 crore floor is a **filter against uninformed investors**; insiders are not uninformed. It also lets the investment team invest alongside clients, aligning interests.
A) Income level is irrelevant to the rule. B) A lower ticket raises, not lowers, participation. D) The concession is deliberate and expressly stated.
</details>

**Q38.** The 1000-investor cap exists because:

**Correct: A)** — Without a numeric cap, a "private" placement to thousands of people would become a **disguised public issue**, escaping the disclosure obligations of a public offering.
B) There is no cap on corpus, only on investor count. C) Fund size is unrestricted. D) An administrative excuse, not the regulatory rationale.
</details>

Before comparing categories at all, the adviser must confirm that the **remaining ₹20 crore** meets every liquidity need for the next decade, that the ₹50 lakh of undrawn commitment stays available for capital calls, and that Mr Raghavan can absorb a poor outcome. **Suitability is decided before category selection, never after.**
