# Chapter 13: Overview of Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) — 100 MCQ Question Bank

> Three tiers — 🟢 Easy (Recall), 🟡 Medium (Application), 🔴 Hard (Numerical & Scenario). **Category placement, the "lower of" sponsor commitment and pass-through taxation for I and II but not III carry the most marks.** Remember the **25% negative marking**.

## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Alternative Investment Funds in India are governed by:
A) SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996  B) SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020  C) SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012  D) SEBI (Collective Investment Schemes) Regulations, 1999
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The **SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012**, notified in May 2012.
A) Governs mutual funds. B) Governs portfolio managers. D) Governs collective investment schemes — a vehicle expressly excluded from the AIF definition.
</details>

**Q2.** The AIF Regulations replaced which earlier framework?
A) SEBI (Venture Capital Funds) Regulations, 1996  B) SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996  C) The Companies Act, 2013  D) SEBI (Debenture Trustees) Regulations, 1993
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The **SEBI (Venture Capital Funds) Regulations, 1996** were repealed. Funds already registered under them continue until wound up; **all new funds register under the AIF Regulations**.
B) The mutual fund regulations remain fully in force. C) Company law was never the framework for pooled funds. D) Concerns debenture trustees, an unrelated intermediary.
</details>

**Q3.** An AIF is best defined as:
A) A publicly offered pooled scheme  B) A privately pooled investment vehicle established in India with a defined investment policy  C) An unregulated offshore fund  D) A bank deposit product for wealthy clients
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Privately pooled**, **established in India** as a trust, company, LLP or body corporate, investing per a **defined investment policy** for investors' benefit.
A) Public offering is exactly what an AIF may **not** do. C) An AIF is registered with SEBI and must be set up in India. D) An AIF is a pooled investment vehicle, not a deposit.
</details>

**Q36.** Which category may receive incentives or concessions from the government or regulators?

**Q37.** Why is the minimum for employees and directors set at ₹25 lakh rather than ₹1 crore?

</details>

A) There is no cap on corpus, only on investor count. C) Fund size is unrestricted. D) An administrative excuse, not the regulatory rationale.
</details>

**QC5.** Mr Raghavan asks whether he should switch the commitment to a Category III hedge-strategy AIF instead. What is the best advice?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Three things change together. **Leverage and complex strategies** become available, raising both return potential and loss potential. **Pass-through is lost**, so income is taxed **at the fund level** rather than at his own rate. And the **sponsor's continuing interest doubles** to the lower of 5% of corpus or ₹10 crore, which tells the adviser something about how much riskier SEBI considers the category.

**The wider lesson:** with a ₹22 crore family office committing ₹2 crore, roughly **9% of the portfolio** is going into a single illiquid, close-ended vehicle. Before comparing categories at all, the adviser must confirm that the **remaining ₹20 crore** meets every liquidity need for the next decade, that the ₹50 lakh of undrawn commitment stays available for capital calls, and that Mr Raghavan can absorb a poor outcome. **Suitability is decided before category selection, never after.**
