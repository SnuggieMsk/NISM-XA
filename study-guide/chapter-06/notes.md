# Chapter 6: Securities Market Segments — Short Notes

> **Module 2 · Indian Financial Markets — 10 marks shared with Chapter 5.**
> Mostly definitional. The highest-yield areas are **types of issues**, **book building**, and **corporate actions** (especially rights vs bonus, and the effect of each on price and holdings).

## 6.1 The Primary Market

### 6.1.1 Functions of the primary market

The primary market is where **securities are created and sold for the first time**, so capital flows **to the issuer**.

Its functions:
1. **Capital formation** — channels savings into productive investment
2. **Origination** — appraising and structuring the issue
3. **Underwriting** — guaranteeing subscription
4. **Distribution** — placing securities with investors

### 6.1.2 ⭐ Types of Issues

| Type | Who it goes to | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Public issue** | The general public | IPO (first time) or FPO (already listed). Requires a prospectus and full SEBI compliance |
| **Private placement** | A select group | Offered to **fewer than 200 investors** in a financial year (excluding QIBs and employees under ESOP); beyond that it is deemed a public issue |
| **Preferential issue** | Selected identified persons | Made by a **listed** company to specific investors (promoters, strategic partners) at a price determined under SEBI ICDR rules; carries a **lock-in** |
| **Qualified Institutions Placement (QIP)** | **Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIBs)** only | A fast, low-cost route for listed companies to raise money from institutions without a full prospectus |
| **Rights issue** | **Existing shareholders**, pro rata | Offered at a **discount** to market price; the entitlement is **renounceable** (can be sold) |
| **Bonus issue** | **Existing shareholders**, free | Capitalisation of reserves — **no money is raised** |

> ⚠️ **The most-tested contrast: a RIGHTS issue raises money; a BONUS issue does not.** A rights issue is an *offer to buy more* at a discount; a bonus issue is *free additional shares* funded by converting reserves into share capital.

### 6.1.3 Categories of issuers

- **Central, State and local governments** — dated securities, T-bills, state development loans, municipal bonds
- **Public sector units (PSUs)** — equity and bonds
- **Private sector companies** — equity, debentures, commercial paper
- **Banks, financial institutions and NBFCs** — bonds, certificates of deposit, infrastructure bonds
- **Mutual funds** — units
- **REITs and InvITs** — units representing real estate / infrastructure assets
- **AIFs** — units to sophisticated investors

### 6.1.4 Types of investors

| Category | Who | Typical IPO allocation |
|---|---|---|
| **QIBs** | Mutual funds, banks, insurers, FPIs, pension funds | Up to **50%** in a book-built issue |
| **Non-Institutional Investors (NIIs/HNIs)** | Applications **above ₹2 lakh** | At least **15%** |
| **Retail Individual Investors (RIIs)** | Applications **up to ₹2 lakh** | At least **35%** |
| **Anchor investors** | QIBs allotted a day before the issue opens | Carved out of the QIB portion; carries a lock-in |

> 🧠 The **₹2 lakh** line separates retail from non-institutional applicants — a common exam detail.

### 6.1.5 Public issues of equity shares

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Initial Public Offer (IPO)** | The company's **first** offer of shares to the public; the company becomes listed |
| **Follow-on / Further Public Offer (FPO)** | A **further** public offer by an **already listed** company |
| **Fresh issue** | **New** shares are created; money goes **to the company** |
| **Offer for Sale (OFS)** | **Existing** shareholders (usually promoters) sell their shares; money goes **to the selling shareholders, not the company** |

> ⚠️ **Guaranteed question:** in an **offer for sale the company receives nothing** — only the selling shareholders do. Many IPOs combine a fresh issue *and* an OFS.

### 6.1.6 ⭐ Pricing a public issue

| | **Fixed price issue** | **Book-built issue** |
|---|---|---|
| **Price** | Stated in the prospectus **upfront** | A **price band** is given; the final price is discovered from bids |
| **Demand visibility** | Known only **after** the issue closes | Visible **during** the issue (daily bidding data) |
| **Investor pays** | Full amount on application | Bids at or above the floor; final **cut-off price** applies |
| **Price band** | Not applicable | Cap must be **within 120%** of the floor price |

**Book building mechanics:**
- The company and merchant banker set a **floor price** and a **cap**.
- Investors bid quantities at prices within the band; retail investors may bid at **"cut-off"**, meaning they accept whatever final price emerges.
- The **cut-off price** is the price at which the issue is fully subscribed — it becomes the issue price.
- Bidding is typically open **3 working days** (extendable if the band is revised).

### 6.1.8 Applying to a public issue — ASBA

**ASBA = Applications Supported by Blocked Amount.** The application money is **blocked in the investor's own bank account** rather than debited. It is:
- **Mandatory** for public issues
- Debited **only to the extent of the allotment**; the rest is simply unblocked
- The investor **continues to earn interest** on the blocked amount

**UPI mechanism:** retail investors applying up to **₹5 lakh** may use a UPI ID to block funds.

### 6.1.9 Public issue of debt securities
Governed by SEBI's regulations on issue and listing of **non-convertible securities**. Requires **credit rating**, a **debenture trustee**, and creation of a **debenture redemption reserve** where applicable.

### 6.1.10 Rights offer
- Offered to existing shareholders **in proportion to their holding** (e.g. 1:4 = one new share for every four held).
- Priced at a **discount** to the market price.
- The entitlement is **renounceable** — a shareholder who does not wish to subscribe can **sell the rights entitlement** in the market.
- A shareholder who neither subscribes nor renounces suffers **dilution** and forfeits value.

### 6.1.11 & 6.1.12 Private placement and QIP
- **Private placement**: securities offered to a **select group of fewer than 200 persons** in a financial year (excluding QIBs and ESOP employees). Exceeding that limit makes it a deemed public issue.
- **QIP**: a listed company issues to **QIBs only**. Fast and low-cost — no draft prospectus filing with SEBI is required, and pricing follows a prescribed formula based on recent market prices.

## 6.2 The Secondary Market

### 6.2.1 Role and function
- **Liquidity** — investors can exit
- **Price discovery** — continuous valuation of securities
- **Transfer of ownership** without affecting the company's capital
- **Benchmark** for pricing new issues and for corporate decisions

### 6.2.2 Market structure and participants
Exchanges (NSE, BSE), clearing corporations, depositories, brokers and authorised persons, custodians, and investors (retail, HNI, institutional, FPI).

**Trading and settlement:** orders are matched electronically on a price–time priority basis. Indian equity settlement is on a **T+1** rolling basis (settlement on the working day after the trade).

### 6.2.3 ⭐ Market indicators

| Indicator | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Market capitalisation** | Share price × number of outstanding shares. For the market, the sum across all listed companies. Measures **size** |
| **Market turnover** | Total value of trades over a period. Measures **activity/liquidity** |
| **Market indices** | A basket of representative stocks tracking overall or sectoral movement — Nifty 50, Sensex |

**Index construction:** Indian indices such as the Nifty 50 and Sensex use **free-float market capitalisation weighting** — only shares genuinely available for trading (excluding promoter and locked-in holdings) are counted.

**Uses of an index:** a market barometer, a benchmark for portfolio performance, the underlying for index funds/ETFs and derivatives, and an economic indicator.

### 6.2.4 ⭐ Risk Management Systems in the secondary market

| Mechanism | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Capital adequacy norms** | Minimum net worth and deposits for brokers, scaled to business volume |
| **Margins** | Collateral collected upfront to cover potential losses — initial margin, mark-to-market margin, exposure margin, VaR margin |
| **Circuit breakers** | **Market-wide** halts when an index moves ±10%, ±15% or ±20%, with halt durations depending on the level and time of day |
| **Price bands** | **Stock-specific** limits (e.g. 2%, 5%, 10%, 20%) beyond which a stock cannot move in a day |
| **Settlement guarantee mechanism** | The clearing corporation's fund guaranteeing settlement even if a member defaults |
| **Online surveillance** | Real-time monitoring of trading for manipulation and unusual activity |
| **Price monitoring and action** | Investigation of abnormal price/volume movements; moving stocks to restricted categories (e.g. trade-for-trade) |
| **Inspection of books** | Periodic inspection of members' records by the exchange and SEBI |

> 🧠 **Circuit breaker vs price band:** a **circuit breaker is market-wide** (halts the whole market on index moves); a **price band is stock-specific** (caps one scrip's daily movement).

## 6.3 ⭐ Corporate Actions

A **corporate action** is an event initiated by a company that affects its securities and shareholders.

### 6.3.1 Rights Issue
Covered above. **Raises capital.** Price falls to a theoretical ex-rights price after the issue.

### 6.3.2 Bonus Issue
Free additional shares from **capitalisation of reserves**.
- **No money is raised.**
- Number of shares **rises**; price falls proportionately.
- **The shareholder's total value and percentage holding are unchanged.**

**Example — 1:1 bonus:** an investor holding 100 shares at ₹800 (value ₹80,000) receives 100 more. Now 200 shares, and the price adjusts to ₹400. Value = **₹80,000, unchanged.**

### 6.3.3 Dividend
Distribution of profits to shareholders.
- **Interim** (declared during the year) or **final** (at year-end, approved at the AGM).
- The share price typically falls by roughly the dividend amount on the **ex-dividend date**.
- **Record date** — the date on which the company's register determines who is entitled.
- **Ex-date** — from this date the share trades **without** the dividend entitlement. Under T+1 settlement the ex-date is normally **the same as the record date** (buy before it to be entitled).
- Dividends are **taxable in the hands of the shareholder** at slab rates, with **TDS** applicable above the prescribed threshold.

### 6.3.4 Stock Split
The face value of each share is **reduced**, increasing the number of shares.
- **Example:** a ₹10 face value share split 1:5 becomes five ₹2 face value shares. 100 shares at ₹1,000 become 500 shares at ₹200 — **value unchanged**.
- **Purpose:** improve **affordability and liquidity** by lowering the per-share price.

> ⚠️ **Bonus vs split — a classic exam pair.**
>
> | | **Bonus issue** | **Stock split** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Face value** | **Unchanged** | **Reduced** |
> | **Source** | Capitalisation of **reserves** | No reserves involved |
> | **Share capital** | **Increases** | **Unchanged** |
> | **Number of shares** | Increases | Increases |
> | **Shareholder value** | Unchanged | Unchanged |

### 6.3.5 Share Buy-back
The company **repurchases its own shares** from shareholders.
- Reduces the number of outstanding shares, raising EPS.
- Routes: **tender offer** (proportionate, at a fixed price) or **open market** purchase.
- Signals that management considers the shares undervalued, and returns surplus cash.

### 6.3.6 Delisting
The company's shares are **removed from the exchange**.
- **Voluntary** — usually via a **reverse book building** process in which public shareholders bid the exit price.
- **Compulsory** — imposed by the exchange for non-compliance; promoters must still provide an exit to public shareholders.

### 6.3.7 Mergers and Acquisitions
- **Merger/amalgamation** — two or more companies combine; shareholders of the merging company receive shares of the merged entity in a set **swap ratio**.
- **Acquisition/takeover** — one company acquires control of another. Triggers **SEBI (SAST) Regulations**, requiring an **open offer** to public shareholders once specified thresholds of shares or voting rights are crossed.
- **Demerger** — a division is spun off into a separate listed company; shareholders receive shares in the new entity.

### 6.3.8 Offer for Sale (OFS)
A mechanism for **promoters to sell** their shares through the exchange platform — used to raise public shareholding or to divest.
**The company receives nothing**; the selling shareholder does.

## ⚡ Quick Revision Sheet

| Concept | The one-line answer |
|---|---|
| Primary market | Securities **issued**; funds go to the **issuer** |
| Secondary market | Investors trade **among themselves**; provides liquidity |
| IPO vs FPO | **First** public offer vs a **further** offer by a listed company |
| Fresh issue vs OFS | New shares, money **to the company** vs existing shares, money **to the seller** |
| Private placement limit | Fewer than **200 investors** in a financial year (excl. QIBs, ESOP) |
| QIP | Listed company issues to **QIBs only**; fast, no draft prospectus filing |
| Preferential issue | To identified persons by a listed company; carries a **lock-in** |
| Rights issue | To **existing** shareholders pro rata, at a **discount**; **renounceable**; **raises money** |
| Bonus issue | **Free** shares from **reserves**; **raises no money**; value unchanged |
| Fixed price vs book building | Price stated upfront vs **discovered** within a price band |
| Price band cap rule | Cap within **120%** of the floor price |
| Cut-off price | The price at which the issue is fully subscribed |
| Retail vs NII threshold | **₹2 lakh** |
| IPO allocation (book-built) | QIB up to **50%**, NII at least **15%**, retail at least **35%** |
| ASBA | Money **blocked** in the investor's own account; interest continues |
| UPI in IPOs | Retail applications up to **₹5 lakh** |
| Settlement cycle | **T+1** rolling |
| Market capitalisation | Price × shares outstanding — measures **size** |
| Market turnover | Value traded — measures **activity** |
| Index weighting in India | **Free-float market capitalisation** |
| Circuit breaker | **Market-wide** halt at ±10%, ±15%, ±20% index moves |
| Price band | **Stock-specific** daily movement limit |
| Settlement guarantee | Provided by the **clearing corporation** |
| Stock split | **Face value reduced**; share capital unchanged |
| Bonus issue | **Face value unchanged**; share capital increases |
| Buy-back | Company repurchases its shares; **EPS rises** |
| Delisting | Voluntary via **reverse book building**, or compulsory |
| Open offer trigger | **SEBI (SAST) Regulations** on acquiring control/thresholds |
| Dividend taxation | Taxable **in the shareholder's hands** at slab rates; TDS above threshold |

> **Exam tip:** for any corporate action question, ask two things: **(1) does money move?** (rights and buy-back yes; bonus and split no) and **(2) does the shareholder's total value change?** (for bonus and split, no — only the share count and price adjust).
