# Chapter 6: Securities Market Segments — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **Corporate actions and the rights-vs-bonus contrast are the highest-yield items.**

**Q1.** What are the four functions of the primary market?
> **A:** **Capital formation**, **origination** (appraising and structuring), **underwriting** (guaranteeing subscription) and **distribution**.

**Q2.** What is the difference between an IPO and an FPO?
> **A:** An **IPO** is a company's **first** public offer (it becomes listed). An **FPO/further public offer** is a **subsequent** public offer by an **already listed** company.

**Q3.** In an Offer for Sale, who receives the money?
> **A:** The **selling shareholders** (usually promoters) — **the company receives nothing**. Contrast with a **fresh issue**, where new shares are created and the money goes to the company.

**Q4.** What is the private placement limit?
> **A:** Offered to **fewer than 200 investors** in a financial year (excluding QIBs and employees under ESOP). Beyond that it is deemed a **public issue**.

**Q5.** What is a QIP and why is it attractive to issuers?
> **A:** A **Qualified Institutions Placement** — a listed company issuing to **QIBs only**. It is **fast and low-cost**: no draft prospectus filing with SEBI, and pricing follows a prescribed formula based on recent market prices.

**Q6.** What is a preferential issue?
> **A:** An issue by a **listed** company to **specific identified persons** (promoters, strategic partners), priced under SEBI ICDR rules and carrying a **lock-in**.

**Q7.** State the key difference between a rights issue and a bonus issue.
> **A:** A **rights issue RAISES money** (shareholders buy more shares at a discount). A **bonus issue raises NO money** — it is free shares from the **capitalisation of reserves**.

**Q8.** What does "renounceable" mean in a rights issue?
> **A:** A shareholder who does not want to subscribe can **sell the rights entitlement** in the market rather than let it lapse. Doing neither means **dilution and forfeited value**.

**Q9.** Compare fixed price and book-built issues.
> **A:** **Fixed price** — the price is stated **upfront** in the prospectus and demand is known only after closing. **Book-built** — a **price band** is given, investors bid, and the final price is **discovered**; demand is visible daily.

**Q10.** What is the rule on the price band's cap?
> **A:** The cap must be **within 120% of the floor price**.

**Q11.** What is the cut-off price?
> **A:** The price at which the issue is **fully subscribed** — it becomes the final issue price. Retail investors may bid "at cut-off", accepting whatever price emerges.

**Q12.** What separates a Retail Individual Investor from a Non-Institutional Investor?
> **A:** The **₹2 lakh** application threshold. Up to ₹2 lakh = **retail**; above ₹2 lakh = **non-institutional (HNI)**.

**Q13.** State the IPO allocation limits in a book-built issue.
> **A:** **QIBs up to 50%**, **NIIs at least 15%**, **retail at least 35%**.

**Q14.** What is an anchor investor?
> **A:** A **QIB allotted shares a day before** the issue opens, carved out of the QIB portion, subject to a **lock-in**. Their participation signals confidence to other investors.

**Q15.** What is ASBA and what are its three benefits?
> **A:** **Applications Supported by Blocked Amount.** The money is **blocked in the investor's own bank account**: (1) it is debited **only to the extent of allotment**, (2) the balance is simply unblocked, and (3) the investor **keeps earning interest** meanwhile. It is **mandatory** for public issues.

**Q16.** What is the UPI limit for retail IPO applications?
> **A:** Retail investors may apply using a UPI ID for amounts up to **₹5 lakh**.

**Q17.** What is India's equity settlement cycle?
> **A:** **T+1 rolling settlement** — settlement on the working day after the trade.

**Q18.** Distinguish market capitalisation from market turnover.
> **A:** **Market capitalisation** = price × shares outstanding — it measures **size**. **Market turnover** = value of trades over a period — it measures **activity/liquidity**.

**Q19.** How are Indian indices such as the Nifty 50 and Sensex weighted?
> **A:** By **free-float market capitalisation** — only shares genuinely available for trading are counted, excluding promoter and locked-in holdings.

**Q20.** Name four uses of a market index.
> **A:** A **market barometer**; a **benchmark** for portfolio performance; the **underlying** for index funds, ETFs and derivatives; and an **economic indicator**.

**Q21.** What is the difference between a circuit breaker and a price band?
> **A:** A **circuit breaker is MARKET-WIDE** — it halts the whole market when an index moves **±10%, ±15% or ±20%**. A **price band is STOCK-SPECIFIC** — it caps one scrip's daily movement (e.g. 2%, 5%, 10%, 20%).

**Q22.** Name the risk management systems in the secondary market.
> **A:** **Capital adequacy** norms, **margins**, **circuit breakers**, **price bands**, the **settlement guarantee mechanism**, **online surveillance**, **price monitoring and action**, and **inspection of books**.

**Q23.** What types of margin are collected?
> **A:** **Initial margin**, **mark-to-market margin**, **exposure margin** and **VaR margin** — all collateral against potential losses.

**Q24.** In a 1:1 bonus issue, what happens to a holding of 100 shares priced at ₹800?
> **A:** The investor receives 100 more shares → **200 shares**, and the price adjusts to **₹400**. Total value stays **₹80,000 — unchanged**.

**Q25.** Compare a bonus issue and a stock split on face value and share capital.
> **A:** **Bonus** — face value **unchanged**, share capital **increases** (reserves are capitalised). **Split** — face value **reduced**, share capital **unchanged**. Both increase the share count and leave shareholder value unchanged.

**Q26.** What is the purpose of a stock split?
> **A:** To improve **affordability and liquidity** by lowering the per-share price.

**Q27.** What is the record date, and what is the ex-date?
> **A:** The **record date** is when the company's register determines entitlement. The **ex-date** is when the share starts trading **without** the entitlement. Under T+1 the ex-date is normally the **same as** the record date.

**Q28.** How are dividends taxed in India now?
> **A:** **Taxable in the shareholder's hands** at their slab rate, with **TDS** applicable above the prescribed threshold.

**Q29.** What happens to a share price on the ex-dividend date?
> **A:** It typically **falls by roughly the dividend amount** — the value has left the company.

**Q30.** What is a share buy-back, and what are the two routes?
> **A:** The company **repurchases its own shares**, reducing the share count and **raising EPS**. Routes: a **tender offer** (proportionate, fixed price) or **open market** purchase.

**Q31.** How does voluntary delisting normally work?
> **A:** Through **reverse book building**, in which public shareholders bid the exit price. **Compulsory** delisting is imposed by the exchange for non-compliance, and promoters must still provide an exit.

**Q32.** What triggers an open offer in an acquisition?
> **A:** Crossing prescribed thresholds of shares or voting rights (or acquiring control) under the **SEBI (SAST) Regulations** — the acquirer must then make an **open offer** to public shareholders.

**Q33.** What is a demerger?
> **A:** A division is **spun off into a separate listed company**, and existing shareholders receive shares in the new entity.

**Q34.** What is a swap ratio?
> **A:** The ratio in which shareholders of a **merging** company receive shares of the **merged** entity.

**Q35.** Which corporate actions actually move money, and which do not?
> **A:** **Money moves:** rights issue (in), dividend (out), buy-back (out), OFS (to the seller). **No money moves:** bonus issue, stock split.

**Q36.** Name the categories of issuer in the primary market.
> **A:** **Governments** (central, state, local), **PSUs**, **private companies**, **banks/FIs/NBFCs**, **mutual funds**, **REITs and InvITs**, and **AIFs**.

**Q37.** What does a public issue of debt securities require?
> **A:** A **credit rating**, a **debenture trustee**, and creation of a **debenture redemption reserve** where applicable.

**Q38.** How long is bidding typically open in a book-built issue?
> **A:** Usually **3 working days**, extendable if the price band is revised.

**Q39.** What are the four functions of the secondary market?
> **A:** **Liquidity**, **price discovery**, **transfer of ownership** (without affecting the company's capital), and providing a **benchmark** for new issues and corporate decisions.

**Q40.** In a rights issue of 1:4, what does a holder of 400 shares receive?
> **A:** The entitlement to buy **100 new shares** (one for every four held) at the discounted rights price.
