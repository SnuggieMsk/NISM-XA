## 7.1 Types of Investment

Every investment can be placed on two axes: **what you own** and **how you own it**.

### What you own — the asset classes

| Asset class | You are… | Return comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity** | An **owner** of the business | Capital appreciation + dividends |
| **Fixed income (debt)** | A **lender** | Interest + capital gain/loss on price |
| **Commodities** | An owner of a **physical good** | Price movement only |
| **Real estate** | An owner of **property** | Rent + capital appreciation |
| **Cash / money market** | A very short-term lender | Interest |

> 🧠 **The single most useful distinction in finance: equity = ownership; debt = lending.** An owner gets what is left after everyone else is paid (residual claim, unlimited upside, first loss). A lender gets a fixed promise (contractual claim, capped upside, paid before owners).

### The risk–return spectrum

$$\text{Higher expected return} \Longleftrightarrow \text{Higher risk}$$

Roughly ascending: cash → government debt → high-grade corporate debt → high-yield debt → real estate → equity → derivatives/distressed.

> ⚠️ **Never** treat this as a promise. Higher risk means a **wider range of outcomes**, including worse ones. If risk guaranteed return, it would not be risk.

## 7.2 Equity

**Investment characteristics:**
- **Residual claim** — paid last, after lenders and preference shareholders
- **Unlimited upside**, but the whole capital is at risk
- **No maturity** — a perpetual instrument
- **Voting rights** (ordinary shares) — a say in governance
- **High volatility** in the short run; historically the best long-run inflation-beater
- Returns are **not contractual** — dividends are discretionary

**Role in a portfolio:** the **growth engine**. Suitable for goals **5–7 years or more** away, where volatility has time to average out.

### Listed vs unlisted equity

| | **Listed** | **Unlisted** |
|---|---|---|
| **Traded on** | A stock exchange | Private transactions |
| **Liquidity** | High — exit any trading day | Very low |
| **Price discovery** | Continuous, public | Negotiated, infrequent |
| **Disclosure** | Extensive, regulated | Minimal |
| **Valuation** | Market price | Estimated, subjective |
| **Regulation** | SEBI, listing rules | Limited |

Unlisted equity (private equity, venture capital, start-ups) may offer higher returns but carries **illiquidity, valuation and information risk**.

---

## 7.3 Fixed Income

**Investment characteristics:**
- A **contractual** obligation to pay interest and repay principal
- **Predictable cash flows** — the main attraction
- **Capped upside** — you can never earn more than the promised yield if held to maturity
- **Senior** to equity in the event of liquidation
- Has a **defined maturity**
- Prices move **inversely** to interest rates

**Role in a portfolio:** **stability, income and capital preservation**. Suitable for short-horizon goals and for dampening overall portfolio volatility.

### Government vs corporate debt

| | **Government** | **Corporate** |
|---|---|---|
| **Credit risk** | Effectively nil (sovereign) | Real — the issuer can default |
| **Yield** | Lower | Higher (a **credit spread** over government) |
| **Liquidity** | High (G-secs) | Varies, often lower |

The extra yield on corporate debt is the **credit spread** — compensation for default risk.

### Investment grade vs high yield

| | **Investment grade** | **High yield ("junk")** |
|---|---|---|
| **Rating** | **BBB−/Baa3 and above** | Below BBB− |
| **Default risk** | Low | Elevated |
| **Yield** | Lower | Higher |
| **Behaviour in a crisis** | Defensive | Behaves more like equity |

> ⚠️ **BBB− is the dividing line.** A downgrade from BBB− to BB+ ("falling angel") can force many institutional investors to sell, because mandates often prohibit sub-investment-grade holdings.

## 7.4 Commodities

**Characteristics:**
- **No cash flow** — no interest, no dividend, no rent. The entire return is **price movement**.
- Often carries **storage and insurance costs** (a negative carry).
- Historically an **inflation hedge**, especially energy and metals.
- **Low correlation** with equity and debt, so it can improve diversification.
- Access routes in India: **gold ETFs, Sovereign Gold Bonds, commodity derivatives**, and physical holding.

**Role:** diversification and inflation protection — typically a **small satellite allocation**, not a core holding.

---

## 7.5 Real Estate

**Characteristics:**
- Generates **rental income** plus potential **capital appreciation**
- **Large ticket size** — poor divisibility (you cannot sell one bedroom)
- **Highly illiquid** — sale takes months
- **High transaction costs** — stamp duty, registration, brokerage
- **Location-specific** and hard to diversify
- Valuation is **subjective**, with no continuous price discovery

**Route to liquidity: REITs** (Real Estate Investment Trusts) — listed, SEBI-regulated trusts holding rent-yielding property. They give **exposure to real estate with the liquidity of a listed security** and a small ticket size. **InvITs** do the same for infrastructure assets.

> ⚠️ A **self-occupied house is consumption, not investment** — it produces no income and cannot be partly liquidated (see Chapter 3's financial assets ratio).

---

## 7.6 Structured Products

A **structured product** is a pre-packaged instrument combining a **bond (or deposit)** with a **derivative**, engineered to deliver a specific payoff.

**Typical construction:** most of the money goes into a zero-coupon bond that grows back to par (providing capital protection), and the small remainder buys options on an index for the upside.

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Customised payoff profiles | **Complex** and hard to value |
| Possible capital protection | **Credit risk of the issuer** — "capital protected" is only as good as the issuer |
| Access to otherwise hard-to-reach exposures | **Illiquid**, often with no secondary market |
| | Costs and margins are often **opaque** |

> ⚠️ **Exam point:** "capital protection" is **not** a guarantee — it depends entirely on the **issuer's creditworthiness**. And structured products are typically **not principal-protected before maturity**.

---

## 7.7 Distressed Securities

Securities of companies in or near **financial distress, default or bankruptcy**.

- Trade at **deep discounts** to face value
- Potential for very high returns if the company recovers or the recovery in resolution exceeds the purchase price
- **Very high risk** of total loss
- Require **specialist legal and restructuring expertise** — resolution under the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016** in India
- **Highly illiquid**

**Suitability:** sophisticated, institutional or very high-net-worth investors only — typically accessed through **Category II or III AIFs**.

---

## 7.8 Other Investment Opportunities

**Art, paintings, antiques, collectibles, wine, vintage cars.**

- **No cash flow**; return is purely price appreciation
- **Highly illiquid** with wide bid–ask spreads
- **Valuation is subjective** and expertise-dependent
- **Authenticity and storage/insurance risk**
- **Unregulated** — no investor protection framework
- Often driven by **taste and scarcity** rather than fundamentals

**Suitability:** a **passion allocation** for wealthy investors, not a core portfolio holding.

## 7.9 ⭐ Channels for Making Investments

### Direct investments
The investor buys securities themselves — shares, bonds, deposits.

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| **Lowest cost** — no management fee | Requires **time, skill and temperament** |
| Full **control** | Hard to **diversify** with a small corpus |
| Complete **transparency** | All research and monitoring is the investor's own |

### The role of Registered Investment Advisers (RIAs)
An RIA provides **advice**, for a **fee from the client**, under a **fiduciary duty**, with **advice segregated from distribution**. The investor still holds the assets directly; the adviser supplies the strategy, discipline and review.

### Investments through managed portfolios

Three vehicles — and the comparison below is **essential exam material**.

## ⭐ MF vs PMS vs AIF — The Comparison Table

| Feature | **Mutual Fund** | **Portfolio Management Service** | **Alternative Investment Fund** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Minimum investment** | As low as **₹100–₹500** (SIP) | **₹50 lakh** | **₹1 crore** (₹25 lakh for employees/directors of the AIF) |
| **What you hold** | **Units** of a pooled scheme | **Securities in your own name / demat account** | **Units** of the fund |
| **Pooling** | Fully pooled | **Not pooled** — an individual portfolio | Pooled |
| **Customisation** | None — everyone gets the same portfolio | **Possible** (especially non-discretionary) | Limited — defined by the fund's strategy |
| **Regulation** | SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, **1996** | SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, **2020** | SEBI (AIF) Regulations, **2012** |
| **Target investor** | Retail | HNI | HNI / institutional / sophisticated |
| **Liquidity** | High (open-ended: any business day) | Moderate | Low — usually **close-ended with a lock-in** |
| **Disclosure** | Highest — daily NAV, monthly portfolio | Moderate, to the client | Lowest — to investors only |
| **Fees** | Capped **TER** | Fixed and/or **performance-linked** | Management + **carried interest** |
| **Strategy freedom** | Most restricted | Moderate | **Widest** — can use leverage, derivatives, unlisted assets |

> 🧠 **The one-line memory hook:**
> **MF = pooled units, ₹500, retail, most regulated.**
> **PMS = your own securities, ₹50 lakh, HNI.**
> **AIF = pooled units, ₹1 crore, sophisticated, widest freedom.**
>
> Note the ladder: **as the minimum investment rises, regulation and disclosure fall and strategy freedom rises.** That is the whole logic — regulators protect small investors most tightly and assume large investors can protect themselves.
