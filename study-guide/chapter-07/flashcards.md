# Chapter 7: Introduction to Investments — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **The MF vs PMS vs AIF table is the single highest-yield item in this chapter.**

**Q1.** State the fundamental difference between equity and debt.
> **A:** **Equity = ownership** — a **residual** claim, paid last, unlimited upside, first loss. **Debt = lending** — a **contractual** claim, paid before owners, capped upside.

**Q2.** What is equity's role in a portfolio, and what horizon suits it?
> **A:** The **growth engine** and long-run inflation-beater. Suitable for goals **5–7 years or more** away, where short-run volatility has time to average out.

**Q3.** What is debt's role in a portfolio?
> **A:** **Stability, income and capital preservation** — and dampening overall portfolio volatility. Suitable for short-horizon goals.

**Q4.** Give four differences between listed and unlisted equity.
> **A:** **Liquidity** (high vs very low), **price discovery** (continuous and public vs negotiated), **disclosure** (extensive vs minimal), and **valuation** (market price vs subjective estimate).

**Q5.** What is the credit spread?
> **A:** The **extra yield** a corporate bond offers over a comparable government bond — compensation for **default (credit) risk**.

**Q6.** What is the dividing line between investment grade and high yield?
> **A:** **BBB− / Baa3.** At or above is **investment grade**; below is **high yield ("junk")**.

**Q7.** Why does a downgrade from BBB− to BB+ matter so much?
> **A:** Many institutional mandates **prohibit sub-investment-grade holdings**, so a "falling angel" can trigger forced selling well beyond what the credit change alone would justify.

**Q8.** How do bond prices respond to interest rates?
> **A:** **Inversely** — when rates rise, existing bond prices fall, and vice versa.

**Q9.** What is distinctive about commodities as an asset class?
> **A:** They generate **no cash flow** — no interest, dividend or rent — so the **entire return is price movement**. They often carry storage/insurance costs, act as an **inflation hedge**, and have **low correlation** with equity and debt.

**Q10.** How can an Indian investor gain commodity exposure?
> **A:** **Gold ETFs, Sovereign Gold Bonds, commodity derivatives**, and physical holding.

**Q11.** Name five drawbacks of direct real estate investment.
> **A:** **Illiquidity**, **indivisibility** (large ticket size), **high transaction costs** (stamp duty, registration, brokerage), **location concentration**, and **subjective valuation** with no continuous price discovery.

**Q12.** What is a REIT, and what problem does it solve?
> **A:** A listed, SEBI-regulated trust holding rent-yielding property. It solves real estate's **illiquidity and large ticket size** — giving property exposure with the **liquidity of a listed security**. **InvITs** do the same for infrastructure.

**Q13.** Is a self-occupied house an investment?
> **A:** **No — it is consumption.** It produces no income and cannot be partly liquidated.

**Q14.** What is a structured product, and how is it typically built?
> **A:** A pre-packaged combination of a **bond (or deposit) plus a derivative**. Typically most of the money buys a **zero-coupon bond** that grows back to par (capital protection), and the remainder buys **options** for upside.

**Q15.** Why is "capital protection" in a structured product not a guarantee?
> **A:** It depends entirely on the **issuer's creditworthiness** — if the issuer defaults, the protection fails. Such products are also typically **not principal-protected before maturity**.

**Q16.** What are distressed securities, and who should hold them?
> **A:** Securities of companies in or near **default or bankruptcy**, trading at deep discounts. Very high risk of total loss, requiring **specialist legal and restructuring expertise** (in India, resolution under the **IBC, 2016**). Suitable only for **sophisticated/institutional investors**, typically via Category II or III AIFs.

**Q17.** What are the drawbacks of art and collectibles as investments?
> **A:** **No cash flow**, **highly illiquid** with wide spreads, **subjective valuation**, **authenticity and storage risk**, and **completely unregulated** — no investor protection framework.

**Q18.** What are the trade-offs of investing directly?
> **A:** **Lowest cost** and full control, but it demands **time, skill and temperament**, and diversification is hard with a small corpus.

**Q19.** What defines a Registered Investment Adviser's model?
> **A:** **Advice for a fee paid by the client**, under a **fiduciary duty**, with **advice segregated from distribution**.

**Q20.** State the minimum investment for a mutual fund, PMS and AIF.
> **A:** **MF ₹100–₹500** · **PMS ₹50 lakh** · **AIF ₹1 crore** (₹25 lakh for employees/directors of the AIF).

**Q21.** What is the crucial structural difference between a mutual fund and a PMS?
> **A:** A mutual fund gives you **units of a pooled scheme**. A PMS holds **securities in your own name / demat account** — it is **not pooled**.

**Q22.** Which regulations govern MFs, PMS and AIFs, and from which years?
> **A:** SEBI **(Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996**; SEBI **(Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020**; SEBI **(Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012**.

**Q23.** Rank MF, PMS and AIF by disclosure and by strategy freedom.
> **A:** **Disclosure:** MF (highest — daily NAV, monthly portfolio) > PMS > AIF (lowest). **Strategy freedom:** AIF (widest — leverage, derivatives, unlisted) > PMS > MF (most restricted).

**Q24.** What is the underlying logic of the MF/PMS/AIF regulatory ladder?
> **A:** As the **minimum investment rises**, regulation and disclosure **fall** and strategy freedom **rises** — regulators protect small investors most tightly and assume large investors can protect themselves.

**Q25.** Which asset classes produce no cash flow at all?
> **A:** **Commodities** and **art/collectibles** — their entire return is price appreciation.

**Q26.** How are MF, PMS and AIF fees structured?
> **A:** **MF** — a capped **Total Expense Ratio**. **PMS** — fixed and/or **performance-linked** fees. **AIF** — management fee plus **carried interest**.

**Q27.** State the risk–return principle and its critical caveat.
> **A:** **Higher expected return goes with higher risk.** The caveat: higher risk means a **wider range of outcomes, including worse ones** — it is never a promise of higher return. If risk guaranteed return, it would not be risk.

**Q28.** Roughly order the asset classes by ascending risk.
> **A:** **Cash → government debt → high-grade corporate debt → high-yield debt → real estate → equity → derivatives/distressed.**

**Q29.** Why does equity have no maturity?
> **A:** It is a **perpetual** instrument representing **ownership** — there is no date on which the company must repay a shareholder, unlike a bond's principal.

**Q30.** Which is senior in liquidation — equity or debt?
> **A:** **Debt.** Lenders are paid before preference shareholders, who are paid before ordinary shareholders. Equity holders receive only the **residual**.
