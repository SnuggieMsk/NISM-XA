# Chapter 17: Operational Aspects of Investment Management — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **The nominee-is-a-trustee rule, the third-party payment exceptions and the NRE/NRO/FCNR grid are near-certain exam marks.**

**Q1.** What is the maximum number of holders in a folio or demat account, who is the "tax owner", and what is the default mode of holding if the box is left blank?
> **A:** **Three** holders. The **first holder** is the tax owner — their **PAN, KYC and tax status** govern the folio and **all payouts go to their registered bank account**. If the mode of holding is left blank with more than one holder, the default is **Joint** — every holder must then sign every instruction.

**Q2.** Distinguish the three modes of holding.
> **A:** **Single** — the sole holder signs. **Joint** — **all** holders must sign. **Anyone or Survivor** — **any one** holder can sign, and the survivors continue the folio on a death.

**Q3.** Why must an investment in a minor's name be made by a guardian, and which investor class did RBI de-recognise in 2003?
> **A:** Because a **minor's contract is void** under the **Indian Contract Act, 1872** — the folio is in the minor's **sole name**, operated by a **natural guardian (father/mother)** or a **court-appointed guardian**. **Overseas Corporate Bodies (OCBs)** were de-recognised by RBI in **2003**.

**Q4.** Name the terms-of-offer document for a mutual fund, a PMS and an AIF.
> **A:** Mutual fund — **SID + SAI + KIM**. PMS — the **Disclosure Document** plus the PMS agreement. AIF — the **Private Placement Memorandum (PPM)** plus the contribution agreement.

**Q5.** List the seven steps of client on-boarding.
> **A:** (1) **Understand the client** — fact-find, **risk profiling**, suitability; (2) explain the **terms of offer**; (3) sign the **agreement**; (4) **PAN and KYC** with IPV/V-CIP; (5) complete the **application** with bank mandate, nomination and FATCA/CRS; (6) **payment from the investor's own account**; (7) **folio/account creation**.

**Q6.** Under which section and Act is PAN issued, what is its format, and what does the 4th character mean?
> **A:** **Section 139A of the Income Tax Act, 1961**; **10 characters — 5 letters, 4 digits, 1 letter**. The **4th character is the holder's status**: **P** = individual, **C** = Company, **H** = HUF, **F** = Firm/LLP, **A** = AOP, **T** = Trust, **B** = BOI, **L** = Local authority, **J** = juridical person, **G** = Government. The **5th** is the first letter of the **surname/entity name**.

**Q7.** What is the micro-SIP PAN exemption, and who can use it?
> **A:** Investments up to **₹50,000 per investor per financial year**, aggregated across the schemes of one mutual fund, are **exempt from PAN**; a **PEKRN** is issued instead. Available only to **individuals, NRIs, minors and sole proprietors** — not to HUFs or other non-individuals. **Residents of Sikkim** enjoy a separate PAN exemption.

**Q8.** Which law is the parent of KYC, and what are the six Officially Valid Documents?
> **A:** The **Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002** with the **PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005**. The six OVDs: **passport, driving licence, proof of possession of Aadhaar, Voter's Identity Card, NREGA job card** signed by a State Government officer, and the **National Population Register letter**. **PAN is NOT an OVD** — it is separately mandatory.

**Q9.** What is a "deemed OVD" and what are its time limits?
> **A:** Where the OVD carries an **outdated address**, a **utility bill not more than 2 months old**, a municipal/property tax receipt, a pension payment order or an employer-allotted accommodation letter may be used temporarily — and a proper **OVD with the updated address must follow within 3 months**.

**Q10.** What is V-CIP, and what is the cap on Aadhaar OTP-based e-KYC?
> **A:** **V-CIP** is the **Video-based Customer Identification Process**, a permitted alternative to physical IPV. **Aadhaar OTP-based e-KYC** caps the investment at **₹50,000 per financial year per mutual fund**; **biometric** Aadhaar e-KYC has **no such cap**.

**Q11.** Distinguish a KRA from the CKYCR.
> **A:** A **KRA** is set up under the **SEBI (KYC Registration Agency) Regulations, 2011** and covers the **securities market only** (CVL, NDML, CAMS, KFin, DotEx). The **CKYCR** is operated by **CERSAI** under **Rule 9(1A) of the PML Rules**, covers **all financial sectors — banking, securities, insurance, pensions** — and issues a **14-digit KIN**.

**Q12.** Within how many days must KYC data reach the CKYCR, how long must PMLA records be preserved, and which reports go to FIU-IND?
> **A:** Uploaded **within 10 days** of commencing the relationship; records preserved for at least **5 years**. **CTR** (Cash Transaction Reports) and **STR** (Suspicious Transaction Reports) go to **FIU-IND**. **Tipping off** the client about an STR is **an offence**.

**Q13.** Describe dematerialisation, naming the form, the reference number and the time frame.
> **A:** Converting **physical certificates into electronic form**: the investor submits a **DRF** with certificates **defaced "Surrendered for Dematerialisation"**, the DP generates a **DRN**, the RTA verifies and cancels the certificates, and the depository **CREDITS** the demat account — typically within about **30 days**.

**Q14.** Describe rematerialisation and state what changes.
> **A:** Converting **electronic holdings back into physical certificates** using an **RRF** and an **RRN**; the demat account is **DEBITED** and the investor receives **fresh certificates with NEW distinctive numbers**. It is largely impractical now, because **physical transfer of listed shares has been barred since 1 April 2019**.

**Q15.** Why can a certificate in the names "A and B" not be dematerialised into an account of "B and A"?
> **A:** Because the **names AND their order must match exactly**. The fix is **transposition** — changing the order of the **same** names, which is permitted and is **not** treated as a transfer.

**Q16.** What is an ISIN, and how do NSDL and CDSL account numbers differ?
> **A:** An **ISIN** is the **12-character** International Securities Identification Number, unique to each security. **NSDL** accounts are **"IN" + an 8-character DP ID + an 8-digit client ID**; **CDSL** accounts are a **16-digit numeric BO ID**.

**Q17.** Distinguish a General Power of Attorney from a Specific/Limited PoA.
> **A:** A **GPoA** gives **broad authority over all or most of the donor's affairs** — very high risk. A **Specific/Limited PoA** authorises **only the acts expressly listed** (e.g., debiting a demat account for settlement obligations, or operating one account for one portfolio). Financial services always prefer the **limited** form.

**Q18.** State four SEBI restrictions on a broker PoA, and say what a DDPI covers.
> **A:** A PoA is **optional, never mandatory**; it may authorise transfer of securities **only for the client's own settlement obligations**; it must **not** permit **merging of client balances**, opening/closing accounts or loans against securities; and it must state the client's **right to revoke**. The modern replacement, the **DDPI (Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction)**, permits **only two** things — **settlement delivery** and **pledge/re-pledge for margin**.

**Q19.** When does a PoA cease, and can a PoA holder make a nomination?
> **A:** It ceases on **revocation, the death, insanity or insolvency of the donor**, completion of the purpose, or expiry of its term. A **PoA holder can NEVER make or change a nomination** — nomination is a personal act of the investor.

**Q20.** How does FEMA residency differ from Income Tax residency?
> **A:** **FEMA** looks at **days plus the PURPOSE of the stay** — someone leaving for employment or an uncertain period becomes non-resident **immediately** — and it decides **which accounts and assets you may hold**. The **Income Tax Act** applies a pure **day-count** test (182 days, or 60 + 365) and decides **how you are taxed**. A person can be resident under one and non-resident under the other in the same year.

**Q21.** Compare NRE, NRO and FCNR(B) on currency, source of funds, repatriability and tax.
> **A:** **NRE** — rupees, **foreign** earnings, **fully repatriable**, interest **tax-exempt**. **NRO** — rupees, **Indian** income (rent, dividend, pension), **taxable**, principal repatriation capped at **USD 1 million per financial year**. **FCNR(B)** — **foreign currency**, **term deposits only of 1 to 5 years**, fully repatriable, interest **tax-exempt**, and **no exchange-rate risk** because the deposit stays in the foreign currency.

**Q22.** What happens to an NRI's accounts on returning to India permanently?
> **A:** NRE and NRO accounts are **re-designated as resident accounts**; NRE balances may move to an **RFC (Resident Foreign Currency)** account; **FCNR deposits may run to maturity** at the contracted rate and then convert to RFC. **NRE/FCNR interest ceases to be tax-exempt**, and the person may enjoy **RNOR** status for a couple of years, during which foreign income is generally not taxed in India.

**Q23.** State the NRI limits under the Portfolio Investment Scheme and the demat structure required.
> **A:** **5% of a company's paid-up capital per individual NRI**; **10% for all NRIs in aggregate**, raisable to **24%** by a board resolution plus a **special resolution**. Trades route through **one designated AD bank branch** and are **delivery-based only** — no intraday, no short selling. **Separate demat accounts** are needed for **repatriable (NRE)** and **non-repatriable (NRO)** holdings, with **PIS** shares kept apart from non-PIS shares.

**Q24.** Which investments are closed to NRIs, and what happens to an existing PPF?
> **A:** **New PPF, NSC, SCSS and KVP** accounts, and **agricultural land, plantations and farmhouses**. An existing **PPF may be continued to maturity but not extended**; an **SCSS account must be closed**.

**Q25.** Contrast PMLA and FEMA in one line each.
> **A:** **PMLA, 2002** is a **criminal** law asking "**is this money clean?**" — KYC, 5-year records, UBO/PEP, CTR/STR to FIU-IND, no third-party payments. **FEMA, 1999** is a **civil** law (it replaced the criminal FERA, 1973; contraventions can be **compounded**) asking "**may this money cross the border?**" — account types, PIS limits, repatriation ceilings and the **LRS limit of USD 2,50,000 per financial year**.

**Q26.** Where does a KYC-compliant client change their address, and in what order must a name change be done?
> **A:** Through the **KRA/CKYC** — the update then flows to **every** intermediary (a non-KYC-compliant investor updates folio by folio with the AMC/RTA). A name change must go **PAN first → then KRA/CKYC → then folios and demat accounts**, or every record mismatches.

**Q27.** What is a lien, and what is its demat equivalent?
> **A:** A **charge created over an investment as security for a debt** — the units are **frozen** and cannot be redeemed, switched or transferred without the lienholder's release, though the **investor still owns them**. On repayment the lien is **lifted**; on default it is **invoked**. In **demat** form the equivalent is a **pledge** (pledgor/pledgee) recorded through the depository.

**Q28.** Distinguish transfer from transmission.
> **A:** **Transfer** is a **voluntary act between living persons** (sale or gift). **Transmission** is the passing of an investment to survivors, nominees or legal heirs **on DEATH** — an operation of law. Open-ended mutual fund units in SoA form **cannot be transferred**, but they can always be **transmitted**.

**Q29.** A sole holder dies with no nomination registered. What does the claimant need?
> **A:** Death certificate, KYC and bank proof of the claimant, **plus a succession certificate, probate of the will or letter of administration** — or, under **AMFI's simplified route for smaller claims (currently up to ₹5 lakh per folio)**, an **indemnity bond, affidavits from all legal heirs and NOCs from the non-claimant heirs**.

**Q30.** State the single most important rule about nominees — and its one exception.
> **A:** **A nominee is a TRUSTEE, not an owner.** The nominee receives the asset so the intermediary gets a valid discharge, but holds it **for the legal heirs**; nomination **does not override succession law**. **The exception is insurance** — under **Section 39 of the Insurance Act, 1938** (as amended in 2015), a **beneficial nominee** (**parent, spouse or child**) **is** beneficially entitled to the claim.

**Q31.** Who may NOT make a nomination, and how many holders must sign one?
> **A:** **Non-individuals** — companies, trusts, societies, partnership firms — the **Karta of an HUF**, and a **PoA holder**. Nomination is also **not permitted in a minor's folio**. **ALL joint holders must sign** a nomination, even in an **"Anyone or Survivor"** folio. Mutual funds have traditionally allowed **up to three nominees** with percentages (equal if unstated), and a fresh nomination **cancels** the earlier one.

**Q32.** Distinguish absolute from conditional assignment, and state the governing section.
> **A:** **Section 38 of the Insurance Act, 1938.** **Absolute** — **all rights transfer permanently and unconditionally**, the assignee becomes owner, and the existing **nomination is cancelled**. **Conditional** — rights transfer **subject to conditions** and **revert to the assignor** on, say, repayment of the loan or survival to maturity; typically used when assigning a policy to a **bank as collateral**.

**Q33.** From when is an assignment effective, and what may the insurer do?
> **A:** From the **date the notice of assignment is served on the insurer**; where there are several assignments, **priority follows the date of notice**. The insurer may **decline** an assignment that is not bona fide, not in the policyholder's interest or against public interest, recording reasons **within 30 days**; the aggrieved party may refer it to **IRDAI within 30 days**. **MWP Act, 1874 policies cannot be assigned.**

**Q34.** Give three differences between nomination and assignment.
> **A:** Nomination **names who receives money on death**, **does not transfer ownership**, is **freely revocable**, and sits in **Section 39**. Assignment **transfers ownership during the assignor's lifetime**, is **irrevocable if absolute**, and sits in **Section 38**. Also: **health and general insurance policies are generally not assignable**, while life policies are.

**Q35.** What happens on the day a minor turns 18, and how is it fixed?
> **A:** The folio is **frozen for financial transactions** and **all standing instructions — SIP, SWP, STP — cease**. To unfreeze, the now-major files the **MAM form** with a **banker-attested signature**, **PAN and KYC in their own name**, **a bank account in their own name**, and a **fresh nomination**. The guardian's authority **ends completely**.

**Q36.** From which accounts may a minor's investment be funded, and how is a minor's income taxed?
> **A:** Only the **minor's own account**, the **guardian's account**, or a **joint account of minor and guardian**; anything else is a **third-party payment**, permitted only for a **parent/grandparent/related person up to ₹50,000 per transaction** with a declaration. The minor's income is **clubbed with the parent's** under **Section 64(1A)**.

**Q37.** What must a resident do on becoming an NRI?
> **A:** **Re-designate the resident savings account as an NRO account** (it cannot continue as a resident account), open **NRE/FCNR** if needed, change the **KYC status to NRI** with the KRA and every intermediary, register a **new bank mandate**, re-declare **FATCA/CRS**, and move demat holdings to a **non-repatriable (NRO) demat**, opening a **PIS** account for secondary-market equity trading.

**Q38.** Can a name be added to, or deleted from, an existing folio or demat account?
> **A:** **No.** A holder **cannot be added** — a **new folio/account** must be opened — and a holder **can only be deleted on DEATH**, through transmission. What *is* permitted is **transposition**: changing the **order of the same names**.

**Q39.** Explain the change-of-bank-mandate cooling-off rule and why it exists.
> **A:** If a **redemption is requested within the cooling-off window (commonly 10 calendar days) of a change-of-bank request**, the proceeds go to the **OLD, already-registered** account. It defeats the classic fraud of changing bank details and immediately redeeming. A folio may carry **up to about 5 registered accounts for individuals and 10 for non-individuals**, one marked **default**, all in the name of the **first/sole holder**.

**Q40.** How much cash may a mutual fund accept, and how long is a cheque valid?
> **A:** Cash **up to ₹50,000 per investor per financial year** from specified small investors — and **all repayments must go through the banking channel only**, never in cash. A cheque is **valid for 3 months** from issue (reduced from six months with effect from **1 April 2012**) under the **Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881**, and must be **CTS-2010** compliant.

**Q41.** Contrast NEFT and RTGS, and say which systems NPCI runs.
> **A:** Both are **RBI** systems. **NEFT** settles on a **deferred net** basis in batches with **no minimum or maximum**, 24×7×365. **RTGS** settles in **real time on a gross basis**, **minimum ₹2,00,000**, **no upper limit**, also 24×7. **NPCI** runs **IMPS, UPI, NACH, AePS, BBPS and RuPay** — and **NACH** (which replaced ECS) powers **SIP** debits, alongside **UPI AutoPay/e-mandates**.

**Q42.** Define a third-party payment, say why it is prohibited, and list the exceptions.
> **A:** A payment from a **bank account other than the investor's (the first named holder's)**. It is barred under **PMLA** because it **breaks the audit trail of the money**. Exceptions: (1) **parent/grandparent/related person paying for a MINOR — up to ₹50,000 per transaction**; (2) an **employer paying for an employee through payroll deduction**; (3) a **custodian paying for an FPI/client**; (4) an **AMC or corporate paying commission in units** to an empanelled distributor. Each needs a **Third Party Payment Declaration** from both parties. On the payout side there is **no exception** — proceeds always go to the **first holder's registered account**.

**Q43.** What is the Positive Pay System, and when does a customer bear zero liability for an unauthorised electronic transaction?
> **A:** **Positive Pay** (RBI, from **1 January 2021**) requires the **issuer of a cheque to re-confirm its date, beneficiary and amount electronically** before payment, for cheques **above ₹50,000**. A customer bears **zero liability** where the loss arises from the **bank's negligence or a third-party breach** and is **reported within 3 working days**; **4–7 working days** brings a **capped** liability; beyond that, the bank's board policy applies. Loss from the **customer's own negligence** (sharing an OTP) is borne entirely by the customer. Report fraud on **1930** / cybercrime.gov.in.

**Q44.** What records must a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser maintain, for how long, and what may an IA never do?
> **A:** **KYC**, **risk profiling with the client's acceptance**, **suitability assessments**, the **signed advisory agreement**, terms & conditions and disclosures, **the advice given AND its RATIONALE**, **fee invoices and receipts**, and complaint records — for a **minimum of 5 years**, and **until conclusion** where a dispute is pending. An IA may **never take custody of client funds or securities and never accept cash**; fees come only in the adviser's registered name through the banking channel.

**Q45.** Name the mutual fund exchange platforms, say who may use them, and explain transaction feeds.
> **A:** **BSE StAR MF** and **NSE MFSS/NMF II** — plus the AMCs' own **MF Utility (MFU)** with its **Common Account Number (CAN)**, which is not an exchange. Permitted since **November 2009** for brokers, later extended to **clearing members and DPs**, then to **SEBI-registered Investment Advisers** and to **MFDs for execution only**. A **transaction feed** is a **daily machine-readable file of transactions and holdings** from **RTAs, exchanges and depositories** that removes manual data entry and enables **daily reconciliation, consolidated reporting and compliance evidence**. The client's own equivalent is the **CAS** — monthly where there were transactions, half-yearly otherwise.
