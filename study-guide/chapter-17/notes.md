# Chapter 17: Operational Aspects of Investment Management — Short Notes

> **Module 6 · Operations, Regulatory Environment, Compliance and Ethics (30 marks).**
> This is the **procedural** chapter — the plumbing that sits under every piece of advice you will ever give. There is almost no mathematics here, but there are a very large number of **exact rules, exact document lists and exact rupee thresholds**, and the exam tests them literally. The good news: almost every rule in this chapter exists for one of only **three reasons** — (1) *prove who the investor is* (PMLA), (2) *prove the money is the investor's own* (PMLA again), (3) *make sure the right person gets the money back* (succession law). If you can attach a rule to its reason, you will remember it.

## 17.1 Investor Types and the On-boarding Process

### 17.1.1 ⭐ Who Can Invest?

Indian financial products are open to a long list of investor categories. The list matters because **the documents you collect change with the category**.

| Category | Who exactly | Signs / represented by |
|---|---|---|
| **Resident individuals** | Any person aged **18 or above**, singly or jointly (**maximum 3 holders**) | Themselves |
| **Minors** | Below **18** — folio must be in the minor's **sole** name | **Natural guardian** (father/mother) or **court-appointed** guardian |
| **HUF** | Hindu Undivided Family | The **Karta** |
| **Partnership firms / LLPs** | Registered or unregistered firms; LLPs under the LLP Act, 2008 | Authorised partner(s) |
| **Companies / bodies corporate** | Private, public, PSUs | Authorised signatories under a **board resolution** |
| **Trusts, societies, charitable and religious institutions** | Where their own trust deed / bye-laws permit the investment | Trustees / office bearers |
| **Banks, co-operative banks, RRBs, NBFCs, financial institutions** | Subject to their own regulator's exposure rules | Authorised officials |
| **Mutual funds, AIFs, insurance companies, pension/PF/gratuity/superannuation funds** | Institutional money | Authorised officials |
| **NRIs, PIOs and OCIs** | On a **repatriable** or **non-repatriable** basis | Themselves or a **PoA holder** |
| **FPIs** | Foreign Portfolio Investors registered with SEBI | Custodian / authorised representative |
| **Armed forces and paramilitary funds; scientific and industrial research organisations** | Specific approved funds | Authorised officials |
| **International multilateral agencies** | Approved by the Government of India | Authorised officials |

> 🧠 **Memory hook — "the three gates."** An investor can walk in only if all three gates open: **capacity** (18+, or a guardian for a minor, or a valid authorisation for an entity), **eligibility** (FEMA lets this person hold this asset), and **compliance** (PAN + KYC done).

**Who generally CANNOT invest (or is restricted):**

| Blocked / restricted | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Minors in their own right** | A minor's contract is **void** under the Indian Contract Act, 1872 — hence the guardian |
| **Overseas Corporate Bodies (OCBs)** | **De-recognised by RBI in 2003** as an eligible investor class |
| **US persons and residents of Canada** (in most mutual fund schemes) | The reporting burden of **FATCA**; each AMC states its own position in the SID |
| **Persons in FATF non-compliant / high-risk jurisdictions** | PMLA and FATF country risk |
| **Persons barred by SEBI or a court** | Debarment orders |
| **NRIs — for PPF, NSC, SCSS, KVP, agricultural land, plantations, farmhouses** | FEMA and small-savings scheme rules |

> ⚠️ **Trap.** "Who can invest" is *product-specific*, not universal. An NRI can freely buy Indian **mutual funds** but cannot open a **new PPF** account. A charitable trust can buy mutual funds only if **its own trust deed** permits it. Never answer this question in the abstract.

### 17.1.2 ⭐ The Client On-boarding Process

Think of on-boarding as a **seven-step assembly line**. Miss a step and the application is rejected — the single largest cause of operational grief in an advisory practice.

| # | Step | What actually happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Understand the client** | Fact-find, financial position, goals, **risk profiling** — and for an IA, a documented **suitability** assessment |
| 2 | **Explain the terms of offer** | Give and explain the offer document for the product chosen (see 17.1.3) |
| 3 | **Sign the agreement** | For an SEBI-registered IA, a **mandatory written investment advisory agreement**; for PMS, the PMS agreement; for AIF, the contribution agreement |
| 4 | **PAN and KYC** | PAN, KYC through a **KRA** / **CKYC**, **In-Person Verification (IPV)** or **V-CIP** |
| 5 | **Complete the application** | Mandatory investor information, bank mandate, **nomination or opt-out**, FATCA/CRS declaration, UBO for non-individuals |
| 6 | **Make the payment** | From the **investor's own bank account** — third-party payments are prohibited except in narrow cases |
| 7 | **Folio / account creation** | The AMC/RTA/DP opens the **folio or account** and issues a statement of account or credits the demat account |

> 🧠 **"KAP-BAN":** **K**YC → **A**greement → **P**AN → **B**ank mandate → **A**pplication → **N**omination. If any letter is missing, so is the account.

### 17.1.3 Terms of Offer

The **terms of offer** is the document that legally defines what the investor is buying. Different products, different documents — and the adviser's duty is to make sure the client has actually seen the right one.

| Product | Terms-of-offer document |
|---|---|
| **Mutual fund** | **SID** (Scheme Information Document) + **SAI** (Statement of Additional Information) + **KIM** (Key Information Memorandum, attached to the application form) |
| **Portfolio Management Service** | **Disclosure Document** + the **PMS agreement** |
| **Alternative Investment Fund** | **Private Placement Memorandum (PPM)** + contribution agreement |
| **Life / general insurance** | **Prospectus**, **benefit illustration** and the **policy document** |
| **NPS** | Offer document / subscriber registration form through a **PoP** |
| **Bonds, NCDs, IPOs** | **Prospectus** / information memorandum / term sheet |
| **Bank fixed deposit** | Application form plus the bank's schedule of terms |
| **Investment advice itself** | The **advisory agreement**, terms & conditions and the **risk profile** signed off by the client |

### 17.1.5 ⭐ Mandatory Investor Information

What every application form must capture, and **why**:

| Information | Why it is mandatory |
|---|---|
| **Full name** (as per PAN) | Must match PAN and KYC records exactly, or the folio is rejected |
| **PAN** | Tax trail and unique identification |
| **Date of birth** | Age/capacity; senior-citizen benefits; minor status |
| **Address** (Indian + overseas for NRIs) | Communication, jurisdiction, FEMA status |
| **Mobile number and email** | Mandatory — statements, OTPs, alerts. Must be the **investor's own** |
| **Status** — resident/NRI, individual/non-individual | Determines taxation, repatriability, documents |
| **Occupation and gross annual income / net worth** | **AML risk profiling** — does the investment fit the declared means? |
| **Bank account details** — name, number, IFSC, MICR | Payouts go **only** here; also anchors the anti-third-party rule |
| **Mode of holding** | Who can sign, and who gets the money |
| **Nomination or opt-out** | Smooth transmission on death |
| **FATCA/CRS**: tax residency, TIN, country of birth | Global tax information exchange |
| **PEP declaration** | Enhanced due diligence under PMLA |
| **Guardian details** (minor) / **UBO** (non-individual) | Who really controls the money |
| **Signature** (and specimen) | Authenticates all later instructions |

> ⚠️ **Trap.** SEBI/AMFI require the **mobile number and email to belong to the investor** (or to specified close relatives, with a declaration of relationship). A distributor or adviser inserting **their own** contact details into a client's folio is a serious compliance breach — it cuts the investor off from the alerts designed to protect them.

### 17.1.6 ⭐ The Investor Folio or Account

A **folio** is the investor's unique account number with a **mutual fund/AMC**, maintained by the **RTA** (CAMS, KFin Technologies etc.). It is the container that holds: the names of holders, **mode of holding**, PAN and KYC status, address and contact details, **bank mandate(s)**, **nomination**, tax status and all transactions.

**Modes of holding** — the single most consequential choice on the form:

| Mode | Who must sign a transaction | On death of a holder |
|---|---|---|
| **Single** | The sole holder | Goes to the **nominee**, else legal heirs |
| **Joint** | **ALL holders must sign** — every time | Units are **transmitted to survivors** |
| **Anyone or Survivor** | **Any one** holder can sign | The **survivor(s)** continue the folio |

> 🧠 **Default rule:** if there is more than one holder and the mode of holding is **left blank**, the folio is treated as **"Joint"** — meaning every future instruction needs every signature. This causes enormous practical pain, so the adviser should always check the box.

**Other folio rules worth memorising:**

- **Maximum three holders**. The **first holder** is the tax owner — their PAN, KYC and tax status govern the folio, and **all payouts go to the first holder's bank account**.
- Nomination and the **change of address must be signed as per the mode of holding**, *except* **nomination, which always needs ALL holders' signatures** — even in an "Anyone or Survivor" folio.
- Units may be held in **Statement of Account (SoA)** form with the RTA, or in **demat** form with a DP.
- **Consolidation of folios**: an investor with several folios in the same AMC, with **identical holders, identical order of names, identical mode of holding and identical PAN/KYC**, can request consolidation into one folio. It reduces paperwork and produces one clean statement. Folios with **different** holders or a different order of names **cannot** be merged.
- **CAS (Consolidated Account Statement)** stitches everything together across AMCs and depositories against a common PAN — issued **monthly** where there were transactions, and **half-yearly** (for March and September) including zero-balance folios.

## 17.2 PAN, KYC and the Central KYC Process

### 17.2.1 ⭐ Permanent Account Number (PAN)

A **10-character alphanumeric** identifier issued by the **Income Tax Department** under **Section 139A of the Income Tax Act, 1961**. Format: **5 letters, 4 digits, 1 letter** (e.g., ABCPD1234E).

The **4th character encodes the holder's status** — a free mark in the exam:

| 4th character | Status |
|---|---|
| **P** | Individual (**P**erson) |
| **C** | **C**ompany |
| **H** | **H**UF |
| **F** | **F**irm / LLP |
| **A** | **A**ssociation of Persons (AOP) |
| **T** | **T**rust |
| **B** | **B**ody of Individuals |
| **L** | **L**ocal authority |
| **J** | Artificial **j**uridical person |
| **G** | **G**overnment |

The **5th character** is the first letter of the **surname** for an individual, or of the **entity's name** otherwise.

**PAN exemptions — the two you must know:**

| Exemption | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Micro-SIP / small investments** | Investments up to **₹50,000 per investor per financial year, aggregated across schemes of one mutual fund**, are exempt from PAN. The investor is issued a **PEKRN** (PAN Exempt KYC Reference Number). Available only to **individuals, NRIs, minors and sole proprietors** — **not** to HUFs, companies or other non-individuals |
| **Residents of Sikkim** | Exempt from PAN for mutual fund investments, on production of proof of Sikkim residence |

> ⚠️ **Trap.** PAN must be **linked to Aadhaar**. An unlinked PAN becomes **inoperative**, which means higher TDS and rejected transactions — a live operational issue an adviser must check for every client.

---

### 17.2.2 ⭐ The KYC Process

KYC exists because of the **Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002** and the **PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005**. Every SEBI-registered intermediary is a "reporting entity" and must perform it.

**The four pillars of KYC:**

1. **Customer identification** — Proof of Identity (PoI)
2. **Address verification** — Proof of Address (PoA)
3. **In-Person Verification (IPV)** or **V-CIP**
4. **Ongoing due diligence** — monitoring transactions against the declared profile

**Officially Valid Documents (OVDs)** under the PML Rules — the definitive list of **six**:

| # | OVD |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Passport** |
| 2 | **Driving licence** |
| 3 | **Proof of possession of Aadhaar number** (with the first 8 digits redacted where required) |
| 4 | **Voter's Identity Card** issued by the Election Commission |
| 5 | **NREGA job card** duly signed by a State Government officer |
| 6 | **Letter issued by the National Population Register** containing name and address |

> ⚠️ **Trap.** **PAN is NOT an OVD.** PAN is *separately and additionally* mandatory. Nor is a **ration card, bank passbook or utility bill** an OVD in its own right.

**Deemed OVDs** — if the OVD carries an **outdated address**, the client may temporarily submit a **utility bill not more than two months old** (electricity, telephone, post-paid mobile, piped gas, water), a **municipal/property tax receipt**, a **pension payment order**, or an **employer-allotted accommodation letter** (from Government, PSU, bank, financial institution or listed company). The client must then produce an **OVD with the updated address within three months**.

**IPV and its digital equivalents:**

| Method | Detail |
|---|---|
| **IPV** | Mandatory. Can be done by AMC/KRA officials, **NISM-certified distributors holding an ARN** (for their own clients), or scheduled commercial bank officials |
| **V-CIP** | **Video-based Customer Identification Process** — a live, consent-based video call with liveness checks; a permitted alternative to physical IPV |
| **Aadhaar OTP-based e-KYC** | Fully digital, but the investment is capped at **₹50,000 per financial year per mutual fund** |
| **Aadhaar biometric e-KYC** | Done at an authorised point; **no investment cap** |

**KYC statuses** — a modern refinement worth knowing. A client's KYC record with a KRA now carries a status such as **Validated**, **Registered/Verified** or **On Hold**. A **KYC-Validated** record (PAN–Aadhaar linked and validated against the income tax database) travels seamlessly to any new intermediary; a merely **Registered** record may require the client to redo KYC when opening an account elsewhere. "On Hold" transactions are blocked until the deficiency is cured.

**Additional non-individual requirements:** board resolution / list of authorised signatories, certificate of incorporation, memorandum & articles, trust deed or partnership deed, and identification of the **Ultimate Beneficial Owner** — the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the entity.

### 17.2.3 ⭐ KRAs and the Central KYC Registry

The whole point of both is to make the client do KYC **once**.

| | **KRA (KYC Registration Agency)** | **CKYCR (Central KYC Records Registry)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Set up under** | **SEBI (KRA) Regulations, 2011** | **Rule 9(1A) of the PML Rules**; operated by **CERSAI** |
| **Scope** | **Securities market only** — SEBI-registered intermediaries | **All financial sectors** — banking, securities, **insurance, pensions** |
| **Regulator** | SEBI | Ministry of Finance / Department of Revenue |
| **Identifier issued** | KYC record against **PAN** | **14-digit KIN** (KYC Identification Number) |
| **Who uploads** | The intermediary that first does the KYC | Every reporting entity, **within 10 days** of starting the relationship |
| **Who can download** | Any other SEBI intermediary, with client consent | Any reporting entity, with client consent |
| **Examples** | **CVL** (CDSL Ventures), **NDML** (NSDL Database Management), **CAMS**, **KFin Technologies**, **DotEx** (NSE) | A single registry — **CERSAI** |

> 🧠 **The clean distinction:** **KRA = securities market. CKYC = the whole financial system.** If the question mentions an insurance policy or a bank account in the same breath as KYC portability, the answer is **CKYC**.

**Record keeping:** under PMLA, records of identity and transactions must be preserved for at least **five years** — identity records for five years from the end of the business relationship, transaction records for five years from the date of the transaction. KRAs preserve KYC records for the longer period SEBI prescribes.

**Reporting under PMLA:** reporting entities file **Cash Transaction Reports (CTR)**, **Suspicious Transaction Reports (STR)** and other prescribed reports with **FIU-IND** (Financial Intelligence Unit – India). An STR must **never** be disclosed to the client — that is **tipping off**, and it is an offence.

## 17.3 Dematerialisation and Rematerialisation

### The architecture

- Governed by the **Depositories Act, 1996**.
- Two depositories: **NSDL** (1996) and **CDSL** (1999).
- The investor never deals with the depository directly — always through a **Depository Participant (DP)**: a bank, broker, NBFC or financial institution.
- Every security has a unique **ISIN** — International Securities Identification Number, **12 characters**.
- Account numbering: **NSDL** accounts are **8-character DP ID + 8-digit client ID**, written with an **"IN" prefix**; **CDSL** accounts are a **16-digit numeric BO ID**.
- **Maximum three holders** in a demat account, as with a folio.

### 17.3.1 ⭐ Dematerialisation

**Converting physical certificates into electronic book-entry form.**

| Step | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Investor | Submits a **Dematerialisation Request Form (DRF)** with the **original certificates** to the DP |
| 2 | Investor | Defaces the certificates by writing **"Surrendered for Dematerialisation"** across them |
| 3 | DP | Verifies, generates a **Dematerialisation Request Number (DRN)**, sends an electronic request to the depository and the physical certificates to the **issuer/RTA** |
| 4 | Depository | Forwards the request to the **issuer/RTA** |
| 5 | RTA | Verifies, **cancels the certificates**, confirms to the depository |
| 6 | Depository | **Credits** the investor's demat account; the DP issues a statement |

**Time frame: normally completed within about 30 days** of lodging the request.

**The non-negotiable matching rule:** the **names and the ORDER of names** on the certificate must be **identical** to those on the demat account. A certificate in "A and B" cannot be dematerialised into an account of "B and A" — that requires **transposition** first (see 17.7.5).

**Benefits:** no bad delivery, forgery, theft, mutilation or postal loss; instant transfer; lower cost (no stamp duty on transfer of dematerialised securities in the old sense); automatic credit of bonus/rights/splits; a single account for equity, bonds, ETFs, mutual fund units and Sovereign Gold Bonds; easy pledging; **nomination at account level**.

**Costs:** account opening (often nil), **annual maintenance charge (AMC)**, transaction/debit charges (charged on **debits, not credits**), demat/remat charges per certificate, pledge charges.

**BSDA — Basic Services Demat Account:** a low-cost demat account for small investors who hold **only one** demat account as sole/first holder. SEBI links eligibility to a **holding-value ceiling (raised to ₹10 lakh)** and prescribes **nil or concessional annual maintenance charges** in the lower slabs. Eligible investors are to be offered BSDA **by default**.

> ⚠️ **Since 1 April 2019, listed securities cannot be TRANSFERRED in physical form** — transfer requires demat. **Transmission** (on death) and **transposition** are the exceptions, and even then the securities are now issued in **demat form only**.

### 17.3.2 ⭐ Rematerialisation

**Converting electronic holdings back into physical certificates** — the exact reverse.

| Step | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Investor | Submits a **Rematerialisation Request Form (RRF)** to the DP |
| 2 | DP | Verifies the balance, **blocks** the holding, generates a **Rematerialisation Request Number (RRN)** |
| 3 | Depository | Forwards the request to the **issuer/RTA** |
| 4 | RTA | **Prints and despatches** certificates directly to the investor |
| 5 | Depository | **Debits** the demat account |

**Time frame: normally about 30 days.** The certificates issued carry **new distinctive numbers** — they are not the original certificates back.

| | **Dematerialisation** | **Rematerialisation** |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | Physical → **electronic** | Electronic → **physical** |
| **Form** | **DRF** | **RRF** |
| **Reference number** | **DRN** | **RRN** |
| **Effect on demat account** | **Credit** | **Debit** |
| **Certificates** | Surrendered and **cancelled** | **Fresh** certificates with **new distinctive numbers** |
| **Practical usefulness today** | Essential | Very limited — physical transfer of listed shares is barred |

**Mutual fund units** follow the same logic: units held in **SoA** form can be converted to demat through a **Conversion Request Form (CRF)** submitted to the DP, and back to SoA through a **Re-conversion Request Form (RRF)**.

## 17.4 Power of Attorney and Other Agreements

A **Power of Attorney (PoA)** is a legal instrument under the **Powers of Attorney Act, 1882** by which the **donor/principal** authorises the **donee/attorney** to act on their behalf. It must be **properly stamped**, and **notarised or registered** as the law of the state requires. The KYC of **both** the investor and the PoA holder must be completed, and the PoA must be **registered with the AMC/RTA/DP** with the attorney's **specimen signature**, before it can be acted upon.

### 17.4.1 vs 17.4.2 ⭐ General versus Specific/Limited PoA

| | **General Power of Attorney (GPoA)** | **Specific / Limited PoA (SPoA / LPoA)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | **Broad** — all or most of the donor's affairs | **Narrow** — only the acts expressly listed |
| **Typical powers** | Operate all bank accounts, buy and sell property, sign contracts, conduct litigation, manage investments | Debit a demat account for **settlement obligations only**; operate one bank and one demat account for a **specific portfolio**; sign one specified transaction |
| **Risk to the donor** | **Very high** — the attorney can do almost anything | **Contained** — anything not listed is not authorised |
| **Typical use** | An NRI authorising a trusted relative to handle affairs in India | A **PMS client** authorising the portfolio manager; a **broker PoA** for pay-in of securities |
| **Preferred in financial services** | **No** | **Yes** — always |

> 🧠 **One-line test:** does the document list the powers, or list the *person*? If it lists **powers**, it is specific/limited. If it hands over the **person's whole life**, it is general.

### SEBI's protections on broker/DP Powers of Attorney

Because PoAs to brokers were once abused, SEBI's rules are strict:

- A PoA is **optional, never a pre-condition** for opening a trading or demat account.
- It may authorise transfer of securities **only for the client's own settlement obligations** — not for arbitrary off-market transfers.
- It must **not** allow the broker to **merge client balances**, open or close accounts, or take a loan against the client's securities.
- It must be executed in favour of the **broker as an entity**, not in favour of its employees.
- It must state the client's **right to revoke at any time**, with notice.
- Modern alternatives now largely replace it: the **DDPI (Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction)**, which permits **only two** things — transfer of securities for **settlement obligations** and **pledging/re-pledging for margin** — and **e-DIS with a TPIN/OTP**, where the client authorises each debit personally.

### When a PoA ends

**Revocation** by the donor; **death, insanity or insolvency** of the donor; **completion of the purpose**; **expiry of the stated term**; or **renunciation** by the attorney.

> ⚠️ **Two traps.** (1) A **PoA holder cannot make or change a nomination** — nomination is a personal act of the unit holder. (2) A PoA **dies with the donor**; an attorney who transacts after the donor's death acts without authority.

**Other agreements at on-boarding:** the **investment advisory agreement** (mandatory for SEBI-registered IAs), the **PMS agreement**, the **AIF contribution agreement**, the DP–BO agreement, the broker–client **Rights and Obligations** document, and the **NACH/e-mandate** authorising periodic debits.

## 17.5 Account Opening for Non-Resident Investors (NRIs)

### Residency — two different definitions

| | **FEMA, 1999** | **Income Tax Act, 1961** |
|---|---|---|
| **Test** | Days **plus the purpose/intent** of the stay | Purely a **day-count** test |
| **Basic rule** | Residing in India for **more than 182 days in the preceding financial year** — but a person leaving for **employment, business or an uncertain period** becomes non-resident **immediately**, and a person coming to India for those reasons becomes resident **immediately** | **182 days** in the year, or **60 days in the year plus 365 days in the preceding four years** (with relaxations for Indian citizens leaving for employment) |
| **Governs** | **Which accounts and assets you may hold** | **How you are taxed** |

> ⚠️ **Classic trap.** A person can be a **resident under FEMA and a non-resident under the Income Tax Act in the same year**, or the reverse. FEMA decides the *account type*; income tax decides the *tax*. Never mix them.

### ⭐ The three NRI bank accounts

| Feature | **NRE** (Non-Resident External) | **NRO** (Non-Resident Ordinary) | **FCNR(B)** (Foreign Currency Non-Resident – Bank) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Currency held in** | **Indian rupees** | **Indian rupees** | **Foreign currency** (USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, AUD, CAD and other freely convertible currencies) |
| **What you may credit** | **Foreign earnings** remitted from abroad | **Indian income** — rent, dividend, pension, interest, sale proceeds — **plus** foreign remittances | **Foreign earnings** remitted from abroad |
| **Repatriability** | **Fully repatriable** — principal and interest | Interest fully repatriable; **principal up to USD 1 million per financial year**, net of taxes, with Form 15CA/15CB | **Fully repatriable** |
| **Indian tax on interest** | **Exempt** | **Taxable**, with TDS deducted at source | **Exempt** |
| **Account types allowed** | Savings, current, recurring, fixed deposit | Savings, current, recurring, fixed deposit | **Term deposit only**, tenure **1 to 5 years** |
| **Exchange-rate risk** | **Yes** — rupee-denominated | **Yes** | **No** — the deposit stays in foreign currency |
| **Joint holding** | With other NRIs; with a **resident close relative on a "former or survivor" basis** | With NRIs and with residents | With other NRIs; with a resident close relative on "former or survivor" basis |

> 🧠 **The one-line mnemonic.** **NRE = money from outside, fully free to go back out, tax-free.** **NRO = money earned inside India, taxed, capped repatriation.** **FCNR = the same as NRE but you keep the dollars, so no currency risk.**

**On returning to India:** NRE and NRO accounts must be **re-designated as resident accounts**; NRE balances may be moved to an **RFC (Resident Foreign Currency)** account; **FCNR deposits may be run to maturity at the contracted rate** and then converted to RFC.

### What an NRI may and may not invest in

| Permitted | Not permitted / restricted |
|---|---|
| **Mutual funds** — repatriable (from NRE/FCNR/inward remittance) or non-repatriable (from NRO) | **New PPF, NSC, SCSS, KVP** accounts |
| **Direct equity in the secondary market** under the **Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS)** | **Agricultural land, plantations, farmhouses** |
| **IPOs and primary market** — no PIS route needed | **Intraday trading and short selling** in equity — NRIs are **delivery-based only** |
| **Government securities, T-bills, NCDs, bonds** | Currency and commodity derivatives are restricted; equity derivatives are permitted only on a **non-repatriable** basis with prescribed limits |
| **Real estate** other than the barred categories | Some AMCs decline **US and Canada** residents |
| **Life insurance; NPS Tier I** (including OCIs) | |

### ⭐ PIS and the demat account limits

- Secondary-market equity purchases by an NRI must be routed through a **designated branch of one authorised dealer bank** under the **PIS**. Only **one bank** may hold the PIS mandate at a time.
- **Individual NRI limit: 5% of the paid-up capital** of a company. **Aggregate NRI limit: 10%**, which the company may raise to **24%** by a board resolution followed by a **special resolution** of shareholders.
- **Separate demat accounts are mandatory**: one **NRE/repatriable** demat linked to the NRE bank account, and one **NRO/non-repatriable** demat linked to the NRO account. Shares bought under **PIS** must be held separately from shares acquired **otherwise** (IPO, gift, inheritance, ESOP, or under FDI).
- Purchases are reported by the designated bank to the RBI; the bank monitors the sectoral caps daily.

### The PMLA and FEMA overlay

| **PMLA, 2002** — "is this money clean?" | **FEMA, 1999** — "is this money allowed to cross the border?" |
|---|---|
| A **criminal** statute; the offence is money laundering | A **civil** statute (it replaced the criminal **FERA, 1973**); contraventions can be **compounded** |
| Requires KYC, record keeping for **5 years**, ongoing monitoring, **UBO** and **PEP** identification, and reporting of **CTR/STR to FIU-IND** | Regulates **current** and **capital** account transactions; administered by the **RBI** |
| Bans **third-party payments** and unexplained cash | Fixes **who may hold what**: NRE/NRO/FCNR rules, PIS limits, repatriation ceilings |
| **Tipping off** the client about an STR is an offence | Residents may remit up to **USD 2,50,000 per financial year** under the **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)**; remittances above a prescribed annual threshold attract **TCS**, at concessional rates for education and medical purposes |

## 17.6 Consolidating, Reorganising and Maintaining a Folio

### 17.6.1 Change of Address and Contact Details

| Client's KYC status | Where the change is made |
|---|---|
| **KYC compliant** (registered with a KRA/CKYC) | Through the **KRA/CKYC**, using the prescribed **KYC change form** — the update then flows to **every** intermediary |
| **Not KYC compliant** (e.g., a PEKRN holder) | Directly with the **AMC/RTA**, folio by folio |

**Documents:** the change form signed **as per the mode of holding**, a **self-attested copy of a new OVD showing the new address** (with the original for verification, or a true copy attested by a permitted authority), and PAN. **NRIs must additionally provide proof of the overseas address.**

**Controls against fraud:** the change is confirmed by SMS and email to **both** the old and the new contact details, and a change of address is often followed by a **cooling-off period** before redemption proceeds are released.

### 17.6.2 Change in Name

Arises on **marriage**, **divorce**, or a change by **deed poll/gazette notification**.

**Documents:** the request signed by the holder, plus **marriage certificate** or **gazette notification** or **deed poll**, and — critically — **the PAN card in the new name**. Update **PAN first, then KRA/CKYC, then the folios and demat accounts**. Doing it in any other order guarantees a mismatch rejection.

### 17.6.3 Change in Status

"Status" means the investor's **regulatory and tax classification**. The main changes:

| Change | What must be redone |
|---|---|
| **Resident → NRI**, or **NRI → Resident** | Bank accounts re-designated, KYC status changed at the KRA, new bank mandate, fresh FATCA/CRS declaration, demat reorganised |
| **Minor → Major** | Fresh KYC, PAN, signature, bank account and nomination in the now-major's own name |
| **Individual → HUF / firm → LLP / private → public company** | A **new folio** in the new legal entity's name; the old entity's holdings are transferred or transmitted |
| **Sole proprietorship → partnership** | New entity, new PAN, new folio |
| **Change of tax residency (FATCA/CRS)** | Fresh self-certification within **30 days** of the change in circumstances |

> ⚠️ **The rule that catches everyone:** a change of **tax status almost always needs a new folio**, because a folio's tax status is fixed at the first holder's PAN. You cannot simply flip a resident folio to "NRI" and carry on.

### 17.6.4 ⭐ Marking a Lien

A **lien** is a **charge** created over an investment as **security for a debt**. Once marked, the investor **cannot redeem, switch or transfer** the units without the lienholder's consent.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | The investor pledges the units to a **bank or NBFC** for a loan against securities |
| 2 | The lender sends a **lien-marking request** with the investor's letter to the **AMC/RTA** |
| 3 | The AMC/RTA marks the lien, **freezes the specified units**, and confirms to **both** parties |
| 4 | On **repayment**, the lender issues a **release/no-objection letter** and the lien is **lifted** |
| 5 | On **default**, the lender can **invoke the lien** and have the units redeemed, with proceeds paid to the lender |

**Key points:**
- A lien can be marked on **part** of a holding — only those units are frozen.
- Income distributions (**IDCW**) usually continue to reach the investor unless agreed otherwise.
- In **demat** form, the equivalent action is a **pledge** created through the depository — **pledgor** (investor) and **pledgee** (lender), with the depository recording the charge. In **SoA** form, the RTA marks a **lien**.
- Liens are also marked on **bank fixed deposits** (loan against FD) and on **insurance policies** (usually by way of a conditional assignment).

> 🧠 **Lien ≠ transfer of ownership.** The investor still owns the units; the lender merely holds the right to block and, if unpaid, to sell.

### 17.6.5 ⭐ Transmission

**Transmission is the passing of an investment to survivors, nominees or legal heirs on the DEATH of a holder.** It is an operation of law, not a voluntary act.

| | **Transfer** | **Transmission** |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A **voluntary** act between living persons | The **death** of a holder |
| **Who initiates** | The seller/donor | The **claimant** |
| **Consideration** | Usually paid | None |
| **Mutual fund units** | **Not permitted** for open-ended units (units are non-transferable in SoA form) | Always permitted |

**The four scenarios and their documents:**

| Scenario | Who receives | Core documents |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Death of one of joint holders** | The **surviving holder(s)** | Transmission request form, **death certificate**, KYC of survivors, fresh bank details and **fresh nomination** |
| **2. Death of the sole holder, nomination registered** | The **nominee(s)** | Transmission request form, death certificate, **nominee's KYC**, nominee's bank proof, indemnity/declaration |
| **3. Death of the sole holder, NO nomination** | The **legal heirs** | Death certificate, KYC and bank proof of claimants, **plus** a **succession certificate, probate of will or letter of administration** — or, under **AMFI's simplified route for smaller claims (currently up to ₹5 lakh per folio)**, an **indemnity bond, affidavits from all legal heirs and NOCs from the non-claimant heirs** |
| **4. Death of the Karta of an HUF** | The **new Karta** | Death certificate, HUF declaration naming the new Karta, KYC and bank details of the new Karta |

> ⚠️ **The single most tested idea in this chapter: A NOMINEE IS A TRUSTEE, NOT AN OWNER.** In mutual funds, shares and bank deposits, the nominee receives the asset so that the intermediary gets a **valid discharge** — but the nominee then holds it **on behalf of the legal heirs**, who take under a will or under succession law. Nomination **does not override succession law**.
> **The one exception is insurance:** under **Section 39 of the Insurance Act, 1938** (as amended in 2015), where the nominee is a **parent, spouse or child** ("beneficial nominee"), the nominee is **beneficially entitled** to the claim and other heirs cannot claim it.

### 17.6.6 ⭐ Nomination and Change of Nomination

**Nomination** is the investor's instruction naming who should receive the investment on death.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who may nominate** | **Only individuals**, holding singly or jointly. **Not** companies, trusts, societies, partnership firms, the **Karta of an HUF**, or a **PoA holder** |
| **How many nominees** | Mutual funds have long permitted **up to three nominees** with a **percentage allocation**; if percentages are not stated, the allocation is **equal**. *(SEBI has since expanded the permitted number of nominees for demat accounts and mutual fund folios well beyond three.)* |
| **Who may be a nominee** | Any person — including a **minor** (guardian's details must be given), an **NRI**, a **trust**, a **charitable or religious institution**, a **local authority** or the **Central/State Government** |
| **Signature requirement** | **ALL joint holders must sign** the nomination — even where the mode of holding is **"Anyone or Survivor"** |
| **Level** | At the **folio level** in mutual funds; at the **account level** in a demat account |
| **Change** | Can be changed **any number of times**; the **latest valid nomination prevails** and automatically **cancels the previous one** |
| **Not permitted** | In a folio held **on behalf of a minor**; and a **PoA holder cannot nominate** for the investor |
| **Mandatory or opt out** | New folios and demat accounts require either a **nomination** or a signed **opt-out declaration** |

> ⚠️ **Trap.** People assume "Anyone or Survivor" means any one holder can also change the nomination. **It does not.** Transactions can be signed by one; **nomination needs everyone**.

### 17.6.7 ⭐ Assignment — Especially in Insurance

**Assignment is the transfer of the rights, title and interest in a policy from the ASSIGNOR to the ASSIGNEE.** It is governed by **Section 38 of the Insurance Act, 1938**, as amended by the Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015.

| | **Absolute assignment** | **Conditional assignment** |
|---|---|---|
| **What transfers** | **All rights, permanently and unconditionally** | Rights transfer **subject to stated conditions** |
| **Reverts to the assignor?** | **No** | **Yes** — typically on repayment of the loan, or on the assignor surviving to maturity |
| **Typical use** | Gifting a policy; selling the interest in a policy | Assigning a policy to a **bank as loan collateral** |
| **Effect on nomination** | The existing nomination is **automatically cancelled** | Nomination is **not cancelled** where the assignment is to an insurer/lender against a loan on the policy |

**Procedural rules:**
- Must be made by an **endorsement on the policy** or by a **separate instrument**, signed by the transferor/assignor, **attested by at least one witness**, and must state the **fact and the reasons** for the assignment.
- It is **effective from the date of the notice of assignment served on the insurer**. The insurer records it and issues an acknowledgement.
- Where there are **several assignments**, priority is decided by the **date on which the notice was delivered to the insurer**.
- The insurer may **decline to act** on an assignment it believes is **not bona fide, not in the policyholder's interest, or against public interest** — it must record its reasons and communicate them **within 30 days**, and the aggrieved party may refer the matter to **IRDAI within 30 days**.
- **Policies under the Married Women's Property Act, 1874 cannot be assigned** — the Act creates a trust for the wife and children, beyond the reach of the policyholder's creditors.
- **Life policies are assignable; health and general insurance policies generally are not** (motor cover moves with the vehicle).

| | **Nomination** | **Assignment** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it does** | Names who **receives the money on death** | **Transfers ownership** of the policy |
| **Effective from** | Death of the life assured | Date of **notice to the insurer** |
| **Who may do it** | The **policyholder** | The **policyholder (assignor)** |
| **Ownership changes?** | **No** | **Yes** (absolute) / conditionally (conditional) |
| **Revocable?** | **Yes**, freely | **Absolute — no**; conditional — reverts on the condition |
| **Governing section** | **Section 39**, Insurance Act, 1938 | **Section 38**, Insurance Act, 1938 |

> 🧠 **38 before 39 — Assignment before Nomination**, both alphabetically and in the Act. A neat way to remember which section is which.

## 17.7 Operations in Special Situations

### 17.7.1 ⭐ Minors as Investors

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who is a minor** | A person below **18** (Indian Majority Act, 1875). Where a **court appoints a guardian**, majority is **21** |
| **Holding** | The folio must be in the **minor's SOLE name**. A minor can **never** be a joint holder, and there can be **no second holder** |
| **Represented by** | A **natural guardian** — **father or mother** — or a **court-appointed legal guardian** |
| **Documents** | **Proof of date of birth** (birth certificate, school leaving certificate/mark sheet, passport) **and proof of the guardian's relationship** with the minor, or the **court order** for a legal guardian |
| **KYC** | **The guardian's KYC and PAN** are required; the guardian's details drive the folio |
| **Payment** | Only from the **minor's own account**, the **guardian's account**, or a **joint account of minor and guardian**. Any other source is a **third-party payment** |
| **Nomination** | **Not permitted** in a minor's folio |
| **Taxation** | The minor's income is **clubbed with the parent's** under **Section 64(1A)** of the Income Tax Act, except income from the minor's **own skill or manual work**, or where the minor is **disabled** under Section 80U |

### 17.7.2 ⭐ Minor Turns Major

This is a hard operational stop, and a favourite exam scenario.

- **On the date the minor attains 18, the folio is frozen for financial transactions.** No purchases, redemptions or switches until the status change is completed.
- **All standing instructions — SIP, SWP, STP — cease** on that date. The AMC must send **advance notice** to the registered address before the birthday.
- To unfreeze, the now-major submits the **"Minor Attaining Majority" (MAM) form** with:
  - **Signature attested by the banker** (or by the notary/gazetted officer, as permitted)
  - **PAN and completed KYC** in their own name
  - **A bank account in the major's own name**, with a cancelled cheque
  - **A fresh nomination**
- The **guardian's authority ends completely** on that date — a redemption signed by the erstwhile guardian afterwards is invalid.

> 🧠 **Memory hook — "18 and everything stops."** The freeze is deliberate: the account has changed owner in law, and the AMC must not act on the old authority.

### 17.7.3 NRI → RI (a returning Indian)

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Inform the bank immediately.** NRE and NRO accounts are **re-designated as resident accounts** |
| 2 | NRE balances may be moved to an **RFC account**; **FCNR deposits run to maturity** at the contracted rate, then convert to RFC |
| 3 | Update **KYC status with the KRA/CKYC**, and with every AMC/RTA/DP via a **change of status form** |
| 4 | Register a **resident bank mandate**; fresh **FATCA/CRS** self-certification |
| 5 | **Close the PIS account**; move PIS-held shares to a **resident demat account** and inform the broker |
| 6 | **Tax:** NRE/FCNR interest **ceases to be exempt** from the date of becoming resident. The person may qualify as **RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident)** for a couple of years, during which **foreign income is generally not taxed in India** — a valuable planning window an adviser should flag |

### 17.7.4 RI → NRI (a resident going abroad)

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | The **resident savings account must be re-designated as an NRO account** — it cannot continue as a resident account |
| 2 | Open **NRE / FCNR** accounts if foreign earnings will be remitted |
| 3 | Update **KYC status to NRI** with the KRA/CKYC, AMCs and DP; **new bank mandate**; fresh **FATCA/CRS** declaration |
| 4 | **Demat:** holdings move to a **non-repatriable (NRO) demat account**; open a **PIS account** if secondary-market equity trading will continue |
| 5 | **Small savings:** an existing **PPF account may be continued to maturity but cannot be extended**; **SCSS must be closed**; **NSC/KVP** are held to maturity |
| 6 | Insurance policies **continue** unaffected; premiums may be paid from NRE/NRO or by inward remittance |

> ⚠️ **Trap.** Both changes require the client to **inform the intermediaries** — the status does not update itself. Continuing to operate a resident account after becoming an NRI is a **FEMA contravention**, and continuing to claim NRE interest exemption after returning is a **tax** contravention.

### 17.7.5 ⭐ Addition or Deletion of Names

| Situation | Mutual fund folio | Demat account | Bank account |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Add a holder** | **Not permitted** — a **new folio** must be opened | **Not permitted** — open a new account and transfer the holdings | Generally **permitted** with the bank's consent and all signatures |
| **Delete a holder** | Only on **death** (transmission). Not on request | Only on **death** (transmission) | Generally permitted, with all signatures |
| **Change the ORDER of the same names** | **Transposition** — permitted | **Transposition** — permitted, via a **Transposition Request Form**; it is **not** treated as a transfer | Permitted |

> 🧠 **The distinction that earns marks: TRANSPOSITION changes the ORDER of the SAME names. TRANSMISSION changes the names because someone DIED. TRANSFER changes the names because someone SOLD or GIFTED.**

Transposition matters practically: certificates in the name of "A and B" cannot be dematerialised into an account of "B and A", so the investor files a transposition request and demats in the same step.

### 17.7.6 Addition or Deletion of a Bank Mandate

- A mutual fund folio may carry **multiple registered bank accounts** — commonly **up to five for individuals and up to ten for non-individuals** — one of which is marked as the **default** for payouts.
- The account **must be in the name of the first/sole holder** (or of a joint holder of the folio).
- **Documents:** the multiple-bank-account registration form, plus for the **new** account an **original cancelled cheque with the investor's printed name** (or a bank statement/passbook copy attested by the bank), and proof of the **existing registered** account.
- **⭐ The cooling-off rule:** if a **redemption request is received within a short window (commonly 10 calendar days) of a change-of-bank-mandate request**, the proceeds are credited to the **OLD, already-registered** bank account, not the new one. This is a deliberate **anti-fraud control** — it defeats the classic scam of changing the bank details and immediately redeeming.

## 17.8 Payment Instruments

### 17.8.1 Traditional Payment Instruments

| Instrument | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Cash** | Mutual funds may accept cash **only up to ₹50,000 per investor per financial year**, and only from specified small investors. **All repayments — redemption, dividend — must be through the banking channel only.** Beyond that, cash is barred under PMLA |
| **Cheque** | Governed by the **Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881**. **Valid for 3 months** from the date of issue (reduced from six months with effect from **1 April 2012**). Must be **CTS-2010 compliant**. Dishonour attracts **Section 138** liability |
| **Demand draft / pay order / banker's cheque** | Bank-guaranteed; the bank debits the customer up front. DD charges are generally **not reimbursed** by AMCs except in specified cases |
| **Post-dated cheques** | Once used for SIPs; now largely replaced by mandates |
| **ECS / NACH mandate** | The standing authority to debit a bank account periodically — the backbone of **SIPs** |
| **Warrants** | Dividend/interest warrants — now almost fully replaced by direct credit |

### 17.8.2 ⭐ Digital Payment Systems

| System | Operated by | Character | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NEFT** | **RBI** | **Deferred net settlement**, in batches | **No minimum or maximum**; available **24×7×365** |
| **RTGS** | **RBI** | **Real-time, gross (transaction by transaction)** | **Minimum ₹2,00,000**; **no upper limit**; available **24×7** |
| **IMPS** | **NPCI** | Instant, account-to-account | **24×7**; per-transaction ceiling of the order of **₹5 lakh** |
| **UPI** | **NPCI** | Instant, mobile-first, using a **VPA** | Standard per-transaction limit around **₹1 lakh**, raised to **₹5 lakh** for specified categories including **capital markets, IPO/ASBA and insurance** |
| **NACH** | **NPCI** | Bulk, recurring debits and credits | Powers **SIPs**, salary, dividend, EMI. Replaced ECS |
| **UPI AutoPay / e-mandate** | NPCI / RBI framework | Recurring debit without re-entering credentials | The **e-mandate ceiling for recurring mutual fund subscriptions, insurance premiums and credit-card repayments has been raised substantially** (to the order of ₹1 lakh per transaction) without an additional factor of authentication |
| **ASBA** | Exchanges/banks | **Application Supported by Blocked Amount** | Funds are **blocked in the investor's own account** until allotment; interest continues to accrue. Mandatory for IPOs. A **UPI block mechanism** has been extended to secondary-market trading |
| **Cards, net banking, wallets/PPIs, AePS, BBPS** | Banks / NPCI | Various | Cards are generally **not** accepted for mutual fund subscriptions |

> 🧠 **RTGS vs NEFT in one line:** **RTGS is Real-Time and Gross — big, immediate, ₹2 lakh minimum. NEFT is batched and has no minimum — small and cheap.** IMPS and UPI are the **retail instant** rails run by NPCI, not the RBI.

### 17.8.3 ⭐ PMLA and Third-Party Payments

**The rule:** a mutual fund (and, by extension, any intermediary) **must not accept a subscription where the payment comes from a bank account other than that of the investor** — for a folio, the **first named holder**. That is a **third-party payment**, and PMLA prohibits it because the audit trail of the money would break.

**The only recognised exceptions — and each needs a signed Third Party Payment Declaration from BOTH the investor and the payer, with the payer's KYC and bank details and the relationship stated:**

| # | Exception | Ceiling / condition |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Payment by **parents, grandparents or a related person** on behalf of a **minor**, out of natural love and affection or as a gift | **Up to ₹50,000** per transaction — i.e., per lump-sum purchase or per SIP instalment. The guardian's own payment is **not** third-party |
| **2** | Payment by an **employer on behalf of an employee**, through **payroll deduction**, for SIPs or lump-sum subscriptions | No specified ceiling; the payroll linkage is the control |
| **3** | Payment by a **custodian on behalf of an FPI or a client** | Standard institutional route |
| **4** | Payment by an **AMC to an empanelled distributor**, or by a **corporate to its agent/distributor/dealer**, of **commission or incentive in the form of mutual fund units** | Against the payee's own investment |

**The mirror-image rule on the way out:** **redemption, IDCW and maturity proceeds are ALWAYS paid only into the registered bank account of the first/sole holder.** There is **no** third-party exception on the payout side. This is the rule that makes the whole anti-laundering chain work — money must come from the investor and must go back to the investor.

> ⚠️ **Trap.** A husband paying for his wife's mutual fund investment from his own account **is a third-party payment and is not permitted** — spouse is not one of the exceptions. So is a company paying for its director's personal investment. The minor exception is the **only** family exception, it is capped at **₹50,000**, and it applies **only where the investor is a minor**.

### 17.8.4 ⭐ Fraud Prevention and Operational Risk

**Cheque-related risks and controls**

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Forged signature, material alteration, counterfeit cheques | **CTS-2010** standardised cheques; **image-based clearing**; UV and micro-lettering features |
| Large-value cheque fraud | **Positive Pay System** (RBI, from **1 January 2021**) — the issuer re-confirms the cheque's date, beneficiary and amount electronically before it is paid. Applies to cheques **above ₹50,000**, with banks free to make it compulsory for higher-value cheques |
| Stale or post-dated cheques | The **3-month validity** rule |

**Digital payment risks**

Phishing, vishing and smishing; **SIM-swap** attacks; **remote-access apps** (AnyDesk, TeamViewer) installed under the pretext of "help"; **fake UPI collect requests** ("scan this QR to RECEIVE money" — scanning a QR **never** receives money, it only sends it); fake customer-care numbers found by searching online; card skimming; malware and keyloggers; **mule accounts**.

**Controls every investor and adviser must apply**

- **Never share** OTP, PIN, CVV, passwords or card details — with anyone, including someone claiming to be from the bank, the AMC or SEBI.
- Use **two-factor authentication**; transact only on the official app or a URL you typed yourself.
- Enable **SMS and email alerts** on every account, and reconcile the **CAS** each month.
- For an advisory firm: **maker-checker**, **segregation of duties**, **dual authorisation** for large transfers, **call-back verification** of any change in bank details, **audit trails**, and periodic reconciliation.
- **Report unauthorised transactions immediately** — the **national cybercrime helpline 1930** and **cybercrime.gov.in**, plus the bank.

**RBI's limited-liability framework for unauthorised electronic transactions** (a genuinely valuable client-protection fact):

| When the customer reports | Customer's liability |
|---|---|
| Loss due to **the bank's negligence or a third-party system breach**, reported within **3 working days** | **ZERO** |
| Third-party breach reported within **4 to 7 working days** | **Limited** — capped by account type (of the order of **₹5,000 / ₹10,000 / ₹25,000**) |
| Reported **beyond 7 working days** | As per the **bank's board-approved policy** |
| Loss due to the **customer's own negligence** (e.g., sharing the OTP), before reporting | **Entire loss borne by the customer** |

> ⚠️ **An adviser's own hard rules.** Under the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, an IA **must never take custody of client funds or securities** and must **never accept cash**. Fees are received **only in the adviser's registered name, through the banking channel, against an invoice**. Any adviser asking a client to transfer investment money to the adviser's personal account is committing fraud.

## 17.9 Documentation Required for Financial Advice

Under the **SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013**, the adviser must **maintain records** — this is the list the exam draws from:

| Record | Note |
|---|---|
| **KYC records** of every client | Including IPV/V-CIP evidence |
| **Risk profiling and risk assessment** | Including the **client's signed acceptance** of the risk profile |
| **Suitability assessment** | Showing that the advice fits the profile |
| **The investment advisory agreement** | **Mandatory** — signed before any advice is given, on SEBI's prescribed terms |
| **Terms and conditions document** and **disclosures** | Including conflicts of interest and holdings/consideration disclosures |
| **A record of the advice given AND its RATIONALE** | The item advisers most often neglect — and the one that decides a complaint |
| **Fee particulars, invoices and receipts** | Fees only through the **banking channel** |
| **Complaints and their redressal** | Feeding the grievance mechanism |

**Retention:** records must be kept for a **minimum of five years**. Where any **dispute, enquiry or proceeding is pending**, records must be kept until it is **concluded**. Records may be maintained **physically or electronically**; if electronic, they must be **digitally signed** with proper **backup**.

**Other ongoing compliance documentation:** an **annual compliance audit** by a qualified auditor (with the report and any adverse observations dealt with and reported), a displayed **Investor Charter**, periodic **complaints disclosure**, **client-level segregation** of advisory from distribution, and — from the client's side — a signed **acknowledgement of the risk profile, the fee structure and the mode of fee payment**.

> 🧠 **The one-line reason all of this exists:** if a client complains in year four, the only defence an adviser has is **the file**. "I told him" is worth nothing; **"here is the signed risk profile, the suitability note, the rationale for the recommendation and the client's acknowledgement"** is worth everything.

## 17.10 Investing in Mutual Funds Through Stock Exchange Platforms

### 17.10.1 ⭐ The Platforms

| Platform | Run by | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **BSE StAR MF** | **BSE** | "**St**ock exchange platform for **A**llotment and **R**epurchase of **M**utual **F**unds" |
| **NSE MFSS** (now **NMF II**) | **NSE** | "**M**utual **F**und **S**ervice **S**ystem" |
| **MF Utility (MFU)** | **The AMCs themselves**, through AMFI — **not** a stock exchange | Gives the investor a single **Common Account Number (CAN)** across participating AMCs |

**How access widened over time:** SEBI permitted mutual fund transactions through the **stock exchange infrastructure in November 2009**, initially only for **stock brokers**. Access was later extended to **clearing members and depository participants**, and then — importantly for this exam — to **SEBI-registered Investment Advisers**, who may use the platforms to **purchase and redeem units on behalf of their clients**, and to **mutual fund distributors for execution-only purposes**.

### 17.10.2 How an Investment Adviser Helps a Client Transact

| Benefit | Why it matters to the client |
|---|---|
| **One window for every AMC** | No separate form, cheque and folio per fund house |
| **Paperless and fast** | Orders are placed electronically; the exchange settles the funds and units |
| **Units in demat or SoA** | The client chooses; demat holdings appear in the CAS with everything else |
| **All order types** | Lump sum, **SIP**, redemption, switch, STP/SWP registration |
| **Direct plans available** | The adviser can transact **direct plans**, so the client pays **no distribution commission** — which is exactly what a fee-only IA should be doing |
| **Uniform cut-off and audit trail** | The exchange time-stamps the order — clean evidence of the applicable NAV |
| **Consolidated reporting** | The adviser's system reconciles every AMC from one source |

The mechanics for the client: complete KYC, register with the adviser/broker on the platform, receive a **unique client code**, and thereafter place orders that the platform routes to the AMC/RTA. Funds move through the exchange's settlement mechanism, and units are allotted subject to the **usual cut-off timing and realisation-of-funds rules**.

### 17.10.3 ⭐ Transaction Feeds for Advisers

A **transaction feed** is a machine-readable daily file of a client's transactions and holdings, supplied by the **RTAs (CAMS, KFin)**, the **exchange platforms** and the **depositories**, which the adviser's portfolio-management software ingests automatically.

**Why feeds matter:**

- **No manual data entry**, and therefore no typing errors in a client's portfolio.
- **Daily valuation and reconciliation** — the adviser sees the true position, not a stale one.
- **Consolidated, multi-AMC, multi-asset reporting** for review meetings.
- **Monitoring** of SIP bounces, redemptions the client did on their own, exit-load windows, and capital-gains positions.
- **Evidence for compliance** — an unarguable record of what was actually transacted, supporting the adviser's record-keeping obligation.

Feeds are provided **only with the client's consent/mandate**, and the adviser must protect the data — client information is confidential, and misuse is both an ethical and a regulatory breach.

**And for the client directly:** the **Consolidated Account Statement (CAS)** does the same job on a monthly/half-yearly cycle across all mutual funds and both depositories against a single PAN.

> **Exam tip:** three ideas generate a disproportionate share of the marks in this chapter, and they are all about **who is entitled to the money**. First, **a nominee is a trustee, not an owner — except a beneficial nominee under Section 39 of the Insurance Act.** Second, **money must come from the investor's own account and go back to the investor's own account** — learn the **three third-party exceptions and the ₹50,000 minor cap** cold. Third, **names on an account can never simply be added or deleted** — you may only **transpose** the order, or **transmit** on death. If you also memorise the **NRE/NRO/FCNR grid** and the **₹50,000 / ₹2 lakh / 3-month / 10-day / 5-year** numbers, the chapter is largely secured.
