## 📋 What this exam is actually about (read this first!)

An **Investment Adviser** is a financial doctor. A doctor examines your body and prescribes medicine. An Investment Adviser examines your **money situation** — what you earn, what you owe, what you're saving for (a house, a child's education, retirement), and how much loss you can stomach — and then prescribes the right financial "medicines."

**Level 1 (Series X-A)** is the *foundation* exam. It tests whether you can:

1. **Plan** — build a personal financial plan, do the maths of money (time value, EMIs, ratios).
2. **Know the market** — how India's financial system and securities markets are wired, and who regulates whom.
3. **Know the products** — equity, bonds, derivatives, mutual funds, PMS, AIFs.
4. **Build a portfolio** — asset allocation, Modern Portfolio Theory, and measuring whether the portfolio actually performed.
5. **Operate legally and ethically** — KYC and onboarding, the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, ethics, and how a client complains about you.

> **Level 1 vs Level 2 in one line:** Level 1 (X-A) is *markets, products, portfolios and rules*. Level 2 (X-B) is *insurance, retirement, tax and estate planning*. You must pass **both** to be a registered Investment Adviser under the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013.

## 📝 Exam Structure (know this cold — it changes how you study)

| Part | What | Marks |
|------|------|-------|
| **Independent MCQs** | 90 questions × 1 mark | **90** |
| **Caselets (1-mark)** | 6 caselets × 5 questions × 1 mark | **30** |
| **Caselets (2-mark)** | 3 caselets × 5 questions × 2 marks | **30** |
| **TOTAL** | **105 questions** | **150** |

- ⏱️ **Time:** 3 hours
- ✅ **Passing score:** 60% — i.e. **90 out of 150 marks**
- ⚠️ **Negative marking:** 25% of the marks for that question. A wrong 1-mark MCQ costs **−0.25**; a wrong 2-mark caselet answer costs **−0.5**.
- 💰 **Fee:** ₹3,000 (check nism.ac.in for the current fee)
- 🧮 **Calculator:** the test machine has a spreadsheet — practise `=PV()`, `=FV()`, `=PMT()`, `=RATE()`, `=NPER()` until they're automatic.

## 📚 How to use this study guide

Each chapter folder contains **three files**:

| File | What it is | How to use it |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| `notes.md` | **Short Notes** — the whole chapter explained plainly, with every formula *and the logic behind it* | Read first, 2–3 times |
| `flashcards.md` | **Flashcards** — question on one side, answer on the other | Revise daily; cover the answer and test yourself |
| `questions.md` | **Question Bank — 100 MCQs**, each with a full explanation (the formula used, *and* why every wrong option is wrong) + a worked caselet | Attempt *after* reading the notes |

That's **~2,000 practice MCQs + 20 worked caselets** across the guide. Each `questions.md` is organised into three difficulty tiers so you build up gradually:

- 🟢 **Tier 1 — Easy (Recall):** definitions and facts (≈Q1–35)
- 🟡 **Tier 2 — Medium (Application):** "which rule applies here", comparisons (≈Q36–70)
- 🔴 **Tier 3 — Hard (Numerical & Scenario):** multi-step calculations and exam-style scenarios (≈Q71–100)

Every answer sits inside a collapsible *"Answer & Explanation"* block — attempt first, then expand to see **why the right option is right, why the others are wrong, and the exact calculation behind it.**

**Plus, across the whole guide:**

- 📑 **10 full-length mock papers** — each built to the exact 150-mark blueprint (90 MCQs + 6 one-mark caselets + 3 two-mark caselets), auto-scored with real negative marking and a 3-hour timer.
- 🔢 **Financial Maths Primer** — every formula in the syllabus, derived and worked.
- 📊 **Excel/Calc TVM guide** — the spreadsheet functions you'll actually use in the exam hall.

**Recommended cycle per chapter:**
> Read `notes.md` → drill `flashcards.md` → attempt `questions.md` (Easy → Medium → Hard) → re-read the notes for anything you got wrong.

## 🧠 The 12 Facts You Must Know Cold

Burn these into memory — they recur across chapters and caselets:

1. **Exam:** 150 marks · 105 questions · 3 hours · pass **90/150 (60%)** · **25% negative marking**.
2. **FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ** — and PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ. Everything else in Module 1 is a variation of this.
3. **Effective annual rate** with *m* compoundings: **(1 + r/m)^m − 1**. Monthly compounding always beats annual.
4. **Real return** = [(1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 1 — *not* simply nominal minus inflation.
5. **Annuity due > ordinary annuity** by exactly one period's interest: **FV(due) = FV(ordinary) × (1 + r)**.
6. **Solvency ratio** = Net worth ÷ Total assets. **Liquidity ratio** = Liquid assets ÷ Monthly expenses. **Savings ratio** = Savings ÷ Income.
7. **EMI** = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1], where r is the *monthly* rate and n the number of months.
8. **Regulator map:** SEBI → securities; RBI → banking, money & forex; IRDAI → insurance; PFRDA → pensions; MCA/RoC → companies.
9. **Bond price and yield move in opposite directions.** Longer duration = bigger price swing.
10. **Sharpe = (Rp − Rf) ÷ σ** (total risk). **Treynor = (Rp − Rf) ÷ β** (market risk). **Alpha** = actual return − CAPM-expected return.
11. **IA net worth:** ₹5 lakh (individual) / ₹50 lakh (non-individual); an IA must do **risk profiling** and ensure **suitability**, and must **segregate advice from distribution**.
12. **Complaint escalation:** the intermediary first → then **SCORES** (SEBI) / **RBI Ombudsman** / **Insurance Ombudsman** / **PFRDA** → then the **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)**.
