## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** A bond's price is:
A) Its face value always  B) The present value of all its future cash flows  C) The coupon rate  D) The sum of all coupons
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A bond is valued by **discounting its coupons and redemption** at the required yield.
A) Price equals face value only when the coupon equals the yield. C) The coupon is one input. D) Undiscounted coupons ignore the time value of money and the redemption.
</details>

**Q2.** Bond prices and yields move:
A) In the same direction  B) In opposite directions  C) Independently  D) Only upward
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The single most important relationship in fixed income.
A) and C) are incorrect. D) Bond prices fall as well as rise.
</details>

**Q3.** A bond trades at a premium when:
A) Coupon rate < required yield  B) Coupon rate = required yield  C) Coupon rate > required yield  D) It is a zero-coupon bond
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — An above-market coupon is worth paying extra for, so the price exceeds face value.
A) Gives a **discount**. B) Gives **par**. D) Zero-coupon bonds always trade at a discount.
</details>

**Q4.** For a bond trading at a discount, the correct ordering is:
A) Coupon > Current yield > YTM  B) Coupon < Current yield < YTM  C) All three equal  D) YTM < Coupon
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A discount bond delivers a **capital gain at maturity**, which YTM counts and current yield ignores — so YTM is highest.
A) Is the **premium** bond ordering. C) Applies only at par. D) Contradicts the discount case.
</details>

**Q5.** The current yield is:
A) Coupon ÷ Face value  B) Coupon ÷ Market price  C) The YTM  D) Coupon × Years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Current yield relates the coupon to what you **actually paid**.
A) Is the **coupon rate**. C) YTM also accounts for capital gain/loss and timing. D) Is meaningless.
</details>

**Q6.** YTM assumes that:

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** A ₹1,000 face value bond pays an 8% annual coupon, has 5 years to maturity, and the required yield is 10%. What is its price?
A) ₹1,000  B) ₹924.18  C) ₹1,075.82  D) ₹800
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Coupon = ₹80. Annuity factor = [1 − (1.10)⁻⁵] ÷ 0.10 = (1 − 0.62092) ÷ 0.10 = **3.7908**. PV of coupons = 80 × 3.7908 = **₹303.26**. PV of redemption = 1,000 ÷ 1.61051 = **₹620.92**. Price = **₹924.18**.
A) Would apply only if coupon = yield. C) Is the premium case (coupon above yield). D) Over-discounts. **Coupon (8%) < yield (10%), so a discount is expected ✓**
</details>

**Q37.** A ₹1,000 bond pays a 12% annual coupon with 4 years remaining and a required yield of 9%. Will it trade at a premium or discount?
A) Discount, because 12% > 9%  B) Premium, because the coupon exceeds the required yield  C) At par  D) Cannot say
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Coupon (12%) > yield (9%)** means an above-market income stream, so buyers bid the price **above face value**.
A) Gets the direction backwards. C) Par applies only when coupon = yield. D) The rule is deterministic.
</details>

**Q38.** A zero-coupon bond with ₹1,00,000 face value matures in 6 years. At an 8% yield, what is its price?
A) ₹63,017  B) ₹1,00,000  C) ₹52,000  D) ₹1,58,687
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — P = F ÷ (1+y)ⁿ = 1,00,000 ÷ (1.08)⁶ = 1,00,000 ÷ **1.58687** = **₹63,017**.
B) Zero-coupon bonds never trade at par before maturity. C) Over-discounts. D) Is the future value, not the price.
</details>

**Q39.** A bond with a ₹1,000 face value and 9% coupon trades at ₹900. What is the current yield?
A) 9.0%  B) 10.0%  C) 8.1%  D) 11.1%

**Correct: B)** — Current yield = annual coupon ÷ market price = ₹90 ÷ ₹900 = **10.0%**.
A) Is the coupon **rate** (based on face value). C) Divides by the wrong figure. D) Is 1,000 ÷ 900. **Since it trades at a discount, YTM will be higher still than 10%.**
</details>

**Q41.** A semi-annual bond has a 10% coupon, 6 years to maturity and a 12% required yield. What inputs go into the pricing formula?

</details>

Mr Bhatia is in the **30%** tax bracket. He expects to need **regular income** from age 60 and believes **interest rates will fall** over the next two years.

---

**QC1.** What is Bond P's current yield, and how does it rank against its coupon rate and YTM?
A) 9.27%; coupon 10% > current yield 9.27% > YTM  B) 10%; all equal  C) 9.27%; YTM is highest  D) 8.0%; coupon is lowest
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Current yield = annual coupon ÷ price = ₹100 ÷ ₹1,079 = **9.27%**. Bond P trades at a **premium** (₹1,079 > ₹1,000), so the ordering is **coupon (10%) > current yield (9.27%) > YTM**.
B) They differ because the price is above par. C) For a **premium** bond, YTM is the **lowest** of the three — the buyer suffers a capital loss of ₹79 at redemption. D) Misassigns the figures.
</details>

**QC2.** What yield does Bond Q offer?
A) 5.08%  B) About 7.02%  C) 10.0%  D) 49.2%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — For a zero-coupon bond, y = (F ÷ P)^(1/n) − 1 = (1,000 ÷ 508)^(1/10) − 1 = (1.96850)^0.1 − 1. ln(1.96850) = 0.67717; ÷ 10 = 0.067717; e^0.067717 = **1.07006**. **Yield ≈ 7.01%**.
A) Is the price expressed as a fraction. C) Is Bond P's coupon rate. D) Is the total gain over ten years, not the annual rate. *(Same formula as CAGR in Chapter 2.)*
</details>

**QC3.** Given Mr Bhatia's expectation that rates will fall, which instrument benefits MOST, and why?
A) SCSS, because it pays the highest rate  B) Bond Q — a 10-year zero-coupon bond has Macaulay duration of exactly 10, the highest of the options, so its price gains most when yields fall  C) PPF, because it is EEE  D) Bond P, because of its high coupon
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **zero-coupon bond's duration equals its maturity**, so Bond Q's duration is **10** — far above Bond P's (roughly 4.2, since its coupons return value early). With **% price change ≈ −MD × Δy**, Bond Q gains most from falling yields.
A) SCSS pays a **fixed** administered rate; its value does not rise when market yields fall. C) PPF's rate is administered, not market-priced. D) Bond P's **high coupon shortens** its duration, reducing the gain.
</details>

**QC4.** Comparing SCSS at 8.2% (taxable) with PPF at 7.1% (EEE) for a 30% taxpayer, which delivers the higher post-tax return — and what is the countervailing consideration?
A) SCSS, at 8.2%  B) PPF at 7.10% beats SCSS's post-tax 5.74% — but SCSS pays quarterly income, which Mr Bhatia needs from 60, while PPF pays nothing until maturity  C) They are equal  D) Neither is suitable
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **SCSS post-tax** = 8.2% × (1 − 0.30) = **5.74%**. **PPF** is EEE, so its **7.10%** is received in full — a **1.36 percentage point** advantage.
But return is not the only criterion: Mr Bhatia needs **regular income from 60**, and **SCSS pays quarterly** while **PPF pays nothing until maturity**. Note also that his PPF account, opened 12 years ago, matures in about 3 years — conveniently close to his retirement.
A) Ranks on the pre-tax headline. C) They differ materially. D) Both have a legitimate role.
</details>

**QC5.** What is the most appropriate overall structure for Mr Bhatia?
A) Put all ₹60,00,000 into Bond Q to maximise the rate-fall gain  B) Blend: SCSS (up to ₹30 lakh) for quarterly income from 60, continue PPF for tax-free accumulation, and hold a moderate allocation to Bond P/Q for the rate view — while recognising that a single AA corporate bond concentrates credit risk  C) Put everything into SCSS  D) Hold only cash
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Three objectives must be served at once. **Income:** SCSS is purpose-built, capped at **₹30 lakh**, paying **quarterly**. **Tax efficiency:** PPF's EEE status makes it the best post-tax rate available to him, and it matures near his retirement. **The rate view:** some duration exposure captures a rate fall — but it is a *view*, not a certainty, and Bond Q's duration of 10 cuts both ways.

Two risk points a good adviser would raise: **Bond P is a single AA corporate issuer**, so concentrating there means undiversified credit risk (a fund would spread it); and Mr Bhatia is **2 years from retirement**, so the portion funding near-term income should not sit in a 10-year duration instrument.

A) Concentrating everything in a 10-year zero at age 58 is a large, undiversified bet on one macro view. C) SCSS is capped at ₹30 lakh and would leave half the corpus unallocated. D) Cash guarantees a negative real return.

> ⚠️ **The lesson:** the highest headline rate (SCSS at 8.2%) is not the highest post-tax return (PPF at 7.1%), and the instrument that gains most from his rate view (Bond Q) is the least suited to his income need. **Match each rupee to the job it must do.**
</details>
