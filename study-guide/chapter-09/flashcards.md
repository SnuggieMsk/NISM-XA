# Chapter 9: Investing in Fixed Income Securities — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **Bond pricing, the yield orderings and duration are the highest-yield items.**

**Q1.** State the bond pricing principle and formula.
> **A:** A bond's price is the **present value of all its future cash flows**: **P = C × [(1 − (1+y)⁻ⁿ) ÷ y] + F ÷ (1+y)ⁿ** — an annuity (coupons) plus a lump sum (redemption).

**Q2.** How do you adjust the formula for a semi-annual bond?
> **A:** **Halve the coupon and the yield; double the number of periods.** A 10% coupon over 6 years at a 12% yield becomes C = 5%, y = 6%, n = 12.

**Q3.** How is a zero-coupon bond priced?
> **A:** **P = F ÷ (1+y)ⁿ** — there are no coupons, so it always trades/issues at a **discount** to face value.

**Q4.** How is a perpetual bond priced?
> **A:** **P = C ÷ y** — a perpetuity, since there is no redemption.

**Q5.** What is the difference between clean and dirty price?
> **A:** **Dirty price = clean price + accrued interest.** Between coupon dates the buyer must compensate the seller for interest earned but not yet paid.

**Q6.** State the relationship between price and yield.
> **A:** They move **inversely**, and the relationship is **convex** (curved), not linear.

**Q7.** When does a bond trade at a premium, at par, and at a discount?
> **A:** **Coupon > yield → premium.** **Coupon = yield → par.** **Coupon < yield → discount.**

**Q8.** Explain intuitively why a bond paying 8% trades below par when the market demands 10%.
> **A:** Nobody will pay full price for a below-market coupon. The **price must fall** until the buyer's total return — coupons **plus the capital gain at redemption** — equals the 10% the market demands.

**Q9.** Define current yield and state its limitation.
> **A:** **Current yield = annual coupon ÷ market price.** It **ignores the capital gain or loss at maturity** and the timing of cash flows.

**Q10.** What is YTM, and what two assumptions does it make?
> **A:** The single discount rate that makes the PV of all cash flows equal the market price. It assumes **(1) all coupons are reinvested at the YTM** and **(2) the bond is held to maturity**.

**Q11.** State the ordering of coupon rate, current yield and YTM for a DISCOUNT bond.
> **A:** **Coupon rate < Current yield < YTM.** (For a **premium** bond the ordering reverses; at **par** all three are equal.)

**Q12.** Why is YTM highest for a discount bond?
> **A:** Because the investor also receives a **capital gain at maturity** (paid less than face value), which YTM captures but current yield ignores.

**Q13.** What are Yield to Call and Yield to Worst?
> **A:** **YTC** is the YTM computed to the **call date and call price**. **Yield to Worst** is the lowest of all possible yields across call dates — the conservative measure.

**Q14.** Name the ten risks in fixed income.
> **A:** **Interest rate, call, reinvestment, credit** (downgrade / spread / default), **liquidity, exchange rate, inflation, volatility, political-legal** and **event** risk.

**Q15.** How do interest rate risk and reinvestment risk offset each other?
> **A:** If rates **rise**, the bond's **price falls** (bad) but **coupons are reinvested at higher rates** (good). This offsetting is exactly what **duration** captures.

**Q16.** What is duration, in two senses?
> **A:** A measure of a bond's **price sensitivity to interest rate changes**, and equivalently the **weighted-average time** to receive its cash flows.

**Q17.** State the modified duration formula and the price-change approximation.
> **A:** **Modified Duration = Macaulay Duration ÷ (1 + y)**, and **% change in price ≈ −Modified Duration × Δy**.

**Q18.** A bond has modified duration 6.2 and yields rise 75 basis points. What happens to the price?
> **A:** % change ≈ −6.2 × 0.0075 = **−4.65%**. On a ₹10,00,000 holding that is a loss of about **₹46,500**.

**Q19.** What is a zero-coupon bond's Macaulay duration?
> **A:** **Exactly equal to its maturity** — because the only cash flow arrives at the end. This is the single most-tested duration fact.

**Q20.** How do maturity, coupon and yield each affect duration?
> **A:** **Longer maturity → higher** duration. **Higher coupon → lower** duration (more value arrives sooner). **Higher yield → lower** duration.

**Q21.** If you expect interest rates to fall, how should you position duration?
> **A:** Go **LONG duration** — longer-duration bonds gain most when yields fall. If you expect rates to **rise**, go **short duration**.

**Q22.** What is convexity, and who does it benefit?
> **A:** The **curvature** of the price–yield relationship — the second-order effect duration alone misses. **Positive convexity benefits the bondholder**: prices rise more when yields fall than they fall when yields rise equally.

**Q23.** Describe the four yield curve shapes and what each signals.
> **A:** **Normal (upward)** — healthy expansion, term premium. **Flat** — transition/uncertainty. **Inverted** (short > long) — expected **rate cuts/slowdown**, historically a recession signal. **Humped** — mixed expectations.

**Q24.** Name the three theories explaining the yield curve's shape.
> **A:** **Expectations theory** (long rates reflect expected future short rates), **liquidity preference theory** (a premium is demanded for longer maturities), and **market segmentation theory** (different investors have fixed maturity preferences).

**Q25.** Which regulator covers government securities, and which covers corporate bonds?
> **A:** **RBI** — government securities and the money market. **SEBI** — corporate bonds and their listing.

**Q26.** State the T-bill tenors and their key feature.
> **A:** **91, 182 and 364 days.** They are **zero-coupon**, issued at a discount and redeemed at face value, and are **risk-free**.

**Q27.** What is commercial paper?
> **A:** An **unsecured** short-term promissory note issued by corporates, PDs and FIs, with a tenor of **7 days to 1 year**, requiring a **credit rating**.

**Q28.** What is a certificate of deposit?
> **A:** A **negotiable term deposit** issued by banks and select financial institutions, with a tenor of **7 days to 1 year**.

**Q29.** Distinguish call, notice and term money.
> **A:** **Call money** — **overnight** inter-bank. **Notice money** — 2–14 days. **Term money** — 15 days to 1 year.

**Q30.** Explain repo and reverse repo from the RBI's perspective.
> **A:** In a **repo**, **banks borrow from the RBI** against securities (the **repo rate** — the main policy rate). In a **reverse repo**, **banks lend to the RBI**.

**Q31.** How often do dated government securities pay coupons?
> **A:** **Half-yearly.**

**Q32.** What are SDLs and STRIPS?
> **A:** **SDLs** are State Development Loans — bonds issued by state governments, yielding slightly more than central G-secs. **STRIPS** separate a bond's coupons and principal into individually traded **zero-coupon** securities.

**Q33.** What is a Masala bond?
> **A:** A **rupee-denominated bond issued OUTSIDE India**. Because it is denominated in rupees, the **issuer bears no currency risk — the investor does**.

**Q34.** What is an AT-1 (perpetual) bond, and what makes it risky?
> **A:** A bank capital instrument with **no maturity**. Despite the steady coupon it carries **write-down risk** — it can be written off entirely if the bank is in trouble.

**Q35.** How much does DICGC deposit insurance cover?
> **A:** **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank** (principal + interest), across all branches of that bank.

**Q36.** State PPF's tenor, annual limit and tax status.
> **A:** **15 years** (extendable in 5-year blocks), **maximum ₹1.5 lakh a year**, and **EEE** — exempt at contribution, accrual and withdrawal.

**Q37.** State SCSS's eligibility, limit, tenor and payout frequency.
> **A:** Age **60+** (or 55+ on superannuation), maximum **₹30 lakh**, **5 years** (extendable by 3), with **quarterly** interest. The interest is **taxable**.

**Q38.** What is distinctive about NSC's interest?
> **A:** It is **compounded annually but paid only at maturity**, and the **reinvested interest also qualifies for Section 80C** in the intervening years.

**Q39.** What is Kisan Vikas Patra's distinguishing feature — and its tax drawback?
> **A:** It runs until the **amount doubles**, with **no maximum limit** — but it carries **no 80C benefit** and the interest is taxable.

**Q40.** Who is Sukanya Samriddhi for, and what is its tax status?
> **A:** A **girl child under 10**; deposits for 15 years, maturing when she turns 21. Maximum **₹1.5 lakh a year**, and it is **EEE**.

**Q41.** Describe the Floating Rate Savings Bonds, 2020.
> **A:** A **7-year** Government of India bond whose interest **resets half-yearly at the NSC rate + 0.35%**. The interest is **fully taxable** and the bond is **not tradable**.

**Q42.** Are company deposits covered by deposit insurance?
> **A:** **No.** DICGC covers **bank** deposits only. A company deposit carries the **company's own credit risk**, which is why it pays more.

**Q43.** Why do corporates issue debt rather than equity?
> **A:** **No dilution of ownership**, a **known** cost, and **interest is tax-deductible** (unlike dividends). The trade-off is a **fixed obligation** payable regardless of profits.

**Q44.** What does a debenture trustee do?
> **A:** **Protects bondholders' interests** and enforces the security on their behalf — a mandatory appointment for public debt issues.

**Q45.** What single question resolves most bond exam problems?
> **A:** Which of three tasks is being asked — **price it** (discount the cash flows), **compare coupon with yield** (premium/par/discount), or **apply duration** (% change ≈ −MD × Δy)?
