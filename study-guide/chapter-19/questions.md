# Chapter 19: Ethical Issues — 100 MCQ Question Bank

> Three tiers — 🟢 Easy (Recall), 🟡 Medium (Application), 🔴 Hard (Numerical & Scenario). **Most marks here come from putting the right LABEL on a described behaviour.** Remember the **25% negative marking**.

## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Ethics is best defined as:
A) The law of the land  B) Moral principles that distinguish right conduct from wrong conduct  C) SEBI circulars  D) A firm's marketing policy
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Ethics is the set of **moral principles** guiding right and wrong behaviour, followed **even when no rule compels it**.
A) Law is the minimum written standard, not ethics itself. C) Circulars are regulation. D) Marketing policy is a commercial document.
</details>

**Q2.** Ethical restraint means:
A) Restraining the client  B) Voluntarily holding back from a profitable act that would harm the client  C) A SEBI penalty  D) Restricting portfolio turnover by law
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Restraint is **voluntary and self-imposed** — declining an available, profitable and possibly even legal action because it is wrong.
A) It restrains the professional, not the client. C) A penalty is external enforcement, the opposite of restraint. D) Turnover limits are regulation, not restraint.
</details>

**Q3.** Compared with the law, ethics is:
A) A lower standard  B) The higher standard, internally enforced  C) Identical to the law  D) Optional for professionals
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Law sets the **minimum**; ethics sets the **higher, aspirational** standard enforced by conscience and reputation.
A) Ethics is above the law, not below. C) They overlap but are not identical — conduct can be legal yet unethical. D) For a fiduciary, ethical conduct is a professional obligation.
</details>

**Q4.** "Ethics begins where the law ends" means:
A) Ethics applies only abroad  B) Rules are the minimum and ethics fills the gaps rules do not cover  C) Ethics replaces the law  D) Once the law is obeyed nothing more is needed
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Regulations are written **after** a harm occurs; ethics anticipates harms no rule has yet addressed.
A) It is a general principle, not jurisdictional. C) Ethics supplements the law, never replaces it. D) That is exactly the attitude the phrase criticises.
</details>

**Q5.** Financial advice is called a "credence good" because:
A) It is cheap  B) The client may not be able to judge its quality for many years  C) It is guaranteed  D) It requires no skill
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Bad advice may only become visible **after the goal it was meant to fund has passed**, so the client cannot police quality.
A) Cost is irrelevant to the definition. C) Advice carries no guarantee. D) It is a highly skilled service.
</details>

**Q6.** Information asymmetry in advice means:
A) The client knows more than the adviser  B) The adviser knows far more about cost, risk and suitability than the client  C) Both know the same  D) Neither knows anything
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The gap in knowledge is precisely **why the client hired the adviser** — and it can be exploited.
A) Reverses the relationship. C) If it were true no adviser would be needed. D) Absurd.
</details>

**Q7.** In the principal–agent problem applied to advice, the client is the:
A) Agent  B) Principal  C) Regulator  D) Custodian
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The client is the **principal**; the adviser is the **agent** engaged to act on the principal's behalf.
A) The adviser is the agent. C) SEBI is the regulator, external to the relationship. D) A custodian holds assets and is not a party to the advice.
</details>

**Q8.** Moral hazard in an advisory relationship refers to the fact that:
A) The adviser shares the client's loss  B) The adviser bears none of the loss from bad advice but keeps the commission  C) Clients always lose  D) Markets are risky
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The **payoff is one-sided**, which is what creates the incentive to take risk with someone else's money.
A) That is the opposite of moral hazard. C) An overgeneralisation. D) Market risk is not moral hazard.
</details>

**Q9.** Mis-selling is:
A) Selling at the wrong price  B) Recommending a product that does not suit the client's needs or profile  C) Selling too often  D) Selling to a registered investor
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The product is right for the **seller**, not the buyer — typically because it pays more.
A) Price errors are operational, not ethical labels. C) That describes **churning**. D) Not an issue at all.
</details>

**Q10.** Churning is:
A) A portfolio rebalancing rule  B) Excessive trading or switching to generate brokerage or commission  C) Refusing to trade  D) A SEBI-approved strategy
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Lots of **activity with no change in the underlying strategy**, done for the intermediary's income.
A) Disciplined rebalancing is legitimate and infrequent. C) Inactivity is not churning. D) It is a prohibited practice.
</details>

**Q11.** Front-running is:
A) Trading ahead of a client's known impending order  B) Executing a client's order first  C) Running a fund  D) Advertising performance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The adviser's own trade is placed **just before** the client's, to profit from the price move that order will cause.
B) Executing the client first is precisely what is required. C) Fund management is a different activity. D) That relates to return misrepresentation.
</details>

**Q12.** Front-running is dealt with primarily under:
A) PFUTP Regulations, 2003  B) The Companies Act  C) FEMA  D) The Consumer Protection Act
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The **SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations, 2003**.
B) Governs companies. C) Governs foreign exchange. D) Governs consumer disputes generally.
</details>

**Q13.** Insider trading is governed by SEBI regulations of the year:
A) 1992  B) 2003  C) 2013  D) 2015
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — The **SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015**.
A) The SEBI Act year. B) PFUTP. C) The Investment Advisers Regulations.
</details>

**Q14.** The provision that makes an Indian investment adviser a fiduciary is:
A) Regulation 15(1) of the IA Regulations, 2013  B) Section 11 of the SEBI Act  C) Rule 206 of the Companies Act  D) The Third Schedule of FEMA
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — **Reg 15(1)**: an adviser shall act in a **fiduciary capacity** and disclose all conflicts of interest as and when they arise.
B) Section 11 sets out SEBI's general functions. C) No such rule. D) FEMA has no such schedule on advice.
</details>

**Q15.** Under Regulation 15(1), conflicts of interest must be disclosed:
A) Annually only  B) As and when they arise  C) Only if the client asks  D) Only at termination
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The wording is **"as and when they arise"** — disclosure is contemporaneous, not periodic.
A) An annual sweep would be too late. C) The duty is proactive, not on demand. D) Disclosure after the fact is worthless.
</details>

**Q16.** The two limbs of fiduciary duty are:
A) Loyalty and care  B) Speed and accuracy  C) Profit and growth  D) Sales and service
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — **Duty of loyalty** and **duty of care**.
B), C) and D) are operational or commercial qualities, not fiduciary duties.
</details>

**Q17.** The duty of loyalty requires an adviser to:
A) Be loyal to his employer above all  B) Put the client's interest first and disclose or avoid conflicts  C) Maximise commission  D) Follow market consensus
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Loyalty answers the question **"whose side are you on?"**
A) The client outranks the employer where the two conflict. C) The opposite of loyalty. D) Consensus-following is not a duty.
</details>

**Q18.** The duty of care requires an adviser to:
A) Be polite  B) Know the client, risk-profile him, research products and monitor the advice  C) Guarantee returns  D) Trade frequently
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Care answers **"did you do the work?"** — and it continues after the sale.
A) Courtesy is not diligence. C) Guaranteeing returns is prohibited. D) Frequent trading is churning.
</details>

**Q19.** The fiduciary standard asks:
A) Is this not unsuitable?  B) Is this the best available option for the client?  C) Is this legal?  D) Is this profitable for the firm?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The fiduciary must seek the client's **best** outcome, not merely an acceptable one.
A) That is the lower **suitability** standard. C) Legality is necessary but not sufficient. D) Firm profit is the conflict, not the test.
</details>

**Q20.** The suitability standard applied to a distributor asks:
A) Is it the best product available?  B) Is it not unsuitable for this client?  C) Is it the cheapest?  D) Does the client like it?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Suitability is a **negative** test — the product must not be unsuitable, but need not be optimal.
A) That is the fiduciary test. C) Cost alone is not the standard. D) Preference is not suitability.
</details>

**Q21.** The Code of Conduct for Investment Advisers is set out in:
A) The Third Schedule of the IA Regulations, 2013  B) The SEBI Act  C) The PFUTP Regulations  D) The Companies Act
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The **Third Schedule** of the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013.

</details>

**Q22.** Which of these is NOT one of the heads of the IA Code of Conduct?

**Q23.** Under the IA Code of Conduct, primary responsibility for compliance and ethical standards rests with:

**Q24.** May a SEBI-registered investment adviser guarantee returns?

**Q25.** What must an investment adviser convey about its SEBI registration?

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** An adviser switches a client between six similar large-cap funds in one year with no change in strategy. This is:
A) Rebalancing  B) Churning  C) Front-running  D) Good diversification
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Activity generating fresh commission with **no change in the underlying plan** is the definition of churning.
A) Rebalancing restores target weights and is infrequent. C) Front-running involves trading ahead of an order. D) Six similar funds add cost, not diversification.
</details>

**Q37.** An adviser buys a mid-cap stock in his own account knowing his largest client will place a ₹50 crore buy order tomorrow. This is:
A) Smart investing  B) Front-running  C) Insider trading  D) Rebalancing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — He is exploiting knowledge of an **impending order**, which is front-running under the PFUTP Regulations.
A) It is a prohibited practice. C) Insider trading requires unpublished price-sensitive information **about the company**. D) Unrelated.
</details>

**Q38.** An adviser sells a 70-year-old a policy with a 15-year lock-in and heavy early-exit penalties. This is best labelled:
A) Prudent planning  B) Mis-selling  C) Churning  D) Front-running
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The horizon is grossly mismatched to the client's needs, so the product suits the seller, not the buyer.
A) It is the opposite of prudent. C) Only one transaction is involved. D) No order is being anticipated.
</details>

**Q39.** A client who is a CFO mentions an unannounced merger; the adviser buys the stock. This is:
A) Front-running  B) Insider trading  C) Churning  D) Mis-selling
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — This is **unpublished price-sensitive information about the company**, caught by the PIT Regulations, 2015 — and it is also a breach of client confidentiality.
A) No client order is being anticipated. C) No excessive trading. D) Nothing was sold to a client.
</details>

**Q40.** A brochure states "180% returns!" without mentioning the nine-year period involved. This is:
A) Fair advertising  B) Misrepresentation of returns  C) Front-running  D) A hidden cost
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Absolute return quoted without the period** is the commonest form of return misrepresentation; the honest figure is a CAGR of about 12.1% p.a.
A) It is designed to impress rather than inform. C) No order anticipation. D) Costs are a separate issue.
</details>

**Q41.** An adviser does not tell a client that the recommended fund is distributed by his brother's firm, which earns commission. The failure is:
A) Trivial  B) Non-disclosure of a conflict of interest, breaching Reg 15(1)  C) Insider trading  D) Churning
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **related-party interest** is a material conflict and must be disclosed **as and when it arises**.
A) It goes to the core of the fiduciary duty of loyalty. C) No price-sensitive company information. D) No excessive trading.
</details>

**Q42.** An audit finds recommendations on file with no documented risk profile. This primarily breaches:
A) The duty of care  B) The duty of loyalty  C) PMLA  D) Nothing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Care requires the adviser to **know the client and evidence suitability**; without a profile there is no basis for the advice.
B) Loyalty concerns conflicts and self-dealing. C) PMLA concerns money laundering. D) It is a serious material lapse.
</details>

**Q43.** Which of these is a genuine ethical DILEMMA rather than plain wrongdoing?
A) Stealing from a client account  B) A client insists on a portfolio far riskier than his profile  C) Forging a signature  D) Trading on insider information
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Two legitimate duties collide — **client autonomy** versus the **duty of care**. There is no obviously costless answer.
A), C) and D) are simply wrong; a choice between right and wrong is a temptation, not a dilemma.
</details>

**Q44.** The correct order of preference for handling a conflict of interest is:
A) Disclose, then avoid  B) Avoid, then manage, then disclose  C) Ignore it  D) Disclose only if asked
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Avoidance is best**; management next; **disclosure is the last resort**, because some conflicts are too severe for disclosure to cure.
A) Reverses the hierarchy. C) Ignoring a conflict breaches Reg 15(1). D) Disclosure is proactive, not on demand.
</details>

**Q45.** The "publicity test" asks:
A) Will this generate publicity?  B) Would I be comfortable seeing this on tomorrow's front page with my name on it?  C) Is this advertised?  D) Will SEBI notice?
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Also called the sunlight or newspaper test; it exposes conduct that depends on remaining hidden.
A) Marketing is irrelevant. C) Advertising is unrelated. D) "Will I be caught?" is precisely the wrong question.
</details>

**Q46.** The first step in resolving an ethical dilemma is to:
A) Decide immediately  B) Get the complete facts  C) Ask the client to choose  D) Consult a lawyer
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Most apparent dilemmas **dissolve once the facts are complete**; deciding on assumptions is the commonest error.
A) Speed without facts is recklessness. C) The adviser cannot outsource his own professional judgement. D) Legal advice may help later, but only after the facts.
</details>

**Q47.** The final step in the dilemma framework is:
A) Forget it  B) Decide, act, document and review  C) Report to the press  D) Resign
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Contemporaneous documentation matters because **undocumented good conduct is indistinguishable from misconduct two years later**.
A) Avoidance is not resolution. C) Internal escalation comes first. D) Resignation is rarely required and does not resolve the client's position.
</details>

**Q48.** A conservative client insists on an unsuitable high-risk allocation. The correct professional response is to:
A) Silently comply  B) Advise against it in writing, quantify the risk, and record his written instruction if he persists  C) Refuse and terminate immediately without explanation  D) Comply and hide the file

**Correct: B)** — The file must show **what you advised**, not only what he wanted. Educate, quantify, offer a compromise, and document.
A) Silent compliance breaches the duty of care. C) Abrupt termination without explanation abandons the client. D) Concealment compounds the breach.
</details>

**Q49.** An adviser realises a recommendation made last year was wrong and the client has not noticed the ₹4 lakh loss. He should:

**Correct: B)** — Honesty is not conditional on being caught; proactive disclosure is a direct requirement of the duty of loyalty.
A) and C) both make the duty depend on the client's vigilance. D) Burying a loss in a statement is active concealment.
</details>

**Q50.** A client offers to pay the advisory fee in cash to "keep it simple". The adviser should:
A) Accept it  B) Decline and insist on payment through the banking channel  C) Accept half in cash  D) Accept and issue no receipt
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Cash dealing is prohibited and untraceable; payments must run through the banking channel from the client's own account.
A), C) and D) all involve accepting cash, and D) adds a documentation failure.
</details>

**Q51.** An adviser discovers a colleague is systematically mis-selling. The right first step is to:
A) Ignore it out of loyalty  B) Escalate internally to the compliance officer / senior management  C) Tell the clients' rivals  D) Post about it publicly

**Q52.** Which statement about ethics and compliance is correct?

**Q53.** Why is disclosure alone sometimes an insufficient response to a conflict?

**Q54.** SEBI permits an investment adviser to charge a client family:

### Case: Mr Deshmukh and the Adviser Who Wore Two Hats

**Mr Ramesh Deshmukh**, 58, of Nagpur, retires in two years and needs about **₹60,000 a month** from age 60. He hands his **₹1,20,00,000** corpus to *Shreyas Wealth*, a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser, and is profiled as **moderately conservative**.

Over the year, the following happens:

| # | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | The promoter's brother owns the mutual fund distribution firm that receives commission on the funds recommended. Mr Deshmukh is never told. |
| 2 | He is advised into a **₹15,00,000** single-premium policy with a 5-year lock-in that pays a **6% upfront commission**. |
| 3 | His **₹40,00,000** equity fund holding is switched four times in the year — **₹1,60,00,000** of switch value in total — each switch attracting a **1% exit load**. |
| 4 | The pitch used a "past performance" of **24% p.a.**, being the best three-year window of one scheme, quoted gross of expenses. |
| 5 | An advisory fee of **₹1,50,000** is charged for the year under the **fixed-fee mode**. |
| 6 | The FY 2025-26 compliance audit flags: no risk profile on record for three clients, the fee cap breach, and undisclosed conflicts. |

---

**QC1.** Event 1 — the undisclosed family distribution firm — is best described as:
A) Normal business  B) An undisclosed conflict of interest, breaching Regulation 15(1)  C) Insider trading  D) Churning
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Reg 15(1) requires an adviser to act in a **fiduciary capacity** and to **disclose all conflicts of interest as and when they arise**. A related party earning commission on the very products recommended is a textbook material conflict, and here it was concealed. Preferably it should have been **avoided** altogether, since advice and distribution must be segregated.
A) A concealed related-party payment is not "normal business". C) No unpublished price-sensitive company information is involved. D) Churning concerns transaction frequency, which is event 3.
</details>

**QC2.** Event 2 — how much upfront commission does the ₹15,00,000 policy generate?
A) ₹15,000  B) ₹90,000  C) ₹9,000  D) ₹1,50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 6% × ₹15,00,000 = **₹90,000**, paid immediately and borne by the client through the product's charges. A 5-year lock-in on a man who needs monthly income from age 60 is also a **horizon mismatch**, making this **mis-selling** as well as a conflict.
A) Applies 1%. C) Applies 0.6%. D) Applies 10%.
</details>

**QC3.** Event 3 — what do the four switches cost in exit loads, and as what percentage of the ₹40,00,000 holding?
A) ₹40,000; 1.0%  B) ₹1,60,000; 4.0%  C) ₹16,000; 0.4%  D) ₹4,00,000; 10.0%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Exit load = 1% × ₹1,60,00,000 of switch value = **₹1,60,000**, which is 1,60,000 ÷ 40,00,000 = **4.0% of the holding**, before short-term capital gains tax. Four switches with no change of strategy is **churning**.
A) Charges the load only once on the holding. C) Applies 0.1%. D) Applies 10% instead of 1%.
</details>

**QC4.** Event 5 — the ₹1,50,000 fee under the fixed-fee mode. What is the position, and what would the AUA mode have allowed?
A) Permitted; AUA would allow ₹1,20,000  B) It exceeds the ₹1,25,000 cap by ₹25,000; AUA mode would have allowed up to ₹3,00,000  C) It exceeds the cap by ₹1,50,000  D) Permitted because the client signed
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The fixed-fee cap is **₹1,25,000 per annum per family**, so the excess is 1,50,000 − 1,25,000 = **₹25,000**, which must be refunded. Under the alternative **AUA mode**, 2.5% × ₹1,20,00,000 = **₹3,00,000** would have been permissible — but an adviser may use only **one mode at a time**, and cannot retrospectively pick the more convenient one.
A) Misstates the AUA figure and treats the breach as permitted. C) The whole fee is not the excess. D) Client consent cannot override a mandatory cap.
</details>

**QC5.** Event 6 — how should Shreyas Wealth address the audit observations?

**Correct: C)** — Proper remediation reaches the **client** as well as the process: refund the excess fee, build and evidence the risk profiles, disclose the related-party conflict, fix the root cause (on-boarding that allowed activation without a signed profile, and billing that did not enforce the cap), obtain **management/board approval**, and report the **adverse findings together with the action taken** within **one month of the date of the audit report**, through the administration and supervisory body.

**The wider lesson:** every one of the six events traces back to a single failure — the firm was **paid by the manufacturer while claiming to serve the client**. Remove that, and mis-selling, churning and the inflated performance pitch all lose their motive. This is exactly why Australia **bans** conflicted remuneration and India **segregates** advice from distribution, rather than relying on disclosure alone.
