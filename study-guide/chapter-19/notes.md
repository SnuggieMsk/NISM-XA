# Chapter 19: Ethical Issues — Short Notes

> **Module 6 · Operations, Regulatory Environment, Compliance & Ethics (30 marks).**
> The most *conceptual* chapter in the syllabus — almost no arithmetic, but a very high density of **named issues** (mis-selling, churning, front-running), **named duties** (loyalty, care) and **named regimes** (SEBI Reg 15(1), the US Advisers Act 1940, Australia's FOFA). ## 19.1 Ethics and Ethical Restraint

**Ethics** is the set of **moral principles that distinguish right conduct from wrong conduct**. Applied to a profession, it is the standard of behaviour a practitioner is expected to follow **even when no rule compels it and no one is watching**.

**Ethical restraint** is the other half of the idea, and it is the half candidates forget. It is the **voluntary, self-imposed decision to hold back from an action that is available, profitable and possibly even legal, because it would harm the client, the market or the profession.**

Restraint matters because the tempting acts in finance are rarely blocked by a physical barrier. Nothing physically stops an adviser from recommending the product that pays him most. Only restraint does.

### Law vs ethics — the single most tested distinction

| | **Law / Regulation** | **Ethics** |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | The **minimum** acceptable standard | The **higher**, aspirational standard |
| **Source** | External — Parliament, SEBI, courts | Internal — conscience, professional norms |
| **Enforcement** | Penalties, suspension, prosecution | Self-restraint, peer pressure, reputation |
| **Scope** | Only what has been written down | Fills the **gaps** where rules are silent |
| **Timing** | Reactive — rules follow scandals | Proactive — anticipates the harm |
| **Test** | "Is it **permitted**?" | "Is it **right**?" |

> 🧠 **Ethics begins where the law ends.** Every regulation in the SEBI rulebook exists because somebody once did something legal-but-wrong, and then it had to be banned. An adviser who only asks *"is this allowed?"* is permanently one scandal behind. An adviser who asks *"is this right for my client?"* is permanently one step ahead.

**Something can be perfectly legal and still deeply unethical.** Selling a 63-year-old a 15-year lock-in product is not illegal. It is indefensible.

## 19.2 Why Ethical Conduct Matters — Especially in Advice

Every business benefits from ethics. Financial advice **depends** on it, for three structural reasons.

### (a) Advice is a "credence good" — the client cannot judge it

You can tell a bad haircut in five minutes and a bad car in five weeks. You may not be able to tell bad financial advice for **fifteen years** — by which time the retirement it was meant to fund has already happened. The client therefore cannot police quality. Only the adviser's ethics can.

### (b) Information asymmetry

The adviser knows the product's cost structure, its commission, its liquidity, its true risk. The client typically knows the brochure. This gap is not a temporary inconvenience — it is the **reason the client hired the adviser in the first place**. An asymmetry that large can be used *for* the client or *against* him.

| Concept | Meaning in an advisory setting |
|---|---|
| **Information asymmetry** | The adviser knows much more than the client about cost, risk and suitability |
| **Adverse selection** | Poor-quality products get pushed hardest because they pay the highest commission |
| **Moral hazard** | The adviser bears none of the loss from bad advice, but keeps the commission |
| **Agency problem** | The **agent** (adviser) may act in his own interest rather than the **principal's** (client's) |

### (c) The agency (principal–agent) problem

The client is the **principal**; the adviser is the **agent** engaged to act on the principal's behalf. The problem is that the agent has his own interests — commission, targets, business volume, his employer's product shelf. Wherever the agent's payoff differs from the principal's payoff, there is a **conflict of interest**, and the conflict resolves itself in the agent's favour unless something prevents it. That "something" is regulation plus ethics.

### The business case — ethics is not charity

| Benefit of ethical conduct | Why it pays |
|---|---|
| **Trust** | Advice is bought on trust; it is the only real product |
| **Client retention** | Advisory economics depend on multi-year relationships, not one-off sales |
| **Referrals** | The cheapest client acquisition channel in the business, and it runs entirely on reputation |
| **Lower cost** | Fewer complaints, fewer SEBI inspections escalating, less litigation, lower staff turnover |
| **Licence protection** | Registration can be suspended or cancelled; the firm's existence is at stake |
| **Market confidence** | Retail participation depends on the belief that the system is not rigged |

**The cost of the opposite:** monetary penalty under the SEBI Act, suspension or cancellation of registration, disgorgement of unlawful gains, debarment from the securities market, criminal liability in fraud cases — and, above all, a **reputation that cannot be repurchased at any price**.

> 🧠 **The trust asymmetry.** Trust is built in drops and lost in buckets. A ten-year relationship worth ₹1.5 lakh a year in fees can be destroyed by a single ₹40,000 commission taken quietly. Unethical conduct in advice is almost always a **bad trade even in pure money terms** — it swaps a long annuity for a short lump sum.

## 19.3 ⭐ Ethical Issues in Providing Financial Advice

This is the heart of the chapter. Learn each label and its one-line definition.

| Issue | What it is | The give-away sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Mis-selling** | Recommending a product that does **not suit** the client's needs, risk profile or horizon — usually because it pays the adviser more | The product is right for the *seller*, not the buyer |
| **Churning** | **Excessive trading, switching or turnover** in a client's portfolio to generate brokerage or fresh commission | Lots of activity, no change in the underlying strategy |
| **Front-running** | Dealing in a security **ahead of a client's known impending order** to profit from the price move that order will cause | The adviser's own trade sits just before the client's |
| **Insider trading** | Trading on **unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI)** about a company | The information came from inside the *company*, not from an order book |
| **Conflict of interest** | The adviser's interest (commission, in-house product, related party, personal holding) diverges from the client's | Ask: "who else pays this adviser?" |
| **Unsuitable recommendation** | Advice given without proper **risk profiling**, or contrary to the profile on record | No documented basis for the recommendation |
| **Misrepresenting returns** | Cherry-picked periods, gross-of-cost figures, absolute returns dressed as annual, past performance implied as assured | A number without a period, a basis or a caveat |
| **Hidden costs** | Commission, upfront loads, exit loads, expense ratios, mortality charges not disclosed | The client cannot tell you what he is paying |
| **Assured-return claims** | Promising or implying a guaranteed return on a market-linked product | The word "guaranteed" attached to a market product |
| **Breach of confidentiality** | Using or revealing client information without consent | Client data used for anything other than serving that client |
| **Unauthorised transactions** | Transacting in a client's account without a mandate | Trades the client did not approve |
| **Failure to disclose** | Not revealing commissions, related-party interest, disciplinary history or complaints | Silence where disclosure is required |
| **Rebating / inducements** | Passing back commission or offering gifts to win business | The client is bought, not advised |
| **Advice without diligence** | Recommending a product the adviser has not researched or does not understand | "Everyone is buying it" |

### Worked example — what churning actually costs

Mrs Iyer's ₹25,00,000 equity portfolio. Her broker-adviser turns it over **8 times** in a year.

- Value traded = 8 × ₹25,00,000 = **₹2,00,00,000**
- Round-trip cost (brokerage, STT, exchange charges, GST, impact) ≈ **0.50%**
- Total cost = 0.50% × ₹2,00,00,000 = **₹1,00,000**
- As a share of her portfolio = 1,00,000 ÷ 25,00,000 = **4.0% per year**

A disciplined portfolio with 0.5× turnover would have cost 0.50% × ₹12,50,000 = **₹6,250**, i.e. **0.25%**. The adviser has taken **3.75 percentage points a year** out of her return **and produced no strategy change at all**.

### Worked example — what hidden cost does over time

Mr Rao invests **₹10,00,000**. Gross return 12% p.a. for 15 years.

| | Regular plan (TER 2.00%) | Direct plan (TER 1.10%) |
|---|---|---|
| Net return | **10.00%** | **10.90%** |
| Value after 15 years | 10,00,000 × 1.10¹⁵ = **₹41,77,000** | 10,00,000 × 1.109¹⁵ = **₹47,21,000** |

**Difference = ₹5,44,000** — more than **half the original investment**, created by a cost difference of **0.90% a year** that never appears as a line item on any statement. This is why non-disclosure of cost is treated as a serious ethical failure, not a technicality.

### Worked example — misrepresenting returns

A scheme's NAV rose from **₹10 to ₹28 over 9 years**.

- The brochure headline: **"180% return!"** — arithmetically true (18 ÷ 10).
- The honest number: CAGR = (28 ÷ 10)^(1/9) − 1 = 2.8^0.1111 − 1 = **12.1% per annum**.

Both describe the same fund. One is designed to be understood; the other is designed to impress. **Absolute return quoted without the period is the commonest form of return misrepresentation in India.**

Other variants to recognise:
- **Cherry-picked window** — quoting the best 3-year stretch out of ten years of data
- **Gross of expenses** — showing returns before TER, exit load and tax
- **Survivorship bias** — showing only the schemes that survived
- **Back-tested "model portfolio" returns** presented as if actually earned
- **Point-to-point instead of rolling returns** — a single lucky start date

> ⚠️ **Front-running vs insider trading.** Both are prohibited, both involve trading on information others do not have, and the exam loves to swap them.
> **Front-running** uses knowledge of an **impending order** (a client's or an employer's). It is dealt with under the **SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations, 2003**.
> **Insider trading** uses **unpublished price-sensitive information about the company itself**. It is dealt with under the **SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015**.
> Memory hook: **front-run an ORDER, insider-trade on NEWS.**

> ⚠️ **Mis-selling vs churning.** **Mis-selling is about the WRONG product** — one transaction can be mis-selling. **Churning is about TOO MANY transactions** — the individual product may be perfectly fine; it is the *frequency* that is the offence.

## 19.4 Ethical Dilemmas — and How to Resolve Them

An **ethical dilemma** is **not** a choice between right and wrong. That is merely a temptation, and it has an obvious answer. A dilemma is a situation in which:

- two **legitimate duties conflict** (loyalty to the client vs loyalty to the employer), **or**
- **every available option carries some ethical cost**, **or**
- the right course is clear but **expensive** — it costs the fee, the client or the job.

**Typical dilemmas in an advisory practice**

| Situation | The conflict |
|---|---|
| Client insists on a portfolio far riskier than his profile | **Client autonomy** vs **duty of care** |
| The in-house product pays the firm; a cheaper external one is better | **Employer's interest** vs **client's interest** |
| A client casually reveals price-sensitive news about his employer | **Confidentiality** vs **the duty of every other client** |
| Last year's recommendation was wrong; the client has not noticed | **Self-protection** vs **honesty** |
| A large client asks for the fee bill to be raised in another name | **Business retention** vs **the law** |
| A colleague is mis-selling; reporting him will end his career | **Loyalty to a peer** vs **duty to clients** |

### ⭐ A six-step framework for resolving a dilemma

1. **Get the facts.** Most "dilemmas" dissolve once the facts are complete. Never decide on assumptions.
2. **Identify the stakeholders.** Client, other clients, the firm, the regulator, the market, yourself — and note **whose interest must rank first** (the client's).
3. **Name the ethical issue and the conflicting duties.** Put a label on it — conflict of interest, confidentiality, suitability.
4. **Generate alternatives.** There are almost never only two options. "Disclose and proceed", "restructure the arrangement", "decline the mandate" and "escalate internally" are all options.
5. **Test each alternative** (see the four tests below) and check it against the **law, the SEBI Code of Conduct and the firm's policy**. Consult the **compliance officer** — that is what the role exists for.
6. **Decide, act, DOCUMENT, and review.** Record the reasoning contemporaneously. Undocumented good conduct is indistinguishable from misconduct two years later.

### ⭐ The four quick tests

| Test | The question you ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **The legality test** | Does this breach any law, regulation or the Code of Conduct? *(A "no" is necessary but never sufficient.)* |
| **The publicity / sunlight test** | Would I be comfortable if this appeared **on tomorrow's front page** with my name on it? |
| **The mirror / family test** | Would I recommend exactly this to **my own parent** with the same money? |
| **The role-model test** | Would a **respected senior professional** in my field do this — and would I be happy to explain it to a new joinee as "how we work here"? |

> 🧠 **The one-line default.** When a dilemma is genuinely balanced, apply the tie-breaker built into the regulations: **the client's interest comes first, and whatever you cannot avoid, you disclose.** "Avoid, manage, or disclose" — in that order of preference.

**Worked dilemma.** Mr Shah, 63, retiring in 18 months, profiled as *conservative*, insists on putting 90% of his ₹80 lakh corpus into small-cap funds after a friend's tip.
*Wrong responses:* silently comply (breaches duty of care); or simply refuse and end the conversation (abandons the client).
*Right response:* explain the specific risk in rupee terms — "a 40% drawdown is **₹28.8 lakh** off a ₹72 lakh allocation, and you have 18 months, not 18 years, to recover it"; offer a compromise consistent with the profile; if he still insists, **record the advice given, record his written instruction that it is contrary to advice, and reassess whether the mandate can be continued at all.** Never let the file show only what he wanted and not what you advised.

## 19.5 ⭐ Fiduciary Responsibility of Investment Advisers

**The provision to memorise: SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013, Regulation 15(1)** —

> *"An investment adviser shall act in a fiduciary capacity towards its clients and shall disclose all conflicts of interest as and when they arise."*

A **fiduciary** is someone entrusted with another's affairs who must place that person's interest **above his own**. It is the **highest duty the law recognises in a commercial relationship** — higher than the duty of an ordinary seller, higher than the duty of a distributor.

### The two limbs of fiduciary duty

| Limb | What it demands | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Duty of LOYALTY** | Put the client first; do not profit at his expense | Avoid conflicts; where unavoidable, **disclose fully and obtain informed consent**; no undisclosed commission; no front-running; no misuse of client information |
| **Duty of CARE** | Give competent, diligent, suitable advice | Know the client; **risk-profile** him; research the product; recommend what fits; **monitor and review** the advice over time |

> 🧠 **Two words, one duty: LOYALTY and CARE.** Loyalty answers *"whose side are you on?"*. Care answers *"did you do the work?"*. Every fiduciary breach in the syllabus is a failure of one or the other.

### Fiduciary standard vs suitability standard — a classic exam comparison

| | **Fiduciary standard** (Investment Adviser) | **Suitability standard** (Distributor / seller) |
|---|---|---|
| **The test** | Is this the **best** available option for the client? | Is this **not unsuitable** for the client? |
| **Whose interest ranks first** | The **client's**, above the adviser's | The seller's own interest may legitimately co-exist |
| **Conflicts** | Must be **avoided or fully disclosed** | Disclosure requirements are lighter |
| **Duration** | **Continuing** — advice must be monitored | Largely **point-of-sale** |
| **Remuneration** | **Fee from the client**; advice segregated from distribution | **Commission from the manufacturer** |
| **Legal basis in India** | **Reg 15(1)**, IA Regulations 2013 | Distributor codes and product-level rules |

### The Code of Conduct for Investment Advisers (Third Schedule, IA Regulations 2013)

Nine heads — the exam quotes them almost verbatim:

| # | Head | Substance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Honesty and fairness** | Act honestly, fairly and in the **best interests of clients** and the integrity of the market |
| 2 | **Diligence** | Act with due skill, care and diligence in the best interests of clients |
| 3 | **Capability** | Have and employ effectively the **resources and procedures** needed to perform the activities |
| 4 | **Information about clients** | Seek from clients the information about their **circumstances and investment objectives** relevant to the service |
| 5 | **Information to clients** | Make **adequate disclosures** of relevant material information |
| 6 | **Fair and reasonable charges** | Charges must be **fair and reasonable**; the basis must be disclosed |
| 7 | **Conflicts of interest** | Try to **avoid** conflicts; where they arise, ensure **fair treatment** and **disclosure** |
| 8 | **Compliance** | Comply with all regulatory requirements applicable to the conduct of the business |
| 9 | **Responsibility of senior management** | Senior management bears **primary responsibility** for compliance and ethical standards |

### The other fiduciary building blocks in the IA Regulations

- **Risk profiling and suitability** — mandatory, documented, and periodically reviewed; the client must be told his profile.
- **Segregation of advisory and distribution** — an individual adviser cannot both advise and distribute to the same client; a non-individual adviser must keep the activities in **separate identifiable divisions** with client-level segregation.
- **Fees** — a client (per family) may be charged either up to **₹1,25,000 per annum under the fixed-fee mode** or up to **2.5% of Assets Under Advice (AUA) per annum**; only one mode at a time, and any advance fee is capped.
- **No assured returns** — an adviser may not guarantee returns, and must state that **SEBI registration is not an endorsement** and gives no assurance of returns or performance.
- **Disclosure** — of holdings/positions in recommended products, of any consideration received from third parties, of the adviser's terms, and of any disciplinary history.
- **Records** — KYC, risk profile, suitability rationale, advice given, and interactions to be maintained (in physical or electronic form) for the prescribed period.
- **Compliance officer** and a documented **grievance redressal** process.

## 19.6 SEBI Do's and Don'ts for Investors

SEBI issues investor-education "Do's and Don'ts". An adviser is expected to **teach these to the client** — client education is itself part of the fiduciary duty of care.

### ✅ Do's

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| **Deal only with SEBI-registered intermediaries** and verify the registration number on the SEBI website | The whole investor-protection architecture applies only to registered entities |
| **Read the offer document / scheme document** before investing | The risks are always in the document, never in the advertisement |
| **Complete KYC** properly and give correct PAN, bank and contact details | Payouts, statements and grievance redress all depend on it |
| **Insist on proper documentation** — contract note, statement of account, receipt | Without documents there is no proof and no remedy |
| **Give instructions in writing**, clearly and unambiguously | Verbal instructions are the source of most disputes |
| **Pay only through the banking channel from your own account** | Third-party payments are restricted under PMLA |
| **Register a nomination** | It is what makes transmission workable for the family |
| **Monitor your investments** — read statements, check holdings periodically | Errors and unauthorised trades are caught only by reading |
| **Understand the product's risk, cost, liquidity and tax** before committing | Suitability is ultimately the investor's own responsibility too |
| **Know the grievance route — SCORES**, and the escalation ladder | Complaints have time limits |

### ❌ Don'ts

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| **Don't deal with unregistered entities** or "advisers" without a registration number | No regulatory recourse whatsoever |
| **Don't believe assured/guaranteed return promises** on market-linked products | Guaranteeing return is prohibited; the promise itself is the red flag |
| **Don't act on tips, rumours, SMS, WhatsApp or social-media "calls"** | These are the standard delivery mechanism for pump-and-dump schemes |
| **Don't sign blank forms, blank cheques or blank delivery instruction slips** | A blank signed document is a signed blank cheque |
| **Don't deal in cash** or hand cash to any intermediary | Prohibited and untraceable |
| **Don't share passwords, PINs or OTPs** with anyone, including your adviser | The account is yours and so is the loss |
| **Don't be swayed by advertisements or celebrity endorsements** | An endorsement is not a suitability assessment |
| **Don't give a wide power of attorney** without understanding its exact scope | It is the instrument through which unauthorised trading happens |
| **Don't invest in a product you cannot explain** to someone else | If you cannot explain it, you cannot monitor it |
| **Don't chase unrealistically high returns** | Above-market return promises are the definition of a red flag |

> 🧠 **Compress the whole list into five words: REGISTERED, WRITTEN, BANKING CHANNEL, READ, NEVER-BLANK.** Registered intermediary; written instructions; payment through your own bank account; read the document; never sign anything blank.

## 19.7 Addressing Annual Audit Observations

### What the audit is

A SEBI-registered Investment Adviser must have an **annual audit of its compliance with the IA Regulations**, carried out by a **member of the ICAI (Chartered Accountant) or the ICSI (Company Secretary)**.

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Compliance with the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations and circulars issued under them |
| **Auditor** | A member of **ICAI or ICSI** |
| **Frequency** | **Annual**, for each financial year |
| **Completion** | Within **six months of the end of the financial year** (i.e. by **30 September** for a year ending 31 March) |
| **Adverse findings** | Reported **together with the action taken**, duly approved by the management / board / proprietor, **within one month of the date of the audit report** |
| **Reported to** | SEBI, through the **administration and supervisory body for investment advisers (BASL)** |

**Worked timeline.** Financial year ends **31 March 2026** → audit to be completed by **30 September 2026** → if the report is dated 20 September 2026, the adverse findings and the action taken on them must be reported by **20 October 2026**.

### ⭐ How to address the observations — the eight steps

1. **Read them as a diagnostic, not an attack.** An audit observation is free information about where the firm will fail an inspection.
2. **Classify each observation** — procedural/minor, material, or systemic (see the table below). The response must match the severity.
3. **Find the root cause**, not just the instance. "Three risk profiles missing" is a symptom; "on-boarding does not block account activation until the profile is signed" is the cause.
4. **Assign a named owner and a deadline** for each observation, tracked in a **remediation register** by the compliance officer.
5. **Remediate the CLIENT, not only the process.** Where a client has suffered — an excess fee, an unsuitable product, a missing disclosure — refund, reverse or correct it. Fixing the checklist while keeping the money is not remediation.
6. **Get management/board approval** of the action-taken report — the Code of Conduct puts primary responsibility on **senior management**.
7. **Report to SEBI/BASL within the prescribed time**, with the findings *and* the action taken. Never suppress an adverse finding — non-reporting is a fresh and more serious violation than the original lapse.
8. **Change the system and verify next year** — update SOPs, checklists, software controls and staff training, then confirm closure in the following audit.

| Type of observation | Correct response |
|---|---|
| **Procedural / documentation** (a missing signature, an undated form) | Correct the record, tighten the checklist, close it |
| **Material** (risk profiling not done, disclosure not made, fee cap breached) | Remediate the affected clients, fix the process, report with action taken |
| **Systemic** (no compliance officer, no records, advisory and distribution not segregated) | Escalate to management, redesign the process, consider external help, expect regulatory follow-up |
| **REPEAT observation from last year** | The most serious of all — it proves the earlier "fix" was cosmetic. Expect it to be treated as wilful. |

> ⚠️ **Exam trap.** The reportable item is not merely the finding — it is the **adverse finding TOGETHER WITH the action taken on it, approved by the management**. An answer that says "report the observations to SEBI" is incomplete; the action taken is the operative half.

## 19.8 Global Best Practices on Ethical Issues

### 19.8.1 United States — the SEC

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Governing law** | **Investment Advisers Act of 1940**, administered by the **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** |
| **Core standard** | An investment adviser is a **fiduciary** to its clients — established through the anti-fraud provisions of the Act and confirmed by the US Supreme Court in **SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau (1963)** |
| **Duty of care** | Provide advice in the client's **best interest**, seek **best execution**, and **monitor** the advice over the relationship |
| **Duty of loyalty** | Do not subordinate the client's interest to the adviser's; make **full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts** and obtain **informed consent** |
| **Disclosure document** | **Form ADV** — including the plain-English **Part 2 "brochure"** given to clients, covering services, fees, conflicts, disciplinary history |
| **Code of ethics rule** | Registered advisers must adopt a **written code of ethics** — standards of conduct, compliance with securities laws, reporting of **personal securities transactions** by access persons, pre-clearance of IPOs/private placements, and reporting of violations |
| **Compliance rule** | Written **policies and procedures**, reviewed **annually**, with a designated **Chief Compliance Officer** |
| **Brokers** | Held to **Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI)** — a best-interest standard at the point of recommendation, still distinct from the adviser's full fiduciary duty |

> 🧠 **The SEC model in five words: DISCLOSE, DOCUMENT, DESIGNATE, REVIEW, RESTRICT** — disclose conflicts (Form ADV), document policies, designate a CCO, review annually, restrict personal trading.

### 19.8.2 Australia — ASIC and the FOFA reforms

Australia's regime is regarded as the strictest in the world on **remuneration conflicts**, because it does not rely on disclosure alone — it **bans** the conflicted payment.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Regulator / law** | **ASIC** (Australian Securities and Investments Commission), under the **Corporations Act 2001** |
| **Licensing** | Advisers must hold or operate under an **AFS licence** |
| **The reform package** | **FOFA — Future of Financial Advice**, effective from 2012–13 |
| **Best interests duty** | A statutory duty to act in the **client's best interests** when giving personal advice to a retail client, supported by a **"safe harbour"** list of steps the adviser must show he took |
| **Ban on conflicted remuneration** | **Commissions and volume-based benefits are banned** for personal advice on relevant products — the conflict is removed, not merely disclosed |
| **Opt-in** | Ongoing fee arrangements must be **periodically renewed by the client**, not left running indefinitely |
| **Fee Disclosure Statement (FDS)** | An **annual statement** of the fees paid and the services actually provided |
| **Client documents** | **Financial Services Guide (FSG)** up front, **Statement of Advice (SoA)** setting out the advice, its basis, the fees and any interests that could influence it, and the product's **PDS** |
| **Professional standards** | Education, an exam, continuing professional development, and a binding **Code of Ethics for financial advisers** (in force from 2020) built on trustworthiness, competence, honesty, fairness and diligence |

**The "safe harbour" steps** — the checklist an Australian adviser must be able to evidence: identify the client's objectives, situation and needs; identify the subject matter of the advice; identify what further information is reasonably needed and make reasonable enquiries; assess whether the adviser has the necessary expertise (and refer on if not); conduct a **reasonable investigation of the products**; base all judgements on the client's relevant circumstances; and take **any other step** that would reasonably be in the client's best interests.

### Comparing the three regimes

| | **India** | **United States** | **Australia** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regulator** | **SEBI** | **SEC** | **ASIC** |
| **Key instrument** | IA Regulations **2013** | Advisers Act **1940** | Corporations Act 2001 + **FOFA** |
| **Standard** | **Fiduciary** — Reg **15(1)** | **Fiduciary** — care + loyalty | Statutory **best interests duty** |
| **Conflicts approach** | Avoid, manage, **disclose**; advice segregated from distribution | **Full and fair disclosure** + informed consent | **Ban** conflicted remuneration outright |
| **Client document** | Terms, risk profile, suitability rationale, disclosures | **Form ADV Part 2** brochure | **FSG + SoA + PDS** |
| **Ongoing fee control** | Fee **caps** per family, advance fee capped | Disclosure-based | **Opt-in + annual Fee Disclosure Statement** |
| **Compliance machinery** | Compliance officer, **annual compliance audit** | CCO, written policies, **annual review** | AFS licence obligations, Code of Ethics |

**The common thread across all three:** *know the client, act in his best interest, control conflicts, disclose fully, document everything, and review it periodically.* The jurisdictions differ only in how much they trust disclosure versus prohibition.

*(A widely used voluntary global benchmark is the **CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct**, which likewise requires members to place the integrity of the profession and the interests of clients above their own.)*

| Information asymmetry | The adviser knows far more than the client about cost, risk and suitability |
| Agency problem | The **agent** (adviser) may serve his own interest instead of the **principal's** (client's) |
| Mis-selling | Recommending a product that **does not suit** the client |
| Churning | **Excessive trading/switching** to generate commission or brokerage |
| Front-running | Trading **ahead of a client's known impending ORDER** — **PFUTP Regulations 2003** |
| Insider trading | Trading on **UPSI about the company** — **PIT Regulations 2015** |
| Front-run vs insider-trade | Front-run an **ORDER**; insider-trade on **NEWS** |
| Misrepresenting returns | Cherry-picked windows, gross-of-cost figures, absolute passed off as annual |
| ₹10 → ₹28 over 9 years | **180% absolute** but only **≈12.1% CAGR** |
| Hidden cost | Commissions, loads, TER, mortality charges not disclosed |
| Ethical dilemma | A conflict between **two legitimate duties**, or where the right course is costly — **not** simply right vs wrong |
| Dilemma framework | Facts → stakeholders → name the issue → alternatives → test & consult compliance → **decide, act, document, review** |
| The four tests | **Legality**, **publicity/newspaper**, **mirror/family**, **role model** |
| Conflict hierarchy | **Avoid → manage → disclose** (in that order) |
| Fiduciary provision | **Regulation 15(1)**, SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, **2013** |
| Reg 15(1) wording | Act in a **fiduciary capacity** and **disclose all conflicts of interest as and when they arise** |
| Two limbs of fiduciary duty | **Duty of LOYALTY** and **duty of CARE** |
| Fiduciary vs suitability | "Is it the **best**?" vs "Is it **not unsuitable**?" |
| Code of Conduct heads | Honesty & fairness, **diligence**, capability, information **about** clients, information **to** clients, **fair charges**, conflicts, compliance, **senior management responsibility** |
| IA fee cap | **₹1,25,000 p.a. per family** (fixed-fee mode) **or 2.5% of AUA p.a.** — one mode only |
| Assured returns | **Prohibited**; SEBI registration is **not an endorsement** |
| SEBI Do's — five words | **Registered, written, banking channel, read, never-blank** |
| Biggest Don't | Never believe an **assured/guaranteed return** on a market-linked product |
| Annual compliance audit | By a member of **ICAI or ICSI**, within **six months of the financial year end** |
| Adverse findings reporting | Findings **plus action taken**, management-approved, **within one month of the audit report** |
| Repeat audit observation | The most serious kind — proves the earlier fix was cosmetic |
| US law | **Investment Advisers Act of 1940**; regulator **SEC** |
| US case establishing fiduciary duty | **SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau (1963)** |
| US disclosure document | **Form ADV** (Part 2 brochure) |
| US compliance machinery | Written **code of ethics**, personal-trading reporting, written policies, **annual review**, a **CCO** |
| Australian regulator / reform | **ASIC** / **FOFA** (from 2012–13) |
| Australia's distinctive rule | **Ban on conflicted remuneration** — the conflict is removed, not merely disclosed |
| Australia's other pillars | **Best interests duty + safe harbour**, **opt-in**, **annual Fee Disclosure Statement**, **SoA/FSG/PDS** |
| Common thread globally | Know the client, best interest, control conflicts, disclose, document, review |

> **Exam tip:** the three highest-yield items are (1) **Regulation 15(1)** and the **loyalty/care** split, (2) telling **mis-selling, churning and front-running** apart from a one-sentence scenario, and (3) Australia's **ban** on conflicted remuneration versus the US/India preference for **disclosure**. If a scenario question offers "disclose it" and "avoid it" as separate options, remember the order — **avoid first, disclose only what cannot be avoided.**
