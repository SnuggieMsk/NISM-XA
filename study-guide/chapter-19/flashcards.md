# Chapter 19: Ethical Issues — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **Regulation 15(1), the loyalty/care split, and telling mis-selling from churning from front-running are near-certain exam marks.**

**Q1.** Define ethics.
> **A:** The set of **moral principles that distinguish right conduct from wrong conduct** — the standard a professional follows **even when no rule compels it and no one is watching**.

**Q2.** What is ethical restraint?
> **A:** The **voluntary, self-imposed decision to hold back** from an action that is available, profitable and possibly even **legal**, because it would harm the client, the market or the profession.

**Q3.** Give the sharpest distinction between law and ethics.
> **A:** Law is the **minimum** standard, external and enforced by penalties; ethics is the **higher** standard, internal and enforced by conscience and reputation. Law asks **"is it permitted?"**; ethics asks **"is it right?"** — **ethics begins where the law ends**.

**Q4.** Can conduct be legal and still unethical? Give an example.
> **A:** **Yes.** Selling a 63-year-old with a 2-year horizon a product with a **15-year lock-in** breaks no specific rule but is indefensible.

**Q5.** Why does financial advice depend on ethics more than most businesses?
> **A:** Advice is a **credence good** — the client may not be able to judge its quality **for fifteen years**, by which time the goal it was meant to fund has already passed. The client cannot police quality; only the adviser's ethics can.

**Q6.** What is information asymmetry in an advisory relationship?
> **A:** The **adviser knows far more** than the client about a product's cost, commission, liquidity and true risk. That gap is the very reason the client hired the adviser — and it can be used **for** or **against** him.

**Q7.** Explain the agency (principal–agent) problem in advice.
> **A:** The client is the **principal**, the adviser the **agent**. The agent has his own interests — commission, targets, an employer's product shelf. Wherever the payoffs diverge there is a **conflict of interest**, which resolves in the agent's favour unless regulation and ethics prevent it.

**Q8.** Define moral hazard and adverse selection in this context.
> **A:** **Moral hazard** — the adviser bears **none of the loss** from bad advice but keeps the commission. **Adverse selection** — the **worst products get pushed hardest** because they pay the highest commission.

**Q9.** Name four business benefits of ethical conduct for an advisory firm.
> **A:** **Trust** (the only real product), **client retention** (advisory economics are multi-year), **referrals** (the cheapest acquisition channel), and **lower cost** (fewer complaints, inspections and lawsuits) — plus protection of the **registration** itself.

**Q10.** Define mis-selling.
> **A:** Recommending a product that **does not suit** the client's needs, risk profile or horizon — usually because it **pays the adviser more**. The product is right for the seller, not the buyer.

**Q11.** Define churning.
> **A:** **Excessive trading, switching or portfolio turnover** carried out to generate **brokerage or fresh commission**, not to change the strategy. Lots of activity, no change in the underlying plan.

**Q12.** A ₹25,00,000 portfolio is turned over 8 times a year at a 0.50% round-trip cost. What is the annual cost drag?
> **A:** Value traded = 8 × ₹25,00,000 = **₹2,00,00,000**; cost = 0.50% × ₹2 crore = **₹1,00,000**; as a share of the portfolio = **4.0% per year**. A 0.5× turnover would have cost only **₹6,250 (0.25%)**.

**Q13.** Define front-running.
> **A:** Dealing in a security **ahead of a client's (or employer's) known impending ORDER**, to profit from the price movement that order will cause. Prohibited under the **SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations, 2003**.

**Q14.** How does front-running differ from insider trading?
> **A:** **Front-run an ORDER; insider-trade on NEWS.** Front-running exploits knowledge of an **impending order** (PFUTP Regulations, **2003**); insider trading exploits **unpublished price-sensitive information about the company** (PIT Regulations, **2015**).

**Q15.** How does mis-selling differ from churning?
> **A:** **Mis-selling is about the WRONG product** — a single transaction can be mis-selling. **Churning is about TOO MANY transactions** — the products may each be fine; the **frequency** is the offence.

**Q16.** List five ways returns are misrepresented.
> **A:** **Absolute return passed off as annual**; a **cherry-picked best window**; figures **gross of expenses and tax**; **back-tested "model" returns** shown as actually earned; and **point-to-point instead of rolling** returns (one lucky start date). Add **survivorship bias** — showing only the schemes that survived.

**Q17.** A NAV goes from ₹10 to ₹28 in 9 years. What does the brochure say and what is the honest number?
> **A:** The brochure says **"180% return"**. The honest number is the **CAGR = (28/10)^(1/9) − 1 ≈ 12.1% per annum**. Both describe the same fund; only one is designed to be understood.

**Q18.** ₹10,00,000 grows for 15 years at 12% gross. Compare a 2.00% TER with a 1.10% TER.
> **A:** Net **10.00%** → 10,00,000 × 1.10¹⁵ = **₹41.77 lakh**. Net **10.90%** → 10,00,000 × 1.109¹⁵ = **₹47.21 lakh**. The **0.90% a year gap costs ₹5.44 lakh** — more than half the original investment, and it never appears as a line item on a statement.

**Q19.** Name six ethical issues in financial advice besides mis-selling and churning.
> **A:** **Front-running**, **conflicts of interest**, **unsuitable recommendations** (no risk profiling), **misrepresenting returns**, **hidden costs**, and **breach of client confidentiality**. Also: unauthorised transactions, assured-return claims, rebating/inducements, and advice without diligence.

**Q20.** What exactly is an ethical dilemma?
> **A:** **Not** a choice between right and wrong (that is merely temptation). It is a situation where **two legitimate duties conflict**, where **every option carries some ethical cost**, or where the right course is clear but **expensive** — it costs the fee, the client or the job.

**Q21.** Give the six-step framework for resolving an ethical dilemma.
> **A:** **1 Get the facts** → **2 identify the stakeholders** (client's interest ranks first) → **3 name the ethical issue and conflicting duties** → **4 generate alternatives** (never only two) → **5 test them and consult the compliance officer / Code of Conduct** → **6 decide, act, DOCUMENT and review**.

**Q22.** Name the four quick ethical tests.
> **A:** The **legality test** (does it breach any rule?), the **publicity/newspaper test** (front page tomorrow with my name?), the **mirror/family test** (would I sell this to my own parent?), and the **role-model test** (would a respected senior professional do it?).

**Q23.** What is the correct order of preference for handling a conflict of interest?
> **A:** **Avoid → manage → disclose.** Disclosure is the **last** resort, not the first — some conflicts are too severe for disclosure to cure.

**Q24.** A conservative 63-year-old insists on 90% small caps. What is the right response?
> **A:** Neither silent compliance (breach of the **duty of care**) nor a flat refusal (abandoning the client). **Quantify the risk in rupees**, offer a profile-consistent compromise, and if he insists, **record the advice given and his written instruction that it is contrary to advice** — the file must show what you advised, not only what he wanted.

**Q25.** Quote the provision that makes an Indian investment adviser a fiduciary.
> **A:** **Regulation 15(1) of the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013:** *"An investment adviser shall act in a fiduciary capacity towards its clients and shall disclose all conflicts of interest as and when they arise."*

**Q26.** What is a fiduciary?
> **A:** Someone **entrusted with another's affairs who must place that person's interest above his own** — the **highest duty the law recognises** in a commercial relationship.

**Q27.** Name and explain the two limbs of fiduciary duty.
> **A:** **Duty of LOYALTY** — put the client first, avoid or fully disclose conflicts, take no undisclosed compensation ("whose side are you on?"). **Duty of CARE** — know the client, risk-profile him, research the product, recommend what fits, and **monitor** it ("did you do the work?").

**Q28.** Contrast the fiduciary standard with the suitability standard.
> **A:** Fiduciary asks **"is this the BEST option for the client?"**; suitability asks only **"is it NOT UNSUITABLE?"**. Fiduciary is **continuing**, client-fee-based and conflict-averse; suitability is largely **point-of-sale** and commission-based.

**Q29.** List the nine heads of the Code of Conduct in the Third Schedule of the IA Regulations.
> **A:** **Honesty and fairness; diligence; capability; information about clients; information to clients; fair and reasonable charges; conflicts of interest; compliance; responsibility of senior management.**

**Q30.** Which Code of Conduct head makes ethics a management problem rather than a staff problem?
> **A:** **Responsibility of senior management** — senior management bears **primary responsibility** for compliance and for the firm's ethical standards.

**Q31.** What are the SEBI limits on an investment adviser's fees?
> **A:** Per client family, either up to **₹1,25,000 per annum under the fixed-fee mode** or up to **2.5% of Assets Under Advice per annum** — **only one mode at a time**, with advance fees also capped.

**Q32.** May an investment adviser guarantee a return, and what must he say about his registration?
> **A:** **No** — assured or guaranteed returns are prohibited on market-linked advice. He must also make clear that **SEBI registration is not an endorsement** and gives **no assurance of returns or performance**.

**Q33.** Give five items from SEBI's "Do's" for investors.
> **A:** Deal **only with SEBI-registered intermediaries** (verify the registration number); **read the offer document**; complete **KYC** with correct PAN and bank details; insist on **proper documentation** (contract note, statement); and give **instructions in writing**. Also: pay through your **own bank account**, **nominate**, monitor statements, and know **SCORES**.

**Q34.** Give five items from SEBI's "Don'ts" for investors.
> **A:** Don't deal with **unregistered entities**; don't believe **assured-return promises**; don't act on **tips, rumours or WhatsApp calls**; **never sign blank forms, cheques or delivery instruction slips**; and **never deal in cash** or share **passwords/OTPs**.

**Q35.** Compress SEBI's Do's and Don'ts into a five-word hook.
> **A:** **REGISTERED, WRITTEN, BANKING CHANNEL, READ, NEVER-BLANK.**

**Q36.** Who must conduct an investment adviser's annual compliance audit, and by when?
> **A:** A **member of the ICAI (Chartered Accountant) or ICSI (Company Secretary)**, covering compliance with the IA Regulations, to be completed within **six months of the end of the financial year** — i.e. by **30 September** for a year ending 31 March.

**Q37.** What exactly must be reported to SEBI after the audit, and within what time?
> **A:** The **adverse findings TOGETHER WITH the action taken on them**, duly **approved by the management/board/proprietor**, **within one month of the date of the audit report** — submitted through the administration and supervisory body (**BASL**).

**Q38.** Give the eight steps for addressing annual audit observations.
> **A:** **1** Treat them as a diagnostic → **2 classify** by severity → **3** find the **root cause** → **4** assign an **owner and deadline** in a remediation register → **5 remediate the CLIENT**, not just the process → **6** obtain **management approval** of the action-taken report → **7 report to SEBI/BASL on time** → **8** change the system and **verify closure next year**.

**Q39.** Why is a repeat audit observation the most serious kind?
> **A:** Because it **proves the earlier fix was cosmetic** — the firm knew of the failure and did not truly correct it, so it is liable to be treated as **wilful** rather than inadvertent.

**Q40.** Summarise the US SEC framework for investment advisers.
> **A:** The **Investment Advisers Act of 1940**, enforced by the **SEC**. Advisers are **fiduciaries** (confirmed in **SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 1963**) owing a **duty of care** (best interest, best execution, ongoing monitoring) and a **duty of loyalty** (full and fair disclosure of material conflicts, informed consent). Disclosure is via **Form ADV Part 2**; firms need a written **code of ethics**, **personal-trading reporting**, written policies **reviewed annually**, and a **Chief Compliance Officer**.

**Q41.** What does Australia's FOFA package require, and what makes it distinctive?
> **A:** Under **ASIC** and the Corporations Act: a statutory **best interests duty** with a **"safe harbour"** checklist, **opt-in** renewal of ongoing fee arrangements, an annual **Fee Disclosure Statement**, and **FSG / Statement of Advice / PDS** documents. What makes it distinctive is the outright **BAN on conflicted remuneration** — commissions and volume benefits are **removed**, not merely disclosed.

**Q42.** State the common thread across the Indian, US and Australian regimes.
> **A:** **Know the client, act in his best interest, control conflicts, disclose fully, document everything, and review periodically.** They differ only in **how far they trust disclosure versus outright prohibition** — Australia bans the conflicted payment, while India and the US rely more on **avoidance plus disclosure**.
