# Chapter 11: Mutual Funds — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **NAV, cut-off timing, scheme categories and systematic transactions carry the most marks.**

**Q1.** Define a mutual fund in one sentence.
> **A:** A vehicle that **pools money from many investors** and invests it, according to a stated objective, in a **professionally managed portfolio**; investors hold **units** and share gains and losses proportionately.

**Q2.** Describe the three-tier structure of an Indian mutual fund.
> **A:** **Sponsor → Trust (Trustees) → AMC.** The sponsor promotes the fund, the **trustees hold the assets in trust for unit-holders** and oversee the AMC, and the **AMC manages the investments** for a fee.

**Q3.** Why does the trust structure matter to investors?
> **A:** The scheme's assets belong to the **unit-holders through the trust, not to the AMC**. If the AMC fails, the assets are **ring-fenced** — a fundamental investor protection.

**Q4.** State the sponsor's minimum contribution and the AMC's minimum net worth.
> **A:** The sponsor must contribute at least **40% of the AMC's net worth**; the AMC must maintain a minimum net worth of **₹50 crore**.

**Q5.** What proportion of trustees must be independent?
> **A:** At least **two-thirds**.

**Q6.** State the NAV formula.
> **A:** **NAV = (Total assets − Total liabilities) ÷ Number of units outstanding** — equivalently, net assets ÷ units.

**Q7.** A scheme has securities ₹520 crore, cash ₹15 crore, liabilities ₹7 crore and 25 crore units. What is the NAV?
> **A:** (520 + 15 − 7) ÷ 25 = 528 ÷ 25 = **₹21.12**.

**Q8.** Is a fund with a ₹10 NAV "cheaper" than one at ₹500?
> **A:** **No.** Two funds can hold identical portfolios and differ only in unit count. **Returns depend on the portfolio, never on the NAV level** — the most common retail misconception.

**Q9.** State the cut-off times for purchases and redemptions.
> **A:** **Liquid/overnight fund purchases: 1:30 p.m.** **All other scheme purchases: 3:00 p.m.** **Redemptions (all schemes): 3:00 p.m.**

**Q10.** What is the decisive rule for which NAV you receive on a purchase?
> **A:** The **funds must be realised (credited to the scheme) before the cut-off** — not merely the application submitted. Applying at 2 p.m. with money arriving next morning gets the **next day's** NAV.

**Q11.** Which regulations govern mutual funds, and from which year?
> **A:** The **SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996**.

**Q12.** Name the three key scheme documents.
> **A:** **SID** (Scheme Information Document — scheme specifics), **SAI** (Statement of Additional Information — about the fund house), and **KIM** (Key Information Memorandum — the abridged summary with the application form).

**Q13.** Compare open-ended, close-ended and interval schemes.
> **A:** **Open-ended** — transact with the AMC any business day, variable corpus. **Close-ended** — subscribe only at NFO, then trade on an **exchange** (often at a discount to NAV); listing is mandatory. **Interval** — transact only during specified **transaction periods**.

**Q14.** What is an ETF and what does it require?
> **A:** A fund tracking an **index or commodity**, bought and sold **on the exchange at market price**. It requires a **demat account** and typically has a **lower TER** than an active fund.

**Q15.** What is the Total Expense Ratio, and is it charged separately?
> **A:** Annual scheme costs as a percentage of average net assets, **capped by SEBI**. It is **already deducted from the NAV** — never billed separately.

**Q16.** What is the minimum equity allocation for an equity scheme?
> **A:** **65%**.

**Q17.** State the defining allocation for large cap, mid cap and small cap funds.
> **A:** **Large cap** — ≥80% in the **top 100** companies. **Mid cap** — ≥65% in ranks **101–250**. **Small cap** — ≥65% in ranks **251 onwards**.

**Q18.** What defines a flexi cap versus a multi cap fund?
> **A:** **Multi cap** — at least **25% each** in large, mid and small. **Flexi cap** — ≥65% equity with **no cap-size restriction**, so the manager can go anywhere.

**Q19.** State ELSS's three defining features.
> **A:** **≥80% in equity**, a **3-year lock-in** (the shortest of the 80C options), and eligibility for a **Section 80C** deduction.

**Q20.** What is a focused fund?
> **A:** An equity fund holding a **maximum of 30 stocks** — concentrated, therefore higher stock-specific risk.

**Q21.** What is the maximum maturity for a liquid fund?
> **A:** **91 days.**

**Q22.** Distinguish a gilt fund, a corporate bond fund and a credit risk fund.
> **A:** **Gilt** — ≥80% in **government securities** (no credit risk, full interest rate risk). **Corporate bond** — ≥80% in **AA+ and above**. **Credit risk** — ≥65% in **below AA+** (higher yield, higher default risk).

**Q23.** State the equity allocation for conservative and aggressive hybrid funds.
> **A:** **Conservative hybrid** — 10–25% equity, 75–90% debt. **Aggressive hybrid** — **65–80% equity**, 20–35% debt.

**Q24.** What is an arbitrage fund and why is its tax treatment notable?
> **A:** It holds **≥65% in arbitrage positions**, giving low-risk, debt-like returns — but because equity exposure exceeds 65% it is **taxed as an equity fund**, which is often more favourable.

**Q25.** What defines a multi asset allocation fund?
> **A:** Investment in **at least three asset classes**, with a **minimum 10% each**.

**Q26.** State the lock-in for retirement and children's funds.
> **A:** **Retirement fund** — 5 years or until retirement, whichever is earlier. **Children's fund** — 5 years or until the child turns 18, whichever is earlier.

**Q27.** What proportion must an index fund hold in the index's securities?
> **A:** At least **95%**.

**Q28.** Compare the Growth and IDCW options.
> **A:** **Growth** — gains stay invested, NAV rises, **no payouts** (most tax-efficient for accumulation). **IDCW** — a distribution is paid out or reinvested, and the **NAV falls by the amount distributed**.

**Q29.** Why was "dividend" renamed IDCW?
> **A:** Because **Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal** is honest: part of any distribution is simply the **investor's own capital returned**, and the NAV drops correspondingly. It is **not extra income**, and is taxed at slab rates.

**Q30.** Is a switch between schemes a taxable event?
> **A:** **Yes.** A switch is treated as a **redemption plus a fresh purchase**, so capital gains tax applies — even though no money reaches the investor's bank account.

**Q31.** Define SIP, SWP and STP.
> **A:** **SIP** — invest a fixed amount at fixed intervals (accumulation). **SWP** — withdraw a fixed amount at fixed intervals (income). **STP** — transfer periodically from one scheme to another (typically debt → equity, to deploy a lump sum gradually).

**Q32.** What are DTP and VIP?
> **A:** **DTP (Dividend Transfer Plan)** — distributions from one scheme are invested into another. **VIP (Value Averaging Investment Plan)** — invest **more when markets fall and less when they rise** to reach a target value.

**Q33.** Explain how rupee-cost averaging works.
> **A:** A **fixed rupee amount buys more units when the NAV is low** and fewer when it is high, so the **average cost per unit falls below the average NAV**.

**Q34.** ₹10,000 a month is invested at NAVs of ₹50, ₹40, ₹25 and ₹50. What is the average cost versus the average NAV?
> **A:** Units = 200 + 250 + 400 + 200 = **1,050**; invested ₹40,000; **average cost = ₹38.10**. Simple average NAV = **₹41.25**. The cost is **below** the average NAV — the averaging benefit.

**Q35.** What does a SIP NOT do?
> **A:** It does **not guarantee profits** and does not protect against a continuously falling market. It manages **timing risk, not market risk**.

**Q36.** Compare direct and regular plans.
> **A:** **Identical portfolio.** The **direct** plan carries **no distribution commission**, so its **TER is lower and NAV higher** over time. The **regular** plan embeds commission in the TER.

**Q37.** Why does an Investment Adviser recommend direct plans while a distributor cannot?
> **A:** An **IA is paid a fee by the client**, so recommending the lower-cost option costs the adviser nothing. A **distributor is paid by commission**, which direct plans do not pay.

**Q38.** How can an IA help a client transact in direct plans?
> **A:** By guiding them through **AMC/RTA platforms, MF Utilities and exchange platforms** (BSE StAR MF, NSE MFSS), and assisting with KYC, folio consolidation and execution-only transaction feeds.

**Q39.** What is an exit load and why does it exist?
> **A:** A charge on redemption within a specified period, designed to **discourage short-term churn** that would disrupt the portfolio and disadvantage remaining investors.

**Q40.** What is mark to market in a mutual fund context?
> **A:** Valuing the scheme's securities at **current market prices daily**, so the published NAV reflects what the portfolio is genuinely worth today.
