## 11.1 What a Mutual Fund Is

**A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it, according to a stated objective, in a portfolio managed by professionals. Investors hold UNITS and share the gains and losses in proportion to their holding.**

### Key features

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pooling** | Small amounts aggregate into a large, efficiently managed corpus |
| **Professional management** | A qualified fund manager makes the decisions |
| **Diversification** | Even ₹500 buys a slice of dozens of securities |
| **Liquidity** | Open-ended schemes redeem on any business day |
| **Transparency** | **Daily NAV**, monthly portfolio disclosure, scheme documents |
| **Regulation** | SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, **1996** |
| **Economies of scale** | Lower per-unit transaction and research costs |
| **Variety** | Schemes for every objective and horizon |

### The three-tier structure

**Sponsor → Trust (Trustees) → AMC**

| Entity | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sponsor** | Promotes the fund; must contribute at least **40% of the AMC's net worth** |
| **Trust / Trustees** | Hold the assets **in trust for unit-holders**; oversee the AMC. At least **two-thirds independent** trustees |
| **AMC (Asset Management Company)** | Manages the investments for a fee. Minimum net worth **₹50 crore** |
| **Custodian** | Holds the securities |
| **RTA** | Maintains investor records (CAMS, KFin) |

> 🧠 **Why the trust structure matters:** the scheme's assets belong to the **unit-holders through the trust**, *not* to the AMC. If the AMC fails, the scheme's assets are ring-fenced — a fundamental investor protection.

## 11.2 ⭐ Key Concepts and Terms

### Net Asset Value

$$\boxed{NAV = \frac{\text{Total assets} - \text{Total liabilities}}{\text{Number of units outstanding}}}$$

Or equivalently, **Net Assets ÷ Units outstanding**.

**Example:** a scheme holds securities worth ₹520 crore, cash ₹15 crore, and owes ₹7 crore in expenses. Units outstanding: 25 crore.
NAV = (520 + 15 − 7) ÷ 25 = 528 ÷ 25 = **₹21.12**

> ⚠️ **A low NAV is NOT "cheap".** A ₹10 NAV fund and a ₹500 NAV fund can hold identical portfolios; only the unit count differs. **Returns depend on the portfolio, never on the NAV level.** This is the single most common retail misconception.

### Other terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Investment objective** | What the scheme aims to achieve — stated in the SID; determines the category |
| **Units** | The investor's proportionate share of the scheme |
| **Net assets** | Total assets minus liabilities |
| **Mark to market** | Securities valued at **current market prices** daily, so the NAV reflects reality |
| **Total Expense Ratio (TER)** | Annual costs as a % of average net assets — **capped by SEBI** and already deducted from NAV |
| **Exit load** | A charge on redemption within a specified period, to discourage short-term churn |
| **AUM** | Assets Under Management |

### ⭐ Cut-off timing

The NAV you receive depends on when the application **and the money** reach the fund.

| Scheme type | Cut-off | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquid and overnight funds** — purchase | **1:30 p.m.** | **Previous day's NAV** if funds are realised before cut-off |
| **All other schemes** — purchase | **3:00 p.m.** | Same day's NAV |
| **Redemption (all schemes)** | **3:00 p.m.** | Same day's NAV |

> ⚠️ **The decisive rule:** for **purchases of any amount**, the applicable NAV depends on **funds being realised (credited to the scheme) before the cut-off**, not merely on when the application was submitted. Submitting at 2 p.m. with money arriving next morning gets the **next day's** NAV.

## 11.3 Scheme Structures

| | **Open-ended** | **Close-ended** | **Interval** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Subscription/redemption** | Any business day, with the AMC | Only at NFO; then traded on an exchange | Only during specified **transaction periods** |
| **Corpus** | Variable | Fixed | Fixed between intervals |
| **Liquidity** | High | Via the exchange, often at a discount to NAV | Only at intervals |
| **Listing** | Not required | **Mandatory** | Mandatory |

### Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)
- Track an **index or commodity**; bought and sold **on the exchange** like a share, at market price.
- Require a **demat account**.
- **Lower TER** than active funds; typically passive.
- Large investors transact directly with the AMC in **creation units**; arbitrage keeps the market price close to NAV.

## 11.4 Regulatory Framework

Governed by the **SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996**.

**Key documents:**
- **SID (Scheme Information Document)** — scheme-specific: objective, strategy, risk, expenses
- **SAI (Statement of Additional Information)** — about the fund house
- **KIM (Key Information Memorandum)** — the abridged summary accompanying the application form

**Investor service standards:** dispatch of account statements, redemption proceeds within prescribed timelines, mandatory **Riskometer** disclosure, and monthly portfolio disclosure.

**Other protections:** investment restrictions (single issuer and sector caps), mandatory **NAV computation and publication daily**, and segregation of assets under the trust.

## 11.5 ⭐ Mutual Fund Products (SEBI Categorisation)

SEBI's 2017 categorisation standardised scheme types so that funds are comparable.

### 11.5.1 Equity schemes (minimum 65% in equity)

| Category | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Large cap** | ≥80% in the top 100 companies |
| **Mid cap** | ≥65% in companies ranked 101–250 |
| **Small cap** | ≥65% in companies ranked 251 onwards |
| **Large & mid cap** | ≥35% each in large and mid |
| **Multi cap** | ≥25% each in large, mid and small |
| **Flexi cap** | ≥65% equity, **no cap-size restriction** |
| **ELSS** | ≥80% equity; **3-year lock-in**; Section 80C benefit |
| **Sectoral/thematic** | ≥80% in a sector or theme |
| **Focused** | Maximum **30 stocks** |
| **Value / Contra** | ≥65% following that strategy |
| **Dividend yield** | ≥65% in dividend-yielding stocks |

### 11.5.2 Debt schemes (16 categories, defined by duration or credit)

| Category | Duration / feature |
|---|---|
| **Overnight** | 1 day |
| **Liquid** | Up to **91 days** |
| **Ultra short** | 3–6 months (Macaulay duration) |
| **Low duration** | 6–12 months |
| **Money market** | Up to 1 year |
| **Short duration** | 1–3 years |
| **Medium duration** | 3–4 years |
| **Medium to long** | 4–7 years |
| **Long duration** | Over 7 years |
| **Dynamic bond** | Duration managed actively |
| **Corporate bond** | ≥80% in **AA+ and above** |
| **Credit risk** | ≥65% in **below AA+** — higher yield, higher risk |
| **Banking & PSU** | ≥80% in bank/PSU/PFI debt |
| **Gilt** | ≥80% in **government securities** — no credit risk, but full interest rate risk |
| **Floater** | ≥65% in floating rate instruments |

### 11.5.3 Hybrid schemes

| Category | Allocation |
|---|---|
| **Conservative hybrid** | 10–25% equity, 75–90% debt |
| **Balanced hybrid** | 40–60% equity, no arbitrage |
| **Aggressive hybrid** | **65–80% equity**, 20–35% debt |
| **Dynamic asset allocation / BAF** | Equity and debt **managed dynamically** (0–100%) |
| **Multi asset allocation** | At least **three** asset classes, ≥10% each |
| **Arbitrage** | ≥65% in arbitrage positions — **taxed as equity**, low risk |
| **Equity savings** | Equity, arbitrage and debt |

### 11.5.4 Solution-oriented schemes
- **Retirement fund** — lock-in of **5 years or until retirement**, whichever is earlier
- **Children's fund** — lock-in of **5 years or until the child turns 18**, whichever is earlier

### 11.5.5 Other schemes
- **Index funds / ETFs** — ≥95% in the securities of the tracked index
- **Fund of Funds** — ≥95% in the underlying funds

## 11.6 Investment Options

| Option | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Growth** | Gains stay invested; NAV rises; **no payouts** — the most tax-efficient for accumulation |
| **IDCW — Payout** | *Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal* is paid out; **NAV falls by the amount distributed** |
| **IDCW — Reinvestment** | The distribution buys fresh units at the ex-NAV |

> ⚠️ **The "dividend" was renamed IDCW precisely because it is NOT extra income.** Part of any distribution is simply the investor's own capital returned, and the NAV drops correspondingly. It is taxable in the investor's hands at slab rates.

---

## 11.7 Triggers
Pre-set instructions that act automatically on a defined event — e.g. redeem when the NAV reaches a target, or switch when a value threshold is crossed. They enforce discipline and remove emotion.

---

## 11.8 Transactions

| Transaction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fresh purchase** | First investment in a scheme (a new folio) |
| **Additional purchase** | Further investment in an existing folio |
| **Redemption** | Selling units back to the fund (open-ended) |
| **Switch** | Moving from one scheme to another within the same fund house — **treated as a redemption plus a purchase, so it is a taxable event** |
| **IDCW reinvestment** | Distribution used to buy fresh units |

## 11.9 ⭐ Systematic Transactions

| Plan | What it does | Best used for |
|---|---|---|
| **SIP — Systematic Investment Plan** | Invest a **fixed amount at fixed intervals** | Accumulation; **rupee-cost averaging** and discipline |
| **SWP — Systematic Withdrawal Plan** | Withdraw a **fixed amount at fixed intervals** | Regular income in retirement; more tax-efficient than IDCW |
| **STP — Systematic Transfer Plan** | Transfer periodically **from one scheme to another** (typically debt → equity) | Deploying a lump sum gradually |
| **DTP — Dividend Transfer Plan** | Distributions from one scheme are invested into another | Channelling income into growth |
| **VIP — Value Averaging Investment Plan** | Invest **more when markets fall, less when they rise**, to reach a target value | A more aggressive form of averaging |

### How rupee-cost averaging works
A fixed rupee amount buys **more units when the NAV is low** and **fewer when it is high**, so the **average cost per unit falls below the average NAV**.

**Example:** ₹10,000 a month for 4 months at NAVs of ₹50, ₹40, ₹25, ₹50.
- Units bought: 200 + 250 + 400 + 200 = **1,050**
- Total invested = **₹40,000**; average cost = 40,000 ÷ 1,050 = **₹38.10**
- Simple average NAV = (50 + 40 + 25 + 50) ÷ 4 = **₹41.25**

**The average cost (₹38.10) is below the average NAV (₹41.25)** — that gap is the benefit of rupee-cost averaging.

> ⚠️ SIPs **do not guarantee profits** and do not protect against a market that falls continuously. They manage **timing risk**, not market risk.

## 11.10 ⭐ Direct vs Regular Plans

| | **Direct plan** | **Regular plan** |
|---|---|---|
| **Bought through** | The AMC / RTA / the fund's own platform | A distributor |
| **Distribution commission** | **None** | Embedded in the TER |
| **TER** | **Lower** | Higher |
| **NAV** | **Higher** over time | Lower |
| **Portfolio** | **Identical** | Identical |

> 🧠 **The same portfolio, a lower cost.** A 0.75% annual TER difference compounds to a very large sum over 20 years — which is why an **Investment Adviser (who charges the client a fee) recommends direct plans**, while a distributor (paid by commission) cannot.

**How an IA helps a client transact in direct plans:** guiding them through the AMC/RTA platforms, the MF Utilities and exchange platforms (BSE StAR MF, NSE MFSS), and helping with KYC, folio consolidation and execution-only transaction feeds.
