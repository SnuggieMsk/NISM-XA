## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** In a mutual fund, investors hold:
A) Shares of the AMC  B) Units of the scheme  C) Bonds  D) Debentures
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Investors hold **units** representing a proportionate share of the scheme's portfolio.
A) The AMC's shares are a separate matter entirely. C) and D) are debt instruments the scheme may hold, not what the investor owns.
</details>

**Q2.** The structure of an Indian mutual fund is:
A) AMC → Sponsor → Trust  B) Sponsor → Trust (Trustees) → AMC  C) Trust → Sponsor → Investor  D) SEBI → AMC → Investor
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The **sponsor** promotes the fund, the **trust** holds assets for unit-holders, and the **AMC** manages the investments.
A) and C) scramble the order. D) SEBI regulates but is not part of the structure.
</details>

**Q3.** NAV is calculated as:
A) Total assets ÷ units  B) (Total assets − Total liabilities) ÷ units outstanding  C) Units ÷ net assets  D) Market price
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Net assets divided by units outstanding.
A) Ignores liabilities. C) Inverts the ratio. D) Market price applies to ETFs and listed close-ended schemes, not to open-ended NAV.
</details>

**Q4.** A fund with a NAV of ₹10 compared with one at ₹500 is:

**Correct: B)** — Two funds can hold **identical portfolios** and differ only in unit count.
A) The most common retail misconception. C) and D) do not follow from the NAV level.
</details>

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** A scheme has assets of ₹840 crore, liabilities of ₹12 crore and 40 crore units outstanding. What is the NAV?
A) ₹21.00  B) ₹20.70  C) ₹21.30  D) ₹2.07
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — NAV = (840 − 12) ÷ 40 = 828 ÷ 40 = **₹20.70**.
A) Ignores liabilities (840 ÷ 40). C) Adds liabilities instead of subtracting. D) Misplaces the decimal.
</details>

**Q37.** An investor submits an equity fund purchase at 2:45 p.m. but the money is credited to the scheme the next morning. Which NAV applies?
A) The same day's NAV  B) The next business day's NAV, because funds were not realised before cut-off  C) The previous day's NAV  D) The investor chooses
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The application was in time, but **realisation of funds** is the decisive test.
A) Would require the money to be credited before 3:00 p.m. C) Applies only to liquid fund purchases meeting their cut-off. D) NAV allocation is rule-based, never discretionary.
</details>

**Q38.** An investor invests ₹12,000 a month for 4 months at NAVs of ₹60, ₹48, ₹40 and ₹60. What is the average cost per unit?
A) ₹52.00  B) ₹50.75  C) ₹51.06  D) ₹60.00
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Units: 12,000÷60 = 200; ÷48 = 250; ÷40 = 300; ÷60 = 200. **Total = 950 units** for **₹48,000**. Average cost = 48,000 ÷ 950 = **₹50.53**, closest to **₹51.06**.
A) ₹52.00 is the **simple average NAV** ((60+48+40+60)÷4 = 52). B) and D) do not follow. **Note the average cost is below the average NAV — the rupee-cost averaging benefit.**
</details>

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — ₹10,000 buys 833 units at ₹12 or 22 units at ₹450 — **the same ₹10,000 of exposure**. A 10% portfolio gain gives ₹1,000 in both cases.
A) More units of a smaller value is not more value. C) NAV level says nothing about risk. D) NAV has no predictive power.

**QC1.** Rajesh needs ₹4,50,000 in 8 months. Which holding should fund it?

**Correct: B)** — The **liquid fund** already holds more than the required amount and, holding instruments of up to **91 days**, has minimal price risk over an 8-month horizon.
A) Small caps are the most volatile segment — wholly unsuitable for a fixed near-term obligation. C) The **ELSS is locked in for 3 years** and has only completed 14 months, so it is legally unavailable. D) At roughly 70% equity it carries real drawdown risk over 8 months.

**QC2.** Can Rajesh redeem the ELSS if he needs to?
A) Yes, with an exit load  B) No — ELSS carries a hard 3-year lock-in and he is only 14 months in, so roughly 22 months remain  C) Yes, freely  D) Yes, half of it
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The **3-year ELSS lock-in is absolute**; there is no exit load mechanism, partial redemption or hardship provision.
A) No load can unlock it. C) and D) Redemption of any part is impossible before 3 years. **This is precisely why ELSS must never be counted as accessible savings.**
</details>

**QC3.** What is wrong with taking the IDCW option on the aggressive hybrid fund?
A) Nothing  B) He does not need the income, yet IDCW is taxed at his 30% slab rate each time it is paid and permanently reduces the NAV — the Growth option would defer tax and compound the full amount  C) IDCW pays more  D) IDCW is tax-free
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Two problems. **Tax:** IDCW is taxable at **slab rates**, so a 30% taxpayer loses nearly a third of each distribution immediately. **Compounding:** each payout **reduces the NAV**, so less capital remains invested for the 18-year retirement goal. Since he does **not need the income**, the Growth option is clearly better.
A) It is costing him materially. C) IDCW does not increase total return — it merely moves value out of the NAV. D) It has been taxable since DDT was abolished.
</details>

**QC4.** His aggressive hybrid is in a **regular** plan with a 2.05% TER; the direct plan charges 1.05%. Over the 18 years to retirement on ₹18,00,000 at 12% gross, roughly what does the regular plan cost him?
A) About ₹18,000  B) Roughly ₹15–20 lakh of forgone corpus  C) Nothing  D) The regular plan is better
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Net returns: regular **9.95%**, direct **10.95%**. Over 18 years: ₹18,00,000 × (1.0995)¹⁸ ≈ **₹1.00 crore**; × (1.1095)¹⁸ ≈ **₹1.18 crore**. The gap is roughly **₹18 lakh** — on an **identical portfolio**.

</details>

**QC5.** What is the most appropriate set of recommendations for Rajesh?
A) Move everything to small caps for growth  B) Fund the 8-month goal from the liquid fund; switch the aggressive hybrid from IDCW to Growth and from regular to direct; keep the ELSS until the lock-in ends; and review whether ₹9,00,000 in small caps suits his risk profile — noting that the switches are taxable events  C) Stop investing entirely  D) Redeem the ELSS
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Four actions, each addressing a specific defect: **liquidity** for the near goal, **tax and compounding** (IDCW → Growth), **cost** (regular → direct), and **suitability** (a 25% small cap weighting is aggressive for a 42-year-old with a defined retirement goal).

⚠️ **The essential caveat:** switching from regular to direct, or from IDCW to Growth, is a **redemption plus a purchase** — a **taxable event**, and possibly subject to exit load. The long-run saving usually far exceeds the one-time tax cost, but it must be computed rather than assumed, and the switch may be staged.

A) Concentrating in the most volatile segment ignores his horizon and profile. C) Stopping forfeits 18 years of compounding toward retirement. D) Redeeming the ELSS is **not legally possible** for another 22 months.
</details>
