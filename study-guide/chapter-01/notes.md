## 1.1 What is Financial Planning?

**Financial planning is the process of meeting your life goals through the proper management of your finances.**

Unpack that sentence — every word is doing work:

- **Life goals** — buying a house, funding a child's education, retiring comfortably, leaving money to heirs. Not "getting rich"; *specific, dated, costed* objectives.
- **Process** — it's ongoing, not a one-time event. You plan, act, review, adjust.
- **Management of finances** — income, expenses, savings, investments, insurance, loans and taxes are all levers.

> 🧠 **The core idea:** money has no meaning on its own. ₹50 lakh is neither "enough" nor "not enough" until you attach it to a goal (a flat in Pune? 25 years of retirement?). Financial planning is the discipline of connecting **money you will have** to **money you will need**.

### Investment vs. Financial Planning

| | Investment advice | Financial planning |
|---|---|---|
| **Question asked** | "Which fund should I buy?" | "What are you trying to achieve, and what set of decisions gets you there?" |
| **Scope** | One product decision | Cash flow, insurance, debt, investment, tax, retirement, estate |
| **Time horizon** | Often short | Whole lifetime |
| **Success measured by** | Product return | Whether the goal was met |

A product can beat its benchmark and *still* fail you — if it's illiquid when you need the money. That gap is exactly why financial planning exists.

## 1.2 Why is Financial Planning Needed?

Five forces make planning necessary:

1. **Goals cost money and arrive on dates you don't control.** College fees arrive when the child turns 18, not when markets are up.
2. **Inflation quietly destroys purchasing power.** At 6% inflation, ₹1 lakh of expenses today becomes about ₹3.2 lakh in 20 years. Money kept "safe" in a low-interest account *loses* real value.
3. **Income is finite and lumpy.** Most people earn for ~30–35 years but spend for ~60+ years. The earning years must fund the non-earning years.
4. **Life is uncertain.** Death, disability, illness and job loss can wipe out a plan that had no insurance or emergency fund.
5. **Products are complex and conflicted.** Investors face hundreds of schemes and a sales industry paid to push them.

### 1.2.1 Role of the Financial Planner

The planner:

- **Collects** data on income, expenses, assets, liabilities, insurance and taxes;
- **Quantifies** goals — converting "a good retirement" into "₹4.2 crore by 2050";
- **Assesses risk** — both the *ability* and the *willingness* to take risk;
- **Recommends** a coherent strategy across products (never a product in isolation);
- **Implements** or helps implement;
- **Reviews and rebalances** as life and markets change.

Critically, the planner brings **objectivity and discipline**. Investors panic-sell in crashes and chase winners in booms; a planner is the circuit-breaker between emotion and the "confirm" button.

### 1.2.2 Financial Planning vs. a Typical Financial Advisory Service

| Aspect | Typical advisory / distribution | Financial planning |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A product is available to sell | A client goal needs funding |
| **Focus** | Product features and returns | The client's whole balance sheet and cash flow |
| **Coverage** | One need at a time | Integrated across all needs |
| **Compensation** | Often commission from the manufacturer | Often a fee from the client |
| **Duty** | May be a *suitability* standard only | **Fiduciary** — client's interest first |
| **Relationship** | Transaction | Long-term engagement |

> ⚠️ **Exam favourite.** A **fiduciary** must put the client's interest *ahead of their own*. This is the defining obligation of a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (see Chapters 18–19).

## 1.3 Scope of Financial Planning

Financial planning has **eight** components. Expect questions that give you a client situation and ask which component applies.

### 1.3.1 Personal Financial Analysis

The diagnosis stage: where does the client stand *today*?

- Build the **personal balance sheet** (assets, liabilities, net worth) and the **cash flow statement** (income, expenses, surplus).
- Compute the ratios (Chapter 3) — savings, solvency, liquidity, debt-to-income.
- Then **set goals**.

**Goal-setting rules:**

- **Prioritise goals.** Not everything can be funded at once. Retirement and a child's education generally outrank a car upgrade — because you can borrow for education but *nobody lends for retirement*.
- **Ensure adequate focus on important goals.** Long-dated, expensive goals (retirement) get ignored because they feel far away. Yet they benefit most from compounding, so starting early is worth more than investing more later.
- **Stagger the timing of goals.** If three big goals land in the same year, the plan breaks. Move a discretionary goal (a foreign holiday, a car) forward or back so the funding requirement smooths out.

A good goal is **specific, quantified in future rupees (i.e. inflation-adjusted), and dated**. "Save for my daughter's college" is not a goal. "₹42 lakh in March 2038 for a 4-year engineering degree" is.

### 1.3.2 Cash Flow Management and Budgeting
Tracking inflows and outflows, generating a **surplus**, and building an **emergency/contingency fund** (commonly 3–6 months of expenses). Without a surplus there is nothing to invest — this is the engine of the whole plan. *(Chapter 3.)*

### 1.3.3 Insurance Planning
Protecting the plan against catastrophic events — death, disability, illness, property loss. Insurance is about **risk transfer**, not returns. If the earning member dies uninsured, every other goal collapses.

### 1.3.4 Debt Management and Counselling
Keeping borrowing productive and serviceable — the right *purpose*, *cost*, and *maturity* of debt; repayment strategies; and rehabilitation when a borrower is over-leveraged. *(Chapter 4.)*

### 1.3.5 Investment Planning and Asset Allocation
Choosing the mix of asset classes (equity, debt, gold, real estate) that matches each goal's horizon and the client's risk profile — then selecting products within that mix. **Asset allocation, not stock selection, drives most of a portfolio's outcome.** *(Chapters 14–15.)*

### 1.3.6 Tax Planning
Arranging affairs to legally minimise tax — using available exemptions, deductions and the tax treatment of different products. Note: tax efficiency is a *modifier*, never the primary reason to buy a product.

### 1.3.7 Retirement Planning
Estimating the corpus needed to fund post-retirement expenses (inflated to the retirement date) and building it through the earning years. *(Covered in depth in Level 2.)*

### 1.3.8 Estate Planning
Ensuring assets pass to intended beneficiaries smoothly — wills, nominations, trusts, joint holdings. *(Covered in depth in Level 2.)*

## 1.4 Assets, Liabilities and Net Worth

The **personal balance sheet** is a snapshot at a point in time.

$$\textbf{Net Worth} = \textbf{Total Assets} - \textbf{Total Liabilities}$$

### Classifying assets

| Type | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquid assets** | Convertible to cash quickly with little loss of value | Cash, savings balance, liquid funds, sweep-in FDs |
| **Financial assets** | Claims on someone else | Deposits, bonds, shares, mutual funds, insurance cash values, PF/PPF |
| **Physical / real assets** | Tangible property | House, land, gold, jewellery, art |
| **Personal-use assets** | Held for use, not returns | Car, appliances, the home you live in |

> ⚠️ **Trap:** *personal-use assets* (car, furniture) generally **depreciate** and produce no income. Counting them heavily inflates net worth but not the ability to fund goals — which is exactly why the **financial assets ratio** exists (Chapter 3).

### Classifying liabilities

- **Short-term:** credit card outstanding, overdrafts, personal loans, dues within a year.
- **Long-term:** home loan, education loan, vehicle loan.

Record liabilities at the **outstanding principal** on the statement date — not the original loan amount and not the total of remaining EMIs (that would include future interest).

### Reading net worth

- **Positive and growing** = the plan is working.
- **Negative** = liabilities exceed assets — technically insolvent; debt management becomes the first priority.
- Net worth grows in exactly two ways: (a) **saving** out of income, and (b) **appreciation** of assets held.

## 1.5 The Financial Planning Process

The six steps — a favourite for sequence questions. Learn the **order**.

| Step | Name | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Establish the **client–planner relationship** | Define scope, services, duration, compensation, and both parties' responsibilities — **in writing** |
| **2** | Gather data on the **client's financial situation** | Quantitative (income, assets, debts, insurance, taxes) *and* qualitative (attitudes, experience, family) |
| **3** | Define **client needs and financial goals** | Convert wishes into specific, dated, costed goals; prioritise them |
| **4** | **Risk profiling** and asset allocation | Assess capacity + willingness to bear risk; derive the allocation |
| **5** | **Portfolio construction** | Recommend and implement a specific plan/product set |
| **6** | **Reviewing and rebalancing** | Monitor, report, and correct drift as life and markets change |

### 1.5.1 Client–Planner Relationship
Sets the terms of engagement. The adviser must disclose **what services are provided, how they are paid, and any conflicts of interest**. Getting this in writing protects both sides — and for a registered IA it is a regulatory requirement.

### 1.5.2 Client's Financial Situation
Incomplete data produces a wrong plan. Collect income, expenses, existing investments, loans, insurance covers, tax status, employer benefits — plus family structure and dependants.

### 1.5.3 Client Needs and Financial Goals
Translate "I want to be comfortable" into numbers. Apply the goal rules from 1.3.1: prioritise, focus, stagger.

### 1.5.4 Risk Profiling
Two distinct things, often confused:

- **Risk capacity (ability)** — *objective*. Driven by horizon, income stability, net worth, dependants and how essential the goal is. A 28-year-old with a stable job and no dependants has high capacity.
- **Risk tolerance/attitude (willingness)** — *subjective*. Psychological comfort with volatility.

> 🧠 **Rule:** where they conflict, plan to the **lower** of the two. High capacity but low tolerance means a client who panic-sells at the bottom, converting a paper loss into a permanent one. Also note a **risk requirement**: the return the goal actually needs.

### 1.5.5 Portfolio Construction
Asset allocation first, product selection second. Products must fit the goal's **horizon** (don't fund a 1-year goal with equity) and the client's **liquidity** needs.

### 1.5.6 Reviewing and Rebalancing
Two separate triggers:

- **Market drift** — a 60:40 equity:debt mix becomes 70:30 after a rally. Rebalancing sells the winner and buys the laggard, restoring the risk level (and enforcing "sell high, buy low").
- **Life events** — marriage, a child, job change, inheritance, illness. These change the *goals*, not just the weights.

## 1.6 Financial Advisory and Execution

Two distinct activities:

- **Advice** — recommending what the client should do. Value comes from the recommendation itself.
- **Execution / distribution** — actually placing the transaction. Value comes from operational convenience.

The **conflict**: if the adviser earns commission on what they sell, the recommendation may drift toward whatever pays best. SEBI's answer is the **segregation of advice and distribution** — a registered Investment Adviser must keep advisory separate from any distribution activity, and an individual IA cannot do both. *(Chapter 18 covers the exact provisions.)*

### The IA's value proposition

| Adviser adds value by | Because |
|---|---|
| Setting the asset allocation | It drives most of the outcome |
| Preventing behavioural mistakes | Panic-selling and return-chasing destroy more wealth than bad products |
| Choosing **direct plans** | Lower expense ratio compounds over decades |
| Tax-aware structuring | Post-tax return is what the client actually spends |
| Regular review | Keeps the plan aligned with a changing life |
