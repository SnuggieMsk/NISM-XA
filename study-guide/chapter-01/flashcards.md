# Chapter 1: Introduction to Personal Financial Planning — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check.

**Q1.** Define financial planning in one sentence.
> **A:** The process of **meeting your life goals through the proper management of your finances** — connecting money you will have to money you will need.

**Q2.** What are the three things that make a goal a *proper* financial goal?
> **A:** It is **specific**, **quantified in future (inflation-adjusted) rupees**, and **dated**. "Save for college" is not a goal; "₹42 lakh in March 2038" is.

**Q3.** What is the formula for net worth?
> **A:** **Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities.**

**Q4.** Name the six steps of the financial planning process, in order.
> **A:** 1) Establish the **client–planner relationship** → 2) Gather the **client's financial situation** → 3) Define **needs and goals** → 4) **Risk profiling** → 5) **Portfolio construction** → 6) **Review and rebalance**.

**Q5.** List the eight components in the scope of financial planning.
> **A:** Personal financial **analysis**, **cash flow**/budgeting, **insurance**, **debt** management, **investment** planning & asset allocation, **tax**, **retirement**, **estate** planning.

**Q6.** What is the difference between risk *capacity* and risk *tolerance*?
> **A:** **Capacity = ability**, and it is **objective** (horizon, income stability, net worth, dependants). **Tolerance = willingness**, and it is **subjective** (psychological comfort with volatility).

**Q7.** If a client has high risk capacity but low risk tolerance, what do you plan to?
> **A:** The **lower** of the two. A client who panics and sells at the bottom converts a paper loss into a permanent one, regardless of how much risk they could theoretically afford.

**Q8.** What does "fiduciary" mean?
> **A:** The adviser must place the **client's interest ahead of their own** — the defining obligation of a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser.

**Q9.** Why can't you simply borrow your way through retirement?
> **A:** You can borrow for a house or education, but **nobody lends for retirement**. That is why retirement typically outranks discretionary goals in prioritisation.

**Q10.** What does "staggering goals" mean and why do it?
> **A:** Deliberately spacing goals out in time so several large funding requirements **don't land in the same year** and break the plan. Discretionary goals get moved, not essential ones.

**Q11.** Name the four classes of assets on a personal balance sheet.
> **A:** **Liquid** assets, **financial** assets, **physical/real** assets, and **personal-use** assets.

**Q12.** Why are personal-use assets treated carefully in a balance sheet?
> **A:** They generally **depreciate and produce no income**, so counting them inflates net worth without improving the ability to fund goals.

**Q13.** At what value do you record a home loan on the personal balance sheet?
> **A:** The **outstanding principal** on the statement date — *not* the original loan amount, and *not* the sum of remaining EMIs (which would include future interest).

**Q14.** What are the only two ways net worth grows?
> **A:** **Saving** out of income, and **appreciation** of assets already held.

**Q15.** What does a negative net worth mean?
> **A:** Liabilities exceed assets — the person is **technically insolvent**, and debt management becomes the first priority.

**Q16.** Give three differences between financial planning and typical product-led advisory.
> **A:** Planning is triggered by a **client goal** (not a saleable product); it is **integrated** across all needs (not one need at a time); and it usually carries a **fiduciary** duty with fee-based compensation.

**Q17.** What are the two triggers for reviewing and rebalancing a portfolio?
> **A:** **Market drift** (a rally turns 60:40 into 70:30) and **life events** (marriage, child, job change, inheritance, illness).

**Q18.** How does rebalancing enforce good behaviour?
> **A:** It mechanically **sells the asset that has risen and buys the one that lagged** — "sell high, buy low" — while restoring the intended risk level.

**Q19.** What is the difference between advice and execution?
> **A:** **Advice** is recommending what the client should do; **execution/distribution** is placing the transaction. SEBI requires an IA to **segregate** the two.

**Q20.** Why does inflation make financial planning necessary?
> **A:** It destroys purchasing power — at 6% inflation ₹1 lakh of expenses becomes roughly **₹3.2 lakh in 20 years**, so "safe" low-interest money loses real value.

**Q21.** Why is the mismatch between earning years and living years a planning problem?
> **A:** People typically earn for ~30–35 years but spend for ~60+ years, so the earning years must **fund the non-earning years**.

**Q22.** What is a contingency (emergency) fund and how large is it usually?
> **A:** A pool of liquid money for income shocks and emergencies, commonly **3–6 months of expenses**.

**Q23.** Which drives portfolio outcomes more — asset allocation or security selection?
> **A:** **Asset allocation.** It explains most of the variation in portfolio returns, which is why it comes before product selection.

**Q24.** Why should long-dated goals get deliberate focus?
> **A:** They *feel* far away and get neglected, yet they benefit most from **compounding** — starting early is worth more than investing more later.

**Q25.** What must be disclosed when establishing the client–planner relationship?
> **A:** The **scope of services, duration, compensation, responsibilities of both parties, and any conflicts of interest** — in writing.

**Q26.** What two kinds of data are gathered in step 2?
> **A:** **Quantitative** (income, expenses, assets, liabilities, insurance, tax status) and **qualitative** (attitudes, risk experience, family structure, dependants).

**Q27.** What is "risk requirement"?
> **A:** The **return the goal actually needs** — distinct from risk capacity (ability) and risk tolerance (willingness).

**Q28.** Why is tax planning never the *primary* reason to buy a product?
> **A:** Tax efficiency is a **modifier** of return, not a substitute for suitability. A tax-efficient product that doesn't fit the goal's horizon or liquidity need is still the wrong product.

**Q29.** What is the core conflict of interest in commission-based distribution?
> **A:** The recommendation can drift toward **whatever pays the adviser most**, rather than what suits the client best.

**Q30.** Name five ways an Investment Adviser adds measurable value.
> **A:** Setting **asset allocation**; preventing **behavioural mistakes**; using **direct plans** (lower expense ratio); **tax-aware** structuring; and **regular review**.

**Q31.** Can a financial plan succeed if the product beats its benchmark?
> **A:** Not necessarily. A product can outperform and still fail the client — for example if it is **illiquid exactly when the money is needed**. Goal achievement, not product return, is the measure of success.

**Q32.** Insurance planning is about what — returns or protection?
> **A:** **Risk transfer / protection.** Insurance shields the plan from catastrophic events; it is not bought for returns.

**Q33.** What is the role of the planner as a "circuit breaker"?
> **A:** Investors panic-sell in crashes and chase winners in booms. The planner sits **between emotion and the confirm button**, supplying objectivity and discipline.

**Q34.** Why is ₹50 lakh neither "enough" nor "not enough" on its own?
> **A:** Because money has meaning only **relative to a goal**. Enough for a flat in a small town, nowhere near enough for 25 years of retirement.

**Q35.** What four things should debt management get right?
> **A:** The **purpose**, the **cost**, the **maturity** of the debt — and rehabilitation (counselling/rescheduling) when a borrower is over-leveraged.
