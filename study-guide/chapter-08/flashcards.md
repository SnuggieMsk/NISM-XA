# Chapter 8: Investing in Stocks — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **The valuation ratios are the highest-yield items — know each formula AND when it breaks.**

**Q1.** What are the two components of equity total return?
> **A:** **Capital appreciation + dividend yield.**

**Q2.** Why does equity beat inflation over the long run, in principle?
> **A:** Company earnings tend to grow with the economy and with inflation, so equity is a claim on a **growing, inflation-linked stream of profits** — unlike a bond, whose coupon is fixed in nominal terms.

**Q3.** Distinguish cross-sectional from time-series diversification.
> **A:** **Cross-sectional** = across many securities, sectors and asset classes **at one point in time**. **Time-series** = spreading **entry across time**, i.e. a **SIP / rupee-cost averaging**.

**Q4.** What is diversification's fundamental limit?
> **A:** It eliminates **unsystematic (company/sector-specific) risk** but **cannot eliminate systematic (market) risk**. When the whole market falls, diversification does not save you.

**Q5.** Roughly how many stocks are needed for effective diversification, and what is the catch?
> **A:** Around **15–25** well-chosen stocks capture most of the benefit. The catch: they must have **low correlation** — 20 banking stocks are *not* diversified.

**Q6.** Which type of risk is compensated by the market, and why?
> **A:** **Systematic (market) risk** — you are paid a risk premium because it **cannot be avoided**. **Unsystematic risk is NOT compensated**, because you could have diversified it away for free.

**Q7.** Name the five risks of equity investing.
> **A:** **Market** risk, **sector-specific** risk, **company-specific** risk, **transactional** risk and **liquidity** risk.

**Q8.** State SEBI's market-cap definitions for large, mid and small cap.
> **A:** By market-capitalisation rank: **1–100 large cap**, **101–250 mid cap**, **251 onwards small cap**.

**Q9.** What does fundamental analysis try to establish?
> **A:** A security's **intrinsic value** from the underlying business, to compare against the market price. If intrinsic value > market price, the stock is potentially **undervalued**.

**Q10.** Distinguish top-down from bottom-up analysis.
> **A:** **Top-down** starts with the **economy** → industry → company. **Bottom-up** starts with the **company** itself, largely regardless of the macro picture.

**Q11.** Distinguish sell-side from buy-side research.
> **A:** **Sell-side** works for brokers/investment banks and **publishes** research to clients (paid via brokerage). **Buy-side** works for funds/insurers/PMS and keeps research **internal and proprietary**.

**Q12.** State the E → I → C process.
> **A:** **Economy** analysis → **Industry/sector** analysis → **Company** analysis.

**Q13.** What are the two halves of company analysis?
> **A:** **Quantitative** (growth, margins, return on equity, debt, cash flow) and **Qualitative** (management quality, business model, moat, corporate governance).

**Q14.** State the DCF formula and its terminal-value component.
> **A:** **Value = Σ [CFₜ ÷ (1+r)ᵗ] + Terminal Value ÷ (1+r)ⁿ**, where **TV = CF₍ₙ₊₁₎ ÷ (r − g)**, valid only when **g < r**.

**Q15.** What is DCF's greatest weakness?
> **A:** **Extreme sensitivity to the growth rate (g) and discount rate (r)** — small changes swing the answer wildly, and terminal value often dominates. "Garbage in, gospel out."

**Q16.** When is asset-based valuation appropriate, and when does it fail?
> **A:** Appropriate for **asset-heavy or liquidating** businesses. It fails for **service and brand-driven** companies whose value is intangible and off the balance sheet.

**Q17.** State the P/E ratio and its main limitation.
> **A:** **Price ÷ Earnings per share.** It becomes **meaningless when earnings are negative**, and is distorted by one-off items.

**Q18.** For which sector is P/B most useful, and why?
> **A:** **Banks and financials** — their assets are largely financial and marked close to realisable value, so book value is meaningful. It is poor for asset-light firms.

**Q19.** When would you use P/S rather than P/E?
> **A:** For **loss-making or early-stage** companies, where earnings are negative or meaningless. Its weakness: it **ignores profitability entirely**.

**Q20.** What is the PEG ratio, and what value suggests good value?
> **A:** **P/E ÷ earnings growth rate (%)**. A **PEG below 1** often suggests good value — but it depends entirely on a reliable growth estimate.

**Q21.** State the Enterprise Value formula.
> **A:** **EV = Market Capitalisation + Debt − Cash** — what it would cost to buy the whole business including its debts.

**Q22.** Why is EV/EBITDA preferred for comparing companies with different debt levels?
> **A:** Because it is **capital-structure neutral** — EV includes debt and EBITDA is measured before interest, so differently-leveraged firms compare fairly. P/E is distorted by leverage.

**Q23.** What is earnings yield, and what is it useful for?
> **A:** **EPS ÷ Price** — the **inverse of P/E**. It expresses the return as a percentage, making it directly comparable with **bond yields**.

**Q24.** Why can a high dividend yield be a warning sign?
> **A:** Yield = dividend ÷ **price**. A **falling price** mechanically raises the yield, so a high figure may signal distress rather than generosity.

**Q25.** State the EVA formula and what a positive value means.
> **A:** **EVA = NOPAT − (Capital × Cost of Capital).** Positive EVA means the firm earned **more than its cost of capital** — genuine value creation, not merely accounting profit.

**Q26.** What is MVA?
> **A:** **Market Value Added = Market value of the firm − Capital invested** — the market's verdict on cumulative value creation.

**Q27.** Name the industry-specific metrics for banks, telecom and retail.
> **A:** **Banks** — NIM, Gross/Net NPA, CASA ratio, capital adequacy. **Telecom** — ARPU, subscriber count. **Retail** — same-store sales growth, sales per square foot.

**Q28.** State the three assumptions of technical analysis.
> **A:** 1) **The market discounts everything** — all information is in the price. 2) **Prices move in trends.** 3) **History repeats itself**, because human psychology is consistent.

**Q29.** Give the clean one-line split between fundamental and technical analysis.
> **A:** **Fundamental analysis tells you WHAT to buy; technical analysis tells you WHEN.**

**Q30.** Define support and resistance, and state what happens when support breaks.
> **A:** **Support** is a level where buying has historically halted a fall; **resistance** is where selling has capped a rise. **Once support is broken it often becomes resistance** (and vice versa).

**Q31.** What are the conventional RSI thresholds?
> **A:** RSI runs 0–100; conventionally **above 70 is overbought** and **below 30 is oversold**.

**Q32.** What does volume add to a technical signal?
> **A:** **Confirmation.** A price breakout on **high volume** is far more credible than the same move on thin volume.

**Q33.** Name four technical indicators besides support/resistance.
> **A:** **Moving averages** (and crossovers), **RSI**, **MACD**, **Bollinger Bands** — plus chart patterns (head-and-shoulders, double top/bottom, triangles) and candlestick patterns.

**Q34.** Can technical analysis be applied to fixed income?
> **A:** **Yes** — to bond yields, prices and interest-rate futures — though fundamental drivers (monetary policy, inflation, credit) dominate more than in equity.

**Q35.** Name six corporate governance red flags.
> **A:** **Auditor resignation or frequent auditor changes**, high **promoter share pledging**, large **related-party transactions**, repeated **restatements**, opaque group structures, and a persistent gap between **reported profit and operating cash flow**.

**Q36.** Why is high promoter pledging a serious concern?
> **A:** If the share price falls, lenders can **invoke the pledge and dump the shares**, creating forced selling that drives the price down further — a self-reinforcing spiral.

**Q37.** What is the fundamental limitation of relative valuation?
> **A:** It only tells you whether a stock is cheap **relative to something else**. If the entire sector or market is overvalued, relative valuation will **not reveal it**.

**Q38.** Why should DCF and relative valuation be used together?
> **A:** DCF anchors value in the **business's own economics**; relative multiples **sanity-check** that against what the market pays for comparable firms. Sharp divergence means the assumptions need re-examination.

**Q39.** What is the industry life cycle, and why does it matter?
> **A:** **Pioneering → expansion → maturity → decline.** The stage determines expected growth, margins, competitive intensity and the appropriate valuation approach.

**Q40.** What single question resolves most valuation-ratio problems?
> **A:** **"What is in the denominator, and can it be negative or distorted?"** That explains why P/E fails for loss-makers, why P/B suits banks, and why P/S is used for early-stage firms.
