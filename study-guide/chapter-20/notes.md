# Chapter 20: Grievance Redress Mechanism — Short Notes

> **Module 6 · Operations, Regulatory Environment, Compliance & Ethics (30 marks).**
> The final chapter, and one of the easiest to score in — provided you memorise **who you go to, in what order, and within how many days**. Almost every question here is really the same question: *given this complaint, what is the NEXT step?* Learn the ladders and the numbers and the chapter is done.

---

## 20.0 ⭐ The One Idea That Runs Through the Whole Chapter

Every redress system in Indian finance — securities, banking, insurance, pension — is built on the **same three-step ladder**:

> ### 🧠 **INTERMEDIARY first → REGULATOR next → TRIBUNAL / COURT last.**

**Why this order exists.** The firm that caused the problem is the only party that holds the file, the recording, the audit trail and the money. It can fix a genuine error in a day. A regulator supervising 5,000 intermediaries cannot. So the law forces the complaint down to the level where it can actually be solved, and only escalates it if that level fails. A complaint filed straight with the regulator or the tribunal, skipping the firm, is **not maintainable** — it will be bounced back, and the clock will have been wasted.

| Sector | Step 1 — the entity | Step 2 — the regulator's portal | Step 3 — the adjudicator |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Securities** | The intermediary / listed company | **SCORES** (SEBI) | **ODR portal** → arbitration; **SAT** for appeals against SEBI orders |
| **Banking / NBFC** | Branch → Nodal Officer → **Internal Ombudsman** | RBI **CMS** portal | **RBI Ombudsman (RB-IOS 2021)** → Appellate Authority (ED, RBI) |
| **Insurance** | Insurer's **Grievance Redressal Officer** | **Bima Bharosa** (IRDAI) | **Insurance Ombudsman** |
| **Pension (NPS)** | POP / Nodal office / CRA via **CGMS** | **NPS Trust** | **PFRDA Ombudsman** → PFRDA → **SAT** |
| **Any consumer** | The seller / service provider | — | **District → State → National Commission** (CPA 2019) |

> ⚠️ **The single most common exam trap:** an option that sends the investor **straight to SEBI, straight to the Ombudsman, or straight to SAT** without first approaching the firm. It is always wrong.

## 20.1 The Consumer Protection Act, 2019

The **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 and came into force from **20 July 2020**. It is the general, all-sector safety net — a mutual fund investor, a bank depositor, a policyholder and a person who bought a faulty geyser all use the same machinery.

### Who is a "consumer"?

A **consumer** is a person who **buys goods or avails services for a consideration** which has been **paid, or promised, or partly paid and partly promised**, or under any **system of deferred payment**.

Three points the exam pushes on:

1. **Consideration is essential.** Something must have been paid or promised. A **free service** (or a purely gratuitous favour) generally does **not** create a consumer relationship. A fee-paying advisory client clearly does.
2. **The user counts too.** A person who **uses the goods or is a beneficiary of the service with the approval of the buyer** is also a consumer. So a wife availing a service her husband paid for is a consumer.
3. **"Commercial purpose" is excluded — with one big carve-out.** A person who buys goods **for resale or for any commercial purpose** is *not* a consumer. **But** the exclusion does **not** apply where the goods/services are used **exclusively for earning a livelihood by means of self-employment.**

> 🧠 **The livelihood carve-out.** A man who buys one auto-rickshaw and drives it himself to feed his family **is** a consumer. A firm that buys forty auto-rickshaws to run a fleet **is not**. Same vehicle, different purpose.

The 2019 Act expressly extends "buys" and "avails" to **offline and online transactions**, including **electronic means, teleshopping, direct selling and multi-level marketing** — the reason it matters today, when almost every financial product is bought on an app.

### The three-tier redressal machinery

| Commission | Full name | Presided over by | Appeal lies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **District** | District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (DCDRC) | A person qualified to be a **District Judge** | **State Commission** |
| **State** | State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (SCDRC) | A sitting or retired **High Court judge** | **National Commission** |
| **National** | National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) | A sitting or retired **Supreme Court judge** | **Supreme Court** |

### ⭐ Pecuniary limits — read this box twice

The Act as **originally enacted in 2019** set the limits by the *value of the goods or services paid as consideration*:

| Commission | **Original 2019 limit** |
|---|---|
| District | up to **₹1 crore** |
| State | above ₹1 crore up to **₹10 crore** |
| National | above **₹10 crore** |

These were **revised downward** by the Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) **Rules, 2021** (notified 30 December 2021), which are the limits in force today:

| Commission | **Current limit (2021 Rules)** |
|---|---|
| **District** | up to **₹50 lakh** |
| **State** | above ₹50 lakh up to **₹2 crore** |
| **National** | above **₹2 crore** |

> ⚠️ **Exam trap.** Both sets of figures appear in circulating study material. Learn **both**, and read the question stem: if it says *"as revised"* or *"currently"*, use **₹50 lakh / ₹2 crore**; if it says *"as originally enacted in 2019"*, use **₹1 crore / ₹10 crore**. The *structure* (three tiers, lowest tier first) never changes.
>
> ⚠️ Note also **what** is measured: the jurisdiction is fixed by the **value of the consideration paid**, not by the compensation demanded.

### Other provisions you must know

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Limitation to file** | **2 years** from the date the cause of action arose (condonable for sufficient cause) |
| **Where to file** | Where the opposite party resides/carries on business, **or where the cause of action arose, or — new in 2019 — where the COMPLAINANT resides or personally works for gain** |
| **Filing fee** | **No fee** for claims up to **₹5 lakh**; slab-based fees above that |
| **E-filing** | Through the **e-Daakhil** portal |
| **Appeal: District → State** | Within **45 days**, and the appellant must first deposit **50%** of any amount ordered against him |
| **Appeal: State → National** | Within **30 days** |
| **Appeal: National → Supreme Court** | Within **30 days** (against orders in the National Commission's *original* jurisdiction) |
| **Mediation** | Consumer Mediation Cells are attached to each Commission; a case may be referred for settlement |
| **Non-compliance with an order** | Imprisonment of **1 month to 3 years**, and/or fine of **₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh** |
| **Who may complain** | The consumer; one or more consumers with the same interest; a registered consumer association; the Central/State Government; the **CCPA**; a legal heir |

### Two innovations of the 2019 Act

- **CCPA — the Central Consumer Protection Authority.** A *regulator* for consumer rights (the 1986 Act had none). It acts against **unfair trade practices and false or misleading advertisements**, has an **Investigation Wing headed by a Director General**, and can order recalls, refunds and the discontinuation of an advertisement. Penalties for a misleading advertisement run to **₹10 lakh** for a first offence and **₹50 lakh** for repeat offences, and an **endorser** (a celebrity) can be barred from endorsing for **1 year**, and **3 years** for repeat offences.
- **Product liability.** A manufacturer, service provider or seller can be made liable for harm caused by a defective product or deficient service — a claim that did not previously exist in the consumer forum.

### 🧠 The six consumer rights

**Safety · Information · Choice · Be heard · Redressal · Consumer education.**

> 🧠 **Mnemonic — "SIC-HRE"** (*"sick here"*): **S**afety, **I**nformation, **C**hoice, **H**eard, **R**edressal, **E**ducation.

## 20.2 Investor Grievance Redressal — the General Picture

A **grievance** is any expression of dissatisfaction by a client about a service, product or conduct that the client believes fell short of what was promised or required. Note what it is **not**:

| | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Query** | A request for information ("what is my folio balance?") | Answer it; not a complaint |
| **Request** | A service instruction ("change my address") | Execute it; not a complaint |
| **Grievance / complaint** | An allegation of **deficiency, delay, error, loss or misconduct** | **Log it, track it, resolve it within the regulatory clock** |
| **Dispute** | A contested claim on **facts or money** that the parties cannot settle themselves | Goes to **conciliation / arbitration / court** — a regulator cannot decide it |

> ⚠️ **The most under-appreciated point in this chapter:** **SEBI, RBI, IRDAI and PFRDA do not adjudicate individual claims.** Their portals (SCORES, CMS, Bima Bharosa, CGMS) **route, monitor, follow up and enforce timelines**. They do not decide who is right about disputed facts or award damages on the merits. That job belongs to the **Ombudsman, an arbitrator under ODR, a consumer commission or a civil court.** Any option saying "SEBI will order the broker to compensate the investor after examining the evidence" is describing an adjudicator, not a facilitator.

### Why a formal system, rather than "just sort it out"?

- **Regulatory obligation** — SEBI, RBI, IRDAI and PFRDA all *require* a documented mechanism, and inspection/audit will ask to see it.
- **Early warning** — a cluster of complaints of the *same nature* is the cheapest fraud- and mis-selling detector a firm owns.
- **Evidence** — if the matter reaches an Ombudsman, arbitrator or SAT, the contemporaneous log is the firm's defence.
- **Trust** — advisory is a relationship business; how a firm behaves when something goes *wrong* is what clients actually remember.

## 20.3 ⭐ Key Elements of a Robust Grievance Recording and Redress System

The syllabus lists seven elements. Learn them as a **life cycle of one complaint**, and each one becomes obvious.

| # | Element | What is recorded | **Why it matters** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Source of receipt** | Email, letter, phone, walk-in, website form, social media, **SCORES**, **ODR portal**, regulator-forwarded, exchange/AMFI | Tells you which **regulatory clock** applies and whether a regulator is already watching. A SCORES complaint carries a hard 21-day deadline; a walk-in does not. |
| 2 | **Date and time of receipt** | Timestamp, and a **unique complaint reference number** issued to the client | **This starts the clock.** Every timeline in this chapter is measured from receipt. Without a timestamp, ageing and compliance cannot be proved. |
| 3 | **Nature of complaint** | A standard **category** — mis-selling, unsuitable advice, fee/billing dispute, unauthorised transaction, delay in service, non-receipt of documents, staff conduct, technology failure | Enables **root-cause analysis**. Twenty complaints of one type is a **process defect**, not twenty accidents. |
| 4 | **Internal or escalated** | Can this be closed by the firm, or must it go to an external entity (regulator, exchange, Ombudsman, ODR)? | Decides the route and the deadline; also flags matters that must be **reported** to the regulator. |
| 5 | **Status / action taken** | Open → Under investigation → Awaiting client input → Resolved → Closed → Reopened, with the **reasoning and evidence** on file | Nothing may sit in "open" without an owner. The written reason is what an inspector reads. |
| 6 | **Time taken / ageing report** | Days from receipt to closure, bucketed | An **ageing report** exposes complaints quietly going stale. Averages hide the outliers; buckets do not. |
| 7 | **Escalation mechanism** | A published **escalation matrix**: named officer, designation, email, phone and turnaround at **each** level | Gives the client a route upward inside the firm *before* going to a regulator, and gives staff a defined trigger to escalate. |

### The ageing report — worked example

A small advisory firm has **40 open complaints** on 31 March. Its ageing report:

| Ageing bucket | Number | % of open | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–7 days** | 22 | 55% | Normal — within service standard |
| **8–15 days** | 10 | 25% | Watch |
| **16–21 days** | 5 | 12.5% | **Amber — the 21-day SCORES clock is about to expire** |
| **Over 21 days** | 3 | 7.5% | **Red — already in breach; escalate to the Principal Officer today** |

**Reading it:** the *average* age might be a comfortable 9 days, and that average is useless. The **3 complaints beyond 21 days** are the entire regulatory risk, and an ageing report is the only report that surfaces them. This is exactly why the syllabus lists ageing separately from "time taken".

### A model four-level escalation matrix for an IA

| Level | Who | Turnaround |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Client servicing / relationship desk | **Acknowledge within 1–2 working days**; resolve within 7 days |
| **2** | **Grievance Redressal Officer** (named on the website) | Within 15 days |
| **3** | **Principal Officer / Compliance Officer** | Within **21 days of original receipt** |
| **4** | **External** — SEBI SCORES, then the **ODR portal** | Per the regulator's timelines |

> 🧠 **Memory hook — "S-D-N-I-S-T-E".** **S**ource, **D**ate/time, **N**ature, **I**nternal-or-escalated, **S**tatus, **T**ime taken (ageing), **E**scalation. Read as *"Source Date Nature — Internal? Status, Time, Escalate."*

## 20.4 The Grievance System an Investment Adviser Must Maintain

**Regulation 21 of the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013 — "Redressal of client grievances"** — requires an Investment Adviser to:

- **redress client grievances promptly**;
- have **adequate procedure for expeditious grievance redressal**; and
- handle and resolve grievances relating to securities **in the manner and within the time specified by SEBI**.

Practically, an IA's obligations are:

| Obligation | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Documented policy** | A written grievance policy and register, applied consistently |
| **Named officer** | A **Grievance Redressal Officer**; a **Compliance Officer** is mandatory for a **non-individual** IA |
| **Escalation matrix on the website** | Names, designations, email IDs, phone numbers and turnaround at each level |
| **SCORES registration** | Every registered IA must be **registered on SCORES** and respond within the stipulated time |
| **ODR enrolment** | Every IA must be enrolled on the **SMART ODR portal** and display the link |
| **Investor Charter** | SEBI's **Investor Charter for Investment Advisers** must be displayed — services offered, investor rights, DOs and DON'Ts, the grievance mechanism and expected timelines |
| **Complaints data disclosure** | Complaints data (received / resolved / pending, with ageing) must be **disclosed on the IA's website and updated monthly** |
| **Disclosure at onboarding** | The grievance mechanism must be explained in the **terms of engagement / Most Important Terms and Conditions**, so the client knows the route before there is a problem |
| **Record retention** | Grievance records — like other client records — must be maintained for **5 years**; where a matter is **under dispute or litigation, records must be kept until final disposal** |
| **Reporting** | Periodic reporting to SEBI and to **BASL** (BSE Administration and Supervision Ltd, the body administering IAs), including complaints data |
| **Annual compliance audit** | The mandatory annual compliance audit covers grievance handling; **adverse observations must be addressed and reported** |

> ⚠️ **A grievance is not "resolved" because the adviser thinks the client is wrong.** Resolution means the complaint has been **investigated, a reasoned reply given to the client, and the outcome recorded**. A complaint closed without a written reply is, on inspection, an unresolved complaint.

> 🧠 **The IA's own conflict.** Many IA complaints are about the *advice* — unsuitable products, undisclosed conflicts, fee disputes. The person who gave the advice must **not** be the person who decides the complaint about it. Independence of the grievance function is the whole point of naming a separate officer.

## 20.5 Redressal in the Capital Market

### 20.5.1 ⭐ SEBI SCORES

**SCORES** = **SEBI Complaints Redress System**, a **web-based centralised grievance system launched in June 2011** (scores.sebi.gov.in, with a mobile app). **SCORES 2.0**, an upgraded version with **auto-routing, auto-escalation and monitoring by Designated Bodies**, went live on **1 April 2024**.

**What SCORES does:** it **receives, routes, tracks and escalates** complaints against SEBI-registered intermediaries and listed companies, and enforces the response clock. Every complaint gets a **unique registration number** the investor can track online.

**Before you can use it:** the investor must **first have approached the entity directly** and either received **no reply**, an **unsatisfactory reply**, or seen **no action**. Registration on SCORES requires a **valid email ID and mobile number** — **anonymous complaints are not accepted**.

**Limitation:** a complaint should be lodged on SCORES within **one year** from the date of the cause of action (broadly, from when the entity failed to respond satisfactorily).

#### The SCORES 2.0 clock — the numbers to memorise

| Stage | Who acts | **Time** |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Complaint lodged** | Auto-routed to the entity and to the **Designated Body** | Same day |
| **2. Entity files its Action Taken Report (ATR)** | The intermediary / listed company | **21 calendar days** |
| **3. Investor may seek a FIRST review** | The investor, if dissatisfied with the ATR | Within **15 days** of the ATR |
| **4. First review** | The **Designated Body** (e.g. a stock exchange, depository, **AMFI**, **BASL**) | **21 days** |
| **5. Investor may seek a SECOND review** | The investor, if still dissatisfied | Within **15 days** |
| **6. Second review** | **SEBI** | **21 days** |
| **7. Still unresolved** | The investor moves to the **ODR portal** | — |

> 🧠 **The SCORES rhythm: 21 – 15 – 21 – 15 – 21.** *Entity 21, ask 15, Designated Body 21, ask 15, SEBI 21.* If the entity misses its 21 days, the complaint **auto-escalates** to the Designated Body without the investor having to do anything.

#### What SCORES will **not** take

- Complaints against **unlisted / delisted / private companies**
- Matters that are **sub judice** (already before a court, tribunal or arbitrator)
- Matters within the purview of **another regulator** — RBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, CCI, MCA
- Disputes about the **merits of a trade**, price movements or investment losses as such
- **Anonymous**, incomplete, unclear, frivolous or vexatious complaints
- Complaints on which the investor has **not first approached the entity**

### 20.5.2 ⭐ ODR — the Online Dispute Resolution Portal

Introduced by SEBI's circular of **31 July 2023**, "Online Resolution of Disputes in the Indian Securities Market". A **common ODR portal — SMART ODR (Securities Market Approach for Resolution through ODR), smartodr.in** — links investors, market participants and **ODR Institutions empanelled by the Market Infrastructure Institutions (stock exchanges and depositories)**.

**Why it exists.** SCORES can chase an intermediary, but it cannot decide a **disputed money claim**. Before ODR, that meant an exchange arbitration counter in a metro city, or a civil suit. ODR moves conciliation and arbitration **online, time-bound and low-cost**, so a ₹40,000 claim is actually worth pursuing.

#### The sequence

1. Take the dispute **directly to the market participant**.
2. If not satisfied, escalate through **SCORES** (or the exchange/depository mechanism).
3. **After exhausting those**, initiate dispute resolution on the **ODR portal**.

#### The two stages

| Stage | Nature | Who runs it | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Online conciliation** | **Non-binding** — a facilitated settlement | A **conciliator** appointed by the ODR Institution | A settlement agreement, if both agree. Target: **21 calendar days**, extendable by about **10 days** |
| **Online arbitration** | **Binding** — only if conciliation fails or is not opted for | An **arbitrator** appointed by the ODR Institution | An **arbitral award**, time-bound (broadly **30 days**, extendable), enforceable under the **Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996** |

**Other features:** it is available to **both sides** — a market participant may also initiate ODR against a client after a written notice. Fees are **slab-based on the claim value** and deliberately small for small claims, with the MIIs bearing part of the cost. The ordinary **limitation period under the Limitation Act, 1963 (3 years)** applies. Market participants — including **Investment Advisers** — must **register on the portal** and display the link.

> 🧠 **SCORES vs ODR in one line:** **SCORES chases; ODR decides.** SCORES gets you a *reply*. ODR gets you an *award*.

## 20.6 Grievance Redressal in Banking

### Step 1 — inside the bank

Branch manager → the bank's **customer service / grievance cell** → the bank's **Nodal Officer** and **Principal Nodal Officer**. Every regulated entity must display the name and contact of its **Principal Nodal Officer**.

**The Internal Ombudsman.** Banks with **10 or more banking outlets** must appoint an **Internal Ombudsman** — a senior, independent official who is **not an employee of the bank**. The key rule: a complaint the bank proposes to **reject wholly or partly must be referred to the Internal Ombudsman before the rejection is communicated to the customer**. This forces a second, independent look inside the bank, and is why the RBI Ombudsman sees far fewer cases than it otherwise would. (RBI's 2023 Master Direction extends the Internal Ombudsman framework to large NBFCs, Credit Information Companies and non-bank payment system operators.)

### Step 2 — the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021

Launched on **12 November 2021**, the **RB-IOS 2021** **integrated three earlier schemes into one**:

- the Banking Ombudsman Scheme, **2006**
- the Ombudsman Scheme for NBFCs, **2018**
- the Ombudsman Scheme for Digital Transactions, **2019**

> 🧠 **The slogan: "One Nation, One Ombudsman."** The scheme is **jurisdiction-neutral** — the customer no longer has to work out which regional Ombudsman covers the branch. Complaints go to a single **Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (CRPC) at Chandigarh**, or online at the RBI **CMS portal (cms.rbi.org.in)**, or by email or post. **There is no fee.**

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Covered entities** | All **commercial banks**, RRBs, **scheduled primary (urban) co-operative banks with deposits of ₹50 crore and above**, **NBFCs** (deposit-taking, or with customer interface and asset size **₹100 crore and above**), **payment system participants** (including prepaid instrument issuers), and **Credit Information Companies** |
| **Ground of complaint** | **Any "deficiency in service"** — the scheme replaced the old *closed list* of grounds with an open, deficiency-based test, subject to an exclusion list |
| **Precondition** | The complaint must **first** have been made to the bank/NBFC |
| **When you may escalate** | After **30 days** with no reply, **or** on receiving a reply that rejects the complaint wholly/partly or is unsatisfactory |
| **Limitation** | Within **1 year** of the entity's reply; where no reply was received, within **1 year and 30 days** of the date of the complaint to the entity |
| **Compensation the Ombudsman may award** | Up to **₹20 lakh** for the actual loss suffered, **plus up to ₹1 lakh** for loss of time, expenses, harassment and mental anguish |
| **Appeal** | To the **Appellate Authority — the Executive Director in charge of RBI's Consumer Education and Protection Department — within 30 days** of receipt of the award |

**Not maintainable before the Ombudsman:** matters **not first raised with the entity**; matters **already pending or decided** by a court, tribunal or arbitrator; complaints about the entity's **commercial judgement or business decision** (e.g. the rate of interest it chooses to offer) unless there is a deficiency; disputes **between two regulated entities**; **frivolous or vexatious** complaints; and complaints filed beyond the limitation period.

> ⚠️ **Trap:** "₹20 lakh" is the **actual-loss** cap and "₹1 lakh" the **mental-agony** cap; they are **separate and additive**, so the theoretical maximum is **₹21 lakh**. Also note this is the *banking* number — do not swap it with the **Insurance Ombudsman's ₹30 lakh**.

## 20.7 Grievance Redressal in Insurance

### Step 1 — the insurer

Every insurer must have a **board-approved grievance redressal policy**, a **Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO)** at head office and at each servicing office, and an online/offline system for logging complaints. Under the IRDAI **Protection of Policyholders' Interests** framework:

| Action | **Timeline** |
|---|---|
| **Written acknowledgement** of the grievance | Within **3 working days** of receipt |
| **Resolution and final letter**, if not resolved at acknowledgement | Within **2 weeks (14 days)** of receipt |
| Complaint **deemed closed** if the complainant does not respond to the insurer's final resolution | After **8 weeks** |

### Step 2 — **Bima Bharosa** (IRDAI)

**Bima Bharosa** (bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in) is IRDAI's **online policyholder grievance portal**, the renamed **Integrated Grievance Management System (IGMS)**. It is supported by IRDAI's **Grievance Redressal Cell** in the Consumer Affairs Department and a **grievance call centre (155255 / 1800 4254 732)** and the complaints@irdai.gov.in mailbox.

**What Bima Bharosa does:** it **registers the complaint, forwards it to the insurer, tracks it against the prescribed turnaround, gives the policyholder a tracking number, and builds industry-wide complaint analytics for supervision.** What it does **not** do is **adjudicate the claim** — IRDAI does not decide whether the claim was rightly repudiated.

### Step 3 — the **Insurance Ombudsman**

Established under the **Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017** (amended in 2021) and administered by the **Council for Insurance Ombudsmen (CIO)**. There are **17 Insurance Ombudsman offices** across India, each covering a defined territory.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who may complain** | An **individual** policyholder (or their legal heir/nominee/assignee) in a **personal capacity**; the 2021 amendment also brought complaints against **insurance brokers and intermediaries** within scope |
| **Preconditions** | The complainant **must first have made a written representation to the insurer**, and the insurer must have **rejected it**, or given a **reply the complainant finds unsatisfactory**, or **failed to reply within one month** |
| **Limitation** | Within **1 year** of the insurer's rejection or final reply |
| **Not admissible if** | The same matter is **pending before, or already decided by, a court, consumer forum or arbitrator** |
| **Value limit** | Claims (including expenses claimed) **not exceeding ₹30 lakh** |
| **Grounds** | Delay in settlement, **partial or total repudiation of a claim**, premium disputes, **mis-selling**, misrepresentation of policy terms, non-issuance of documents, policy servicing failures, and **any deficiency of service** arising from the policy |
| **As conciliator** | Where the parties agree, the Ombudsman makes a **recommendation within 1 month** of receiving the complaint |
| **As adjudicator** | Where no settlement is reached, the Ombudsman passes an **award within 3 months** of receiving all requirements |
| **Binding effect** | The award is **binding on the INSURER but not on the complainant** — who remains free to go to a consumer commission or civil court |
| **Compliance** | The complainant confirms acceptance in writing within **30 days**; the insurer must **comply within 30 days** of receiving that acceptance and inform the Ombudsman |
| **Cost** | **Free** — no fee, and no lawyer is required |

> 🧠 **Why is the award binding on only one side?** Because the Ombudsman is an **investor-protection** device, not a contract. Making it one-way binding gives the policyholder a free, fast, low-risk shot at redress with **nothing to lose** — if the award disappoints, the courts are still open. If it bound the policyholder too, the cautious would never file.

## 20.8 Redress in Pension — PFRDA and CGMS

Grievances of NPS and APY subscribers are governed by the **PFRDA (Redressal of Subscriber Grievance) Regulations, 2015**.

**CGMS — the Central Grievance Management System** — is the online grievance module operated through the **Central Recordkeeping Agency (CRA)**. A subscriber can raise a grievance through the **CRA website, the NPS mobile app, the call centre, or a physical form (Form G1)** submitted to the POP/nodal office, and gets a **token number** to track it.

### The pension ladder

| Level | Who | Time allowed |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The **intermediary** — POP, nodal office, CRA, aggregator, custodian, pension fund — through **CGMS** | Resolve within **30 days** |
| **2** | The **National Pension System Trust (NPS Trust)** — its Grievance Redressal Officer | If not resolved in 30 days or the subscriber is dissatisfied |
| **3** | The **Ombudsman appointed by PFRDA** | Appeal within **45 days** of the decision (or of the expiry of the 30-day period), extendable for sufficient cause |
| **4** | **PFRDA** — appeal against the Ombudsman's order | Within **30 days** |
| **5** | **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)** — appeal against PFRDA's order | Under **Section 36 of the PFRDA Act, 2013** |
| **6** | **Supreme Court** — on a question of law | Within **60 days** of SAT's order |

> 🧠 **Note the crossover.** Pension appeals end up at **SAT**, the same tribunal that hears SEBI appeals. SAT is not a "securities-only" tribunal — it is the appellate tribunal for **SEBI, PFRDA and IRDAI** matters alike. That is a favourite exam point.

The PFRDA Ombudsman first tries to **settle by conciliation or mediation**; where that fails, it passes a reasoned order. PFRDA also runs a **grievance monitoring and reporting framework**, and intermediaries must report grievance statistics periodically.

## 20.9 ⭐ The Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)

**SAT is a statutory tribunal established under Section 15K of the SEBI Act, 1992, with its headquarters in Mumbai.**

**What SAT is for — the distinction that decides half the questions on it.** SAT is an **appellate** body. It hears appeals by a **person aggrieved by an ORDER of a regulator** — it is **not** a complaint counter for an investor who is unhappy with a broker. An investor with a money claim against an intermediary goes to **ODR / arbitration / the consumer commission**, never to SAT.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Established under** | **Section 15K, SEBI Act, 1992** |
| **Headquarters** | **Mumbai** |
| **Composition** | A **Presiding Officer** plus **Judicial and Technical Members** |
| **Presiding Officer's qualification** | A sitting or retired **Judge of the Supreme Court**, or a sitting or retired **Chief Justice of a High Court** |
| **Appeals lie to SAT against orders of** | **SEBI** and its **Adjudicating Officers**; **PFRDA** (PFRDA Act, 2013); **IRDAI** (Insurance Act); and orders of **stock exchanges and depositories** in specified matters |
| **Time to file an appeal** | Within **45 days** of receiving a copy of the order; SAT may **condone delay** for sufficient cause |
| **Disposal** | SAT shall dispose of the appeal **as expeditiously as possible** and endeavour to do so within **6 months** |
| **Powers** | **Not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure**; guided by **natural justice**; but has the powers of a **civil court** for summoning, discovery and evidence. It may **confirm, modify or set aside** the order appealed against |
| **Representation** | The appellant may appear in person or be represented by a **chartered accountant, company secretary, cost accountant or legal practitioner** |
| **Further appeal** | To the **Supreme Court** within **60 days**, on any **question of law**, extendable by a further **60 days** for sufficient cause (**Section 15Z, SEBI Act**) |

> 🧠 **The three numbers for SAT: 45 – 6 – 60.** **45 days** to appeal to SAT, **6 months** target for disposal, **60 days** to appeal onward to the Supreme Court.

> ⚠️ **Trap:** the appeal from SAT to the Supreme Court lies **only on a question of law** — the Supreme Court will not re-open findings of fact.

## 20.10 Other Redressal Fora

| Forum | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Stock exchange IGRC and arbitration** | Investor complaints against **trading members**; the exchange's **Investor Grievance Redressal Committee** hears the matter, followed by arbitration/appellate arbitration — now largely routed through **ODR** |
| **Investor Protection Fund (IPF)** | Compensates investors up to prescribed limits when a **trading member is declared a defaulter** |
| **Depositories & RTAs** | Demat and transfer-agent complaints, escalated via the depository and then SCORES |
| **AMFI** | Complaints against **mutual fund distributors** (conduct, mis-selling); AMFI can act against an ARN holder |
| **BASL** | The body administering **Investment Advisers**, which receives IA complaints data and can act on conduct |
| **IEPF Authority (MCA)** | Recovery of **unclaimed dividends and shares** transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund |
| **NCLT / MCA / Registrar of Companies** | Company-law matters — oppression and mismanagement, non-receipt of statutory documents |
| **Consumer commissions (CPA 2019)** | Deficiency of service by any financial service provider |
| **Civil courts** | Contractual claims, and any matter outside a specialised forum's jurisdiction |
| **Police / Economic Offences Wing / Cyber Crime cell** | **Fraud, forgery, impersonation, unauthorised transactions, cyber fraud** — a criminal matter, not merely a service deficiency |
| **National Consumer Helpline (1915) and the e-Daakhil portal** | First-line consumer help and electronic filing |
| **SEBI's toll-free investor helpline** | Guidance on where and how to complain in the securities market |
| **Lok Adalat / mediation** | Amicable settlement, with the award of a Lok Adalat having the force of a civil decree |

> ⚠️ **Choosing the forum — the two questions to ask.**
> **(1) Is this a service deficiency, a money dispute, or a crime?** Deficiency → the entity, then the regulator's portal, then the Ombudsman/consumer commission. Money dispute → **ODR/arbitration**. Crime → **police/EOW**, *and* the regulator.
> **(2) Am I challenging a REGULATOR'S ORDER, or complaining about a FIRM?** A regulator's order → **SAT**. A firm → everything else.

## ⚡ Quick Revision Sheet

| Concept | The one-line answer |
|---|---|
| The universal ladder | **Intermediary → Regulator → Tribunal/Court** |
| Consumer Protection Act | **2019**, in force from **20 July 2020**, replacing the 1986 Act |
| Consumer | Buys goods / avails services **for consideration**; includes the **user with the buyer's approval** |
| Excluded from "consumer" | Purchase for **resale or commercial purpose** — **unless** exclusively for **livelihood by self-employment** |
| Consumer commissions | **District → State → National**, appeal onward to the **Supreme Court** |
| Pecuniary limits (current, 2021 Rules) | District **up to ₹50 lakh**; State **₹50 lakh–₹2 crore**; National **above ₹2 crore** |
| Pecuniary limits (as originally enacted, 2019) | District **up to ₹1 crore**; State **₹1–10 crore**; National **above ₹10 crore** |
| Jurisdiction measured by | The **value of the consideration paid** — not the compensation claimed |
| Limitation to file a consumer complaint | **2 years** from the cause of action |
| New territorial rule in 2019 | May file where the **complainant resides or works for gain** |
| Consumer filing fee | **Nil** up to a claim of **₹5 lakh**; e-filing via **e-Daakhil** |
| Consumer appeals | District→State **45 days** (deposit **50%**); State→National **30 days**; National→SC **30 days** |
| CCPA | Central Consumer Protection Authority — acts on **unfair trade practices and misleading ads**; Investigation Wing under a **Director General** |
| Misleading-ad penalties | **₹10 lakh** first offence, **₹50 lakh** repeat; endorser ban **1 year / 3 years** |
| Six consumer rights | Safety, Information, Choice, Be heard, Redressal, Education (**SIC-HRE**) |
| Seven elements of a grievance system | **Source · Date/time · Nature · Internal-or-escalated · Status · Time taken (ageing) · Escalation** |
| Why the date/time matters | It **starts the regulatory clock** for every timeline |
| Why an ageing report | Averages hide breaches; **buckets expose stale complaints** |
| IA's governing provision | **Regulation 21**, SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, **2013** |
| IA must be registered on | **SCORES** and the **SMART ODR** portal |
| IA record retention | **5 years**; longer — **till final disposal** — where a matter is in dispute/litigation |
| IA website disclosures | **Investor Charter**, escalation matrix, and **monthly complaints data** |
| SCORES | **SEBI Complaints Redress System**, launched **June 2011**; **SCORES 2.0** from **1 April 2024** |
| SCORES precondition | Must have **first approached the entity**; **no anonymous** complaints |
| SCORES limitation | Lodge within **1 year** of the cause of action |
| SCORES clock | Entity **21 days** → review request **15** → Designated Body **21** → review request **15** → SEBI **21** |
| SCORES will not take | Unlisted companies, **sub judice** matters, other regulators' subjects, anonymous complaints |
| ODR portal | **SMART ODR** (smartodr.in), from SEBI's circular of **31 July 2023** |
| ODR stages | **Online conciliation (non-binding, ~21 days + ~10)** → **online arbitration (binding award)** |
| ODR award enforceable under | The **Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996** |
| SCORES vs ODR | **SCORES chases; ODR decides** |
| Banking step 1 | Branch → Nodal / Principal Nodal Officer → **Internal Ombudsman** |
| Internal Ombudsman trigger | Any complaint the bank proposes to **reject wholly or partly** |
| RBI scheme | **RB-IOS 2021**, effective **12 Nov 2021** — "One Nation, One Ombudsman" |
| RB-IOS merged | Banking Ombudsman **2006** + NBFC Ombudsman **2018** + Digital Transactions **2019** |
| RB-IOS ground | Any **"deficiency in service"** (open-ended, not a closed list) |
| RB-IOS escalation trigger | **30 days** with no reply, or an unsatisfactory/rejecting reply |
| RB-IOS limitation | **1 year** from the reply; **1 year + 30 days** if no reply |
| RBI Ombudsman award cap | **₹20 lakh** actual loss **+ up to ₹1 lakh** for time/harassment |
| RB-IOS filing | Free, via **CMS portal** or the **CRPC, Chandigarh** |
| RB-IOS appeal | **Executive Director, Consumer Education & Protection Dept, RBI**, within **30 days** |
| Insurer's timelines | Acknowledge in **3 working days**; resolve in **2 weeks**; deemed closed after **8 weeks** of silence |
| IRDAI portal | **Bima Bharosa** (formerly **IGMS**) |
| Insurance Ombudsman rules | **Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017** (amended 2021); **17 offices**; **Council for Insurance Ombudsmen** |
| Insurance Ombudsman preconditions | Written representation first; **rejection, unsatisfactory reply, or no reply in 1 month** |
| Insurance Ombudsman limitation | **1 year** from rejection/final reply |
| Insurance Ombudsman value limit | **₹30 lakh** |
| Insurance Ombudsman timelines | Recommendation in **1 month**; award in **3 months**; insurer complies in **30 days** |
| Binding effect | Binding on the **insurer only**, not the complainant |
| Pension regulations | **PFRDA (Redressal of Subscriber Grievance) Regulations, 2015** |
| Pension portal | **CGMS** — Central Grievance Management System, via the **CRA** |
| Pension ladder | Intermediary (**30 days**) → **NPS Trust** → **PFRDA Ombudsman** (**45 days** to appeal) → **PFRDA** (**30 days**) → **SAT** → **SC (60 days)** |
| SAT established under | **Section 15K, SEBI Act, 1992**; headquartered in **Mumbai** |
| SAT hears appeals from | **SEBI**, **PFRDA**, **IRDAI**, adjudicating officers, and exchanges/depositories |
| SAT Presiding Officer | Sitting/retired **Supreme Court judge** or **Chief Justice of a High Court** |
| Time to appeal to SAT | **45 days** (condonable) |
| SAT disposal target | **6 months** |
| SAT to Supreme Court | **60 days**, on a **question of law** only (**Section 15Z**) |
| SAT is NOT | A first-instance complaint counter for investors against brokers |
| Regulators do NOT | **Adjudicate** individual claims — they route, monitor and enforce timelines |
| Who adjudicates | **Ombudsman, arbitrator (ODR), consumer commission, civil court** |
| Fraud/forgery | Also a **criminal** matter — police / Economic Offences Wing / cyber cell |
| Other fora | Exchange **IGRC**, **IPF**, **AMFI**, **BASL**, **IEPF**, **NCLT**, Lok Adalat |

> **Exam tip:** almost every question in this chapter is answered by asking two things in order — **"has the client already gone to the firm?"** and **"is this a service complaint, a money dispute, or an appeal against a regulator's order?"** Get those two right and the correct forum picks itself. Then memorise the six numbers that carry the most marks: **SCORES 21 days**, **RBI Ombudsman ₹20 lakh + ₹1 lakh**, **Insurance Ombudsman ₹30 lakh**, **SAT 45 days**, **SC 60 days**, and **consumer limitation 2 years**.
