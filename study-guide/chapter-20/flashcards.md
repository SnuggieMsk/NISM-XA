# Chapter 20: Grievance Redress Mechanism — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **The escalation ladders and the timeline numbers are near-certain exam marks.**

**Q1.** State the one principle that runs through every redress system in this chapter.
> **A:** **Intermediary first → Regulator next → Tribunal/Court last.** A complaint that skips the firm and goes straight to the regulator, Ombudsman or SAT is **not maintainable**.

**Q2.** Why does the law insist the firm is approached first?
> **A:** The firm holds the **file, the recording, the audit trail and the money**, so it can fix a genuine error in a day. A regulator supervising thousands of intermediaries cannot. Escalation is reserved for when that level **fails**.

**Q3.** Which Act governs consumer protection in India, when did it come into force, and what did it replace?
> **A:** The **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**, in force from **20 July 2020**, replacing the **Consumer Protection Act, 1986**.

**Q4.** Who is a "consumer" under the Act?
> **A:** A person who **buys goods or avails services for a consideration** paid, promised, or partly paid and partly promised, or under a **system of deferred payment** — **including online and electronic transactions**. A **user or beneficiary with the buyer's approval** also counts.

**Q5.** Who is expressly NOT a consumer, and what is the carve-out?
> **A:** A person who obtains goods/services for **resale or any commercial purpose**. The carve-out: the exclusion does **not** apply where they are used **exclusively for earning a livelihood by means of self-employment**. *(One auto-rickshaw driven by its owner — consumer. A forty-vehicle fleet — not.)*

**Q6.** Name the three-tier consumer redressal machinery and who presides over each.
> **A:** **District Commission** (a person qualified to be a **District Judge**), **State Commission** (a sitting/retired **High Court judge**), **National Commission** (a sitting/retired **Supreme Court judge**).

**Q7.** What are the CURRENT pecuniary limits of the consumer commissions?
> **A:** Under the **2021 Jurisdiction Rules**: **District up to ₹50 lakh**; **State above ₹50 lakh up to ₹2 crore**; **National above ₹2 crore**.

**Q8.** What were the pecuniary limits as ORIGINALLY enacted in the 2019 Act?
> **A:** **District up to ₹1 crore**; **State ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore**; **National above ₹10 crore**. *(Both sets circulate in study material — read the stem carefully.)*

**Q9.** Is the consumer commission's jurisdiction fixed by the compensation claimed?
> **A:** **No** — it is fixed by the **value of the goods or services and the consideration paid**, not by the amount of compensation demanded.

**Q10.** What is the limitation period for filing a consumer complaint, and where may it be filed?
> **A:** **2 years** from the cause of action. It may be filed where the opposite party resides or carries on business, where the cause of action arose, or — **new in the 2019 Act** — **where the complainant resides or personally works for gain**.

**Q11.** State the consumer appeal timelines.
> **A:** **District → State: 45 days** (with a **50% deposit** of any amount ordered). **State → National: 30 days.** **National → Supreme Court: 30 days.**

**Q12.** What is the CCPA and what can it do?
> **A:** The **Central Consumer Protection Authority**, created by the 2019 Act, acts against **unfair trade practices and false or misleading advertisements**. It has an **Investigation Wing headed by a Director General** and can order recalls and refunds. Penalties: **₹10 lakh** first offence, **₹50 lakh** repeat; endorsers can be barred for **1 year**, and **3 years** on repeat.

**Q13.** Name the six consumer rights.
> **A:** **Safety, Information, Choice, Be heard, Redressal, Consumer education** — mnemonic **SIC-HRE**.

**Q14.** List the seven key elements of a robust grievance recording system.
> **A:** **Source** of receipt · **Date and time** of receipt · **Nature** of the complaint · whether **internal or to be escalated** · **Status / action taken** · **Time taken (ageing report)** · **Escalation mechanism**.

**Q15.** Why is the date and time of receipt so important?
> **A:** Because it **starts the regulatory clock**. Every timeline — the 21-day SCORES response, the insurer's 3-day acknowledgement, the bank's 30 days — is measured from receipt. Without a timestamp, compliance cannot be proved.

**Q16.** Why record the SOURCE, and why classify by NATURE?
> **A:** The **source** tells you **which regulatory clock applies** and whether a regulator is already watching (a SCORES complaint carries a hard **21-day** deadline; a walk-in does not). The **nature** enables **root-cause analysis** — twenty complaints of one type are a **process defect**, not twenty accidents.

**Q17.** What does an ageing report show that an average does not, and what is an escalation matrix?
> **A:** The ageing report shows the **outliers** — an average age of 9 days can conceal three complaints past 21 days and already in breach. **Buckets expose stale complaints; averages hide them.** The **escalation matrix** is the published table of **named officer, designation, email, phone and turnaround at each level**, so the client can go upward **inside the firm** first.

**Q18.** Which regulation governs an Investment Adviser's grievance obligations, and what does it require?
> **A:** **Regulation 21 of the SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013** — the IA must **redress grievances promptly**, have **adequate procedure for expeditious redressal**, and resolve securities-related grievances **in the manner and within the time specified by SEBI**.

**Q19.** Name six things an IA must actually do about grievances.
> **A:** Maintain a **documented policy and register**; name a **Grievance Redressal Officer** (and a **Compliance Officer** if non-individual); register on **SCORES** and the **SMART ODR portal**; display the **Investor Charter and escalation matrix**; publish **complaints data on the website monthly**; and **retain records for 5 years**.

**Q20.** How long must an IA keep grievance records, and when is a complaint properly "resolved"?
> **A:** **Five years** — but where a matter is **under dispute or litigation, records must be preserved until final disposal**. A complaint is resolved only when it has been **investigated, a reasoned reply given to the client, and the outcome recorded**; a complaint closed **without a written reply** is, on inspection, **unresolved**.

**Q21.** Why must the person who gave the advice not decide the complaint about it?
> **A:** Because most IA complaints are about the **advice itself** — unsuitability, undisclosed conflicts, fees. Letting the adviser judge his own work destroys the independence of the grievance function, which is exactly why a **separate named officer** is required.

**Q22.** What is SCORES, when was it launched, and what is SCORES 2.0?
> **A:** The **SEBI Complaints Redress System**, a centralised web-based grievance system launched in **June 2011**. **SCORES 2.0**, live from **1 April 2024**, added **auto-routing, auto-escalation and monitoring by Designated Bodies**.

**Q23.** What must an investor have done before lodging on SCORES, and by when must it be lodged?
> **A:** Must have **first approached the entity directly** and received no reply, an unsatisfactory reply, or no action. The complaint should be lodged within **1 year** of the cause of action. **Anonymous complaints are not accepted.**

**Q24.** State the SCORES 2.0 timeline.
> **A:** Entity files its **Action Taken Report within 21 calendar days** → investor may seek a **first review within 15 days** → the **Designated Body** reviews within **21 days** → investor may seek a **second review within 15 days** → **SEBI** reviews within **21 days**. Remember it as **21 – 15 – 21 – 15 – 21**.

**Q25.** Which complaints will SCORES NOT accept?
> **A:** Complaints against **unlisted/private companies**; **sub judice** matters; matters under **another regulator** (RBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, MCA); disputes about the **merits of a trade or investment losses**; and **anonymous, incomplete or frivolous** complaints.

**Q26.** What is the ODR portal, and under which SEBI circular was it introduced?
> **A:** **SMART ODR** — Securities Market Approach for Resolution through ODR (smartodr.in) — a common **Online Dispute Resolution** portal introduced by SEBI's circular of **31 July 2023**, run through **ODR Institutions empanelled by the Market Infrastructure Institutions**.

**Q27.** What are the two stages of ODR, and how do they differ?
> **A:** **Online conciliation** — **non-binding**, a facilitated settlement, targeted at **21 days** (extendable by about **10**); then **online arbitration** — **binding**, producing an **arbitral award enforceable under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996**.

**Q28.** Distinguish SCORES from ODR in one line.
> **A:** **SCORES chases; ODR decides.** SCORES gets the investor a **reply** and enforces timelines; ODR gets the investor an **award** on a disputed money claim.

**Q29.** Describe the banking escalation ladder inside the bank.
> **A:** **Branch → customer service / grievance cell → Nodal and Principal Nodal Officer → Internal Ombudsman.**

**Q30.** What is the Internal Ombudsman, and what triggers a referral to it?
> **A:** A **senior independent official who is not an employee of the bank**, mandatory for banks with **10 or more banking outlets**. Any complaint the bank proposes to **reject wholly or partly must be referred to the Internal Ombudsman before the rejection is communicated** to the customer.

**Q31.** What is RB-IOS 2021, and which schemes did it merge?
> **A:** The **RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021**, effective **12 November 2021** — "**One Nation, One Ombudsman**". It merged the **Banking Ombudsman Scheme 2006**, the **Ombudsman Scheme for NBFCs 2018** and the **Ombudsman Scheme for Digital Transactions 2019**.

**Q32.** On what ground can a complaint be made under RB-IOS, and how is that different from before?
> **A:** Any **"deficiency in service"**. The scheme replaced the earlier **closed list of specified grounds** with an **open, deficiency-based test**, subject only to an exclusion list.

**Q33.** When may a customer escalate to the RBI Ombudsman, and within what limitation?
> **A:** After **30 days** with no reply from the bank/NBFC, or on a reply that **rejects the complaint wholly/partly or is unsatisfactory**. The complaint must be filed within **1 year** of the reply, or within **1 year and 30 days** of the original complaint where no reply came.

**Q34.** What compensation can the RBI Ombudsman award, and how does one appeal?
> **A:** Up to **₹20 lakh for actual loss**, **plus up to ₹1 lakh** for loss of time, expenses, harassment and mental anguish. Appeal lies to the **Executive Director in charge of RBI's Consumer Education and Protection Department within 30 days**. Filing is **free**, through the **CMS portal** or the **CRPC at Chandigarh**.

**Q35.** What are an insurer's own grievance timelines?
> **A:** **Written acknowledgement within 3 working days**; **resolution and final letter within 2 weeks (14 days)**; and the complaint is **deemed closed if the complainant does not respond within 8 weeks** of the insurer's final resolution.

**Q36.** What is Bima Bharosa, and what does it NOT do?
> **A:** IRDAI's **online policyholder grievance portal** (formerly the **Integrated Grievance Management System, IGMS**). It **registers, forwards, tracks and analyses** complaints — but it does **not adjudicate** the claim. IRDAI does not decide whether a repudiation was right.

**Q37.** Under which rules is the Insurance Ombudsman established, how many offices are there, and what is the value limit?
> **A:** The **Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017** (amended 2021), administered by the **Council for Insurance Ombudsmen**. There are **17 offices**, and the value limit is **₹30 lakh**.

**Q38.** State the preconditions and limitation for approaching the Insurance Ombudsman.
> **A:** A **written representation to the insurer first**, followed by **rejection, an unsatisfactory reply, or no reply within one month**; the complaint must then be filed within **1 year**. The matter must **not be pending before or decided by** a court, consumer forum or arbitrator.

**Q39.** State the Insurance Ombudsman's own timelines and who the award binds.
> **A:** As conciliator, a **recommendation within 1 month**; as adjudicator, an **award within 3 months** of receiving all requirements. The insurer must **comply within 30 days** of the complainant's written acceptance. The award is **binding on the insurer but NOT on the complainant**.

**Q40.** Why is the Insurance Ombudsman's award binding on only one side?
> **A:** Because it is an **investor-protection device**, not a contract. One-way binding gives the policyholder a **free, fast, no-risk** attempt at redress with the courts still open if the award disappoints — if it bound them too, cautious complainants would never file.

**Q41.** Which regulations and which portal govern NPS grievances?
> **A:** The **PFRDA (Redressal of Subscriber Grievance) Regulations, 2015**, using **CGMS — the Central Grievance Management System** — operated through the **Central Recordkeeping Agency (CRA)**.

**Q42.** Set out the pension escalation ladder.
> **A:** Intermediary (POP/nodal office/CRA) via **CGMS — 30 days** → **NPS Trust** → **PFRDA Ombudsman** (appeal within **45 days**) → **PFRDA** (within **30 days**) → **Securities Appellate Tribunal** → **Supreme Court** within **60 days**.

**Q43.** Under what provision is SAT established, where does it sit, and whose orders does it hear appeals against?
> **A:** **Section 15K of the SEBI Act, 1992**; headquartered in **Mumbai**. It hears appeals against orders of **SEBI** and its adjudicating officers, **PFRDA**, **IRDAI**, and stock exchanges/depositories in specified matters.

**Q44.** What are the three key SAT numbers, and can an investor take a broker to SAT for a ₹2 lakh loss?
> **A:** **45 days** to file (condonable), a **6-month** disposal target, and **60 days** to appeal onward to the **Supreme Court** on a **question of law** only (**Section 15Z**). And **no** — SAT is an **appellate** body against a **regulator's order**, not a first-instance complaint counter. A money claim goes to the firm, then **SCORES**, then **ODR/arbitration** or a consumer commission.

**Q45.** Do SEBI, RBI, IRDAI and PFRDA adjudicate individual claims — and name five "other" fora.
> **A:** **No.** Their portals **route, monitor and enforce timelines**; adjudication belongs to the **Ombudsman, an ODR arbitrator, a consumer commission or a civil court**. Other fora: the exchange's **IGRC** and arbitration, the **Investor Protection Fund**, **AMFI** (distributor conduct), **BASL** (Investment Advisers) and the **IEPF Authority** (unclaimed dividends/shares) — plus **NCLT/MCA**, the **police/Economic Offences Wing** for fraud, and **Lok Adalat**.
