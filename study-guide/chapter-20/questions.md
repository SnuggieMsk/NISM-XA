# Chapter 20: Grievance Redress Mechanism — 100 MCQ Question Bank

> Three tiers — 🟢 Easy (Recall), 🟡 Medium (Application), 🔴 Hard (Numerical & Scenario). **Almost every question here reduces to one thing: given this complaint, what is the NEXT step and by when?** Remember the **25% negative marking**.

## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** The Consumer Protection Act currently in force in India is of the year:
A) 1986  B) 2005  C) 2019  D) 2013
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** replaced the 1986 Act.
A) The 1986 Act was repealed and replaced. B) 2005 is the Right to Information Act year, not consumer protection. D) 2013 is the year of the SEBI Investment Advisers Regulations and the Companies Act, not the Consumer Protection Act.
</details>

**Q2.** The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 came into force from:
A) 20 July 2020  B) 1 April 2019  C) 12 November 2021  D) 1 April 2024
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The Act was passed in 2019 but its main provisions were notified with effect from **20 July 2020**.
B) The Act had not been notified in April 2019. C) 12 November 2021 is the RB-IOS 2021 date. D) 1 April 2024 is the SCORES 2.0 date.
</details>

**Q3.** Under the Consumer Protection Act, a "consumer" is a person who:
A) Receives goods free of charge  B) Buys goods or avails services for a consideration  C) Buys goods for resale  D) Manufactures goods
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Consideration paid, promised, partly paid, or under deferred payment is **essential**.
A) A purely free or gratuitous service generally creates no consumer relationship. C) Purchase for resale is expressly excluded. D) A manufacturer is the opposite party, not the consumer.
</details>

**Q4.** A person who obtains goods for resale or any commercial purpose is:
A) A consumer  B) A consumer only if registered  C) Not a consumer  D) A consumer above ₹1 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The definition **expressly excludes** resale and commercial purpose.
A) Contradicts the statutory exclusion. B) Registration is irrelevant to the definition. D) There is no value threshold in the definition.
</details>

**Q5.** The "commercial purpose" exclusion does NOT apply where the goods or services are used:
A) By a company  B) For export  C) For resale online  D) Exclusively for earning a livelihood by means of self-employment
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — The livelihood-by-self-employment carve-out protects the small self-employed person: one auto-rickshaw driven by its owner is a consumer, a forty-vehicle fleet is not.
A) A company buying for its business is squarely commercial. B) Export is a commercial purpose. C) Online resale is still resale.
</details>

A) These are financial sector regulators, not consumer fora. C) These are courts and a tribunal, not consumer commissions. D) That is the banking internal ladder.

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** An investor is unhappy with the service of his stock broker. His first step should be to:
A) File a complaint with the broker itself  B) Approach SAT  C) File on SCORES immediately  D) File a police complaint
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — The universal ladder starts with the **intermediary**, which alone holds the file, the recording and the money.
B) SAT hears appeals against regulators' orders. C) SCORES requires the entity to have been approached first. D) A service complaint is not, by itself, a crime.
</details>

**Q37.** A SCORES complaint is rejected because the investor never wrote to the intermediary. The reason is that:
A) SCORES charges a fee  B) SCORES requires the entity to be approached first  C) SCORES is only for listed companies  D) SCORES has been discontinued
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The precondition is a prior approach to the entity with no reply, an unsatisfactory reply, or no action.
A) SCORES is free. C) It covers registered intermediaries as well as listed companies. D) SCORES is very much operational, now as SCORES 2.0.
</details>

**Q38.** Which of the following will SCORES NOT take up:
A) A delay by a registered broker  B) Non-receipt of a demat statement  C) A matter already pending before a civil court  D) Non-redressal by a listed company
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — **Sub judice** matters are outside SCORES; a regulator cannot run a parallel process to a court.
A), B) and D) are all standard, admissible SCORES complaints.
</details>

**Q39.** After SCORES closes his file, an investor still wants ₹3.5 lakh in damages from a broker. He should:
A) Approach SAT  B) Write to the Finance Ministry  C) Initiate dispute resolution on the SMART ODR portal  D) Abandon the claim
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A disputed money claim needs an **adjudicator**; ODR provides online conciliation and then binding arbitration.
A) SAT hears appeals against regulators' orders, not investor money claims. B) The ministry is not a redressal forum. D) A live remedy remains available.
</details>

**Q40.** The essential difference between SCORES and ODR is that:
A) Both adjudicate identically  B) ODR is available only to companies  C) SCORES awards damages  D) SCORES routes and enforces timelines while ODR adjudicates the dispute
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — **SCORES chases; ODR decides.**
A) SCORES does not adjudicate at all. B) ODR is expressly available to investors. C) SCORES cannot award money.
</details>

**Q41.** A bank customer complained 40 days ago and has received no reply. He may now:

**Q42.** Before a bank communicates a wholly or partly rejected complaint to a customer, it must refer the matter to:

**Q43.** Which of the following is NOT covered by RB-IOS 2021:

### Case: Mr Devendra Prabhu's Four Grievances

Mr Devendra Prabhu, 54, a consulting engineer in Pune, is a fee-paying client of **Sahyadri Wealth Advisors**, a SEBI-registered **non-individual** Investment Adviser. Over one difficult half-year, four things go wrong:

| # | What happened | Key date | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An **unauthorised sale of shares** in his broking account | Broker's unsatisfactory reply; SCORES complaint lodged **2 June 2025** | ₹6,20,000 |
| 2 | ₹48,000 debited for a **failed NEFT** and never reversed; written complaint to the bank on **12 February 2025**, **no reply at all** | 12 February 2025 | ₹48,000 |
| 3 | His health insurer **repudiated a hospitalisation claim**; written rejection received **20 March 2025** | 20 March 2025 | ₹9,40,000 |
| 4 | An **NPS contribution not credited**; grievance raised on **CGMS on 1 July 2025** with his POP | 1 July 2025 | ₹1,50,000 |

Separately, on **3 May 2025** he complains to Sahyadri Wealth Advisors that it recommended a product **without disclosing a conflict of interest**. He has paid the firm advisory fees of **₹1,10,000** over two years.

---

**QC1.** For the SCORES complaint lodged on 2 June 2025, by when must the broker file its Action Taken Report, and what happens if it does not:
A) 9 June 2025; the complaint lapses  B) 23 June 2025; the complaint auto-escalates to the Designated Body  C) 17 July 2025; it goes to SAT  D) 2 July 2025; it goes to a consumer commission
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The entity gets **21 calendar days**: 2 June + 21 = **23 June 2025**. Under **SCORES 2.0** a missed deadline triggers **automatic escalation to the Designated Body** without the investor doing anything — the whole point of the 2024 upgrade.
A) 7 days is not the period, and complaints do not lapse on the entity's default. C) 17 July is 45 days, the SAT period, and SAT has no role here. D) 2 July is 30 days, and a consumer commission is a separate parallel route, not the automatic next step within SCORES.
</details>

**QC2.** On the ₹48,000 NEFT complaint made to the bank on 12 February 2025 with no reply, what are the earliest and last dates for approaching the RBI Ombudsman:
A) Earliest 12 February 2025; last 12 February 2026  B) Earliest immediately; there is no last date  C) Earliest about 14 March 2025; last about 14 March 2026  D) He cannot approach the Ombudsman at all
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — He must wait **30 days** for the bank to reply: 12 February 2025 + 30 days = **14 March 2025** (February 2025 has 28 days, so 16 days remain in February and 14 fall in March). Where **no reply** is received, the limitation is **one year and thirty days** from the complaint: 12 February 2026 + 30 days = **14 March 2026**.
A) Ignores both the 30-day waiting period and the extra 30 days in the limitation. B) A limitation period certainly applies. D) The complaint is squarely a deficiency in service under RB-IOS 2021.
</details>

A) Cannot hear it, as the value is too high  B) Can hear it only after three years  C) Will hear it only if he engages a lawyer  D) Can hear it, as ₹9.40 lakh is within the ₹30 lakh limit, provided he files by about 20 March 2026
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: D)** — Two tests are satisfied. **Value:** ₹9,40,000 is comfortably below the **₹30,00,000** ceiling. **Limitation:** the complaint must be filed within **one year** of the rejection, that is by about **20 March 2026**. He has already made a written representation and received a rejection, so the precondition is met.
A) ₹9.40 lakh is well under the ceiling. B) Three years is the civil limitation period, not the Ombudsman's. C) The process is free and no lawyer is required.
</details>

**QC4.** The NPS grievance raised on CGMS on 1 July 2025 is unresolved. The correct route from here is:

**QC5.** On the 3 May 2025 conflict-of-interest complaint against Sahyadri Wealth Advisors, what must the firm do, and where can the ₹1,10,000 fee dispute go if it stays unresolved:

If Mr Prabhu remains dissatisfied he escalates to **SCORES**, and then to the **SMART ODR portal**, where the ₹1,10,000 claim can be conciliated and, failing that, arbitrated.

**The wider lesson:** the ₹1,10,000 is not the real exposure. A conflict-of-interest allegation goes to **Regulation 21 and the code of conduct together**, and an undocumented closure would convert a manageable client complaint into an adverse inspection finding.
