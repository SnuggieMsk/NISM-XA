> The single largest fact-density chapter in the syllabus. Almost every mark here is a **number, a name or a deadline**. The **SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013** alone are worth more marks than most whole chapters — learn them line by line.

## 18.0 The architecture — who regulates what

Before the detail, fix the map in your head. Indian financial regulation is **sector-based**, not product-based.

| Regulator | Governs | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| **SEBI** | Securities market, exchanges, intermediaries, **investment advisers** | SEBI Act **1992**, SCRA **1956** |
| **RBI** | Banks, NBFCs, payment systems, **foreign exchange** | RBI Act 1934, FEMA **1999** |
| **IRDAI** | Insurance | Insurance Act 1938 |
| **PFRDA** | Pension (NPS) | PFRDA Act 2013 |
| **IFSCA** | All financial services inside an **IFSC** (GIFT City) | IFSCA Act **2019** |
| **FIU-IND** | Anti-money-laundering reporting | PMLA **2002** |
| **IBBI** | Insolvency and bankruptcy | IBC **2016** |

> 🧠 **The two-layer idea.** Every Indian securities law works in two layers: a **parent Act** passed by Parliament (SCRA, SEBI Act, PMLA, FEMA) and **regulations** framed under it by the regulator (PFUTP, PIT, IA Regulations). The Act gives the *power*; the regulations give the *detail*. Penalties almost always live in the **Act**, obligations almost always live in the **regulations**.

## 18.1 Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 — SCRA

**Purpose (from the preamble):** *to prevent undesirable transactions in securities by regulating the business of dealing therein and by providing for certain other matters connected therewith.*

SCRA is the **oldest** of the securities statutes and does three big things: it **defines what a "security" is**, it **recognises and regulates stock exchanges**, and it **makes listing compulsory and enforceable**.

### The definition of "securities" — Section 2(h)

Securities **include**:

- **Shares, scrips, stocks, bonds, debentures**, debenture stock or other marketable securities of a like nature in or of any incorporated company or other body corporate
- **Derivatives**
- **Units or any other instrument issued by any collective investment scheme**
- **Security receipts** as defined under the SARFAESI Act
- **Units or any other such instrument issued to investors under any mutual fund scheme**
- **Government securities**
- **Rights or interest** in securities
- Such other instruments as the **Central Government** may declare to be securities

Securities **do not include** a **unit-linked insurance policy (ULIP)** or any similar instrument that combines life risk cover with investment and is issued by an insurer. That exclusion was inserted after the 2010 SEBI–IRDA turf dispute over ULIPs.

> ⚠️ **Exam trap:** a **fixed deposit**, a **bank savings account**, **gold**, **real estate**, an **insurance policy** and a **ULIP** are **not securities** under SCRA. A **mutual fund unit** and a **government security** are. An adviser recommending only insurance is therefore outside SEBI's securities perimeter — which is exactly why insurance agents get an exemption under the IA Regulations (18.6.3).

### Other key SCRA concepts

| Section / concept | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Spot delivery contract** — s.2(i) | Actual delivery of securities **and** payment on the **same day or the next day** |
| **Recognition of stock exchanges** — s.4 | An exchange operates only with **recognition** (granted today by **SEBI**) |
| **Corporatisation and demutualisation** — s.4A/4B | Exchanges converted from member-owned associations into **companies with ownership separated from trading rights** |
| **Listing** — s.21 | Securities may be listed only on a recognised exchange, on the prescribed conditions |
| **Delisting** — s.21A | An exchange may delist for breach of the listing agreement; the company may appeal to **SAT** |
| **Derivatives** — s.18A | A derivative contract is **legal and valid only if** traded on a **recognised stock exchange** and **settled on the clearing house** |
| **Offences** — s.23 | Imprisonment up to **10 years** or fine up to **₹25 crore**, or both |
| **Rule-making** — s.30 | Central Government makes the **Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 (SCRR)** |

> 🧠 **Why Section 18A matters to an adviser.** Section 30 of the **Indian Contract Act** makes wagering agreements **void**. A futures or options contract looks economically like a wager. Section 18A of SCRA rescues it: an **exchange-traded, clearing-house-settled** derivative is a valid contract. An **off-market** "difference contract" outside a recognised exchange is **not** — it is an unenforceable wager. That single link explains why SEBI treats "dabba trading" as illegal.

The **SCRR, 1957** carries the operational detail — including the **minimum public shareholding** requirement of **25%** for listed companies.

## 18.2 The SEBI Act, 1992

SEBI was set up as a **non-statutory body in 1988** and given **statutory teeth by the SEBI Act, 1992** (effective **30 January 1992**). Head office: **Mumbai**.

**Preamble — the three-fold mandate.** SEBI exists:
1. **To protect the interests of investors** in securities;
2. **To promote the development** of the securities market; and
3. **To regulate** the securities market.

> 🧠 **Memory hook — "P-P-R": Protect, Promote, Regulate.** Note the order: **protection comes first**. Whenever an exam question asks "what is SEBI's primary objective?", the answer is **investor protection**.

### Constitution — Section 4

| Member | Number |
|---|---|
| **Chairman** | 1 |
| Members from Ministries dealing with **finance** and **administration of the Companies Act** | 2 |
| Member from the **RBI** | 1 |
| Other members appointed by the Central Government (at least **three whole-time**) | 5 |
| **Total** | **9** |

### Section 11 — functions of the Board

- Regulating the **business in stock exchanges** and any other securities markets
- **Registering and regulating intermediaries** — stock brokers, share transfer agents, bankers to an issue, trustees of trust deeds, registrars to an issue, merchant bankers, underwriters, **portfolio managers, investment advisers** and others associated with the securities market
- Registering and regulating **collective investment schemes including mutual funds**
- Promoting and regulating **self-regulatory organisations**
- **Prohibiting fraudulent and unfair trade practices**
- **Prohibiting insider trading**
- Regulating **substantial acquisition of shares and takeovers**
- Promoting **investor education** and training of intermediaries
- Calling for information, undertaking **inspection**, conducting **inquiries and audits**
- Performing functions under **SCRA**, delegated by the Central Government

Under **Section 11(3)** SEBI has the **powers of a civil court** — summoning attendance, discovery and production of books, and inspection of registers.

| Section | Power |
|---|---|
| **11B** | Issue **directions** in the interest of investors (the workhorse of SEBI enforcement) |
| **11C** | Order **investigation** |
| **11AA** | Defines a **collective investment scheme** |
| **12** | No intermediary may deal in securities **without a certificate of registration** |
| **12A** | Prohibits **manipulative and deceptive devices, insider trading** and fraudulent takeovers |
| **15I** | Appointment of an **Adjudicating Officer** |
| **15J** | Factors for fixing the penalty amount |
| **15T** | Appeal to the **Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT)** |
| **24** | Criminal offences — imprisonment up to **10 years**, fine up to **₹25 crore**, or both |

### ⭐ The penalty sections you must memorise

| Section | Default | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **15A(a)** | Failure to furnish documents/returns/reports to SEBI | **₹1 lakh per day**, max **₹1 crore** |
| **15EB** | **Investment adviser or research analyst** failing to comply with regulations or SEBI directions | **Minimum ₹1 lakh**; may extend to **₹1 lakh for each day** the failure continues, subject to a **maximum of ₹1 crore** |
| **15G** | **Insider trading** | **Minimum ₹10 lakh**; up to **₹25 crore** or **3× the profits** made, **whichever is higher** |
| **15HA** | **Fraudulent and unfair trade practices** | **Minimum ₹5 lakh**; up to **₹25 crore** or **3× the profits**, **whichever is higher** |
| **15HB** | Any contravention with **no specific penalty** provided | **Minimum ₹1 lakh**, up to **₹1 crore** |

**Section 15J — how the amount is fixed.** The adjudicating officer must consider:
1. The **disproportionate gain or unfair advantage**, wherever quantifiable, made as a result of the default;
2. The **loss caused to an investor** or group of investors; and
3. The **repetitive nature** of the default.

> 🧠 **The "10-5-1" ladder.** Minimum penalties climb with the seriousness of the sin: **₹10 lakh** for insider trading, **₹5 lakh** for market fraud, **₹1 lakh** for everything else (including an adviser's compliance failure). The **maximum** for both of the big two is the same: **₹25 crore or 3× profits, whichever is higher**.

### Appeals

| Step | Forum | Time limit |
|---|---|---|
| SEBI / adjudicating officer order | — | — |
| First appeal | **SAT** (Securities Appellate Tribunal) | **45 days** from receipt of the order |
| Second appeal (**question of law only**) | **Supreme Court** | **60 days** |

SAT also hears appeals against **IRDAI** and **PFRDA** orders. SAT is expected to dispose of an appeal within **six months**.

## 18.3 SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations, 2003 — PFUTP

These regulations put flesh on Section 12A of the SEBI Act. They are short, blunt and extremely wide.

### The definition of "fraud" — Regulation 2(1)(c)

Fraud includes **any act, expression, omission or concealment** committed **whether in a deceitful manner or not**, by a person or with his connivance or by his agent, **while dealing in securities**, in order to **induce another person to deal in securities** —

> ⚠️ **…whether or not there is any wrongful gain or avoidance of any loss.** This is the most-tested phrase in PFUTP. You do **not** need to have made money for it to be fraud. Intent to induce is enough.

### Regulation 3 — prohibition of certain dealings

No person shall directly or indirectly:
- **Buy, sell or otherwise deal in securities in a fraudulent manner**
- Use or employ any **manipulative or deceptive device or contrivance**
- Employ any **device, scheme or artifice to defraud**
- Engage in any act, practice or course of business which **operates as a fraud or deceit**

### Regulation 4 — the deemed manipulative and unfair practices

Regulation 4(1) states plainly: **no person shall indulge in a fraudulent or an unfair trade practice in securities.** Regulation 4(2) then *deems* a long list to be such practices, including:

| Practice | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Creating a false or misleading appearance of trading** | Artificial volume to attract onlookers |
| **Wash trades** | Dealing in a security with **no intention of transferring beneficial ownership** |
| **Circular trading** | A closed loop of buyers and sellers passing the same shares around |
| **Price rigging** | Inducing others to deal so as to artificially **inflate, depress or maintain** the price |
| **Pump and dump** | Ramping the price on false information, then selling into the retail buying |
| **Planting false or misleading news** | Fake announcements, forged letters, fabricated orders |
| **Front running** | Dealing **ahead of a substantial impending client transaction** of which one has unpublished knowledge |
| **Misleading advertisement** | Information presented in a distorted manner that may influence investors' decisions |
| **Disseminating advice one does not believe to be true** | Recommending through any medium — **physical or digital** — a security one does not honestly believe in |
| **Fraudulent inducement to invest** | Any inducement to deal based on a false statement |

> 🧠 **Why the last two matter to an adviser more than anything else in this chapter.** An RIA who runs a Telegram channel touting a stock he already owns, or who publishes a "target price" he does not believe in, is not merely breaching the IA Code of Conduct — he is committing **fraud under PFUTP**, exposing himself to a **₹5 lakh–₹25 crore** penalty under **Section 15HA** *and* possible prosecution under **Section 24**.

### Powers under PFUTP

SEBI may **investigate** (Reg 5–10) and then, under **Regulation 11**, issue directions to:
- **Suspend trading** in the security
- **Restrain persons from accessing the securities market** and prohibit them from dealing
- **Prohibit disposal** of securities
- Direct **disgorgement** of unlawful gains

**Front running note:** front running by an *intermediary* is specifically deemed unfair — for example, an intermediary buying (or taking a derivative position) **in advance of a substantial client order**. This is the practice most likely to be tested in the context of an adviser who also trades his own book.

## 18.4 SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008

Before 2008 every intermediary category had its own registration, obligations and enforcement code. The 2008 Regulations created a **single common rulebook** covering:

1. **Registration** (a common application and "fit and proper" test)
2. **General obligations and a common Code of Conduct**
3. **A common procedure for action in case of default**
4. **Action against persons who are not registered at all**

An **investment adviser is an intermediary** — so these regulations sit **behind** the IA Regulations at all times.

### Registration and "fit and proper person"

- Application in **Form A** with the prescribed fee; SEBI may seek clarifications and require a personal representation
- Registration is **permanent**, subject to payment of fees and continued compliance — there is **no periodic renewal**
- SEBI must be satisfied the applicant is a **fit and proper person** under **Schedule II**

**Fit and proper criteria:** financial integrity, **absence of convictions or civil liabilities**, competence (including financial solvency and net worth), **good reputation and character**, efficiency and honesty, and absence of any disqualification.

A person is **deemed not fit and proper** if, for example, he has been:
- **Convicted** of an offence involving **moral turpitude, economic offence, securities laws or fraud**
- **Declared insolvent** and not discharged
- Found to be of **unsound mind** by a competent court
- **Debarred or restrained** by SEBI or another financial sector regulator
- Categorised as a **wilful defaulter**
- Declared a **fugitive economic offender**

### Common Code of Conduct (Schedule III)

Maintain **high standards of integrity, fairness and professional excellence**; act with **due skill, care and diligence**; **not make misleading or exaggerated claims**; **not offer inducements**; maintain **confidentiality**; **avoid conflicts of interest** and disclose them; **redress investor grievances promptly**; **appoint a compliance officer**; **maintain records**; and **cooperate with SEBI** inspections.

### ⭐ Chapter IV — common procedure for action in case of default

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | SEBI appoints a **Designated Authority** (an officer not below the rank of Division Chief) |
| 2 | A **show-cause notice** is issued; the intermediary replies within the time allowed (ordinarily **21 days**) |
| 3 | The Designated Authority holds an **inquiry** and gives an **opportunity of personal hearing** |
| 4 | The Authority submits a **report** to the **Designated Member** |
| 5 | A **post-inquiry show-cause notice** with the report is served; a further hearing follows |
| 6 | The Designated Member passes a **reasoned order** |

**Penalties that may be imposed (Reg 27):**
- **Warning** (censure)
- **Suspension** of the certificate of registration for a specified period
- **Cancellation** of the certificate of registration
- **Prohibition from taking any new assignment or contract or client** for a specified period
- **Debarring a branch or an office** from carrying on activities
- **Debarring a principal officer or employee** from association with any registered intermediary

**Summary proceedings** (a fast-track route with a shorter notice) may be used where, for example, the intermediary **admits the violation**, is **untraceable**, has **failed to file returns**, or has **failed to redress investor grievances**.

**Chapter V** lets SEBI act against **unregistered persons** carrying on an intermediary's activity — the hook used against unregistered "advisory" and "tip" services.

## 18.5 SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 — PIT

The 2015 Regulations replaced the 1992 version. They rest on one idea: **information asymmetry must not be monetised.**

### ⭐ The four definitions everything hangs on

**1. Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI) — Reg 2(1)(n)**

Information relating to a company or its securities, directly or indirectly, that is **not generally available**, which **upon becoming generally available is likely to materially affect the price** of the securities. It **ordinarily includes**:

| # | Category |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Financial results** |
| 2 | **Dividends** |
| 3 | **Change in capital structure** |
| 4 | **Mergers, de-mergers, acquisitions, delistings, disposals and expansion of business** and such other transactions |
| 5 | **Changes in key managerial personnel** |

The list is **inclusive, not exhaustive** — SEBI has progressively widened it to align with the "material events" of the LODR Regulations.

**2. Generally available information — Reg 2(1)(e).** Information that is **accessible to the public on a non-discriminatory basis.**

> 🧠 **The test is not "how many people know" — it is "could anyone have known equally".** Information given to 200 analysts on a private call is still **UPSI**; information in a stock-exchange filing readable by anyone is **generally available**, even if only three people bothered to read it.

**3. Insider — Reg 2(1)(g).** Any person who is:
- (i) a **connected person**; **OR**
- (ii) **in possession of, or having access to, UPSI**

> ⚠️ **Exam trap:** the two limbs are joined by **"or"**. You can be an insider **without any connection to the company at all** — overhearing a board discussion in a lift makes you an insider the moment you possess the UPSI.

**4. Connected person — Reg 2(1)(d).** Any person who **is, or has during the six months prior to the concerned act been**, associated with the company, directly or indirectly, **in any capacity** — by frequent communication with its officers, by a contractual, fiduciary or employment relationship, as a director, officer or employee, or by any professional or business relationship — **that allows, or is reasonably expected to allow, access to UPSI**.

**Deemed connected persons** (unless the contrary is established) include:
- An **immediate relative** of a connected person
- A **holding, associate or subsidiary company**
- An **intermediary** (which includes an **investment adviser**) or its employee or director
- An **investment company, trustee company or AMC**, or its employee or director
- An official of a **stock exchange, clearing house or clearing corporation**
- A member of the board of trustees of a **mutual fund** or the board of an AMC
- A director or employee of a **public financial institution**
- An official or employee of a **self-regulatory organisation**
- A **banker** of the company
- A concern, firm, trust, HUF, company or AOP in which a **director or his immediate relative, or a banker of the company, holds more than 10%** of the holding or interest

**Immediate relative — Reg 2(1)(f):** the **spouse**, and a **parent, sibling or child** of the person or of the spouse — who is either **financially dependent** on the person **or consults the person** in taking trading decisions.

> 🧠 **The immediate-relative test is behavioural, not merely biological.** A financially independent brother who never discusses investments is not an "immediate relative" for PIT purposes; a dependent parent is.

### Regulation 3 — communication and procurement of UPSI

- **No insider shall communicate, provide or allow access to UPSI** to any person, including other insiders, **except in furtherance of legitimate purposes, performance of duties or discharge of legal obligations**.
- **No person shall procure** UPSI from an insider except for those same reasons.
- The board must adopt a **policy defining "legitimate purposes"** as part of the Code of Fair Disclosure.
- **Structured Digital Database (SDD) — Reg 3(5):** the company must maintain a database recording the **nature of the UPSI** and the **names and PANs** of persons who **shared** and **received** it, with time stamps, **non-tamperable** and with audit trails.
- UPSI may be shared for a **due-diligence** exercise: where the deal will trigger an **open offer**, the board's informed opinion that it is in the company's best interests is enough; where it will **not** trigger an open offer, the information must additionally be **made generally available at least two trading days before** the transaction is effected. Recipients must sign **confidentiality and non-disclosure agreements**.

### Regulation 4 — trading when in possession of UPSI

**No insider shall trade in securities that are listed or proposed to be listed when in possession of UPSI.**

> ⚠️ **The reverse burden of proof.** Where an insider who traded **was in possession** of UPSI, the trades are **presumed to have been motivated** by that information. It is for the **insider** to prove innocence — not for SEBI to prove guilt.

**The permitted defences (proviso to Reg 4(1)):**

| # | Defence |
|---|---|
| 1 | An **off-market inter-se transfer between two insiders possessing the same UPSI**, both making a conscious and informed decision (reported to the company within **2 working days**) |
| 2 | A transaction through the **block deal window** between persons possessing the same UPSI |
| 3 | A transaction carried out **pursuant to a statutory or regulatory obligation** |
| 4 | Exercise of **stock options** where the exercise price was **pre-determined** |
| 5 | **Non-individual insiders:** the individuals possessing UPSI were **different** from those taking the trading decision, and adequate **"Chinese walls"** were in place |
| 6 | Trades pursuant to an approved **trading plan** under Regulation 5 |

### ⭐ Regulation 5 — the trading plan

A trading plan lets a permanently-informed person (a CFO, a promoter-director) trade at all without ever "choosing the moment". It is **presented to the compliance officer, approved and publicly disclosed**, and it must:

| Requirement | The classical rule |
|---|---|
| **Cool-off before trading begins** | Not earlier than **six months** after public disclosure of the plan |
| **Results black-out** | No trading between the **20th trading day before the end of any financial period** for which results are due and the **2nd trading day after** those results are disclosed |
| **Minimum coverage** | The plan must cover a period of **not less than twelve months** |
| **No overlap** | It must not overlap with any plan already in existence |
| **Certainty of terms** | It must set out the **value or number of securities**, the **nature of the trade**, and the **intervals or dates** |
| **No market abuse** | It must not entail trading for market abuse |

Once approved, the plan is **irrevocable** and **must be implemented without deviation**. The compliance officer **notifies the plan to the stock exchanges**. Trading-window closure does **not** apply to trades under an approved plan.

> ⚠️ SEBI has since **shortened the cool-off period** and relaxed some of the plan mechanics. For the NISM examination, answer with the classical rule set above unless the question quotes a different figure.

### Regulations 6 and 7 — disclosures

| Disclosure | Who | What | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial** | Promoter, promoter group member, KMP, director | Holding of securities on **appointment / on becoming a promoter** | Within **7 days** |
| **Continual** | Promoter, promoter group, **designated person**, director | Securities acquired or disposed of, where the **traded value exceeds ₹10 lakh** in one transaction or a **series over a calendar quarter** | To the **company within 2 trading days**; the **company to the exchange within 2 trading days** of receipt |

### Regulation 8 — Code of **Fair Disclosure** (Schedule A)

Every listed company's board must formulate and **publish on its website** a code of practices for fair disclosure of UPSI, embodying:
- **Prompt public disclosure** of UPSI that would impact price discovery
- **Uniform and universal dissemination** — no selective disclosure
- A **Chief Investor Relations Officer** to handle dissemination
- Prompt dissemination of UPSI **disclosed selectively, inadvertently or otherwise**
- **Fair response to queries and verification of market rumours**
- Ensuring information shared with **analysts and research personnel is not UPSI**
- Publishing **transcripts or records of analyst meets** on the website
- Handling all UPSI on a **need-to-know basis**

### ⭐ Regulation 9 — Code of **Conduct** (Schedules B and C)

- The board of every **listed company** (Schedule B) and of every **intermediary and fiduciary** (Schedule C) must adopt a code of conduct to **regulate, monitor and report trading by designated persons** and their immediate relatives.
- **Fiduciaries** expressly include **intermediaries, auditors, accountancy firms, law firms, analysts, consultants, insolvency professional entities and banks** assisting or advising listed companies. **An investment adviser is caught by both descriptions.**

**Minimum standards in the code:**

| Control | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Compliance officer** | Must be appointed; for intermediaries, reports to the board |
| **Trading window** | Closed when designated persons can reasonably be expected to possess UPSI; for **financial results** it is closed from the **end of every quarter until 48 hours after the results are declared** |
| **Pre-clearance** | Designated persons must obtain **pre-clearance** for trades above a set threshold; pre-cleared trades must be executed **within 7 trading days** |
| **Contra trade** | A designated person must **not execute a contra trade within six months** of a trade |
| **Breach of contra-trade rule** | Profits are **disgorged and remitted to SEBI** for credit to the **Investor Protection and Education Fund (IPEF)** |
| **Trading plan** | Trading-window restrictions **do not apply** to trades under an approved plan |

**Regulation 9A — institutional mechanism.** The CEO or MD must put in place **internal controls**, a **whistle-blower policy**, and, on any **leak of UPSI**, an **inquiry** and **intimation to SEBI**.

**Penalty:** **Section 15G** — minimum **₹10 lakh**, up to **₹25 crore or 3× the profits, whichever is higher**, plus possible prosecution under Section 24.

## 18.6 ⭐⭐ SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013

Notified **21 January 2013**, effective **21 April 2013**. Overhauled by the **SEBI (Investment Advisers) (Amendment) Regulations, 2020** (notified **3 July 2020**) and the SEBI circular of **23 September 2020** ("Guidelines for Investment Advisers"). **This section is the single densest source of marks in the whole syllabus.**

### 18.6.1 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Investment advice** — 2(1)(l) | Advice relating to **investing in, purchasing, selling or otherwise dealing in securities or investment products**, and advice on an **investment portfolio** containing securities or investment products, **whether written, oral or through any other means**, for the benefit of the client — and it **includes financial planning**. **Excluded:** advice given through **newspapers, magazines, or any electronic, broadcasting or telecommunication medium which is widely available to the public** |
| **Investment adviser** — 2(1)(m) | Any person who, **for consideration**, is engaged in the business of providing investment advice to clients or other persons or a group of persons, **including any person who holds himself out as an investment adviser**, by whatever name called |
| **Assets under advice (AUA)** | The **aggregate net asset value of securities and investment products for which the IA has rendered investment advice**, irrespective of whether implementation is done by the IA, by the client directly, or through others |
| **Person associated with investment advice** | Any member, partner, officer, director, employee or **sales staff** of the IA who is **engaged in providing investment advisory services** to clients |
| **Principal officer** | The MD, designated director, managing partner or executive chairman **responsible for the overall function of the business and affairs** of the IA |
| **Family of client** | The **individual client, dependent spouse, dependent children and dependent parents** |
| **Family of an individual IA** | The **individual IA, spouse, children and parents** |

> ⚠️ **Two words decide whether you need registration: "consideration" and "held out".** Free advice to a friend is not advisory business. Advice **for a fee**, or **calling yourself an adviser**, is. And note the **media exemption is only for genuinely mass, widely-available media** — a paid Telegram group or a subscribers-only WhatsApp channel is **not** "widely available to the public" and therefore **is** investment advice requiring registration.

### 18.6.2 Registration — Regulation 3

- **No person shall act as an investment adviser or hold himself out as one without a certificate of registration from SEBI.**
- Application in **Form A** of the First Schedule with the prescribed **application fee**; registration is granted in **Form B**.
- **Application fees:** approximately **₹5,000 for individuals and firms** and **₹25,000 for body corporates and LLPs**; a further **registration fee** is payable on grant (**₹10,000** for individuals and firms; **₹5,00,000** for body corporates).
- SEBI must be satisfied of the **fit and proper person** criteria of the Intermediaries Regulations.
- Registration is **permanent**, subject to payment of fees and continued compliance.
- **Banks, NBFCs and body corporates** offering distribution or execution services must provide advisory services through a **Separately Identifiable Department or Division (SIDD)**.
- **Corporatisation threshold:** an **individual** IA whose number of clients **exceeds 150** must apply for registration as a **non-individual** IA and must **stop onboarding fresh clients** as an individual until registered.
- **Change in control** of an IA requires **prior approval of SEBI**.

### 18.6.3 Exemption from registration — Regulation 4

The following need **no** IA registration:

| Exempt category | The limiting condition |
|---|---|
| **Anyone giving general comments in good faith** on market trends or the economy | Must **not specify any particular security or investment product** |
| **Insurance agents and insurance brokers** registered with IRDAI | Advice **solely on insurance products** |
| **Pension advisors** registered with PFRDA | Advice **solely on pension products** |
| **Mutual fund distributors** registered with AMFI or a SEBI-recognised SRO | Advice **incidental** to distribution of mutual funds |
| **Advocates, solicitors and law firms** | Advice **incidental to legal practice** |
| Members of **ICAI, ICSI, ICMAI, the Actuarial Society** or other specified professional bodies | Advice **incidental to professional service** |
| **Fund managers** of a mutual fund, AIF or other SEBI-registered entity | In that capacity |
| Persons advising **exclusively clients based outside India** | **But advising an NRI or PIO requires registration** |
| Any other person **specified by SEBI** | — |

> ⚠️ **The two classic traps.** (1) A mutual fund distributor's exemption evaporates the moment the advice stops being *incidental* — if he charges a **separate advisory fee** or advises across asset classes, he needs registration. (2) The **"exclusively outside India"** exemption **does not cover NRIs and PIOs** — advise one NRI and you need to register.

### 18.6.4 Qualification and certification — Regulation 7

Applies **at all times** to the **individual IA**, the **principal officer** of a non-individual IA, and **persons associated with investment advice**.

| Requirement | Individual IA / Principal Officer | Person associated with investment advice |
|---|---|---|
| **Qualification** | Professional qualification, or a **post-graduate degree or post-graduate diploma of at least two years' duration** in finance, accountancy, business management, commerce, economics, capital market, banking, insurance or actuarial science, from a recognised university or institution | **Same** |
| **Experience** | At least **5 years** in advice on financial products, securities, fund, asset or portfolio management | At least **2 years** |
| **Certification** | **NISM certification** (or one from another body, including **FPSB India** or a recognised stock exchange, **accredited by NISM**) — in practice **NISM Series X-A and X-B** | **Same** |

**Fresh certification must be obtained *before* the existing certificate expires** so that compliance is continuous. NISM certificates are valid for **3 years**.

> ⚠️ **SEBI has since been easing the entry norms** (qualification and experience). Answer the exam on the framework above unless the question itself quotes a different rule.

### 18.6.5 Conditions of certificate — Regulation 8

- **Abide** by the Act, regulations and SEBI directions
- **Forthwith inform SEBI in writing** of any material change in the information previously furnished
- **Pay the prescribed fees**
- **Redress client grievances within one month** of receipt
- **Maintain net worth at all times**
- Obtain **prior approval of SEBI for any change in control**
- The certificate is **permanent**, unless surrendered, suspended or cancelled

### 18.6.6 ⭐ Net worth

| Type of adviser | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Individuals and partnership firms** | **Net tangible assets of not less than ₹5 lakh** |
| **Non-individuals** (body corporates, LLPs) | **Net worth of not less than ₹50 lakh** |

**Net worth** = paid-up share capital **+** free reserves (excluding revaluation reserves) **−** accumulated losses **−** deferred expenditure not written off (including miscellaneous expenses not written off).

The 2020 amendment raised these from **₹1 lakh / ₹25 lakh**; existing advisers were given until **30 September 2021** to comply.

> 🧠 **Compare and never confuse:** IA individual **₹5 lakh** · IA non-individual **₹50 lakh** · Research Analyst individual ₹1 lakh · **Portfolio Manager ₹5 crore** · AMC ₹50 crore. Notice the pattern: the more of the client's *money* you touch, the higher the number. An IA only touches **advice** — hence the smallest figures.

> ⚠️ SEBI has been moving the IA framework from a **net-worth** test toward a **deposit-based** requirement. The examinable figures remain **₹5 lakh** and **₹50 lakh**.

### 18.6.7 ⭐ General obligations — Regulation 15

| Reg | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **15(1)** | **An investment adviser shall act in a fiduciary capacity towards its clients and shall disclose all conflicts of interest as and when they arise** |
| **15(2)** | **Shall not receive any consideration — by way of remuneration, compensation or in any other form — from any person other than the client being advised**, in respect of the underlying products or securities advised upon |
| **15(3)** | Any conflict between advisory activities and other activities must be **disclosed to the client** |
| **15(4)** | **Shall not divulge confidential client information** without prior client permission, except where required by law |
| **15(5)** | **Shall not transact on its own account contrary to the advice given to clients for 15 days** from the date of the advice. If within those 15 days the situation genuinely changes, the IA may transact **after giving the revised assessment to the client at least 24 hours in advance** |
| **15(6)** | **Shall follow the KYC procedure** specified by SEBI |
| **15(7)** | **Shall abide by the Code of Conduct** in the **Third Schedule** |
| **15(8)** | **Shall not knowingly sell securities to, or buy securities from, its own client on its own account** |
| **15(9)** | **Prior SEBI approval** for change in control |
| **15(10)** | Furnish information and reports to SEBI as specified |
| **15(11)** | Ensure **certification and qualification** compliance for itself and its representatives at all times |
| **15(12)** | **Shall not manage funds or securities on behalf of the client**; may receive only such monies as are necessary to discharge the client's **fee** liability |

> 🧠 **Regulation 15(1) is the whole profession in one line.** "**Fiduciary**" means the adviser must place the client's interest **above his own** — a higher standard than the "suitability" standard applicable to a distributor. Regulation 15(2) is what makes it real: **only the client may pay you.** Take a trail commission from an AMC on a scheme you advised, and Regulation 15(2) is breached no matter how good the advice was.

**Client agreement.** Since 2020 an IA must execute a **written agreement** with every client **before** providing any advice or charging any fee. It must set out the scope of services, the fee and the fee mode, the **declaration that the IA receives no consideration from any person other than the client**, risk factors, conflict-of-interest disclosure, termination terms and the grievance redressal mechanism.

**Display of details.** The IA must prominently display, on its website, mobile app, letterhead and all materials: its **name as registered**, **type of registration**, **SEBI registration number**, **validity**, **complete address with telephone numbers**, **contact details of the principal officer**, **BASL membership number** and the **address of the corresponding SEBI office**.

### 18.6.8 ⭐⭐ Fees — Regulation 15A and the 23 September 2020 circular

An IA may charge fees **only in one of two modes**, and only **one mode per client** at a time:

| Mode | Cap |
|---|---|
| **(A) Assets under Advice (AUA) mode** | **Maximum 2.5% of AUA per annum, per family of client**, across all services |
| **(B) Fixed fee mode** | **Maximum ₹1,25,000 per annum, per family of client**, across all services |

**The operating conditions:**

1. Fees are charged **per family of client**, not per individual — a family cannot be split to multiply the cap.
2. The IA must be able to **demonstrate the AUA with supporting documents** (demat statements, unit statements).
3. Any portion of the AUA held under a **pre-existing distribution arrangement** with any entity must be **deducted** from the AUA before computing the fee.
4. The **mode may be changed only after 12 months** from onboarding or from the last change of mode.
5. **Advance fees**, if agreed, **shall not exceed fees for two quarters**.
6. On **premature termination**, the client is **refunded the fee for the unexpired period**; the IA may retain a **breakage fee of not more than one quarter's fee**.
7. Fees must be received **through banking channels** — never in cash.

#### 🔑 Worked example — which mode may charge more?

Set the two caps equal:

> 2.5% × AUA = ₹1,25,000 → **AUA = ₹50,00,000**

**The break-even AUA is exactly ₹50 lakh.**

| Client's AUA | Max under **AUA mode** (2.5%) | Max under **fixed fee** | Which mode allows more? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹20,00,000 | 2.5% × 20,00,000 = **₹50,000** | **₹1,25,000** | **Fixed fee** |
| ₹50,00,000 | 2.5% × 50,00,000 = **₹1,25,000** | **₹1,25,000** | **Identical** |
| ₹1,20,00,000 | 2.5% × 1,20,00,000 = **₹3,00,000** | **₹1,25,000** | **AUA mode** |

**A second worked example — the advance and breakage limits.** Mrs Bhatia agrees to a **fixed fee of ₹1,00,000 per annum**.
- Quarterly fee = 1,00,000 ÷ 4 = **₹25,000**
- **Maximum advance** the IA may collect = **2 quarters** = 2 × 25,000 = **₹50,000**
- If she terminates after **5 months** having paid ₹50,000 for 6 months: the unexpired period is **1 month**, worth 1,00,000 × 1/12 = **₹8,333**, which must be refunded — but the IA may instead retain up to **one quarter's fee (₹25,000)** as breakage. Since the retainable breakage (₹25,000) exceeds the ₹8,333 still unearned, in practice the refund here is nil; had she terminated after **1 month**, ₹41,667 would be unearned and the IA could keep only ₹25,000 of it, refunding **₹16,667**.

> ⚠️ **Do not read the caps as entitlements.** They are **ceilings**. The Code of Conduct separately requires that fees be **"fair and reasonable"** — an adviser charging the full 2.5% for a single annual review would be breaching the Code even though the cap is not exceeded.

### 18.6.9 ⭐ Risk profiling — Regulation 16

The IA must:

**(a) Obtain from the client all information necessary to advise**, including:
- **Age**
- **Investment objectives**, including the **time for which the client wishes to stay invested** and the **purpose** of the investment
- **Income details**
- **Existing investments and assets**
- **Risk appetite and tolerance**
- **Liability and borrowing details**

**(b) Have a process for assessing the risk the client is *willing and able* to take**, including:
- Assessing the client's **capacity for absorbing loss**
- Identifying whether the client is **unwilling or unable to accept the risk of loss of capital**
- **Appropriately interpreting** the client's responses and **not attributing inappropriate weight** to particular answers

**(c)** Where a **tool** is used, ensure it is **fit for purpose** and that any **limitations are identified and mitigated**.

**(d)** Ensure questionnaires are **fair, clear and not misleading** — not vague, not using **double negatives** or complex language, and **not containing leading questions**.

**(e) Communicate the risk profile to the client** after the assessment is done.

**(f) Update** the client information and risk assessment **periodically** — a **review at least once a year** is the accepted standard.

> 🧠 **"Willing AND able" — the two-axis idea.** *Willingness* is psychological (risk **attitude**); *ability* is financial (risk **capacity**). A 32-year-old software engineer with a ₹4 crore net worth and no dependants may be **able** to take equity risk but **unwilling**. A retiree living off her corpus may be **willing** (she likes markets) but not **able**. **The lower of the two governs** — an adviser must never advise beyond the *ability*, whatever the willingness.

### 18.6.10 ⭐ Suitability — Regulation 17

The IA must ensure:

- **All investments advised are appropriate to the risk profile of the client**
- It has a **documented process for selecting investments** based on the client's investment objectives and financial situation
- It **understands the nature and risks** of the products or assets selected
- It has a **reasonable basis for believing** that a recommendation:
  1. **meets the client's investment objectives**;
  2. is such that the client is **able to bear the related investment risks** consistent with those objectives and risk tolerance; and
  3. is such that the client has the **necessary experience and knowledge to understand the risks** involved
- For a **complex financial product**, the recommendation must rest on a **reasonable assessment that the structure and risk-reward profile is consistent with the client's experience, knowledge, objectives, risk appetite and capacity for absorbing loss**

> ⚠️ **Risk profiling and suitability are two different obligations, tested separately.** Risk profiling is about **knowing the client** (Reg 16). Suitability is about **matching the product to what you learned** (Reg 17). You can do a perfect risk profile and still breach Regulation 17 by recommending an unsuitable product.

### 18.6.11 Disclosure to clients — Regulation 18

The IA must disclose:

1. To a **prospective** client, **all material information about itself** — its business, **disciplinary history**, terms and conditions of its advisory services, **affiliations with other intermediaries** and anything else the client needs to decide whether to engage it
2. Its own **holding or position in the financial products or securities that are the subject of the advice**
3. **Any actual or potential conflict of interest** arising from connection with or association with any issuer of products or securities — including anything that might **compromise its objectivity or independence**
4. While advising, **all material facts about the key features of the product**, particularly the **performance track record**
5. The **warnings and disclaimers** in the offer documents and advertising material of the product being recommended

### 18.6.12 ⭐ Maintenance of records — Regulation 19

Records to be maintained:

| # | Record |
|---|---|
| 1 | **KYC** records of the client |
| 2 | **Risk profiling and risk assessment** of the client |
| 3 | **Suitability assessment** of the advice provided |
| 4 | Copies of **agreements with clients** |
| 5 | **Investment advice provided, whether written or oral** |
| 6 | **Rationale for arriving at the investment advice, duly signed and dated** |
| 7 | A **register of clients** — date of advice, nature of advice, products/securities advised, and fee charged |

- Records may be kept in **physical or electronic form**; where a record must be signed and is kept electronically, it must be **digitally signed**.
- Records must be preserved for a **minimum of five years**. Where a **dispute is pending**, records must be retained **until the dispute is resolved**.
- **Annual compliance audit:** the IA must have its compliance with these regulations **audited annually by a member of ICAI or ICSI**. The audit must be **completed within six months of the end of the financial year** (i.e. by **30 September**), and **adverse findings with the action taken** must be submitted to SEBI within **one month of the audit report, and in any case by 31 October**.

> 🧠 **The one record advisers most often miss is #6 — the *rationale*, signed and dated.** SEBI's enforcement orders repeatedly turn on it: without a contemporaneous written rationale, the adviser cannot prove that the advice was ever suitable, and the burden is on him.

### 18.6.13 Compliance officer — Regulation 20

> ⚠️ **A *non-individual* investment adviser shall appoint a compliance officer.** An **individual** IA is **not** required to appoint one — he *is* the compliance function. This distinction is a favourite MCQ.

The compliance officer is responsible for **monitoring compliance** with the Act, the regulations, notifications, guidelines and instructions issued by SEBI.

### 18.6.14 Redressal of client grievances — Regulation 21

- The IA must **redress client grievances promptly** and have **adequate procedures for expeditious grievance redressal**.
- Grievances must be handled in a **time-bound** manner; a condition of registration is redressal **within one month** of receipt.
- The IA must **register on SEBI's SCORES** portal and provide clients with SCORES details, so a client may escalate.
- Disputes may be referred to **arbitration** or to the SEBI-administered **Online Dispute Resolution (ODR)** mechanism.

### 18.6.15 ⭐⭐ Segregation of advisory and distribution — Regulation 22

The heart of the 2020 reform. The problem it solves: an adviser who is also a distributor earns **twice on the same client** — a fee for the advice and a commission on the product — and the commission silently corrupts the advice.

| Type of IA | The rule |
|---|---|
| **Individual IA** | **Shall not provide distribution services at all** — and **the family of the individual IA shall not provide distribution services** to the IA's clients |
| **Non-individual IA** | Must maintain **client-level segregation at group level** — a client (and the client's **family**) may take **either advisory or distribution services from the group, never both**. Advisory must be provided through a **separately identifiable department or division (SIDD)**, at **arm's length** from distribution |

Additional points:
- The **family of a client is treated as one unit** — the group cannot advise the husband and distribute to the wife.
- Existing clients receiving both had to be given a **one-time choice** of one or the other.
- The compliance and monitoring process for segregation must be **documented**.

> 🧠 **The one-sentence rule:** *an individual adviser may never distribute; a corporate adviser may distribute, but never to its own advisory clients.*

### 18.6.16 Implementation of advice / execution services — Regulation 22A

An IA **may** provide implementation services to its advisory clients in the securities market, **subject to hard limits**:

- **No consideration** — no commission, referral fee, or any other benefit, **embedded, indirect or otherwise**, may be received **directly or indirectly at the IA's group or family level** for these services
- The **client is under no obligation** to avail implementation services from the IA
- Implementation must be through **direct schemes and direct plans** of products in the securities market
- The IA **shall not charge any separate implementation fee** from the client
- An **individual IA shall not provide execution services through its family**

> ⚠️ **"Implementation" is not "management".** Regulation 15(12) still forbids an IA from **managing funds or securities** on the client's behalf. The IA may help the client **push the button on a direct plan**; it may not take custody, discretion or a commission.

### 18.6.17 ⭐ Code of Conduct — Third Schedule

Nine headings — learn all nine:

| # | Heading | The obligation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Honesty and fairness** | Act **honestly, fairly and in the best interests of clients** and in the integrity of the market |
| 2 | **Diligence** | Act with **due skill, care and diligence**; offer advice only after **thorough analysis** and after considering **available alternatives** |
| 3 | **Capabilities** | Employ effectively the **resources and procedures** needed for the efficient performance of business |
| 4 | **Information about clients** | **Seek** information about the client's financial situation, investment experience and objectives — and keep it **confidential** |
| 5 | **Information to its clients** | Make **adequate disclosure of relevant material information** |
| 6 | **Fair and reasonable charges** | Ensure fees charged are **fair and reasonable** |
| 7 | **Conflicts of interest** | **Avoid** conflicts as far as possible; where unavoidable, **disclose** them and ensure clients are **fairly treated** |
| 8 | **Compliance** | Comply with all applicable regulatory requirements |
| 9 | **Responsibility of senior management** | The **senior management of a body corporate bears primary responsibility** for standards of conduct and proper procedures |

> 🧠 **Memory hook — "H D C I I F C C R":** *Honest Diligent Capable advisers Inform about clients, Inform clients, charge Fairly, manage Conflicts, Comply, and the Chief carries Responsibility.*

### 18.6.18 Procedure for action in case of default

An IA who contravenes the Act, the regulations or SEBI directions is dealt with **under Chapter V of the SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008** — the common enquiry procedure set out in 18.4 above: Designated Authority → show-cause notice → inquiry and hearing → report → post-inquiry notice → reasoned order.

**Possible outcomes:** warning; **suspension** of registration; **cancellation** of registration; **prohibition from onboarding new clients**; **debarment** of a branch, principal officer or employee; and in appropriate cases **refund of fees to clients** and **disgorgement** of unlawful gains.

### 18.6.19 ⭐ Penalty for default by an investment adviser

**SEBI Act, Section 15EB:**

> Where an **investment adviser or research analyst** fails to comply with the regulations made by the Board or directions issued by the Board, he shall be liable to a penalty of **not less than ₹1 lakh**, which may extend to **₹1 lakh for each day** during which the failure continues, subject to a **maximum of ₹1 crore**.

**Worked implication:** ₹1,00,00,000 ÷ ₹1,00,000 per day = **100 days**. A continuing default reaches the statutory ceiling in **100 days**; running longer adds nothing to the monetary penalty — but the **registration consequences** (suspension, cancellation) keep growing.

### 18.6.20 Administration and supervision of investment advisers

SEBI may recognise a body to administer and supervise IAs — an **Investment Adviser Administration and Supervision Body (IAASB)**.

- **BSE Administration and Supervision Limited (BASL)**, a wholly-owned subsidiary of **BSE**, was recognised by SEBI as the IAASB, operational from **1 July 2021**.
- **What BASL does:** grants **membership** to IAs (registration applications now route through it), conducts **supervision** including on-site and off-site inspection, handles **complaints and grievances**, takes **administrative action** and **monitors compliance**.
- Every registered IA — new and existing — must obtain and maintain **BASL membership** and display its **membership number**.
- SEBI has also prescribed an **advertisement code** for IAs, requiring prior approval of advertisements and prohibiting any claim of **assured or guaranteed returns**.

### 18.6.21 IFSC — Investment Advisers in an International Financial Services Centre

- An **IFSC** is set up under the **Special Economic Zones Act, 2005**; India's first is at **GIFT City, Gandhinagar**. For exchange-control purposes an IFSC unit is treated broadly as a **non-resident**, and transactions are conducted in **freely convertible foreign currency**.
- Under the IA Regulations, an **Indian recognised entity or an entity from a foreign jurisdiction** may set up in an IFSC as an **investment adviser** by forming a **company or a limited liability partnership**, obtaining registration and complying with these regulations.
- Since **1 October 2020**, the **IFSCA (International Financial Services Centres Authority)**, established under the **IFSCA Act, 2019**, is the **unified regulator** for all financial services in an IFSC — taking over the roles previously exercised there by **SEBI, RBI, IRDAI and PFRDA**.

## 18.7 ⭐ Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 — PMLA

Enacted in **2002**, brought into force on **1 July 2005**. Objective: **to prevent money laundering and to provide for confiscation of property derived from, or involved in, money laundering.**

### What money laundering is — Section 3

Whoever **directly or indirectly attempts to indulge, knowingly assists, knowingly is a party to, or is actually involved in any process or activity connected with the *proceeds of crime*** — including its **concealment, possession, acquisition or use** — and **projects or claims it as untainted property**, is guilty of money laundering.

### ⭐ The three stages

| Stage | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Placement** | Introducing the **cash** into the financial system | Depositing ₹9.5 lakh cash into several accounts on the same day |
| **2. Layering** | A **series of conversions and movements** to distance the funds from their source | Wiring through three shell companies in two jurisdictions, buying and selling securities |
| **3. Integration** | The funds **re-enter the legitimate economy** as apparently clean wealth | Buying a flat, "profits" from a business, an investment portfolio |

> 🧠 **P-L-I. Placement is where the launderer is most exposed** — cash must physically touch the system. That is precisely why the **Cash Transaction Report** and the **prohibition on third-party and cash payments** exist: they attack stage 1.

### Punishment — Section 4

**Rigorous imprisonment of not less than 3 years, extending to 7 years, plus a fine.** Where the proceeds of crime relate to an offence under the **NDPS Act**, imprisonment may extend to **10 years**.

### ⭐ Obligations of a reporting entity — Section 12

A **reporting entity** — banking companies, financial institutions, **intermediaries** (which includes an **investment adviser**) and persons carrying on a designated business or profession — must:

1. **Maintain a record of all transactions** in a manner enabling **reconstruction of individual transactions**
2. **Furnish information** on prescribed transactions to the **Director, FIU-IND** — **whether attempted or executed**
3. **Verify the identity of clients** (KYC)
4. **Identify the beneficial owner** of the client
5. **Maintain records of identity documents**, account files and business correspondence

**Retention periods:**

| Record | Retain for |
|---|---|
| **Transaction records** | **5 years from the date of the transaction** |
| **Identity / KYC records** | **5 years after the business relationship ends or the account is closed, whichever is later** |

**Governance:** every reporting entity must appoint a **Principal Officer** (responsible for furnishing information to FIU-IND) and a **Designated Director**, maintain a board-approved **AML/CFT policy**, run an **employee training programme**, and have the system **independently audited**.

### ⭐ The reports to FIU-IND

**FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit — India)** was set up in **2004** under the **Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance**. It is the **central national agency** for receiving, processing, analysing and disseminating information on suspect financial transactions.

| Report | Trigger | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **CTR** — Cash Transaction Report | **All cash transactions of more than ₹10 lakh**; and a **series of integrally connected cash transactions** within a month aggregating to more than ₹10 lakh | **15th day of the succeeding month** |
| **STR** — Suspicious Transaction Report | **Any suspicious transaction — no monetary threshold**, whether **attempted or executed** | **Within 7 working days** of arriving at the conclusion that the transaction is suspicious |
| **CCR** — Counterfeit Currency Report | Cash transactions where **forged or counterfeit currency** was used | **15th day of the succeeding month** |
| **NTR** — Non-Profit Organisation Report | **Receipts by an NPO of more than ₹10 lakh** | **15th day of the succeeding month** |
| **Cross-border wire transfer report** | Cross-border wire transfers of **more than ₹5 lakh**, where either origin or destination is in India | **15th day of the succeeding month** |

> ⚠️ **Two traps.** (1) An **STR has no threshold and no minimum amount** — a ₹40,000 transaction with an implausible explanation is reportable. (2) **The client must never be told (tipped off)** that an STR has been filed.

### Client Due Diligence and the beneficial owner

**CDD** must be carried out at the **commencement of an account-based relationship**, when carrying out an **international money transfer**, and **whenever there is doubt about the veracity or adequacy** of previously obtained identification data.

A **beneficial owner** is the **natural person who ultimately owns or controls a client** or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted. The PML Rules identify him by an **ownership or control threshold** — for a company or partnership, ownership of or entitlement to a prescribed percentage of the shares, capital or profits (originally **more than 25%**, **tightened to 10%** by a 2023 amendment); for a **trust**, the settlor, the trustees and beneficiaries with a specified interest. Where no such natural person is identified, the **senior managing official** is treated as the beneficial owner.

### Enforcement machinery

- The **Director (FIU-IND)** and the **Enforcement Directorate** may **provisionally attach** property involved in money laundering (attachment valid up to **180 days**), subject to confirmation by the **Adjudicating Authority**, followed by **confiscation**.
- Appeals lie to the **Appellate Tribunal** and then to the **High Court**.
- The Director may impose a **monetary penalty** on a reporting entity that fails in its Section 12 obligations, and issue directions.

## 18.8 Other laws an investment adviser must know

### 18.8.1 Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 — FEMA

FEMA **replaced FERA, 1973** with effect from **1 June 2000**.

**Objective:** *to consolidate and amend the law relating to foreign exchange with the objective of **facilitating external trade and payments** and for promoting the **orderly development and maintenance of the foreign exchange market** in India.*

| | **FERA 1973** | **FEMA 1999** |
|---|---|---|
| **Philosophy** | **Conservation** of scarce foreign exchange | **Facilitation** of trade and payments |
| **Nature of violation** | **Criminal offence** | **Civil offence** — and **compoundable** |
| **Burden of proof** | On the **accused** | On the **enforcement agency** |
| **Default position** | Everything prohibited unless permitted | **Current account transactions freely permitted** unless restricted |

- **Administered by the RBI**; **enforced by the Directorate of Enforcement**.
- **Current account transactions** are **freely permitted** unless prohibited or restricted. The Current Account Transaction Rules, 2000 divide them: **Schedule I — prohibited** (lottery winnings, income from racing, purchase of lottery tickets and banned publications, football pools); **Schedule II — require Central Government approval**; **Schedule III — require RBI approval beyond the specified limits**.
- **Capital account transactions** are **prohibited unless specifically permitted** by RBI regulations.
- **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS):** a **resident individual** may remit up to **USD 2,50,000 per financial year** for permissible current and capital account transactions — including **overseas securities, property and education**.
- **Person resident in India — Section 2(v):** broadly, a person **residing in India for more than 182 days during the preceding financial year**, subject to exceptions — a person who leaves or stays outside India for **employment, business, vocation or any purpose indicating an intention to stay outside India for an uncertain period** is **not** a resident.

> ⚠️ **FEMA residency is not income-tax residency.** They use different tests, different periods and different purposes. A person may be a **non-resident under FEMA** and a **resident under the Income-tax Act** in the same year. Advisers must apply **FEMA** for account type and repatriation, and the **Income-tax Act** for taxation.

- **NRI accounts:** **NRE** (rupee, **fully repatriable**, interest tax-free in India), **NRO** (rupee, for Indian-source income, repatriation capped at **USD 1 million per financial year**), **FCNR(B)** (foreign currency, fully repatriable). NRIs invest in listed equity through the **Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS)**.
- **Penalty — Section 13:** up to **three times the sum involved** where the amount is quantifiable, or up to **₹2 lakh** where it is not; plus up to **₹5,000 per day** for a continuing contravention. **Compounding** is available.
- **Appeals:** Special Director (Appeals) → **Appellate Tribunal** → **High Court**.

### 18.8.2 Indian Contract Act, 1872

Every adviser–client relationship, every Power of Attorney, every distribution arrangement is a **contract**.

- **Section 2(h):** a **contract is an agreement enforceable by law**. Agreement = **offer + acceptance**, supported by **consideration**.
- **⭐ Section 10 — essentials of a valid contract:** **free consent** of parties **competent to contract**, for a **lawful consideration**, with a **lawful object**, and **not expressly declared void**.
- **⭐ Section 11 — competency.** A person is competent if he is (i) **of the age of majority**, (ii) **of sound mind**, and (iii) **not disqualified** by any law (e.g. an undischarged insolvent, an alien enemy).

> ⚠️ **A contract with a minor is *void ab initio*** — void from the very beginning, not merely voidable *(Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose)*. That is precisely why a **minor invests through a guardian**, why a minor **cannot be a joint holder**, and why a minor's folio must be **regularised on attaining majority**.

- **Section 14 — free consent.** Consent is free when not caused by **coercion (s.15), undue influence (s.16), fraud (s.17), misrepresentation (s.18) or mistake (ss.20–22)**.
  - Coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation → the contract is **voidable at the option of the aggrieved party**
  - A **bilateral mistake of fact** essential to the agreement → the contract is **void**
- **Section 25 — an agreement without consideration is void**, subject to exceptions (a written and registered promise made out of **natural love and affection**; compensation for **past voluntary services**; a written promise to pay a **time-barred debt**).
- **Section 30 — wagering agreements are void.** (See 18.1: SCRA s.18A saves exchange-traded derivatives from this.)
- **Agency — Sections 182 onwards.** An **agent** is a person employed to do an act for another or to represent another in dealings with third persons; the **principal** is the person for whom the act is done. **The principal is bound by acts done by the agent within the scope of his authority.** This is the legal basis of a **Power of Attorney**, and of a distributor acting for an investor.
- **Remedies for breach — Section 73:** compensation for loss **naturally arising in the usual course of things** from the breach, or which the parties **knew when they made the contract** would be likely to result. **Section 74:** where a sum is named in the contract as payable on breach, reasonable compensation not exceeding that sum.

### 18.8.3 Guardians and Wards Act, 1890

A **secular, general law** governing the appointment of guardians of a minor's **person and property**, applicable irrespective of religion.

- A **minor** is a person who has not completed **18 years**. Where a **court appoints a guardian** of the person or property, majority is attained at **21 years**.
- **Three kinds of guardian:** the **natural guardian** (for a Hindu minor, the **father and after him the mother**; for an illegitimate child, the mother; a child below **5 years** is ordinarily in the mother's custody); the **testamentary guardian** (appointed by will); and the **guardian appointed or declared by a court** under this Act.
- The **District Court** has jurisdiction, and the **welfare of the minor is the paramount consideration**.
- A guardian must act **for the minor's benefit**, and **cannot sell, mortgage or transfer the minor's immovable property without the court's permission**.

**Practical operational rules an adviser must apply:**

| Situation | The rule |
|---|---|
| **Opening an account for a minor** | In the **minor's own name**, **represented by a guardian** (natural or court-appointed) — with proof of **date of birth** and proof of the **relationship** or the court order |
| **Joint holding** | **Not permitted** — a minor cannot be a joint holder |
| **Payments in** | Only from the **minor's own account**, the **guardian's account** or a **joint account of minor and guardian** |
| **Payments out** | Only to the **minor's own bank account** |
| **Minor attains majority** | The folio is **frozen for transactions** until it is regularised — fresh **KYC, signature attestation and bank details** in the (now major) investor's own name; the **guardian ceases** to operate the account |

### 18.8.4 Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

| Instrument | Section | Parties | Essence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Promissory note** | 4 | **2** — maker, payee | An unconditional **undertaking** to pay a certain sum |
| **Bill of exchange** | 5 | **3** — drawer, drawee, payee | An unconditional **order** to pay a certain sum |
| **Cheque** | 6 | **3** — drawer, drawee **bank**, payee | A bill of exchange **drawn on a specified banker**, **payable on demand**; includes a **truncated cheque** and a **cheque in electronic form** |

**Section 13:** a **negotiable instrument** means a **promissory note, bill of exchange or cheque** payable either **to order** or **to bearer**.

- **Validity of a cheque: 3 months** from the date of issue (reduced from six months by the RBI with effect from **1 April 2012**).
- **Crossing:**

| Type | Effect |
|---|---|
| **General crossing** | Two parallel transverse lines — must be **paid into a bank account**, not over the counter |
| **Special crossing** | The **name of a banker** appears — payable only through that banker |
| **"Account payee"** | A direction to the **collecting bank** to credit **only the named payee's account** |
| **"Not negotiable"** | The transferee **gets no better title than the transferor** |

- **Endorsement:** a **blank (general) endorsement** is a bare signature (the instrument becomes payable to bearer); a **full (special) endorsement** names the endorsee; a **restrictive endorsement** restricts further negotiation.
- **⭐ Holder in due course — Section 9:** a person who, **for consideration**, became the possessor of the instrument **before it became overdue** and **without notice of any defect** in the transferor's title. A holder in due course obtains a **better title than the transferor had** — the central privilege of negotiability.

**⭐ Section 138 — dishonour of a cheque for insufficiency of funds.** Punishable with imprisonment up to **two years**, or a fine up to **twice the amount of the cheque**, or both. Four conditions must be satisfied:

| # | Condition | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The cheque was presented **within its validity period** | **3 months** |
| 2 | The payee made a **written demand** on the drawer after receiving information of dishonour | Within **30 days** |
| 3 | The drawer **failed to pay** | Within **15 days** of receiving the notice |
| 4 | The payee **filed a complaint** | Within **1 month** of the cause of action arising (i.e. after the 15 days expire) |

> 🧠 **"3-30-15-1":** three months to present, thirty days to notice, fifteen days to pay, one month to file. Miss any one step and the Section 138 remedy is lost — though the civil claim for the debt survives.

Courts may also order **interim compensation of up to 20%** of the cheque amount to the complainant while the case runs.

### 18.8.5 Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 — IBC

A **consolidated law for the reorganisation and insolvency resolution of corporate persons, partnership firms and individuals in a time-bound manner** — for **maximisation of the value of assets**, to **promote entrepreneurship** and the **availability of credit**, and to **balance the interests of all stakeholders**.

**The four pillars:**

| Pillar | Role |
|---|---|
| **Insolvency Professionals (IPs)** and **Insolvency Professional Agencies** | Run the process; take over the management of the debtor |
| **Information Utilities (IUs)** | Store and authenticate **financial information and records of default** |
| **Adjudicating Authorities** | **NCLT** for **companies and LLPs**; **DRT** for **individuals and partnership firms**. Appeals: **NCLAT** → Supreme Court; **DRAT** → Supreme Court |
| **IBBI** | The **regulator**, established on **1 October 2016** |

**Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP):**

- Triggered by a **financial creditor**, an **operational creditor**, or the **corporate debtor itself**, on a **default of at least ₹1 crore** (the threshold was raised from ₹1 lakh to **₹1 crore** in **March 2020**).
- On admission the NCLT declares a **moratorium** — suits and proceedings are stayed, assets cannot be transferred, security interests cannot be enforced, and essential supplies cannot be cut.
- An **Interim Resolution Professional** takes over management and constitutes the **Committee of Creditors (CoC)** of **financial creditors**.
- A **resolution plan** requires approval of **66% of the voting share** of the CoC.
- **Timeline: 180 days**, extendable **once by 90 days** (270 days); with the 2019 amendment the **outer limit, including litigation, is 330 days**.
- If no plan is approved, the company goes into **liquidation**.

**⭐ Section 53 — the liquidation waterfall (the order that matters to an investor):**

| Rank | Claim |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Insolvency resolution process costs and liquidation costs** |
| **2** | **Workmen's dues for 24 months** + **secured creditors who relinquished security** — *pari passu* |
| **3** | **Wages and unpaid dues of other employees for 12 months** |
| **4** | **Financial debts owed to unsecured creditors** |
| **5** | **Government dues (2 years)** + **secured creditors' shortfall after enforcing security** — *pari passu* |
| **6** | **Any remaining debts and dues** |
| **7** | **Preference shareholders** |
| **8** | **Equity shareholders / partners** |

> 🧠 **The single most important investment lesson in this whole chapter.** **Equity ranks dead last.** When a company enters CIRP, the equity shareholder is behind resolution costs, workmen, secured creditors, employees, unsecured lenders, the government and preference shareholders. That is *why* equity carries the highest expected return — and why an adviser must never present a distressed company's shares as a "safe recovery bet". A **bondholder** in the same company sits at **rank 4 or 5**; the **shareholder** sits at **rank 8**.

The Code also provides a **pre-packaged insolvency resolution process (PPIRP)** for **MSMEs** and separate provisions for **personal guarantors** to corporate debtors.

### 18.8.6 FATCA and CRS

Both are frameworks for the **automatic exchange of financial account information** between tax authorities, to defeat offshore tax evasion. Every Indian mutual fund folio, demat account and bank account opened today requires a **FATCA/CRS self-certification**.

| | **FATCA** | **CRS** |
|---|---|---|
| **Full form** | **Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act** | **Common Reporting Standard** |
| **Origin** | A **United States** law, enacted **2010**, effective **1 July 2014** | Developed by the **OECD**, the global standard for **Automatic Exchange of Information (AEOI)** |
| **Nature** | **Unilateral** US law, implemented bilaterally through **Inter-Governmental Agreements (IGAs)** | **Multilateral** — implemented through the **Multilateral Competent Authority Agreement (MCAA)** |
| **Whose accounts are reported** | **US persons** only | Residents of **all participating jurisdictions** (100+) |
| **Sanction for non-compliance** | **30% withholding tax** on US-source payments to non-compliant institutions | Domestic penalties in each jurisdiction |
| **India's position** | Signed a **Model 1 IGA** with the USA in **2015** | Signed the **MCAA**; reporting began from **2017** |

**How it works in India.** Under the **Model 1 IGA**, Indian financial institutions report **not to the US IRS but to the Indian tax authorities (CBDT)**, which then exchange the data with the IRS. The obligation is enacted through **Section 285BA of the Income-tax Act, 1961** and **Rules 114F to 114H** of the Income-tax Rules, with reporting in **Form 61B**.

**Reporting Financial Institutions** in India include **banks, custodians, depositories, mutual funds, insurance companies and NBFCs**.

**What the client must give:** a **self-certification** of **country/countries of tax residence**, the **Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)** for each, **country and place of birth**, address and, for entities, the **controlling persons**. **Failure to provide a valid self-certification can result in the account being blocked for transactions**, and a **false self-certification attracts a penalty of ₹50,000** under the Income-tax Act.

> ⚠️ **Exam trap:** FATCA is **not** an Indian law and **not** administered by SEBI or RBI — it is a **US statute** that India implements through a treaty. CRS is **not** a law at all — it is an **OECD standard** given effect by domestic rules.

## 18.9 Violations by registered investment advisers — and their consequences

The SEBI orders against RIAs cluster into a small number of repeated failures. Learn the pattern, not the case names.

| Violation | The rule broken |
|---|---|
| **Providing advice without registration** — running paid "tip" groups, Telegram or WhatsApp channels | Reg 3, IA Regulations; s.12, SEBI Act |
| **Promising assured or guaranteed returns** ("₹5 lakh profit in 3 months") | Code of Conduct; **PFUTP Reg 4**; advertisement code |
| **Charging fees beyond the prescribed caps**, or in **cash**, or without an agreement | Reg 15A and the fee guidelines |
| **No risk profiling, no suitability assessment** — selling aggressive derivative strategies to retirees | **Reg 16 and Reg 17** |
| **Receiving commissions while charging advisory fees** ("double dipping") | **Reg 15(2)** and **Reg 22** |
| **Failing to segregate advice and distribution** | **Reg 22** |
| **No records, no signed rationale, no annual compliance audit** | **Reg 19** |
| **Not registered on SCORES; grievances unaddressed** | **Reg 21** |
| **Trading against the advice given, or front running** | **Reg 15(5)**; **PFUTP Reg 4(2)** |
| **Misleading performance claims** | Code of Conduct; PFUTP |

**Consequences, in escalating order:** **warning** → **monetary penalty** (Section **15EB**, up to **₹1 crore**; or **15HA**, up to **₹25 crore or 3× profits**, if it amounts to fraud) → **refund of fees to clients** and **disgorgement** of unlawful gains → **prohibition from onboarding new clients** → **suspension** of registration → **cancellation** of registration and **debarment from the securities market** → **prosecution** under Section 24 (imprisonment up to **10 years** and/or fine up to **₹25 crore**).

> 🧠 **The through-line of this whole chapter:** the law does not ask whether the adviser's *view* was right. It asks whether the adviser was **honest, unconflicted, documented and suitable**. An adviser whose recommendation loses money but who profiled, matched, disclosed and recorded is safe. An adviser whose recommendation makes money but who took a commission he did not disclose is not.
