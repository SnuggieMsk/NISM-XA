## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** Ravi runs a paid Telegram channel giving buy and sell calls on specific stocks to 400 subscribers who pay ₹999 a month. He claims the "media exemption". He is:
A) Correct — Telegram is electronic media  B) Wrong — the exemption covers only media widely available to the public, so he needs IA registration  C) Correct, because he is not registered  D) Exempt if he adds a disclaimer
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A **paid, subscribers-only** channel is not "widely available to the public"; he gives **advice on specific securities for consideration** and must register.
A) The medium is electronic but access is restricted and priced. C) Being unregistered is the offence, not the defence. D) A disclaimer cannot convert paid advice into exempt commentary.
</details>

**Q37.** An AMFI-registered mutual fund distributor begins charging clients a separate ₹20,000 annual "advisory fee" for asset allocation across equity, debt and gold. He:
A) Remains exempt  B) Needs IA registration, because the advice is no longer incidental to distribution  C) Only needs AMFI approval  D) Must surrender his ARN
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The distributor exemption survives only while the advice is **incidental** to distribution and unpaid separately. A **separate advisory fee** across asset classes is advisory business.
A) The exemption has been lost. C) AMFI cannot grant an advisory licence. D) Surrendering the ARN is not required — but he cannot advise and distribute to the same client.
</details>

A) The NRI clients defeat the exemption. C) RBI does not licence investment advisers. D) The currency of the fee is irrelevant.
</details>

**Q40.** An IA advises a client to buy a stock on Monday and sells the same stock from her own account on Wednesday. She:

A) No — an individual IA needs ₹50 lakh  B) Yes — an individual IA needs net tangible assets of ₹5 lakh and she has ₹6.20 lakh  C) No — she needs ₹5 crore  D) Yes — there is no net worth requirement for individuals
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — An **individual** IA must hold **net tangible assets of at least ₹5,00,000** at all times. Anjali holds **₹6,20,000**, a surplus of **₹1,20,000**.
A) ₹50 lakh is the **non-individual** requirement. C) ₹5 crore is the **portfolio manager's** net worth. D) A requirement certainly exists, and it must be met **at all times**, not just at registration.
</details>

A) ₹1,90,000  B) ₹1,50,000  C) ₹1,25,000  D) ₹4,50,000

**QC3.** In November 2026 Anjali finds the fixed fee mode simpler and wants to switch the Kulkarnis to it. She may:
A) Switch immediately  B) Switch only on or after 1 April 2027, since a mode change requires 12 months to elapse  C) Switch with SEBI approval  D) Switch if the client requests it in writing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The fee mode may be changed **only after 12 months** from onboarding or the last change of mode. The AUA mode was adopted on **1 April 2026**, so the earliest permissible change is **1 April 2027**.
A) November 2026 is only seven months in. C) SEBI approval is not the mechanism prescribed. D) Client consent does not shorten the regulatory waiting period.
</details>

**QC4.** May Anjali's spouse execute the Kulkarnis' purchases in regular plans?
A) Yes, with disclosure  B) No — the family of an individual IA may not provide distribution services to the IA's clients, and no consideration may accrue at family level  C) Yes, if the commission is donated  D) Yes, because the spouse is separately registered with AMFI
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — An **individual IA may not provide distribution services at all**, and the bar extends to the **family of the individual adviser** in respect of the adviser's clients. Regulation 22A separately requires that **no consideration accrue at group or family level** and that implementation be through **direct plans**.
A) Disclosure cannot cure a prohibition. C) Donating the commission does not remove the prohibited relationship. D) An AMFI registration permits distribution generally, not distribution to the adviser's own advisory clients.
</details>

**QC5.** Anjali's client count reaches 158 in January 2027, and she keeps onboarding new clients while charging ₹1,80,000 to one family under the AUA mode where the chargeable AUA is ₹50 lakh. What is her exposure?
A) None  B) Two breaches — failing to corporatise above 150 clients while continuing to onboard, and exceeding the 2.5% cap by ₹55,000 — exposing her to Section 15EB penalties of up to ₹1 crore and action under the Intermediaries Regulations  C) Only a warning is possible  D) Only the fee must be refunded
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Breach 1:** crossing **150 clients** obliges an individual IA to apply for **non-individual registration** and to **stop onboarding fresh clients** until it is granted; she did neither. **Breach 2:** on a chargeable AUA of ₹50,00,000 the cap is **2.5% × 50,00,000 = ₹1,25,000**, so charging **₹1,80,000** exceeds it by **₹55,000**.

**Exposure:** a penalty under **Section 15EB** of **not less than ₹1 lakh**, running at **₹1 lakh per day** while the default continues, subject to a **maximum of ₹1 crore**; plus proceedings under **Chapter V of the SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008** leading to a warning, **suspension** or **cancellation** of registration, and a direction to **refund the excess fee**.

A) Both obligations are mandatory and both were breached. C) A warning is one possible outcome among several, not the ceiling. D) Refund of the excess is likely, but it does not exhaust the consequences — the client-count breach is separate and continuing.
</details>
