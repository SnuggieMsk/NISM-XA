# Chapter 18: Key Regulations — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **This chapter is pure recall — the numbers, the deadlines and the section names are the marks.**

**Q1.** What is the purpose of the SCRA, 1956, and what does it exclude from "securities"?
> **A:** To **prevent undesirable transactions in securities by regulating the business of dealing therein** — it defines securities, provides for **recognition of stock exchanges** and governs **listing**. Securities **include** shares, debentures, **derivatives**, **mutual fund units**, **government securities** and security receipts; they **exclude ULIPs** (and fixed deposits, insurance, gold and property are not securities at all).

**Q2.** What is a spot delivery contract, and why does SCRA Section 18A matter?
> **A:** A **spot delivery contract** requires **actual delivery and payment on the same day or the next day**. **Section 18A** makes a derivative contract **legal and valid only if traded on a recognised stock exchange and settled on the clearing house** — without it, a derivative would be a **void wagering agreement** under Section 30 of the Indian Contract Act. That is why "dabba trading" is unenforceable.

**Q3.** State SEBI's three-fold mandate and the constitution of its Board.
> **A:** To **protect** the interests of investors, **promote** the development of the securities market, and **regulate** it — **protection first**. The Board has **nine members**: a **Chairman**, **two** from the Ministries dealing with finance and administration of the Companies Act, **one from the RBI**, and **five others** of whom at least **three are whole-time**.

**Q4.** Which SEBI Act sections give the power to issue directions, order an investigation, and require registration?
> **A:** **Section 11B** — **directions**. **Section 11C** — **investigation**. **Section 12** — no intermediary may deal in securities **without a certificate of registration**. **Section 12A** prohibits manipulative devices and insider trading.

**Q5.** State the SEBI Act penalties for insider trading and for fraudulent and unfair trade practices.
> **A:** **Insider trading (s.15G):** minimum **₹10 lakh**, up to **₹25 crore or three times the profits, whichever is higher**. **PFUTP (s.15HA):** minimum **₹5 lakh**, up to **₹25 crore or three times the profits, whichever is higher**.

**Q6.** ⭐ What is the penalty for a default by an investment adviser?
> **A:** **Section 15EB** — **not less than ₹1 lakh**, extending to **₹1 lakh for each day** the failure continues, subject to a **maximum of ₹1 crore**. The cap is therefore reached in exactly **100 days**.

**Q7.** What three factors fix the quantum of a SEBI penalty, and what are the appeal time limits?
> **A:** **Section 15J** — the **disproportionate gain or unfair advantage**, the **loss caused to investors**, and the **repetitive nature** of the default. Appeal to **SAT within 45 days**; then to the **Supreme Court within 60 days**, on a **question of law only**.

**Q8.** What is unusual about the PFUTP definition of "fraud"?
> **A:** It covers an act, expression, omission or concealment **whether or not committed in a deceitful manner**, and it applies **whether or not there is any wrongful gain or avoidance of any loss**. **You need not have profited for it to be fraud.**

**Q9.** Name six practices deemed manipulative or unfair under PFUTP Regulation 4, and define front running.
> **A:** **False or misleading appearance of trading; wash trades; circular trading; price rigging; pump-and-dump and planting false news; misleading advertisements; disseminating advice one does not believe to be true.** **Front running** is dealing **ahead of a substantial impending client transaction** of which one has unpublished knowledge.

**Q10.** What do the SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008 provide, and who is "not fit and proper"?
> **A:** A **common rulebook** for all intermediaries — **registration and the "fit and proper" test**, common **obligations and Code of Conduct**, a **common default procedure**, and powers over **unregistered persons**. A person is deemed **not fit and proper** if **convicted** of an offence involving moral turpitude, an economic offence or securities laws, an **undischarged insolvent**, of **unsound mind**, **debarred by a financial regulator**, a **wilful defaulter**, or a **fugitive economic offender**.

**Q11.** What actions can SEBI take against a defaulting intermediary?
> **A:** **Warning**; **suspension** of registration; **cancellation** of registration; **prohibition from taking new clients or assignments**; and **debarment** of a branch, principal officer or employee — following the Chapter IV/V procedure of show-cause notice, inquiry, personal hearing, report and reasoned order.

**Q12.** ⭐ Define "insider" and "connected person" under the PIT Regulations, 2015.
> **A:** An **insider** is any person who is **(i) a connected person OR (ii) in possession of or having access to UPSI** — the limbs are joined by **"or"**, so you can be an insider with **no connection at all**. A **connected person** is anyone who **is, or during the six months prior to the concerned act was**, associated with the company in any capacity that **allows or is reasonably expected to allow access to UPSI**.

**Q13.** ⭐ What is UPSI, and which five categories does the definition list?
> **A:** Information relating to a company or its securities that is **not generally available** and which, on becoming generally available, is **likely to materially affect the price**. It ordinarily includes **financial results; dividends; change in capital structure; mergers, de-mergers, acquisitions, delistings, disposals and expansion of business; and changes in key managerial personnel.** The list is **inclusive, not exhaustive**.

**Q14.** What makes information "generally available", and who is an "immediate relative"?
> **A:** Information is **generally available** if **accessible to the public on a non-discriminatory basis** — a private call with 200 analysts is still UPSI. An **immediate relative** is the **spouse**, and a **parent, sibling or child** of the person or spouse, who is **financially dependent** on him **or consults him** on trading decisions — a **behavioural, not merely biological, test**.

**Q15.** Why is the burden of proof reversed under PIT Regulation 4, and what defences exist?
> **A:** Trades by a person **in possession of UPSI** are **presumed to have been motivated by it** — the **insider must prove innocence**. Defences: an **off-market inter-se transfer between insiders holding the same UPSI**; a **block deal window** trade between such persons; a **statutory or regulatory obligation**; exercise of **stock options at a pre-determined price**; for **non-individuals**, different decision-makers behind adequate **Chinese walls**; and an approved **trading plan**.

**Q16.** ⭐ State the classical requirements of a trading plan under Regulation 5.
> **A:** Trading may not begin **earlier than six months** after public disclosure; **no trading** from the **20th trading day before the end of a financial period** for which results are due until the **2nd trading day after** their disclosure; the plan must cover **at least twelve months**; it must **not overlap** an existing plan; it must specify **value or number of securities, nature of trade and dates/intervals**; and no **market abuse**. Once approved it is **irrevocable** and must be implemented **without deviation**.

**Q17.** State the PIT disclosure, trading-window, pre-clearance and contra-trade rules.
> **A:** **Continual disclosure** where traded value **exceeds ₹10 lakh** in one transaction or a series over a **calendar quarter** — to the company within **2 trading days**, and by the company to the exchange within **2 trading days**. **Initial disclosure on appointment within 7 days.** The **trading window is closed from quarter-end until 48 hours after results**. **Pre-cleared trades must be executed within 7 trading days.** **No contra trade within six months** — profits are **disgorged to SEBI's Investor Protection and Education Fund**.

**Q18.** What is a Structured Digital Database?
> **A:** A **non-tamperable, audit-trailed database** the company must maintain recording the **nature of the UPSI** and the **names and PANs** of those who **shared** and **received** it, with time stamps.

**Q19.** ⭐ Define "investment advice" and "investment adviser" under the IA Regulations, 2013.
> **A:** **Investment advice** is advice relating to **investing in, purchasing, selling or otherwise dealing in securities or investment products**, and advice on an investment portfolio, **written, oral or by any other means** — and it **includes financial planning**; advice through **media widely available to the public** is excluded. An **investment adviser** is anyone who, **for consideration**, is in the business of providing investment advice, **including anyone who holds himself out as an adviser**.

**Q20.** Is a paid Telegram "tips" channel covered by the media exemption?
> **A:** **No.** The exemption covers only media **widely available to the public**. A **paid, subscribers-only** channel is advice for consideration; running it unregistered breaches **Regulation 3** and **Section 12** of the SEBI Act.

**Q21.** Name the categories exempt from IA registration, and state the two traps.
> **A:** Persons giving **general comments in good faith** naming no security; **IRDAI agents/brokers** (solely insurance); **PFRDA pension advisors** (solely pension); **AMFI-registered mutual fund distributors** (advice **incidental** to distribution); **advocates and law firms**; **members of ICAI/ICSI/ICMAI/Actuarial Society** (incidental to professional service); **fund managers** of mutual funds and AIFs; and persons advising **exclusively clients outside India**. **Traps:** a distributor's exemption dies the moment advice stops being **incidental**, and the "outside India" exemption **does not cover NRIs or PIOs**.

**Q22.** State the qualification, experience and certification requirements for an investment adviser.
> **A:** A professional qualification, or a **post-graduate degree/diploma of at least two years** in finance, accountancy, business management, commerce, economics, capital market, banking, insurance or actuarial science; **five years' experience** for an individual IA or principal officer and **two years** for persons associated with investment advice; and a **NISM-accredited certification**, with **fresh certification obtained before the existing one expires**.

**Q23.** ⭐ State the net worth requirements, and the corporatisation threshold.
> **A:** **Individuals and partnership firms — net tangible assets of at least ₹5 lakh.** **Non-individuals — net worth of at least ₹50 lakh.** (Raised from ₹1 lakh / ₹25 lakh in 2020.) An individual IA whose clients **exceed 150** must register as a **non-individual** IA and **stop onboarding fresh clients** until then.

**Q24.** ⭐ What do Regulations 15(1) and 15(2) require?
> **A:** **15(1):** the IA shall act in a **fiduciary capacity** and **disclose all conflicts of interest as and when they arise**. **15(2):** the IA shall **not receive any consideration — remuneration, compensation or in any other form — from any person other than the client being advised** in respect of the products advised upon. **Only the client may pay you.**

**Q25.** What is the 15-day rule, and may an adviser manage a client's money?
> **A:** **Regulation 15(5):** an IA shall **not transact on its own account contrary to advice given to clients for 15 days**; if the situation genuinely changes within that period it may transact only after giving the client the **revised assessment 24 hours in advance**. **Regulation 15(12):** an IA **shall not manage funds or securities** on the client's behalf, and may receive only sums needed to discharge the **fee** liability.

**Q26.** ⭐ State the two permitted fee modes and their caps.
> **A:** **(A) AUA mode — maximum 2.5% of Assets under Advice per annum per family of client** across all services. **(B) Fixed fee mode — maximum ₹1,25,000 per annum per family of client** across all services. **Only one mode per client** at a time.

**Q27.** At what AUA do the two caps coincide, and what follows?
> **A:** 2.5% × AUA = ₹1,25,000 → **AUA = ₹50 lakh**. **Below ₹50 lakh of AUA the fixed-fee mode permits a larger rupee fee; above ₹50 lakh the AUA mode does.**

**Q28.** State the fee-mode change, advance fee, breakage and AUA-computation rules.
> **A:** The **mode may be changed only after 12 months** from onboarding or the last change. **Advance fees may not exceed two quarters' fee.** On premature termination the client is **refunded the fee for the unexpired period**, the IA retaining **not more than one quarter's fee** as breakage. Any part of the AUA held under a **pre-existing distribution arrangement is deducted** before computing the fee. Fees must come **through banking channels**, never cash.

**Q29.** ⭐ What must an IA obtain and assess under risk profiling (Regulation 16)?
> **A:** **Age; investment objectives including tenure and purpose; income details; existing investments and assets; risk appetite/tolerance; and liability/borrowing details.** It must assess the risk the client is **willing *and* able** to take, including **capacity to absorb loss**; ensure any **tool is fit for purpose**; avoid **vague, double-negative or leading questions**; **communicate the risk profile to the client**; and **update it periodically**.

**Q30.** What is the difference between risk *willingness* and risk *ability*, and which governs?
> **A:** **Willingness** is psychological (attitude); **ability** is financial (capacity to absorb loss). **The lower of the two governs** — an adviser must never advise beyond the client's **ability**, however willing the client is.

**Q31.** ⭐ What does Regulation 17 (suitability) require?
> **A:** That **all advice is appropriate to the client's risk profile**; a **documented process** for selecting investments; that the IA **understands the nature and risks** of the products; and a **reasonable basis** for believing the recommendation **meets the client's objectives**, that the client can **bear the risks**, and that the client has the **experience and knowledge to understand them**. A **complex product** needs a specific assessment that its **structure and risk-reward profile** fits the client.

**Q32.** What must an IA disclose to clients under Regulation 18?
> **A:** All **material information about itself**, including its **disciplinary history**, terms of service and **affiliations with other intermediaries**; its **own holding or position** in the products advised upon; any **actual or potential conflict of interest**; the **material facts and performance track record** of the product; and the **warnings and disclaimers** in the product's documents.

**Q33.** ⭐ List the records an IA must maintain, and for how long.
> **A:** **KYC records; risk profiling and assessment; suitability assessment; client agreements; the advice given, written or oral; the rationale for the advice, duly signed and dated; and a register of clients** with dates, nature of advice, products and fees. Retained for a **minimum of five years**, and **until resolution** where a dispute is pending.

**Q34.** State the annual compliance audit requirement, and who must appoint a compliance officer.
> **A:** An annual audit of compliance by a **member of ICAI or ICSI**, **completed within six months of the financial year end (by 30 September)**, with **adverse findings and the action taken reported to SEBI within one month, and in any case by 31 October**. A **compliance officer is mandatory only for a non-individual IA** — an **individual IA need not appoint one**.

**Q35.** Within what time must an IA redress a grievance, and where can a client escalate?
> **A:** Within **one month** of receipt. The IA must be registered on **SCORES**; a client may escalate to **SEBI through SCORES**, and thereafter to **arbitration or the ODR mechanism**.

**Q36.** ⭐ State the segregation rule for an individual and for a non-individual investment adviser.
> **A:** An **individual IA shall not provide distribution services at all** (nor may the IA's family provide them to the IA's clients). A **non-individual IA** must maintain **client-level segregation at group level** — a client **and the client's family** may take **either advisory or distribution services from the group, never both** — with advisory delivered through a **separately identifiable department or division** at **arm's length**. In one line: **an individual adviser may never distribute; a corporate adviser may distribute, but never to its own advisory clients.**

**Q37.** On what conditions may an IA provide implementation services?
> **A:** **No consideration** of any kind — commission, referral fee, embedded or indirect — at the **IA's group or family level**; the **client is under no obligation** to use them; implementation must be through **direct plans/schemes**; the IA **charges no implementation fee**; and an **individual IA may not provide execution services through its family**.

**Q38.** Name the nine headings of the IA Code of Conduct, and the procedure for action in default.
> **A:** **Honesty and fairness; diligence; capabilities; information about clients; information to its clients; fair and reasonable charges; conflicts of interest; compliance; and responsibility of senior management.** Action in default follows **Chapter V of the SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008**.

**Q39.** What is BASL, and who regulates an investment adviser set up in an IFSC?
> **A:** **BSE Administration and Supervision Limited**, a BSE subsidiary recognised by SEBI as the **Investment Adviser Administration and Supervision Body (IAASB)**, operational from **1 July 2021** — it grants **membership**, conducts **supervision and inspection**, handles grievances and monitors compliance. In an **IFSC (GIFT City)** an entity may set up as a **company or LLP**, and since **1 October 2020** the **IFSCA** (under the IFSCA Act, 2019) is the **unified regulator** there.

**Q40.** ⭐ What are the three stages of money laundering, and the PMLA punishment?
> **A:** **Placement** (introducing cash into the financial system) → **Layering** (conversions and movements to distance the funds from their source) → **Integration** (funds re-enter the economy as apparently clean wealth). **Placement is where the launderer is most exposed.** Punishment: **rigorous imprisonment of 3 to 7 years plus fine** (up to **10 years** where the proceeds relate to an **NDPS Act** offence).

**Q41.** ⭐ State the obligations of a reporting entity, and the CTR and STR rules.
> **A:** **Maintain records of all transactions** so individual transactions can be reconstructed; **furnish information to FIU-IND**, whether the transaction was **attempted or executed**; **verify client identity**; **identify the beneficial owner**; and **retain identity records**. **CTR** — cash **above ₹10 lakh** (or an integrally connected monthly series above that) — by the **15th of the succeeding month**. **STR** — **no monetary threshold** — within **seven working days** of forming the suspicion, and **the client must never be tipped off**.

**Q42.** For how long must PMLA records be kept, and what is FIU-IND?
> **A:** **Transaction records — five years from the date of the transaction; identity records — five years after the relationship ends or the account is closed, whichever is later.** **FIU-IND** is the **Financial Intelligence Unit — India**, set up in **2004** under the **Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance**, the **central national agency** for receiving, analysing and disseminating information on suspect transactions.

**Q43.** ⭐ How does FEMA differ from FERA, and what is the LRS limit?
> **A:** FERA aimed at **conservation** of foreign exchange, treated violations as **criminal** and put the **burden of proof on the accused**. **FEMA** (in force **1 June 2000**) aims at **facilitating external trade and payments**, treats violations as **civil and compoundable**, and puts the burden on the **enforcement agency**. **Current account transactions are free unless restricted; capital account transactions are prohibited unless permitted.** The **LRS** limit is **USD 2,50,000 per financial year** per resident individual.

**Q44.** ⭐ State the essentials of a valid contract, and why a contract with a minor is void.
> **A:** **Section 10** — **free consent** of parties **competent to contract**, for a **lawful consideration**, with a **lawful object**, and **not expressly declared void**. A minor is **not competent** under Section 11, so the contract is **void ab initio** — hence a minor invests **through a guardian**, **cannot be a joint holder**, and the folio must be **regularised on attaining majority**. Coercion, undue influence, fraud and misrepresentation make a contract **voidable**; a **bilateral mistake of fact** makes it **void**.

**Q45.** ⭐ Give the Section 138 timeline, the IBC liquidation position of equity, and the FATCA/CRS distinction.
> **A:** **Section 138:** present within the cheque's **3-month** validity → notice within **30 days** → drawer fails to pay within **15 days** → complaint within **1 month**; punishment up to **2 years** or a fine up to **twice the cheque amount**. **IBC:** **equity shareholders rank last (8th)** in the Section 53 waterfall, behind even preference shareholders — CIRP is triggered by a **₹1 crore** default and runs **180 + 90 = 270 days** (outer limit **330**), with a plan needing **66%** of the CoC. **FATCA** is a **US law (2010)** covering **US persons**, implemented in India through a **Model 1 IGA**; **CRS** is an **OECD multilateral standard** for **automatic exchange of information** covering residents of **all participating jurisdictions**.
