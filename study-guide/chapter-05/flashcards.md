# Chapter 5: Introduction to Indian Financial Markets — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **The regulator map and the three MIIs are the highest-yield items here.**

**Q1.** Which four apex regulators cover which domains?
> **A:** **RBI → Rupees** (banking, monetary policy, money market, forex, NBFCs, payments). **SEBI → Securities**. **IRDAI → Insurance**. **PFRDA → Pensions**.

**Q2.** What is the maturity split between the money market and the capital market, and who regulates each?
> **A:** **Money market = up to 1 year, regulated by the RBI.** **Capital/securities market = beyond 1 year, regulated by SEBI.**

**Q3.** Distinguish the primary and secondary markets.
> **A:** **Primary** — securities are **issued**, and the funds go **to the issuer**. **Secondary** — investors trade **among themselves**; the issuer receives nothing. The secondary market's function is **liquidity and exit**.

**Q4.** When did SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA get their statutory status?
> **A:** **SEBI — 1992** (SEBI Act, 1992). **IRDAI — 1999** (IRDA Act, 1999). **PFRDA — 2013** (PFRDA Act, 2013).

**Q5.** What does the Ministry of Corporate Affairs regulate, and what does the Registrar of Companies do?
> **A:** The **MCA** administers the **Companies Act, 2013** and corporate governance. The **RoC**, which operates **under the MCA**, handles company registration/incorporation and statutory filings.

**Q6.** Name the three Market Infrastructure Institutions.
> **A:** **Stock/commodity exchanges**, **clearing corporations**, and **depositories**.

**Q7.** What is the crucial difference between an exchange and a clearing corporation?
> **A:** The **exchange matches trades** (trading platform and price discovery). The **clearing corporation clears, settles and guarantees** them, acting as the **central counterparty (CCP)** so neither side faces counterparty default.

**Q8.** Name India's two depositories, and what do they hold?
> **A:** **NSDL** and **CDSL** — they hold securities in **electronic (dematerialised)** form.

**Q9.** What is a Depository Participant?
> **A:** The **agent of the depository** — the investor's point of contact for opening and operating a **demat account**.

**Q10.** State the key differences between a bank and an NBFC.
> **A:** Banks can accept **demand deposits** and are part of the **payment and settlement system**; NBFCs **cannot** and are **not**. NBFCs also cannot issue cheques drawn on themselves, and their deposits are **not DICGC-insured**.

**Q11.** What is a payment bank allowed and not allowed to do?
> **A:** It may accept deposits **up to ₹2 lakh per customer** and provide payments and remittances. It **cannot lend** or issue credit cards.

**Q12.** What is a small finance bank?
> **A:** A full-service bank with a **priority-sector focus** — small borrowers, micro-enterprises and unbanked segments.

**Q13.** Who now regulates Housing Finance Companies?
> **A:** The **RBI** (supervision moved across from the National Housing Bank).

**Q14.** What is an NBFC-P2P, and what are its key restrictions?
> **A:** An RBI-registered platform that **matches individual lenders with borrowers**. It is only an intermediary — it **cannot lend from its own book and cannot guarantee returns**.

**Q15.** What is an Account Aggregator, and what does "data blind" mean?
> **A:** An RBI-regulated **NBFC-AA** that, with the customer's **explicit consent**, retrieves and shares their financial data across institutions. **Data blind** means the AA itself **cannot read or store** the data it moves.

**Q16.** Which Act defines "securities", and what does the definition include?
> **A:** The **Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 (SCRA)** — covering shares, scrips, stocks, bonds, debentures, derivatives, units of collective investment schemes, security receipts and government securities.

**Q17.** What is a KYC Registration Agency (KRA)?
> **A:** It maintains **centralised KYC records** so a client's KYC is **portable across SEBI-registered intermediaries** — no need to repeat KYC with each one.

**Q18.** What does a Registrar & Transfer Agent do?
> **A:** Maintains **investor records** and processes transactions and corporate actions on behalf of issuers and mutual funds — e.g. **CAMS** and **KFin**.

**Q19.** What does a custodian do?
> **A:** **Safe-keeps securities** and handles settlement and corporate actions, mainly for **institutional investors**.

**Q20.** What is an Authorised Person?
> **A:** A person acting **on behalf of a stock broker** to reach clients — the role that replaced the former "sub-broker" category.

**Q21.** What is a Self-Regulatory Organisation, and give examples.
> **A:** An industry body empowered to set and enforce standards for its **members**, under the apex regulator's oversight. Examples: **AMFI** (mutual fund distributors); **stock exchanges** as first-level regulators of their brokers.

**Q22.** Why do SROs exist?
> **A:** Closer **industry knowledge**, **faster rule-making**, and **lower regulatory cost** than doing everything through the apex regulator.

**Q23.** What is the core function of any financial market?
> **A:** To move funds efficiently from **surplus units (savers)** to **deficit units (borrowers/users)** through intermediaries.

**Q24.** Name the features of a well-functioning financial market.
> **A:** Efficient **transfer of funds**, **price discovery**, **liquidity**, **information dissemination**, **regulation**, and **risk management**.

**Q25.** Why do institutional investors improve market quality?
> **A:** They are **large and informed**, so they supply **liquidity and stability** and improve **price efficiency** — they can source and evaluate information better than retail investors.

**Q26.** Which markets sit under the RBI besides banking?
> **A:** The **money market** and the **foreign exchange market**, plus payment systems, NBFCs and credit information companies.

**Q27.** Name the credit rating agencies operating in India.
> **A:** **CRISIL, ICRA, CARE and India Ratings** — they assess the creditworthiness of debt issues and are **SEBI-regulated**.

**Q28.** What sits under PFRDA?
> **A:** The **National Pension System (NPS)**, **Atal Pension Yojana**, pension fund managers, the **Central Recordkeeping Agency (CRA)**, points of presence, and the NPS Trust.

**Q29.** What is the minimum investment for a Portfolio Management Service?
> **A:** **₹50 lakh**.

**Q30.** What does "financialisation of savings" mean in the Indian context?
> **A:** The gradual shift of household savings **away from physical assets** (gold, real estate) **toward financial assets** — increasingly via mutual funds and SIPs.

**Q31.** Which money market instruments should you be able to name?
> **A:** **Treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, call money and repo** — all with maturities of **up to one year**.
