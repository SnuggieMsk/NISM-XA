## 5.1 Overview of the Indian Economy

India has shifted from a primarily agrarian economy to one where **services and manufacturing** dominate output. A well-developed financial system supports that growth by:

- Channelling **household savings** into productive investment
- Providing **credit** at efficient cost
- Enabling **risk transfer** (insurance, derivatives)
- Supporting **price discovery** and liquidity
- Facilitating **payments and settlement**

**Key structural features:** a high household savings rate, historically heavy allocation to physical assets (gold, real estate) with a gradual shift toward financial assets ("financialisation of savings"), and increasing retail participation through mutual funds and SIPs.

## 5.2 The Indian Financial Markets

Financial markets exist to move funds from **surplus units** (savers) to **deficit units** (borrowers/users), through intermediaries.

### 5.2.1 Key features of a well-functioning market

| Feature | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Efficient transfer of funds** | Low cost of intermediation, so saving and borrowing are both attractive |
| **Price discovery** | Prices reflect all available information |
| **Liquidity** | Investors can exit and re-allocate |
| **Information dissemination** | All participants get relevant information |
| **Regulation** | Systems and processes that protect participants and maintain integrity |
| **Risk management** | Mechanisms to hedge and to contain systemic risk |

### The basic split

| | **Money Market** | **Capital / Securities Market** |
|---|---|---|
| **Maturity** | **Up to 1 year** (short-term) | **Beyond 1 year** (long-term) |
| **Regulator** | **RBI** | **SEBI** |
| **Instruments** | T-bills, CPs, CDs, call money, repo | Shares, bonds, debentures, derivatives |
| **Participants** | Banks, corporates, MFs, RBI | Companies, investors, MFs, FPIs |

And a second split within the capital market:

- **Primary market** — securities are **issued** by the borrower to investors; funds go **to the issuer**. (IPO, rights, private placement.)
- **Secondary market** — investors trade **among themselves**; the issuer receives nothing. Provides **liquidity and exit**.

## 5.3 ⭐ Regulators of Financial Markets

**Learn this table cold — it is the single most examinable item in the chapter.**

| Regulator | Regulates | Established / Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ministry of Finance (MoF)** | Overall economic and fiscal policy; the Budget; supervises the regulators | Government of India |
| **Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA)** | Companies Act; corporate governance; incorporation | Administers the **Companies Act, 2013** |
| **Registrar of Companies (RoC)** | Registration/incorporation of companies; statutory filings | Operates **under the MCA** |
| **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** | Banking, monetary policy, currency, **money market**, **foreign exchange**, payment systems, NBFCs, credit information companies | Est. **1935**; administers **FEMA** |
| **SEBI** | **Securities markets** — stock exchanges, brokers, mutual funds, portfolio managers, investment advisers, AIFs, credit rating agencies, depositories | Statutory status **1992** (SEBI Act, 1992) |
| **IRDAI** | **Insurance** — life and general insurers, agents, brokers | Est. **1999** (IRDA Act, 1999) |
| **PFRDA** | **Pensions** — NPS, Atal Pension Yojana, pension funds | Statutory status **2013** (PFRDA Act, 2013) |
| **SROs** | First-level regulation of their members, under the apex regulator | e.g. **AMFI** (mutual fund distributors), **ANMI**, exchanges |

> 🧠 **Memory hook — the four apex regulators by asset:**
> **R**BI → **R**upees (banking, money, forex) · **S**EBI → **S**ecurities · **I**RDAI → **I**nsurance · **P**FRDA → **P**ensions.

### 5.3.8 Self-Regulatory Organisations (SROs)
An SRO is an industry body granted authority to set and enforce standards for its members, **subject to the apex regulator's oversight**. Benefits: closer industry knowledge, faster rule-making, lower regulatory cost. Example: **AMFI** for mutual fund distributors; stock exchanges act as first-level regulators of their brokers.

## 5.4 Structure of Financial Markets in India

### 5.4.1 The Banking Sector (RBI's domain)

| Institution | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Commercial banks** | Full-service: accept demand deposits, lend, payments |
| **Payment banks** | Accept deposits **up to ₹2 lakh** per customer; provide payments and remittances; **cannot lend** or issue credit cards |
| **Small finance banks** | Full banking, but with priority-sector focus — small borrowers, micro-enterprises, unbanked segments |
| **NBFCs** | Lend and invest, but **cannot accept demand deposits** and are **not part of the payment system** |
| **Housing Finance Companies (HFCs)** | Specialised housing lenders; now regulated by the **RBI** (supervision moved from the NHB) |
| **P2P lending platforms** | **NBFC-P2P**, RBI-registered; match individual lenders with borrowers. The platform only intermediates — **it cannot lend on its own book or guarantee returns** |
| **Credit Information Companies** | CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark — maintain credit histories |
| **Account Aggregators (AA)** | RBI-regulated **NBFC-AA**: with the customer's **explicit consent**, they retrieve and share the customer's financial data across institutions. They are **data blind** — they cannot read or store the data |

> ⚠️ **Bank vs NBFC — a guaranteed question.** Banks can accept **demand deposits** and are part of the **payment/settlement system**; NBFCs cannot and are not. NBFCs also cannot issue cheques drawn on themselves, and their deposits are **not covered by DICGC insurance**.

**Money market** and the **foreign exchange market** both sit under the **RBI**.

### 5.4.2 Securities Markets (SEBI's domain)

**Definition of "securities"** — under the **Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 (SCRA)**, securities include shares, scrips, stocks, bonds, debentures, debenture stock, derivatives, units of collective investment schemes, security receipts, government securities and rights/interests in securities.

| Segment | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Stock markets** | Equity issuance and trading — NSE, BSE |
| **Bond markets** | Government securities and corporate debt |
| **Derivatives market** | Futures and options on equity, currency, commodities |
| **Mutual funds** | Pooled, professionally managed, SEBI-regulated |
| **Portfolio managers** | Individual portfolios for larger investors (min. **₹50 lakh**) |
| **Investment advisers** | Registered under **SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013** |
| **Alternative Investment Funds** | Categories I, II and III (Chapter 13) |
| **Credit rating agencies** | Assess creditworthiness of debt issues — CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings |

### 5.4.3 Under IRDAI
Life insurers, general (non-life) insurers, health insurers, reinsurers, plus intermediaries (agents, brokers, third-party administrators).

### 5.4.4 Under PFRDA
The **National Pension System (NPS)**, **Atal Pension Yojana**, pension fund managers, the **Central Recordkeeping Agency (CRA)**, points of presence, and the NPS Trust.

## 5.5 ⭐ Participants in the Indian Financial Markets

### Market Infrastructure Institutions (MIIs)

The three "MIIs" — systemically important, so most heavily regulated:

| MII | Role |
|---|---|
| **Stock / commodity exchanges** | Provide the trading platform, price discovery, and first-level regulation of members |
| **Clearing corporations** | **Clear and settle** trades; act as the **central counterparty (CCP)** — guaranteeing settlement so no buyer or seller faces counterparty default. Manage margins and the settlement guarantee fund |
| **Depositories** | Hold securities in **electronic (demat)** form — **NSDL** and **CDSL** in India |

> ⚠️ **Exchange vs clearing corporation:** the **exchange matches trades**; the **clearing corporation guarantees and settles** them. Do not confuse them.

### Other participants

| Participant | Role |
|---|---|
| **Depository Participants (DPs)** | The **agents of the depository** — the investor's point of contact for a demat account |
| **Custodians** | Safe-keep securities and handle settlement/corporate actions for institutional investors |
| **Stock brokers / Authorised Persons** | Execute trades on behalf of clients. An **Authorised Person** acts on behalf of a broker (formerly "sub-broker") |
| **Investment banks / merchant bankers** | Manage issues, underwrite, advise on M&A |
| **Insurance companies** | Provide risk cover; large institutional investors |
| **Pension funds** | Manage retirement money; long-horizon institutional investors |
| **AMCs and portfolio managers** | Manage pooled (MF) or individual (PMS) portfolios |
| **Investment advisers and distributors** | Advise on / distribute financial products |
| **Credit rating agencies** | Rate debt instruments for credit risk |
| **KYC Registration Agencies (KRAs)** | Maintain centralised KYC records so a client's KYC is portable across SEBI intermediaries |
| **Registrar & Transfer Agents (RTAs)** | Maintain investor records, process transactions and corporate actions — CAMS, KFin |
| **NBFCs and HFCs** | Lend outside the banking system |
| **Credit bureaus (CICs)** | Maintain individual credit histories and scores |

> 🧠 **Why institutions matter:** mutual funds, insurers and pension funds are large, informed investors. They supply **liquidity and stability**, and improve **price efficiency** because they can source and evaluate information better than retail investors.
