## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** A derivative is:
A) A share issued by a company  B) A contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset  C) A type of bank deposit  D) A government bond
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A derivative has **no independent value**; its worth depends entirely on the underlying.
A), C) and D) are all primary instruments with intrinsic value of their own.
</details>

**Q2.** An option BUYER has:
A) An obligation but no right  B) A right but no obligation  C) Both a right and an obligation  D) Neither
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The buyer pays a premium for the **right to choose** whether to exercise.
A) Describes the **seller/writer**. C) and D) misstate the asymmetry that defines options.
</details>

**Q3.** An option SELLER (writer) has:
A) A right but no obligation  B) An obligation but no right  C) No exposure  D) Unlimited rights
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The writer receives the premium and **must perform** if the buyer exercises.
A) Describes the buyer. C) The writer's exposure can be unlimited. D) The writer has no rights at all.
</details>

**Q4.** A call option gives the buyer the right to:
A) Sell the underlying  B) Buy the underlying  C) Borrow the underlying  D) Cancel the contract
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Call = right to BUY**, taken by a **bullish** investor.
A) Describes a put. C) and D) are not option rights.
</details>

**Q5.** A put option buyer is:
A) Bullish  B) Bearish  C) Neutral  D) Arbitraging
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A put gives the **right to SELL**, which gains value as the price falls.
A) Describes a call buyer. C) and D) describe different motives.
</details>

**Q6.** The maximum loss for a call option BUYER is:
A) Unlimited  B) The premium paid  C) The strike price  D) Zero
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The worst case is that the option expires worthless and the **premium** is lost.
A) Describes the **short call**. C) and D) are incorrect.
</details>

**Q7.** The maximum loss for a call option SELLER is:
A) The premium received  B) Unlimited  C) Zero  D) The strike price
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Because the underlying can rise without limit, a **naked short call has theoretically unlimited loss** — the riskiest basic position.
A) Is the maximum **gain**. C) and D) are incorrect.
</details>

**Q8.** The break-even for a long call is:
A) Strike − Premium  B) Strike + Premium  C) The premium alone  D) The strike alone
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The buyer must recover the premium before profiting, so break-even = **Strike + Premium**.
A) Is the **long put** break-even. C) and D) ignore one of the two components.
</details>

**Q9.** The break-even for a long put is:
A) Strike + Premium  B) Strike − Premium  C) Premium ÷ Strike  D) Zero
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The price must fall below the strike by at least the premium: **Strike − Premium**.
A) Is the long call break-even. C) and D) are meaningless here.
</details>

**Q10.** The intrinsic value of a call option is:
A) max(Strike − Spot, 0)  B) max(Spot − Strike, 0)  C) Spot − Strike, which can be negative  D) The premium
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **max(Spot − Strike, 0)** — intrinsic value can never be negative, because the buyer would simply not exercise.
A) Is the **put** formula. C) Omits the floor at zero. D) Premium = intrinsic + time value.
</details>

**Q11.** A call option is in the money when:
A) Spot < Strike  B) Spot > Strike  C) Spot = Strike  D) The premium is high
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A call to buy below the market price has value: **Spot > Strike**.
A) Is out of the money for a call (and ITM for a put). C) Is at the money. D) Premium level does not define moneyness.
</details>

**Q12.** A put option is in the money when:
A) Spot > Strike  B) Spot < Strike  C) Spot = Strike  D) Always
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A right to sell above the market price has value: **Spot < Strike**.
A) Is OTM for a put. C) Is ATM. D) Options are frequently OTM.
</details>

**Q13.** Time value of an option:
A) Increases as expiry approaches  B) Decays to zero at expiry  C) Is always zero  D) Equals the strike
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — At expiry the premium equals **intrinsic value only** — all time value has decayed.
A) Reverses the effect. C) Time value is positive before expiry. D) Unrelated.
</details>

**Q14.** European options can be exercised:
A) Any time before expiry  B) Only at expiry  C) Only in Europe  D) Never
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Only at expiry** — and this is the Indian standard for index and stock options.
A) Describes an **American** option. C) The name is conventional, not geographic. D) They can certainly be exercised.
</details>

**Q15.** Which is exchange-traded and standardised?
A) A forward  B) A future  C) A swap  D) An OTC option
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Futures** are standardised and exchange-traded with a CCP.
A), C) and D) are all OTC instruments.
</details>

**Q16.** Counterparty risk in a futures contract is:
A) High  B) Eliminated by the clearing corporation acting as central counterparty  C) Borne by SEBI  D) Unregulated
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The **CCP** guarantees settlement, backed by margins and a guarantee fund.
A) Describes a **forward**. C) SEBI regulates but is not a counterparty. D) Futures are fully regulated.
</details>

**Q17.** Mark to market means:
A) Settlement only at maturity  B) Daily settlement of gains and losses to margin accounts  C) Marketing the contract  D) Setting the strike price
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Daily MTM** is a defining feature of exchange-traded futures.
A) Describes a forward. C) and D) are unrelated.
</details>

**Q18.** Hedging means:
A) Taking risk to profit  B) Reducing or eliminating an existing risk  C) Risk-free arbitrage  D) Borrowing money
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — A hedger **already has an exposure** and uses a derivative to offset it.
A) Is speculation. C) Is arbitrage. D) Is unrelated.
</details>

**Q19.** Speculation in derivatives involves:
A) Offsetting an existing exposure  B) Taking on risk to profit from an expected move, with no underlying exposure  C) Risk-free profit  D) Insurance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The speculator has **no position to protect** — the derivative is the position.
A) Is hedging. C) Is arbitrage. D) A hedge, not speculation, resembles insurance.

**Q20.** Arbitrage means:
A) Taking directional risk  B) Simultaneously buying and selling in two markets to lock in a risk-free profit  C) Hedging an exposure  D) Writing options
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Arbitrage exploits a **price difference** with no net risk.
A) Is speculation. C) Is hedging. D) Is a specific option position, not a strategy category.
</details>

**Q21.** A swap is an agreement to:
A) Exchange cash flows on specified dates  B) Buy a share  C) Sell a bond  D) Borrow from a bank
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Most commonly an **interest rate swap** exchanging fixed for floating.
B), C) and D) are not swaps.
</details>

**Q22.** In an interest rate swap, typically:
A) Both parties pay fixed  B) One party pays fixed and receives floating, the other the reverse  C) Both pay floating  D) No payments occur

**Q23.** Contango describes a market where:
A) Futures price is below spot  B) Futures price is above spot  C) They are equal  D) No futures exist

**Q24.** At expiry, the futures price:
A) Diverges from spot  B) Converges to the spot price  C) Doubles  D) Becomes zero

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** An investor buys a call with strike ₹800 for a premium of ₹25. What is the break-even?
A) ₹775  B) ₹825  C) ₹800  D) ₹25
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Break-even = Strike + Premium = 800 + 25 = **₹825**.
A) Is the **put** break-even formula. C) Ignores the premium. D) Is the premium alone.
</details>

**Q37.** Using Q36's option, what is the profit if the spot at expiry is ₹870?
A) ₹70  B) ₹45  C) ₹95  D) −₹25
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Intrinsic = 870 − 800 = **₹70**. Profit = 70 − 25 = **₹45**.
A) Is the intrinsic value, ignoring the premium paid. C) Adds the premium instead of subtracting. D) Is the loss if the option expires worthless.
</details>

**Q38.** Using Q36's option, what is the outcome if the spot at expiry is ₹790?
A) Profit ₹10  B) Loss of the full ₹25 premium — the option expires worthless  C) Loss ₹10  D) Break-even
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — At ₹790 the call is **out of the money** (spot < strike), so intrinsic = 0 and the entire **₹25 premium** is lost.
A) There is no profit. C) The loss is the full premium, not ₹10. D) Break-even is ₹825.
</details>

**Q39.** An investor buys a put with strike ₹500 for ₹18. What is the break-even and the profit at a spot of ₹450?
A) Break-even ₹518; profit ₹32  B) Break-even ₹482; profit ₹32  C) Break-even ₹482; profit ₹50  D) Break-even ₹500; profit ₹18
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Break-even = Strike − Premium = 500 − 18 = **₹482**. At ₹450, intrinsic = 500 − 450 = **₹50**; profit = 50 − 18 = **₹32**.
A) Uses the call break-even formula. C) Gives intrinsic value, not profit. D) Ignores the premium in both.
</details>

**Q40.** A call with strike ₹1,200 has a premium of ₹65 when spot is ₹1,230. What are its intrinsic and time values?
A) Intrinsic ₹30; time value ₹35  B) Intrinsic ₹65; time value ₹0  C) Intrinsic ₹35; time value ₹30  D) Intrinsic ₹0; time value ₹65
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Intrinsic = max(1,230 − 1,200, 0) = **₹30**. Time value = premium − intrinsic = 65 − 30 = **₹35**.
B) Would apply only at expiry. C) Swaps the two. D) The option is ITM, so intrinsic is not zero.
</details>

**Q41.** An investor writes (sells) a call with strike ₹950 for ₹30. The spot at expiry is ₹1,040. What is her result?
A) Profit ₹30  B) Loss ₹60  C) Loss ₹90  D) Profit ₹90
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The buyer exercises: the writer pays intrinsic = 1,040 − 950 = **₹90**, but keeps the **₹30 premium**. Net = 30 − 90 = **−₹60**.
A) Would apply only if the option expired worthless. C) Forgets the premium received. D) Gets the direction wrong — the writer **loses** when the price rises above the strike.
</details>

**Q42.** A farmer expecting to harvest wheat in 3 months wants to fix his selling price. He should:

A) Buying futures would **double** his exposure to falling prices. C) A call profits from rising prices — the wrong direction for his risk. D) Leaves him fully exposed.
</details>

**Q43.** An importer must pay USD 1,00,000 in 3 months and fears the rupee weakening. He should:

**Q44.** Which position carries theoretically unlimited loss?
A) Long call  B) Long put  C) Naked short call  D) Buying a stock
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — The underlying can rise without limit, so the writer's obligation is unbounded.
A) and B) cap the loss at the premium. D) A stock's loss is capped at the amount invested (price can fall only to zero).
</details>

**Q45.** An investor holds shares worth ₹10,00,000 and fears a market fall but does not want to sell. The appropriate strategy is:

</details>

**Q46.** Spot is ₹500, the risk-free rate 8% and there are 6 months to expiry with no dividend. What is the approximate fair futures price?
A) ₹480  B) ₹520  C) ₹540  D) ₹500
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — F = S × (1 + r)^T = 500 × (1.08)^0.5 = 500 × **1.03923** = **₹519.62 ≈ ₹520**.
A) Would imply backwardation with no reason. C) Applies a full year's interest at double the rate. D) Ignores the cost of carry. **Futures normally trade above spot — contango.**
</details>

**Q47.** A trader observes futures trading well above their fair cost-of-carry value. The arbitrage is to:

**Correct: B)** — Sell the **overpriced** leg (futures) and buy the **underpriced** leg (spot), locking in the difference risk-free.

</details>

**QC4.** The stock falls to ₹880 at expiry. Compare the outcomes of all three options.
A) All three are identical  B) Protective put ≈ ₹37,28,000; covered call ≈ ₹36,08,000; do nothing = ₹35,20,000  C) The covered call is best  D) Doing nothing is best
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Do nothing:** 880 × 4,000 = **₹35,20,000**. **Protective put:** the put is ITM by 960 − 880 = ₹80, worth ₹3,20,000; so 35,20,000 + 3,20,000 − 1,12,000 premium = **₹37,28,000**. **Covered call:** the call expires worthless (880 < 1,060), so she keeps the ₹88,000 premium: 35,20,000 + 88,000 = **₹36,08,000**.
A) They differ by over ₹2 lakh. C) The covered call is better than nothing but ₹1.2 lakh worse than the put. D) Doing nothing is the worst outcome in a falling market. **The protective put wins precisely because that is the scenario it was bought for.**
</details>

**QC5.** The stock instead rises to ₹1,120. Which strategy performs best, and what is the general lesson?
A) The covered call  B) Doing nothing (₹44,80,000), then the protective put (₹43,68,000), then the covered call (₹43,28,000) — protection always costs something when the feared event does not occur  C) The protective put  D) All identical
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Do nothing:** 1,120 × 4,000 = **₹44,80,000**. **Protective put:** the put expires worthless, so 44,80,000 − 1,12,000 = **₹43,68,000**. **Covered call:** shares called away at ₹1,060 → 1,060 × 4,000 = 42,40,000, plus the ₹88,000 premium = **₹43,28,000**.
A) The covered call is the **worst** here, because the upside above ₹1,060 is surrendered. C) The put costs its premium for protection that proved unnecessary. D) They differ by over ₹1.5 lakh.

> ⚠️ **The lesson this caselet is built to teach:** there is **no strategy that wins in every scenario**. The protective put wins when the market falls and costs a known premium when it rises; the covered call earns income in flat markets and surrenders the upside in strong ones; doing nothing wins in a rally and is worst in a fall. **Choose based on which risk you actually want to eliminate — and accept that certainty always has a price.** Given Ms Verma explicitly wants downside protection while keeping the shares, **Option 1 (the protective put) matches her stated objective.**
</details>
