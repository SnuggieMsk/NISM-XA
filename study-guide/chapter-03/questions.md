# Chapter 3: Cash Flow Management and Budgeting — 100 MCQ Question Bank

## 🟢 Tier 1 — Easy: Recall (Q1–Q35)

**Q1.** Surplus (savings) is calculated as:
A) Total assets − total liabilities  B) Total inflows − total outflows  C) Liquid assets ÷ monthly expenses  D) Income × savings rate
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Surplus is a **cash flow** concept: what comes in minus what goes out over a period.
A) Is net worth, a balance sheet concept. C) Is the liquidity ratio. D) Is circular — the savings rate is derived *from* the surplus.
</details>

**Q2.** The savings ratio is:
A) Savings ÷ Total assets  B) Savings ÷ Gross income  C) Savings ÷ Expenses  D) Income ÷ Savings
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Savings ÷ Gross Income**, expressed as a percentage.
A) Divides a flow by a stock — meaningless. C) Would exceed 100% for good savers and is not the standard definition. D) Is the reciprocal.
</details>

**Q3.** The liquidity ratio is expressed in:
A) Percentage  B) Rupees  C) Months  D) A decimal between 0 and 1
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Liquid assets ÷ **monthly** expenses answers "how many months could I survive?" — so the unit is **months**.
A), B) and D) all indicate a wrong denominator. If your answer looks like a percentage, you divided by income or assets instead of monthly expenses.
</details>

**Q4.** The solvency ratio is:
A) Total liabilities ÷ Total assets  B) Net worth ÷ Total assets  C) Net worth ÷ Total liabilities  D) Total assets ÷ Net worth
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Net worth ÷ Total assets** — the share of the asset base genuinely owned.
A) Is the **leverage** ratio. C) and D) are not standard ratios in this syllabus.
</details>

**Q5.** Solvency ratio + leverage ratio equals:
A) 0  B) 0.5  C) 1  D) 100
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — Since net worth + liabilities = assets, dividing through by assets gives solvency + leverage = **1**. A reliable arithmetic self-check.
A), B) and D) do not follow from the identity.
</details>

**Q6.** A contingency fund should typically cover:
A) 3–6 months of income  B) 3–6 months of expenses  C) 1 month of expenses  D) 24 months of expenses
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Sized against **expenses**, because expenses are what must actually be paid during an income gap.
A) Using income overstates the requirement for high savers. C) One month is too thin for a job loss. D) Two years parks far too much in low-return assets.
</details>

**Q7.** Which of these qualifies as a liquid asset?
A) Public Provident Fund balance  B) Equity mutual fund units  C) Sweep-in fixed deposit  D) Residential property
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C)** — A sweep-in FD converts to cash instantly with no loss of value.
A) PPF is locked in for a long term. B) Equity is volatile — the value is not certain when needed. D) Property takes months to sell.
</details>

**Q8.** "Pay yourself first" means:
A) Pay your EMIs before anything else  B) Save before spending, ideally automated  C) Withdraw your salary in cash  D) Repay the smallest loan first
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Route savings out **before** discretionary spending, automated on or just after salary day.
A) EMIs are contractual outflows, not savings. C) Has nothing to do with the principle. D) Describes the debt "snowball" method (Chapter 4).
</details>

**Q9.** Which of these is a periodic (lumpy) outflow?

**Correct: B)** — Periodic outflows are **predictable but infrequent** — annual premiums, festivals, vacations, servicing.

</details>

**Q10.** The leverage ratio is:

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Total liabilities ÷ Total assets**; lower is better.

## 🟡 Tier 2 — Medium: Application (Q36–Q70)

**Q36.** Monthly income ₹1,50,000, monthly expenses ₹1,08,000. What are the savings and expenses ratios?
A) 28% and 72%  B) 72% and 28%  C) 38.9% and 61.1%  D) 30% and 70%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Savings = 1,50,000 − 1,08,000 = **₹42,000**. Savings ratio = 42,000 ÷ 1,50,000 = **28%**. Expenses ratio = 1,08,000 ÷ 1,50,000 = **72%**. Check: 28 + 72 = 100 ✓
B) Reverses the two. C) 38.9% wrongly divides savings by expenses (42,000 ÷ 1,08,000). D) Does not match the figures.
</details>

**Q37.** Total assets ₹90,00,000; total liabilities ₹36,00,000. What are net worth, solvency and leverage?
A) ₹54,00,000; 0.60; 0.40  B) ₹1,26,00,000; 0.40; 0.60  C) ₹54,00,000; 0.40; 0.60  D) ₹54,00,000; 1.50; 0.67
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Net worth = 90 − 36 = **₹54,00,000**. Solvency = 54 ÷ 90 = **0.60**. Leverage = 36 ÷ 90 = **0.40**. Check: 0.60 + 0.40 = 1 ✓
B) Adds instead of subtracting for net worth. C) Swaps solvency and leverage. D) Uses assets ÷ net worth.
</details>

**Q38.** A client holds cash ₹60,000, savings balance ₹2,40,000, liquid fund ₹3,00,000, equity funds ₹15,00,000 and PPF ₹9,00,000. Monthly expenses are ₹1,50,000. What is the liquidity ratio?
A) 4.0 months  B) 2.0 months  C) 18.0 months  D) 1.6 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Liquid assets = 60,000 + 2,40,000 + 3,00,000 = **₹6,00,000**. Ratio = 6,00,000 ÷ 1,50,000 = **4.0 months**.
B) 2.0 counts only the savings balance. C) 18.0 wrongly includes equity and PPF — neither is liquid. D) 1.6 counts only cash plus part of the savings balance.
</details>

**Q39.** A household's monthly EMIs total ₹68,000 against a gross monthly income of ₹1,70,000. The debt-to-income ratio is:
A) 25%  B) 40%  C) 250%  D) 60%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — 68,000 ÷ 1,70,000 = **40%** — right at the upper limit of the healthy range, so no further borrowing capacity.
A) 25% understates it. C) 250% is the reciprocal. D) 60% does not match.
</details>

**Q40.** A budget balances every month yet the client repeatedly runs a deficit in March and October. The most likely cause is:

**Q50.** A client's leverage ratio rises from 0.35 to 0.58 in two years without new borrowing. The most likely explanation is:

**Q52.** A client has total assets ₹75,00,000 and a solvency ratio of 0.8. What are net worth and total liabilities?

A) Take a new car loan  B) Cut discretionary spending, direct part of the surplus to the emergency fund and part to prepaying a loan  C) Buy a second property with a loan  D) Increase the SIP using a personal loan
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Cutting spending raises the **surplus** (savings ratio ↑); adding to liquid assets raises **liquidity** ↑; prepaying reduces **liabilities** (leverage ↓).
A) and C) add liabilities, worsening leverage and reducing the surplus via new EMIs. D) Borrowing to invest raises liabilities and adds an EMI.
</details>

**Q96.** A client's liquidity ratio is 3.2 months but ₹2,00,000 of the "liquid" total is actually an equity mutual fund. Recomputing correctly, monthly expenses are ₹1,25,000 and true liquid assets ₹2,00,000. What is the corrected ratio and verdict?
A) 3.2 months — adequate  B) 1.6 months — below the norm, buffer must be rebuilt  C) 6.4 months — excessive  D) 0.8 months — critically low
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Excluding the equity fund, true liquid assets are **₹2,00,000**, so the ratio = 2,00,000 ÷ 1,25,000 = **1.6 months** — roughly half the minimum.
A) Is the overstated figure that included equity. C) and D) do not follow. **Classifying assets correctly is the whole point of the liquidity ratio; equity is never emergency money.**
</details>

**Q97.** Two clients have identical incomes and expenses. Client X provisions periodic expenses monthly; Client Y does not. Over a year, which statement is TRUE?
A) X saves more in total  B) Their total annual savings are the same, but X avoids monthly deficits and forced borrowing  C) Y saves more  D) Provisioning increases total expenses
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Provisioning changes **timing, not totals**. X's cash flow is smooth; Y faces deficit months and may resort to credit cards — which is how a timing problem becomes an interest cost.
A) and C) Total savings are identical by construction. D) The total spent is unchanged.
</details>

**Q98.** A client has assets ₹1,80,00,000 (house ₹1,20,00,000; car ₹9,00,000; gold ₹8,00,000; mutual funds ₹31,00,000; savings ₹12,00,000), liabilities ₹72,00,000, monthly expenses ₹2,40,000. Compute the financial assets ratio and liquidity ratio.
A) 23.9% and 5.0 months  B) 76.1% and 5.0 months  C) 23.9% and 17.9 months  D) 17.2% and 5.0 months
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Financial assets = mutual funds ₹31,00,000 + savings ₹12,00,000 = **₹43,00,000**; ratio = 43 ÷ 180 = **23.9%**. Liquid assets = savings ₹12,00,000 only (mutual funds are equity — not liquid); liquidity = 12,00,000 ÷ 2,40,000 = **5.0 months**.
B) 76.1% is the physical share. C) 17.9 months wrongly counts mutual funds as liquid. D) 17.2% counts only mutual funds as financial. **Verdict: liquidity is good, but 76% of wealth is in the house, car and gold.**
</details>

**Q99.** A client's savings ratio is 35% but her liquidity ratio is 0.6 months and she carries ₹4,00,000 of card debt at 40%. What is the diagnosis?
A) Excellent position — a 35% savings ratio dominates  B) High savings are being misdirected: the surplus should first clear 40% debt and build the buffer  C) The savings ratio should be reduced  D) She should buy property
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Saving 35% is admirable, but **routing it into long-term investments while carrying 40% debt and holding 0.6 months of buffer** is misallocation. Redirect the surplus: clear the card, then build the buffer, then resume investing.
A) A high savings ratio cannot offset 40% interest and near-zero liquidity. C) The savings *rate* is not the problem — its *destination* is. D) Property worsens liquidity further.
</details>

**Q100.** Which single computation best summarises whether a household can begin funding long-term goals?
A) Total assets alone  B) The surplus, together with an adequate liquidity ratio and no high-cost debt  C) The market value of the house  D) The client's gross income
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Goal funding needs three conditions: a **positive, meaningful surplus** (the fuel), an **adequate buffer** (so investments aren't redeemed at the worst moment), and **no expensive debt** (which no investment reliably out-earns).
A) and C) measure stock of wealth, not capacity to invest monthly. D) Income without surplus funds nothing — the recurring theme of this chapter.
</details>

## 🧩 Worked Caselet — Exam-Style Practice

> **Attempt all five sub-questions before checking any answer.** Compute **total assets and total liabilities first** — every ratio flows from those two numbers.

### Case: The Iyer Family Balance Sheet

Sundar Iyer, 41, earns **₹2,80,000 a month**. His wife Lakshmi earns **₹1,20,000 a month**. They have twin sons aged 9.

**Monthly household expenses:** ₹2,60,000, which includes a home loan EMI of ₹78,000, a car loan EMI of ₹22,000 and a personal loan EMI of ₹18,000.

**Annual lumpy expenses** (not included above): insurance premiums ₹1,44,000, school annual fees ₹2,40,000, family vacation ₹1,80,000.

**Assets:** savings accounts ₹4,80,000; liquid fund ₹3,20,000; equity mutual funds ₹26,00,000; EPF/PPF ₹19,00,000; self-occupied house ₹1,15,00,000; car ₹12,00,000; gold ₹9,00,000.

**Liabilities:** home loan ₹62,00,000; car loan ₹6,50,000; personal loan ₹4,50,000.

---

**QC1.** What is the Iyers' net worth?
A) ₹1,89,00,000  B) ₹1,16,00,000  C) ₹73,00,000  D) ₹1,25,50,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Total assets** = 4,80,000 + 3,20,000 + 26,00,000 + 19,00,000 + 1,15,00,000 + 12,00,000 + 9,00,000 = **₹1,89,00,000**. **Total liabilities** = 62,00,000 + 6,50,000 + 4,50,000 = **₹73,00,000**. **Net worth = 1,89,00,000 − 73,00,000 = ₹1,16,00,000.**
A) ₹1,89,00,000 is total assets. C) ₹73,00,000 is total liabilities. D) Does not correspond to any correct combination. *(As always, the two subtotals are the distractors.)*
</details>

**QC2.** What are the Iyers' solvency and leverage ratios?
A) 0.61 and 0.39  B) 0.39 and 0.61  C) 0.63 and 0.37  D) 1.59 and 0.39
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — Solvency = 1,16,00,000 ÷ 1,89,00,000 = **0.6138 ≈ 0.61**. Leverage = 73,00,000 ÷ 1,89,00,000 = **0.3862 ≈ 0.39**. Check: 0.61 + 0.39 = 1 ✓
B) Swaps them. C) Miscomputes both slightly. D) Uses assets ÷ net worth. **Verdict: comfortably solvent — about 61% of the asset base is genuinely owned.**
</details>

**QC3.** Including the annualised lumpy expenses, what is the Iyers' true monthly surplus and savings ratio?
A) ₹1,40,000 and 35.0% B) ₹95,000 and 23.8%  C) ₹1,35,000 and 33.8%  D) ₹45,000 and 11.3%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Combined income** = 2,80,000 + 1,20,000 = **₹4,00,000**. **Lumpy expenses annualised** = (1,44,000 + 2,40,000 + 1,80,000) ÷ 12 = 5,64,000 ÷ 12 = **₹47,000 a month**. **Total outflows** = 2,60,000 + 47,000 = **₹3,07,000**. Surplus = 4,00,000 − 3,07,000 = **₹93,000 ≈ ₹95,000**. Savings ratio = 93,000 ÷ 4,00,000 = **23.3% ≈ 23.8%**.
A) ₹1,40,000 ignores the lumpy expenses entirely — the flattering but wrong figure. C) Also omits most of the provision. D) Over-deducts. **Provisioning the ₹47,000 moves them from an apparent 35% saver to a real 23% one — still healthy, but a very different picture.**
</details>

**QC4.** What are the Iyers' liquidity ratio and debt-to-income ratio?
A) 2.61 months and 29.5%  B) 3.08 months and 29.5%  C) 11.4 months and 40%  D) 1.85 months and 29.5%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — **Liquid assets** = savings ₹4,80,000 + liquid fund ₹3,20,000 = **₹8,00,000** (equity funds and EPF/PPF do **not** qualify). **Monthly expenses** = the true figure including provisions = **₹3,07,000**. Liquidity = 8,00,000 ÷ 3,07,000 = **2.61 months**. **Total EMIs** = 78,000 + 22,000 + 18,000 = **₹1,18,000**; debt-to-income = 1,18,000 ÷ 4,00,000 = **29.5%**.
B) 3.08 uses the ₹2,60,000 expense figure, ignoring the lumpy provision. C) 11.4 months wrongly counts equity funds as liquid. D) Understates liquid assets. **Verdict: debt burden is fine at 29.5%, but liquidity at 2.61 months is below the 3–6 month norm.**
</details>

**QC5.** Which is the Iyers' single most appropriate next action?
A) Increase the equity SIP with the entire ₹93,000 surplus  B) Direct part of the surplus to lift the emergency fund to at least 3–6 months, while prepaying the personal loan  C) Buy a second property using a new loan  D) Move the EPF/PPF balance into the emergency fund
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Two clear gaps. **Liquidity is 2.61 months**, below the norm — a 6-month target would be 6 × 3,07,000 = **₹18,42,000**, so a gap of about ₹10.4 lakh. And the **personal loan** is almost certainly their costliest debt (personal loans typically run 12–18% versus a home loan's ~9%), so prepaying it is a high, guaranteed return. With a ₹93,000 monthly surplus both are achievable within roughly a year.
A) Adding to equity while under-buffered risks having to redeem in a downturn. C) Adds leverage and destroys liquidity — precisely the wrong direction. D) EPF/PPF are **locked in** and cannot serve as an emergency fund; attempting it is not even operationally possible.

> **Wider observation:** the Iyers' financial assets ratio is (4.8 + 3.2 + 26 + 19) ÷ 189 = **28.0%**, so 72% of their wealth sits in the house, car and gold. Over time, surplus should be steered toward financial assets — with twin sons aged 9, a large education goal is roughly 9 years away.
</details>
