# Chapter 12: Portfolio Manager — Short Notes

> **Module 4 · Investment Through Managed Portfolio (23 marks).**
> A short chapter with a high concentration of **exact numbers** — the ₹50 lakh minimum, the three service types, and the **high watermark / hurdle rate / catch-up** fee mechanics. Learn those and the chapter is largely done.

---

## 12.1 Portfolio Managers in India

**A Portfolio Manager manages a client's securities portfolio individually**, under the **SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020**.

The defining structural feature: **securities are held in the client's OWN name and demat account — the portfolio is NOT pooled.**

| | **PMS** | **Mutual fund** |
|---|---|---|
| **Holding** | Securities in the **client's own name** | **Units** of a pooled scheme |
| **Pooling** | **None** — an individual portfolio | Fully pooled |
| **Minimum** | **₹50 lakh** | ₹100–₹500 |
| **Customisation** | Possible | None |
| **Disclosure** | To the client | Public — daily NAV, monthly portfolio |
| **Fees** | Fixed and/or **performance-linked** | Capped **TER** |

---

## 12.2 ⭐ Types of Portfolio Management Services

| Type | Who decides | Who executes | Client involvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discretionary** | The **portfolio manager** | The manager | None — full authority delegated |
| **Non-discretionary** | The **client** | The manager | The client approves **every** transaction |
| **Advisory** | The **client** | The **client** (the manager only advises) | Complete — the manager never transacts |

> 🧠 **The clean test — who pulls the trigger?**
> **Discretionary** → the manager decides *and* executes.
> **Non-discretionary** → the client decides, the manager executes.
> **Advisory** → the manager only recommends; the client decides *and* executes.

---

## 12.3 Structure of PMS in India

- The client signs a **PMS agreement** with the portfolio manager setting out the service type, investment approach, fees, risks and termination terms.
- The client opens a **separate bank account and demat account**, operated by the manager under a **Power of Attorney** (for discretionary and non-discretionary services).
- A **custodian** holds the securities (mandatory for discretionary and non-discretionary PMS).
- The manager provides **periodic reports** to the client.

## 12.4 Registration Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Registration** | Mandatory with **SEBI** under the 2020 Regulations |
| **Minimum net worth** | **₹5 crore** |
| **Principal Officer** | Required, with prescribed qualifications and experience |
| **Compliance Officer** | Mandatory |
| **Minimum client investment** | **₹50 lakh** |

---

## 12.5 Responsibilities of a Portfolio Manager

- Act in the **client's best interest** — a fiduciary obligation
- Provide a **Disclosure Document** before the agreement (containing fees, risks, performance and the manager's track record)
- Maintain **client-wise segregation** of funds and securities
- **Not commingle** client assets with its own or with other clients'
- Furnish **periodic reports** (portfolio composition, transactions, performance, fees)
- Maintain **records** and appoint a compliance officer
- Redress grievances
- **Not guarantee or assure any return**

## 12.6 ⭐ Costs, Expenses and Fees

Fees may be **fixed**, **performance-linked**, or a combination.

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fixed fee** | A percentage of assets under management, charged regardless of performance |
| **Performance/incentive fee** | A share of profits earned above a threshold |
| **Hurdle rate** | The **minimum return that must be earned before any performance fee applies** |
| **High watermark** | The **highest portfolio value previously achieved** — performance fees are charged only on gains **above that peak** |
| **Catch-up** | Once the hurdle is crossed, the manager "catches up" a larger share of returns between the hurdle and a defined point |
| **No catch-up** | The performance fee applies **only to returns above the hurdle** |
| **Exit load** | A charge for early withdrawal, subject to SEBI caps |

### 🔑 The high watermark principle

**Performance fees are charged only on gains above the highest value the portfolio has previously reached.**

**Why it exists:** without it, a manager could charge a performance fee on the same gains twice — once on the way up, and again after a fall and recovery. The high watermark ensures the client **does not pay twice for recovering ground already lost**.

**Worked example.** Portfolio starts at ₹1 crore.
- **Year 1:** rises to ₹1.30 crore. Performance fee is charged on the ₹30 lakh gain. **High watermark = ₹1.30 crore.**
- **Year 2:** falls to ₹1.10 crore. **No performance fee** — the value is below the watermark.
- **Year 3:** rises to ₹1.45 crore. The fee applies only to the gain **above ₹1.30 crore**, i.e. on **₹15 lakh** — *not* on the ₹35 lakh rise from ₹1.10 crore.

### Hurdle rate example
Hurdle **10%**, performance fee **20%**, no catch-up, on a ₹1 crore portfolio returning **18%**.
- Return = ₹18 lakh; the hurdle absorbs the first **₹10 lakh**.
- Fee = 20% × (18 − 10) = 20% × ₹8 lakh = **₹1.6 lakh**.

> ⚠️ **SEBI's investor protections on fees:** performance fees must be charged on a **high watermark** basis; **no upfront fees** may be charged; and exit loads are capped and taper with holding period.

## 12.7 The "Direct Access" Facility

Since the 2020 Regulations, portfolio managers must offer clients a **direct on-boarding option** — investing **without a distributor**, and therefore **without paying distribution commission**.

| | **Direct access (PMS)** | **Direct plan (mutual fund)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it avoids** | Distributor commission | Distributor commission |
| **Effect** | Lower overall cost to the client | Lower TER, higher NAV |
| **Availability** | Must be offered by every portfolio manager | Must be offered by every scheme |

**How an Investment Adviser helps:** because an IA is paid a **fee by the client** and must **segregate advice from distribution**, the IA can guide the client to the **direct access route**, select the manager and investment approach, and monitor performance — all without any commission incentive.

---

## 12.8 SEBI Requirements on Performance Disclosure

To make managers comparable and prevent cherry-picking:

- Performance must be disclosed **net of all fees and expenses**
- It must be calculated using the **time-weighted rate of return (TWRR)** — the standard that neutralises the effect of client cash flows the manager does not control
- Performance must be shown for the **firm's aggregate**, not just selected accounts
- Disclosure must be provided in the **Disclosure Document** and in periodic client reports
- Managers must report to SEBI and to **APMI (the Association of Portfolio Managers in India)**
- Any claim must be **fair, accurate and not misleading**

> 🧠 **Why TWRR and not money-weighted return?** TWRR removes the distorting effect of when a client added or withdrew money — decisions the *client* made, not the manager. It therefore measures **the manager's skill**. (See Chapter 16.)
