**Q36.** A PMS portfolio starts at ₹2 crore, rises to ₹2.60 crore in year 1, falls to ₹2.20 crore in year 2, then rises to ₹2.90 crore in year 3. On what amount is the year-3 performance fee charged?

A) ₹70 lakh is the rise from ₹2.20 crore — charging on it would mean **billing twice** for recovering the ₹40 lakh already lost. C) ₹90 lakh is the total gain since inception. D) A fee is due, since the watermark was exceeded.
</details>

A) Fee ₹14.4 lakh; watermark ₹4.72 crore  B) Fee ₹6.4 lakh; watermark ₹4.72 crore  C) Fee ₹14.4 lakh; watermark ₹4 crore  D) No fee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — **Gain** = 4,72,00,000 − 4,00,00,000 = **₹72,00,000** (18%). **Hurdle** = 10% × ₹4 crore = **₹40,00,000**. **Performance fee** = 20% × (72 − 40) lakh = 20% × **₹32,00,000** = **₹6,40,000**. The **new high watermark is ₹4,72,00,000**.
A) Applies 20% to the full ₹72 lakh gain, ignoring the hurdle. C) Fails to update the watermark. D) The hurdle was comfortably exceeded.
</details>

**QC2.** What is the year-1 fixed fee, and the total year-1 fee?
A) Fixed ₹7 lakh; total ₹13.4 lakh  B) Fixed ₹8.26 lakh; total ₹14.66 lakh  C) Fixed ₹7 lakh; total ₹7 lakh  D) Fixed ₹6.4 lakh
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A)** — **Fixed fee** = 1.75% × ₹4,00,00,000 = **₹7,00,000** (charged on the opening/average asset base). **Total** = 7,00,000 + 6,40,000 = **₹13,40,000**.
B) Applies the fixed fee to the year-end value — a defensible variant, but the question's base is the ₹4 crore invested. C) Omits the performance fee. D) Is the performance fee alone. *(In practice the fixed fee is usually charged on average daily assets; the exam convention is the stated base.)*
</details>

**QC3.** In year 2 the portfolio falls to ₹4,28,00,000. What performance fee is due?
A) A reduced fee  B) None — the value is below the ₹4.72 crore high watermark  C) 20% of the loss  D) The full fee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — ₹4,28,00,000 is **below** the ₹4,72,00,000 watermark, so **no performance fee arises**. The **fixed fee of about ₹7.49 lakh** (1.75% × ₹4.28 crore) would still be charged.
A) There is no partial performance fee. C) Fees are never charged on losses. D) Would contradict the high watermark entirely.
</details>

**QC4.** In year 3 the portfolio reaches ₹5,16,00,000. On what gain is the performance fee charged, and how much is it?
A) On ₹88 lakh; fee ₹17.6 lakh  B) On ₹44 lakh above the watermark, less the hurdle — fee about ₹0.35 lakh  C) On ₹1.16 crore; fee ₹23.2 lakh  D) No fee
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — The **high watermark is ₹4,72,00,000**. Year 3's ₹5,16,00,000 exceeds it by **₹44,00,000**. The **hurdle** for the year is 10% of the opening value (₹4,28,00,000) = **₹42,80,000**. Performance fee = 20% × (44,00,000 − 42,80,000) = 20% × **₹1,20,000** = **₹24,000**.

So the fee is very small — roughly **₹0.24–0.35 lakh** depending on the exact hurdle base — because the gain above the watermark barely exceeded the hurdle.
A) ₹88 lakh is the rise from ₹4.28 crore, which would **double-charge** the ₹44 lakh recovery. C) ₹1.16 crore is the total gain since inception. D) A small fee is due, since both the watermark and the hurdle were (just) exceeded.
</details>

**QC5.** Over the three years, what has the high watermark saved Mr Kapoor, and what is the wider lesson?
A) Nothing  B) Without the watermark, year 3's fee would have been charged on the ₹88 lakh rise from ₹4.28 crore rather than on the ₹44 lakh above the peak — roughly ₹8.8 lakh more in fees for gains he had already been charged on once  C) It cost him money  D) It guaranteed his returns
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B)** — Without a watermark, the year-3 fee would be computed on the **₹88 lakh rise from the year-2 trough** (less the hurdle), instead of on the **₹44 lakh above the previous peak**. The difference is on the order of **₹8–9 lakh** — a fee for **recovering ground the client had already paid a fee on in year 1**.

**The wider lesson:** the high watermark is the single most important fee protection in a PMS, and its value rises with volatility. When comparing managers, a client should check **three things together — the hurdle rate, whether a high watermark applies, and whether there is a catch-up** — because those three, not the headline percentage, determine how much of the return the client actually keeps.

A) The saving is substantial. C) It reduces fees, never increases them. D) No fee mechanism guarantees returns — and guaranteeing them is prohibited.
</details>
