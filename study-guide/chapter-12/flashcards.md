# Chapter 12: Portfolio Manager — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **The three service types and the high watermark are near-certain exam marks.**

**Q1.** Which regulations govern portfolio managers, and from which year?
> **A:** The **SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2020**.

**Q2.** What is the minimum client investment in a PMS?
> **A:** **₹50 lakh.** *(Do not confuse this with the AIF minimum of ₹1 crore.)*

**Q3.** What is a portfolio manager's minimum net worth requirement?
> **A:** **₹5 crore.**

**Q4.** How are securities held in a PMS, and why does it matter?
> **A:** In the **client's own name and demat account** — the portfolio is **not pooled**. The client therefore owns the underlying securities directly rather than units of a scheme.

**Q5.** Name the three types of portfolio management service.
> **A:** **Discretionary**, **non-discretionary** and **advisory**.

**Q6.** Distinguish the three using the "who pulls the trigger" test.
> **A:** **Discretionary** — the manager **decides and executes**. **Non-discretionary** — the **client decides**, the manager executes. **Advisory** — the manager **only advises**; the client decides **and** executes.

**Q7.** In which PMS types is a custodian mandatory?
> **A:** **Discretionary and non-discretionary** services — because the manager handles the client's securities.

**Q8.** What is the Power of Attorney used for in a PMS?
> **A:** To let the manager **operate the client's bank and demat accounts** for discretionary and non-discretionary services.

**Q9.** What must a portfolio manager give the client before the agreement?
> **A:** A **Disclosure Document**, containing fees, risks, the manager's performance track record and other prescribed information.

**Q10.** Name five responsibilities of a portfolio manager.
> **A:** Act in the **client's best interest**; provide the **Disclosure Document**; maintain **client-wise segregation** (no commingling); furnish **periodic reports**; and **never guarantee or assure any return**.

**Q11.** What is commingling, and is it permitted?
> **A:** Mixing a client's funds or securities with the manager's own or with other clients'. It is **prohibited** — client-wise segregation is required.

**Q12.** What is a hurdle rate?
> **A:** The **minimum return that must be earned before any performance fee applies**.

**Q13.** State the high watermark principle.
> **A:** **Performance fees are charged only on gains above the highest portfolio value previously achieved.**

**Q14.** Why does the high watermark exist?
> **A:** So a client **never pays a performance fee twice on the same gains** — once on the way up, and again after a fall and recovery.

**Q15.** A portfolio starts at ₹1 crore, rises to ₹1.30 crore, falls to ₹1.10 crore, then rises to ₹1.45 crore. On what gain is the year-3 performance fee charged?
> **A:** Only on the **₹15 lakh above the ₹1.30 crore high watermark** — **not** on the ₹35 lakh rise from ₹1.10 crore.

**Q16.** What is the difference between catch-up and no-catch-up?
> **A:** With **catch-up**, once the hurdle is crossed the manager takes a **larger share** of returns between the hurdle and a defined point. With **no catch-up**, the performance fee applies **only to returns above the hurdle**.

**Q17.** Hurdle 10%, performance fee 20%, no catch-up, ₹1 crore portfolio returning 18%. What is the fee?
> **A:** Return = ₹18 lakh; the hurdle absorbs the first ₹10 lakh. Fee = 20% × (18 − 10) = 20% × ₹8 lakh = **₹1.6 lakh**.

**Q18.** Are upfront fees permitted in a PMS?
> **A:** **No** — SEBI prohibits upfront fees. Exit loads are also capped and taper with the holding period.

**Q19.** What is the "direct access" facility, and what does it save?
> **A:** A mandatory **direct on-boarding option** letting a client invest **without a distributor**, and therefore **without paying distribution commission** — the PMS equivalent of a mutual fund's direct plan.

**Q20.** How can an Investment Adviser add value around PMS?
> **A:** Because the IA is **paid a fee by the client** and must **segregate advice from distribution**, the IA can steer the client to the **direct access route**, select the manager and approach, and monitor performance — with **no commission incentive**.

**Q21.** On what basis must PMS performance be disclosed?
> **A:** **Net of all fees and expenses**, using the **time-weighted rate of return (TWRR)**, for the **firm's aggregate** rather than selected accounts.

**Q22.** Why is TWRR used rather than a money-weighted return?
> **A:** TWRR **removes the effect of client cash flows** — decisions the client made, not the manager — so it measures **the manager's skill**.

**Q23.** Which industry association covers portfolio managers?
> **A:** **APMI** — the Association of Portfolio Managers in India.

**Q24.** Give four differences between a PMS and a mutual fund.
> **A:** **Holding** (own securities vs pooled units); **minimum** (₹50 lakh vs ₹100–500); **customisation** (possible vs none); **fees** (fixed and/or performance-linked vs a capped TER). Mutual funds also disclose far more publicly.

**Q25.** Can a portfolio manager promise a client a minimum return?
> **A:** **No.** Guaranteeing or assuring any return is expressly prohibited.

**Q26.** What does the PMS agreement set out?
> **A:** The **service type**, investment approach, **fees**, risks and **termination terms**.

**Q27.** Which service type gives the client the most control, and which the least?
> **A:** **Advisory** gives the most control (the client decides and executes); **discretionary** gives the least (full authority is delegated to the manager).

**Q28.** In a purely advisory PMS, who executes the trades?
> **A:** The **client**. The manager only recommends and never transacts — which is why no custodian or Power of Attorney is required.
