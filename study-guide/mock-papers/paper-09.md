**Q1.** Sandeep Varma, a 41-year-old manufacturing manager in Hyderabad, has just moved to a new employer and asks you to draw up his personal balance sheet. His assets are: savings account Rs 3,10,000, liquid fund Rs 1,90,000, mutual funds Rs 22,50,000, EPF Rs 16,40,000, apartment Rs 72,00,000 and car Rs 6,50,000. His liabilities are: home loan outstanding Rs 41,80,000 and car loan outstanding Rs 3,20,000. What is his net worth?
A) Rs 45,00,000  B) Rs 70,90,000  C) Rs 77,40,000  D) Rs 1,22,40,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Rs 77,40,000**

Total assets = 3,10,000 + 1,90,000 + 22,50,000 + 16,40,000 + 72,00,000 + 6,50,000 = Rs 1,22,40,000. Total liabilities = 41,80,000 + 3,20,000 = Rs 45,00,000. Net worth = Total assets − Total liabilities = 1,22,40,000 − 45,00,000 = **Rs 77,40,000**.

- **A) Rs 45,00,000** - wrong; this is total liabilities, not net worth.
- **B) Rs 70,90,000** - wrong; this drops the car (a personal-use asset) from the asset side. Personal-use assets are still recorded on the balance sheet at market value.
- **D) Rs 1,22,40,000** - wrong; this is total assets before deducting liabilities.

</details>

**Q2.** A 41-year-old client has a stable income, a 19-year horizon to retirement, no dependants other than a spouse who also earns, and a large net worth — but he liquidated his entire equity portfolio during the last market fall and says volatility keeps him awake. How should the adviser set his asset allocation?
A) To his risk capacity, since it is objective and measurable  B) To his risk tolerance, since capacity can be rebuilt later  C) To the lower of his risk capacity and his risk tolerance  D) To the higher of the two, because his horizon is long
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) To the lower of his risk capacity and his risk tolerance**

Risk **capacity** (ability) is objective — horizon, income stability, net worth, dependants — and here it is high. Risk **tolerance** (willingness) is psychological, and his panic-selling shows it is low. Where the two conflict, the plan must be built to the **lower** of the two, otherwise the client converts a temporary paper loss into a permanent one by selling at the bottom.

- **A) To his risk capacity** - wrong; an allocation the client cannot emotionally hold will be abandoned in the next drawdown.
- **B) To his risk tolerance, since capacity can be rebuilt later** - wrong; the conclusion (use tolerance) is right by coincidence but the reasoning is not the rule, and the rule is expressed as "the lower of the two", which is what must be applied when the two differ in the other direction as well.
- **D) To the higher of the two** - wrong; this is the exact opposite of the rule and maximises the chance of behavioural failure.

</details>

The order is: (1) establish the client–planner relationship, (2) gather data on the client's financial situation, (3) define client needs and financial goals, (4) **risk profiling and asset allocation**, (5) portfolio construction, (6) reviewing and rebalancing.

**Q4.** Under the SEBI framework applicable to an individual Registered Investment Adviser, which pairing of activities must be kept segregated?
A) Advice and execution/distribution  B) Financial planning and risk profiling  C) Data gathering and goal setting  D) Reviewing and rebalancing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) Advice and execution/distribution**

Advice is recommending what the client should do; execution/distribution is placing the transaction, often for a commission from the manufacturer. Because commission creates a conflict with the fiduciary duty owed to the client, SEBI requires **segregation of advisory from distribution activity**, and an individual Investment Adviser cannot do both.

- **B) Financial planning and risk profiling** - wrong; risk profiling is a step *within* financial planning, not a conflicting activity.
- **C) Data gathering and goal setting** - wrong; these are sequential steps of the same process.
- **D) Reviewing and rebalancing** - wrong; these are complementary parts of the ongoing monitoring step.

</details>

**Q6.** Sandeep invests his job-change joining bonus of Rs 8,50,000 as a lump sum for 7 years at an expected 9.5% p.a. compounded annually. What is the maturity value?
A) Rs 14,15,250  B) Rs 16,04,419  C) Rs 17,56,839  D) Rs 21,06,493
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 16,04,419**

FV = PV × (1 + r)^n = 8,50,000 × (1.095)^7. (1.095)^7 = 1.887551. FV = 8,50,000 × 1.887551 = **Rs 16,04,419**.

- **A) Rs 14,15,250** - wrong; this applies simple interest, 8,50,000 × (1 + 0.095 × 7).
- **C) Rs 17,56,839** - wrong; this compounds for 8 years instead of 7.
- **D) Rs 21,06,493** - wrong; this compounds for 10 years instead of 7.

**Q7.** An investment of Rs 6,40,000 made by Sandeep nine years ago is worth Rs 17,80,000 today. What is the CAGR?
A) 12.04%  B) 13.64%  C) 19.79%  D) 178.13%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) 12.04%**

CAGR = (FV ÷ PV)^(1/n) − 1. FV ÷ PV = 17,80,000 ÷ 6,40,000 = 2.78125. 2.78125^(1/9) = 1.120366, so the CAGR is **12.04% p.a.**

- **B) 13.64%** - wrong; this uses 8 years of compounding instead of 9.
- **C) 19.79%** - wrong; this is the total gain divided by the number of years (1.78125 ÷ 9), i.e. simple-interest thinking.
- **D) 178.13%** - wrong; this is the total growth over the whole period, not an annualised rate.

</details>

**Q8.** Sandeep starts a SIP of Rs 18,000 at the **end** of every month for 12 years, expecting 11.5% p.a. compounded monthly. What corpus will he accumulate?
A) Rs 25,92,000  B) Rs 29,46,747  C) Rs 55,38,747  D) Rs 55,91,827
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Rs 55,38,747**

r = 0.115 ÷ 12 = 0.00958333; n = 12 × 12 = 144. (1.00958333)^144 = 3.948639. FV = 18,000 × [(3.948639 − 1) ÷ 0.00958333] = 18,000 × 307.7082 = **Rs 55,38,747**.

- **A) Rs 25,92,000** - wrong; this is only the total contributed (18,000 × 144), ignoring all returns.
- **B) Rs 29,46,747** - wrong; this is the return component alone (corpus minus contributions), not the corpus.
- **D) Rs 55,91,827** - wrong; this treats the SIP as an annuity **due** (payment at the beginning of the month), multiplying by (1 + r). The question specifies end-of-month payments.

</details>

**Q9.** Sandeep wants a corpus of Rs 65,00,000 in 14 years for his daughter's postgraduate education. Assuming 12% p.a. compounded monthly, how much must he invest at the end of every month?
A) Rs 14,894  B) Rs 15,043  C) Rs 20,066  D) Rs 38,690
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 15,043**

PMT = FV × r ÷ [(1 + r)^n − 1]. r = 0.01, n = 168. (1.01)^168 = 5.32068. PMT = (65,00,000 × 0.01) ÷ 4.32068 = 65,000 ÷ 4.32068 = **Rs 15,043 a month**.

- **A) Rs 14,894** - wrong; this is the annuity-**due** answer (15,043 ÷ 1.01), for payments made at the beginning of each month.
- **C) Rs 20,066** - wrong; this is the **annual** contribution required at 12% compounded annually, not a monthly figure.
- **D) Rs 38,690** - wrong; this is simply 65,00,000 ÷ 168, ignoring compounding entirely.

</details>

**Q11.** Sandeep's debt portfolio is expected to return a nominal 10.4% p.a. while inflation runs at 5.8%. What is his real return?
A) 4.17%  B) 4.35%  C) 4.60%  D) 16.80%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) 4.35%**

Real return = [(1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 1 = (1.104 ÷ 1.058) − 1 = 1.043478 − 1 = **4.35%**.

- **A) 4.17%** - wrong; this inverts the ratio, (1.058 ÷ 1.104) − 1, which gives a negative figure of the same order and is reported here as its magnitude.

- **D) 16.80%** - wrong; this multiplies (1.104 × 1.058) − 1, compounding the two rates instead of deflating one by the other.

**Q16.** Sandeep's new job carries a variable pay component and his spouse is not working. Which of the following is the most appropriate parking place and size for his contingency fund?

- **D)** - wrong; an ELSS carries a three-year lock-in and equity risk, and three months is thin for a single-earner household with variable pay.

**Q20.** Sandeep has a gold loan, a car loan and a home loan. Which set of security descriptions is correct?
A) Gold loan – hypothecation; car loan – pledge; home loan – mortgage  B) Gold loan – pledge; car loan – hypothecation; home loan – mortgage  C) Gold loan – mortgage; car loan – pledge; home loan – hypothecation  D) Gold loan – pledge; car loan – mortgage; home loan – hypothecation

**Q21.** Sandeep has four debts and decides to clear the one carrying the highest interest rate first, paying only the minimum on the others. Which strategy is he following?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Avalanche**

- **A) Snowball** - wrong; the snowball method clears the **smallest balance** first, for psychological momentum.

**Q28.** Sandeep holds 480 shares of a listed company trading at Rs 1,250 each. The company announces a 3:5 bonus issue (three new shares for every five held). Immediately after the bonus, what is his holding and the theoretical adjusted price?

Bonus shares = 480 × 3 ÷ 5 = 288. New holding = 480 + 288 = **768 shares**. Total value is unchanged at 480 × 1,250 = Rs 6,00,000, so the theoretical price = 6,00,000 ÷ 768 = **Rs 781.25**. A bonus issue raises no money and leaves the shareholder's total value and percentage holding unchanged.

- **A) 288 shares at Rs 781.25** - wrong; 288 is only the number of *new* bonus shares, not the total holding.
- **C) 768 shares at Rs 1,250** - wrong; if the price did not adjust, the shareholder's wealth would rise for free, which a bonus issue does not do.

**Correct: B) Fiduciary asks "is it the best available option for the client?"; suitability asks "is it not unsuitable?"**

Regulation 15(1) of the IA Regulations, 2013 requires an adviser to **act in a fiduciary capacity and disclose all conflicts of interest as and when they arise** - the client's interest ranks above the adviser's, conflicts must be avoided or fully disclosed, and the advice must be **monitored on a continuing basis**. A distributor's suitability standard is a lower, largely point-of-sale test.

- **A) Fiduciary asks "is it not unsuitable?"; suitability asks "is it the best?"** - wrong; the two tests are the right way round in option B, not here.
- **C) Both standards are identical in Indian law** - wrong; the fiduciary duty is the **highest duty the law recognises in a commercial relationship** and is expressly higher than the distributor standard.
- **D) The fiduciary standard applies only at the point of sale** - wrong; the duty of care is **continuing** - the advice must be reviewed and monitored over the relationship.

</details>

**Q84.** A registered Investment Adviser's annual compliance audit report for the year ended 31 March is dated 20 September. What must the adviser do about the adverse findings in it?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Report the findings together with the action taken, duly approved by management, within one month of the audit report**

The audit must be conducted by a member of **ICAI or ICSI** and completed **within six months of the financial year end (by 30 September)**. **Adverse findings together with the action taken on them, approved by the management or board, must be reported within one month of the date of the audit report** - here, by 20 October.

- **A) Nothing, so long as they are corrected internally** - wrong; suppressing an adverse finding is a fresh and more serious violation than the original lapse.
- **B) Report the findings alone to SEBI within one month** - wrong; the **action taken** is the operative half of the report. Findings without remedial action is an incomplete filing.
- **D) Report them in the following year's audit** - wrong; a finding that reappears in the next year's audit is treated as the most serious kind, because it proves the earlier fix was cosmetic.

**Q85.** Which feature most distinguishes Australia's FOFA reforms from the Indian and US approaches to conflicts of interest in financial advice?

- **C) It caps advisory fees per client family** - wrong; per-family fee caps of Rs 1,25,000 or 2.5% of AUA are an **Indian** feature. Australia's control on ongoing fees is the **opt-in requirement and the annual Fee Disclosure Statement**.

### Case 5: The Investment Adviser's compliance position

**Q119.** Reducing the strategic equity target from 60% to 50% because of lower job security and a nearer house purchase is best described as:

**Correct: B) A revision of the strategic asset allocation, driven by a change in the client's circumstances**

Strategic asset allocation is about the **client**; tactical asset allocation is about the **market**. **Q120.** The IPS rule "review every 31 March, but act only if a class is outside its band" is an example of:
A) Pure calendar rebalancing  B) Pure threshold (percentage-of-portfolio) rebalancing  C) A combination of calendar and threshold rebalancing  D) Buy-and-hold
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) A combination of calendar and threshold rebalancing**

The **check** is on a fixed calendar date; the **action** is conditional on the drift being outside the tolerance band. This combination captures most of the risk-control benefit of threshold rebalancing without the cost of continuous monitoring, and is the practical industry standard. Here equity is at 65.37% against a 60% target with a ±5 point band, so the drift of 5.37 points is outside the band and action *is* required.

- **A)** - wrong; pure calendar rebalancing acts on the date regardless of how small the drift is.

</details>

### Case 8: Ranking three fund managers

Sridhar's adviser is comparing three equity funds over the same five-year period. The **risk-free
rate is 6.5%**, the **market return is 12.4%** and the **market standard deviation is 15.0%**.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Fund P** | 17.2% | 19.4% | 1.28 |
| **Fund Q** | 13.6% | 11.5% | 0.86 |
| **Fund R** | 15.0% | 16.0% | 1.10 |

**Q126.** Which fund has the highest Sharpe ratio, and what is that ratio?

**Q127.** Which fund ranks highest on the Treynor ratio, and what is that ratio?

**Correct: A) Fund P, 8.36**

Fund P: 10.7 ÷ 1.28 = **8.36**
Fund Q: 7.1 ÷ 0.86 = **8.26**

- **B) Fund P, 13.44** - wrong; this is 17.2 ÷ 1.28, forgetting to deduct the risk-free rate from the return.

</details>

**Q128.** What is Fund P's Jensen's alpha?

**Correct: C) 3.15%**

**Step 1 — the CAPM-required return.** Rf + β(Rm − Rf) = 6.5 + 1.28 × (12.4 − 6.5) = 6.5 + 1.28 × 5.9 = 6.5 + 7.552 = **14.05%**.

**Q129.** What is the standard deviation of the 65% Fund P / 35% Fund Q portfolio?
A) 13.24%  B) 14.76%  C) 16.64%  D) 19.40%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) 14.76%**

σp = √(w₁²σ₁² + w₂²σ₂² + 2w₁w₂σ₁σ₂ρ)
- w₁²σ₁² = 0.65² × 19.4² = 0.4225 × 376.36 = **159.012**

- **A) 13.24%** - wrong; this assumes zero correlation and drops the covariance term altogether.
- **C) 16.64%** - wrong; this is the simple weighted average of the two SDs (0.65 × 19.4 + 0.35 × 11.5), which is what σp would be only if ρ = 1. Portfolio *return* is a weighted average; portfolio *risk* is not.

**Q130.** What is the Sharpe ratio of that 65/35 portfolio?

- **B) 0.62** - wrong; this is Fund Q's standalone Sharpe ratio.
- **D) 0.71** - wrong; uses the zero-correlation SD of 13.24%, overstating the diversification benefit.

### Case 9: Fixed income and derivatives numericals

Sridhar's adviser is working through three positions.

**(i) A bond.** Face value **Rs 1,000**, **9% coupon paid annually**, exactly **5 years** to
maturity, and the market's required yield is currently **11%**. The adviser also wants to know
what happens if the required yield falls to **9.5%**.

**(ii) A bond fund holding.** Sridhar holds **Rs 18,00,000** in a debt fund whose portfolio has a
**modified duration of 3.89**. The adviser's scenario assumes yields **rise by 60 basis points**.

**(iii) Index derivatives.** The **Nifty spot is 24,600**, the risk-free rate is **7% per annum**
and the index dividend yield is **1.2% per annum**. A **one-year Nifty futures contract is quoting
at 26,150**. Separately, Sridhar has built a bull call spread on the Nifty with a lot size of
**50 units**: **buy one 24,800 call at Rs 265** and **sell one 25,400 call at Rs 92**.

Useful factors: 1.11⁵ = 1.685058; 1.095⁵ = 1.574233.

**Q131.** What is the fair price of the bond at an 11% required yield?
A) Rs 900.00  B) Rs 926.08  C) Rs 980.80  D) Rs 1,000.00
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 926.08**

A bond is an annuity of coupons plus a lump sum at redemption.
**Step 1 — the coupons.** Annuity factor = [1 − 1.11⁻⁵] ÷ 0.11. Since 1.11⁵ = 1.685058, 1.11⁻⁵ = 0.593451, so the factor = (1 − 0.593451) ÷ 0.11 = **3.695897**. PV of coupons = 90 × 3.695897 = **Rs 332.63**.
**Step 2 — the redemption.** 1,000 × 0.593451 = **Rs 593.45**.
**Price = 332.63 + 593.45 = Rs 926.08** — a discount, as it must be, because the 9% coupon is below the 11% required yield.

- **A) Rs 900.00** - wrong; this is not the discounted value of the cash flows, merely a round number below par.
- **C) Rs 980.80** - wrong; this is the price at a 9.5% yield, not 11%.
- **D) Rs 1,000.00** - wrong; the bond would price at par only if the coupon equalled the required yield.

</details>

**Q132.** If the required yield falls from 11% to 9.5%, by what percentage does the bond's price rise?
A) 1.50%  B) 5.51%  C) 5.91%  D) 13.64%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) 5.91%**

**Step 1 — the new price.** 1.095⁵ = 1.574233, so 1.095⁻⁵ = 0.635230. Annuity factor = (1 − 0.635230) ÷ 0.095 = **3.839709**. Price = 90 × 3.839709 + 1,000 × 0.635230 = 345.57 + 635.23 = **Rs 980.80**.
**Step 2 — the percentage change.** (980.80 − 926.08) ÷ 926.08 = 54.72 ÷ 926.08 = **+5.91%**.
Price and yield move in opposite directions; a 150 basis point fall in yield produced a 5.91% price gain.

- **A) 1.50%** - wrong; this is the change in the *yield* (150 basis points), not the change in the price.
- **B) 5.51%** - wrong; this divides the Rs 54.72 gain by the new price of Rs 980.80 rather than by the original price.
- **D) 13.64%** - wrong; this is the proportionate fall in the yield itself (1.5 ÷ 11), which is not the price response.

</details>

**Q133.** Using modified duration, what loss does the scenario of a 60 basis point rise in yields imply for the Rs 18,00,000 debt fund holding?
A) Rs 10,800  B) Rs 42,012  C) Rs 46,633  D) Rs 70,020
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 42,012**

**Step 1 — the percentage price change.** %ΔP ≈ −MD × Δy = −3.89 × 0.60% = **−2.334%**.
**Step 2 — the rupee impact.** 2.334% × 18,00,000 = **Rs 42,012** of loss.

- **A) Rs 10,800** - wrong; this applies the 0.60% yield change to the corpus directly (0.6% × 18,00,000) without multiplying by the modified duration.
- **C) Rs 46,633** - wrong; this multiplies the modified duration by (1 + y) to recover the Macaulay duration and then applies it. The price-change formula uses **modified**, not Macaulay, duration.
- **D) Rs 70,020** - wrong; this treats 3.89 as a percentage price change (3.89% × 18,00,000), forgetting to multiply by the 0.60% yield move.

**Q134.** Relative to its fair value, how is the one-year Nifty futures contract priced?
A) Rs 123.20 overpriced  B) Rs 172.00 underpriced  C) Rs 1,550.00 overpriced  D) Rs 1,845.20 overpriced
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) Rs 123.20 overpriced**

**Step 1 — the fair value.** F = S × (1 + r − q)^T = 24,600 × (1 + 0.07 − 0.012)¹ = 24,600 × 1.058 = **26,026.80**.
**Step 2 — the comparison.** The market quote is 26,150, so the futures is 26,150 − 26,026.80 = **Rs 123.20 above fair value**. The arbitrage is cash-and-carry: buy the index basket in the cash market and sell the futures.

- **B) Rs 172.00 underpriced** - wrong; this ignores the 1.2% dividend yield and uses 24,600 × 1.07 = 26,322 as the fair value.
- **C) Rs 1,550.00 overpriced** - wrong; this compares the futures with the spot itself, ignoring the cost of carry altogether. Futures normally trade above spot; that alone is not mispricing.
- **D) Rs 1,845.20 overpriced** - wrong; this deducts the dividend yield but omits the financing cost (24,600 × 0.988).

</details>

**Q135.** If the Nifty settles at 25,600 on expiry, what is the net profit on the bull call spread?
A) Rs 8,650  B) Rs 21,350  C) Rs 26,750  D) Rs 30,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 21,350**

**Step 1 — net premium paid.** 265 − 92 = **Rs 173 per unit**, i.e. 173 × 50 = **Rs 8,650** per lot, which is also the maximum possible loss.
**Step 2 — intrinsic values at 25,600.** Long 24,800 call = max(25,600 − 24,800, 0) = **+800**. Short 25,400 call = −max(25,600 − 25,400, 0) = **−200**. Net gross payoff = **600 per unit**.
**Step 3 — the net profit.** (600 − 173) × 50 = 427 × 50 = **Rs 21,350**. Because the settlement is above the higher strike, this is also the strategy's maximum profit. Break-even was 24,800 + 173 = 24,973.

- **A) Rs 8,650** - wrong; this is the net premium paid, i.e. the maximum *loss*, not the profit.
- **C) Rs 26,750** - wrong; this is the profit on the long 24,800 call alone, ignoring the short call that was sold to cheapen the position.
- **D) Rs 30,000** - wrong; this is the gross payoff of 600 × 50 before deducting the net premium of Rs 8,650.

</details>
