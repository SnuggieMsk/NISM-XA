**Q1.** An investment adviser in Kochi has just signed a written engagement letter with a new client setting out the scope of services, the duration and how she will be paid. According to the six-step financial planning process, what is the NEXT step?
A) Risk profiling and asset allocation  B) Gathering data on the client's financial situation  C) Portfolio construction and product recommendation  D) Reviewing and rebalancing the plan
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Gathering data on the client's financial situation**

The six steps in order are: (1) establish the client–planner relationship, (2) **gather data on the client's financial situation**, (3) define needs and financial goals, (4) risk profiling and asset allocation, (5) portfolio construction, (6) reviewing and rebalancing. The engagement letter completes Step 1, so Step 2 follows.

- **A) Risk profiling and asset allocation** - wrong; this is Step 4, after data collection and goal definition.
- **C) Portfolio construction and product recommendation** - wrong; Step 5, and it cannot be done before the data and goals are known.
- **D) Reviewing and rebalancing the plan** - wrong; Step 6, the final and ongoing stage.

</details>

**Q2.** Ouseph Varghese, 66, has a retirement corpus of Rs 2.4 crore, no dependants, an assured pension covering his fixed costs and a 25-year planning horizon. However, he liquidated his entire equity holding during the last market fall and now refuses to hold more than 20% in equity. How should the adviser characterise and handle this?
A) High risk capacity and high risk tolerance; plan to the higher of the two  B) High risk capacity but low risk tolerance; plan to the lower of the two  C) Low risk capacity but high risk tolerance; plan to risk capacity alone  D) Low risk capacity and low risk tolerance; plan to the risk requirement alone
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) High risk capacity but low risk tolerance; plan to the lower of the two**

**Risk capacity** is objective — long horizon, secure pension, no dependants and a large corpus all point to high capacity. **Risk tolerance** is subjective — panic-selling and a self-imposed 20% equity cap indicate low willingness. Where the two conflict, the plan must be built to the **lower** of them, because a client who sells at the bottom converts a paper loss into a permanent one.

- **A) High risk capacity and high risk tolerance; plan to the higher of the two** - wrong; his behaviour demonstrates low tolerance, and planning to the higher of the two invites panic-selling.
- **C) Low risk capacity but high risk tolerance; plan to risk capacity alone** - wrong; it reverses both assessments.
- **D) Low risk capacity and low risk tolerance; plan to the risk requirement alone** - wrong; capacity is clearly high, and the risk requirement is a third input, not the binding constraint.

</details>

They owe Rs 16,50,000 on a loan against property and Rs 2,40,000 on a gold loan. What is their net worth?

- **C) Rs 1,94,80,000** - wrong; this is total assets, before any liabilities are deducted.

**Q4.** A retired Kochi couple's wealth consists almost entirely of a self-occupied flat and a modest savings balance. They have no cover against a large hospital bill, which would force them to sell the flat. Which component of the scope of financial planning most directly addresses this gap?
A) Estate planning  B) Insurance planning  C) Tax planning  D) Debt management and counselling
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Insurance planning**

Insurance planning protects the plan from catastrophic events — death, disability, illness and property loss — through **risk transfer**. Adequate health cover stops one hospitalisation from consuming the couple's only substantial asset.

- **A) Estate planning** - wrong; that governs the transmission of assets to beneficiaries, not protection against a living expense shock.
- **C) Tax planning** - wrong; tax efficiency is a modifier and does nothing to fund an unexpected medical bill.
- **D) Debt management and counselling** - wrong; the couple has no debt problem. Borrowing would be the consequence of the gap, not the cure.

</details>

**Q5.** Which statement correctly describes SEBI's approach to the conflict between advice and execution for a registered Investment Adviser?
A) An individual investment adviser may both advise and distribute, provided commissions are disclosed  B) An investment adviser owes a fiduciary duty and must keep advisory activity segregated from distribution  C) An investment adviser owes only a suitability duty, identical to that of a distributor  D) Commission from the product manufacturer is the preferred compensation model for a registered investment adviser
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) An investment adviser owes a fiduciary duty and must keep advisory activity segregated from distribution**

A **fiduciary** must place the client's interest ahead of their own. SEBI's answer to the advice-versus-distribution conflict is **segregation**: advisory activity must be kept separate from distribution, and an individual investment adviser cannot do both.

- **A) An individual investment adviser may both advise and distribute, provided commissions are disclosed** - wrong; disclosure does not cure the conflict, and an individual IA cannot do both.
- **C) An investment adviser owes only a suitability duty, identical to that of a distributor** - wrong; the fiduciary standard is higher than suitability and is the defining obligation of an IA.
- **D) Commission from the product manufacturer is the preferred compensation model for a registered investment adviser** - wrong; the IA model is a **fee from the client**, precisely to remove manufacturer-paid incentives.

</details>

**Q6.** Sreedevi Warrier of Thrissur invests Rs 18,50,000 in a portfolio expected to compound at 8.4% p.a. What will it be worth after 7 years?
A) Rs 29,37,800  B) Rs 32,53,694  C) Rs 35,27,004  D) Rs 27,68,969
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 32,53,694**

FV = PV × (1 + r)^n = 18,50,000 × (1.084)^7. (1.084)^7 = 1.758754. FV = 18,50,000 × 1.758754 = **Rs 32,53,694**.

- **A) Rs 29,37,800** - wrong; this applies **simple** interest: 18,50,000 × (1 + 0.084 × 7).
- **C) Rs 35,27,004** - wrong; this compounds for 8 years instead of 7.
- **D) Rs 27,68,969** - wrong; this compounds for only 5 years.

</details>

**Q7.** A Kochi retiree earns a nominal 7.6% p.a. on his fixed deposits while inflation runs at 5.4%. What is his real rate of return?
A) 2.09%  B) 2.20%  C) 1.89%  D) 2.32%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) 2.09%**

Real return = [(1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation)] − 1 = (1.076 ÷ 1.054) − 1 = 1.020872 − 1 = **2.09%**.

- **B) 2.20%** - wrong; this is the crude subtraction 7.6 − 5.4, which always **overstates** the real return.
- **C) 1.89%** - wrong; this divides by 1.056, using the wrong inflation figure.
- **D) 2.32%** - wrong; this multiplies rather than divides the two factors.

</details>

**Q8.** A Kochi bank quotes a nominal 8.8% p.a. on a deposit, compounded quarterly. What is the effective annual rate?
A) 8.80%  B) 8.99%  C) 9.09%  D) 9.16%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) 9.09%**

EAR = (1 + r/m)^m − 1 with r = 0.088 and m = 4. (1 + 0.022)^4 = 1.090947. EAR = **9.09%**.

- **A) 8.80%** - wrong; this is the **nominal** rate, which never changes with frequency. Only the effective rate does.
- **B) 8.99%** - wrong; this uses half-yearly compounding (m = 2).
- **D) 9.16%** - wrong; this uses monthly compounding (m = 12).

</details>

**Q9.** Zubair Hameed invests Rs 16,500 at the end of every month for 14 years in a fund expected to return 10.5% p.a. compounded monthly. What corpus will he accumulate?
A) Rs 27,72,000  B) Rs 62,63,428  C) Rs 63,18,233  D) Rs 57,44,694
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 62,63,428**

r = 0.105 ÷ 12 = 0.00875; n = 14 × 12 = 168. (1.00875)^168 = 4.321515. FV = PMT × [((1+r)^n − 1) ÷ r] = 16,500 × [(4.321515 − 1) ÷ 0.00875] = 16,500 × 379.6017 = **Rs 62,63,428**.

- **A) Rs 27,72,000** - wrong; this is merely the total contributed (16,500 × 168), ignoring all returns.
- **C) Rs 63,18,233** - wrong; this treats the SIP as an **annuity due** (paid at the beginning of each month), multiplying by (1 + r). The question says end of month.
- **D) Rs 57,44,694** - wrong; this annualises the instalments to Rs 1,98,000 a year and compounds annually, losing the benefit of monthly compounding.

</details>

**Q10.** A charitable trust in Kochi wants to fund a scholarship of Rs 4,80,000 payable one year from now, rising 4% every year thereafter, in perpetuity. The corpus is expected to earn 9% p.a. What corpus is required today?
A) Rs 53,33,333  B) Rs 96,00,000  C) Rs 1,20,00,000  D) Rs 36,92,308
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 96,00,000**

Growing perpetuity: PV = PMT₁ ÷ (r − g) = 4,80,000 ÷ (0.09 − 0.04) = 4,80,000 ÷ 0.05 = **Rs 96,00,000**. The formula requires g < r, which holds here.

- **A) Rs 53,33,333** - wrong; this is the level perpetuity 4,80,000 ÷ 0.09, ignoring the 4% growth.
- **C) Rs 1,20,00,000** - wrong; this uses a 4% discount rate, mixing up r and g.
- **D) Rs 36,92,308** - wrong; this divides by (r + g) = 0.13 instead of (r − g).

</details>

**Q11.** An earmarked corpus of Rs 6,40,000 grew to Rs 17,90,000 over 9 years. What compound annual growth rate did it deliver?
A) 12.11%  B) 19.97%  C) 10.42%  D) 13.72%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) 12.11%**

CAGR = (FV ÷ PV)^(1/n) − 1 = (17,90,000 ÷ 6,40,000)^(1/9) − 1 = (2.796875)^0.11111 − 1 = 1.121064 − 1 = **12.11% p.a.**

- **B) 19.97%** - wrong; this is simple-interest thinking: total gain ÷ original ÷ years.
- **C) 10.42%** - wrong; this understates the rate and does not reproduce the terminal value.
- **D) 13.72%** - wrong; this uses 8 years instead of 9 in the exponent.

</details>

**Q14.** A Kochi client holds a savings balance of Rs 1,26,000, a liquid fund worth Rs 3,60,000, equity mutual funds worth Rs 22,00,000 and a PPF balance of Rs 8,50,000. Monthly household expenses are Rs 90,000. What is the liquidity ratio?

**Q16.** A single-earner Kochi household with irregular professional income spends Rs 78,000 a month. Which contingency arrangement is most appropriate?
A) Rs 2,34,000 held in an equity mutual fund  B) Rs 4,68,000 held in a sweep-in fixed deposit and a liquid fund  C) Rs 4,68,000 invested in PPF  D) Rs 9,36,000 invested in a 5-year tax-saving fixed deposit
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 4,68,000 held in a sweep-in fixed deposit and a liquid fund**

The contingency fund is sized against **expenses**, at 3–6 months, with the upper end appropriate for irregular income and a single earner: 6 × 78,000 = **Rs 4,68,000**. It must offer immediate availability and certainty of value — a sweep-in FD and a liquid fund satisfy both.

- **A) Rs 2,34,000 held in an equity mutual fund** - wrong on both counts: only 3 months for an irregular-income household, and equity may be down precisely when the money is needed.
- **C) Rs 4,68,000 invested in PPF** - wrong; the amount is right but PPF is locked in and fails the immediate-availability test.
- **D) Rs 9,36,000 invested in a 5-year tax-saving fixed deposit** - wrong; a 5-year tax-saving FD cannot be withdrawn prematurely, and 12 months of expenses is excessive.

</details>

**Q17.** A household's budget balances every month but collapses each July, when the annual motor and health insurance premiums totalling Rs 96,000 fall due. Which corrective step reflects sound budgeting practice?
A) Meet the premium on a credit card and repay it over the following months  B) Set aside Rs 8,000 every month as a provision for the periodic outflow  C) Suspend the monthly SIP in the month the premium falls due  D) Redeem part of the equity portfolio every July
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Set aside Rs 8,000 every month as a provision for the periodic outflow**

The most common budgeting failure is ignoring **periodic outflows**. The fix is to divide the annual amount by 12 — 96,000 ÷ 12 = **Rs 8,000 a month** — and set that aside, so the lumpy payment is already funded when it arrives.

- **A) Meet the premium on a credit card and repay it over the following months** - wrong; revolving credit card debt is the costliest borrowing available (36–48% annualised) and converts a planning failure into a debt problem.
- **C) Suspend the monthly SIP in the month the premium falls due** - wrong; savings should be treated as a committed outflow, not the residual that gets cut first.
- **D) Redeem part of the equity portfolio every July** - wrong; funding a predictable annual expense by selling a long-horizon asset exposes the household to market timing risk.

</details>

**Q18.** Anjana Pillai takes a loan against property of Rs 28,00,000 at 9.6% p.a. for 12 years. What is her EMI?
A) Rs 32,818  B) Rs 22,400  C) Rs 19,444  D) Rs 41,897
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) Rs 32,818**

r = 0.096 ÷ 12 = 0.008; n = 12 × 12 = 144. (1.008)^144 = 3.150044. EMI = P × [r(1+r)^n] ÷ [(1+r)^n − 1] = 28,00,000 × (0.008 × 3.150044) ÷ 2.150044 = **Rs 32,818**.

- **B) Rs 22,400** - wrong; this is the **first month's interest** (28,00,000 × 0.008), a classic distractor.
- **C) Rs 19,444** - wrong; this is principal ÷ number of months, with no interest at all.
- **D) Rs 41,897** - wrong; this is the EMI over an 8-year tenure, not 12 years.

</details>

**Q19.** A borrower's outstanding loan balance is Rs 28,00,000, the interest rate is 9.6% p.a. and the EMI is Rs 32,818. How much of the next EMI repays principal?
A) Rs 22,400  B) Rs 10,418  C) Rs 32,818  D) Rs 19,444
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 10,418**

Interest for the month = outstanding × monthly rate = 28,00,000 × (0.096 ÷ 12) = 28,00,000 × 0.008 = Rs 22,400. Principal = EMI − interest = 32,818 − 22,400 = **Rs 10,418**. Note that 68% of this EMI is interest — which is why early prepayments save so much.

- **A) Rs 22,400** - wrong; this is the **interest** component, not the principal component.
- **C) Rs 32,818** - wrong; this is the whole EMI, which includes interest.
- **D) Rs 19,444** - wrong; this is principal ÷ 144, the average principal repayment if the loan amortised in a straight line, which it does not.

</details>

**Q20.** A Kochi retiree takes a gold loan against his family jewellery and, separately, a vehicle loan for a new car. What security interests are created?
A) Gold loan - hypothecation; vehicle loan - pledge  B) Gold loan - pledge; vehicle loan - hypothecation  C) Both are mortgages  D) Gold loan - mortgage; vehicle loan - pledge
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Gold loan - pledge; vehicle loan - hypothecation**

A **pledge** is security over **movable** property where **possession passes to the lender** — the bank holds the gold. A **hypothecation** is security over **movable** property where **possession stays with the borrower** — the borrower drives the car. A **mortgage** is security over **immovable** property.

- **A) Gold loan - hypothecation; vehicle loan - pledge** - wrong; it reverses the two. Possession is the discriminator.
- **C) Both are mortgages** - wrong; a mortgage applies only to immovable property such as land or a house.
- **D) Gold loan - mortgage; vehicle loan - pledge** - wrong on both counts; gold and a vehicle are both movable property.

</details>

**Q21.** A client owes Rs 3,20,000 on a personal loan at 15%, Rs 48,000 on a consumer durable loan at 13% and Rs 1,10,000 on a credit card at 42%. She decides to clear the Rs 48,000 durable loan first, purely to gain a sense of momentum. Which repayment strategy is she following?
A) Avalanche  B) Snowball  C) Blizzard  D) Refinancing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Snowball**

The **snowball** method repays the **smallest balance** first regardless of interest rate, because quick wins build motivation. It costs more in total interest than the mathematically optimal alternative, but it keeps the borrower engaged.

- **A) Avalanche** - wrong; the avalanche attacks the **highest rate** first, which here would be the 42% credit card.
- **C) Blizzard** - wrong; the blizzard is a hybrid — clear one or two small balances, then switch to the highest rate. She has described only the first leg, and the question asks which strategy the single decision reflects.
- **D) Refinancing** - wrong; refinancing replaces an existing loan with a cheaper one. No new loan is being taken.

</details>

**Q25.** Which institution acts as the central counterparty and guarantees the settlement of trades executed on a stock exchange?

<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) The clearing corporation**

**Q26.** Which statement correctly describes an RBI-regulated Account Aggregator (NBFC-AA)?

**Correct: B) It retrieves and shares the customer's financial data only with explicit consent and is data blind**

**Q27.** Which of the following is a money market instrument falling under the RBI's regulatory domain?

**Q83.** What distinguishes the **fiduciary** standard applying to an Investment Adviser from the **suitability** standard applying to a distributor?

**Correct: A) The fiduciary standard asks whether the product is the best available for the client; suitability asks only whether it is not unsuitable**

Regulation 15(1) requires an adviser to act **in a fiduciary capacity**, placing the client's interest **above his own** - the highest duty the law recognises in a commercial relationship. The distributor's lighter test is only that the product is **not unsuitable**.

- **B) The fiduciary standard applies only at the point of sale, suitability applies continuously** - wrong; this reverses the position - the fiduciary duty is **continuing**, requiring monitoring and review, while suitability is largely point-of-sale.
- **C) The fiduciary standard permits commission from the manufacturer, suitability does not** - wrong; it is the fiduciary who is paid **only by the client**, while the distributor is paid by the manufacturer.

**Q84.** An Investment Adviser's annual compliance audit for the year ended 31 March 2026 is signed on 18 September 2026 and contains two adverse observations. What exactly must be submitted, and by when?

**Position 1 — a government security.** Face value Rs 10,00,000, coupon **7.2% a year paid half-yearly**, exactly **6 years** to maturity. The market yield on comparable paper is **8.4% a year**, i.e. **4.2% per half-year**. The bond's **Macaulay duration** at that yield is **4.92 years**. The adviser wants to know the effect of a **60 basis point rise** in yields.

**Position 2 — an equity sleeve and two derivative trades.** The equity sleeve is worth **Rs 46,00,000** and has a **beta of 1.15** to the Nifty. Near-month Nifty futures trade at **24,500** with a **lot size of 25**. The adviser has separately bought **4 lots of the Nifty 24,300 put** at a premium of **Rs 142 per unit**; on expiry day the Nifty settles at **23,650**.

Useful factor: (1.042)^12 = 1.638372

**Q131.** What is the market value today of the Rs 10,00,000 face value government security?
A) Rs 6,10,362  B) Rs 9,44,337  C) Rs 10,00,000  D) Rs 10,58,700
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 9,44,337**

Semi-annual convention: halve the coupon and the yield, double the periods. Per Rs 100 of face value C = 3.6, y = 4.2%, n = 12.
Discount factor = 1 ÷ 1.638372 = **0.610362**.
Annuity factor = (1 − 0.610362) ÷ 0.042 = **9.277096**.
PV of coupons = 3.6 × 9.277096 = 33.3975; PV of redemption = 100 × 0.610362 = 61.0362.
Price = **Rs 94.4337 per Rs 100**, so Rs 10,00,000 face is worth **Rs 9,44,337** — a discount, since the 7.2% coupon is below the 8.4% yield.

- **A) Rs 6,10,362** - wrong; this discounts only the redemption amount and ignores all twelve coupons.
- **C) Rs 10,00,000** - wrong; the bond would trade at par only if the coupon equalled the market yield.
- **D) Rs 10,58,700** - wrong; a premium price, which would require the coupon to exceed the yield. The direction is reversed.

</details>

**Q132.** What is the modified duration of the bond?
A) 4.54 years  B) 4.72 years  C) 4.92 years  D) 5.13 years
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) 4.72 years**

Modified duration = Macaulay duration ÷ (1 + y/n), where y/n is the yield **per coupon period**.
= 4.92 ÷ (1 + 0.084/2) = 4.92 ÷ 1.042 = **4.72 years**.

- **A) 4.54 years** - wrong; this divides by (1 + 0.084) = 1.084, using the annual yield when the bond pays half-yearly.
- **C) 4.92 years** - wrong; this is the Macaulay duration itself, which has not yet been converted.
- **D) 5.13 years** - wrong; this multiplies by 1.042 instead of dividing. Modified duration is always *shorter* than Macaulay duration.

</details>

**Q133.** If yields rise by 60 basis points, approximately how much value does the bond position lose?
A) Rs 26,744  B) Rs 27,877  C) Rs 28,320  D) Rs 2,67,436
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) Rs 26,744**

% price change ≈ −Modified duration × Δy = −4.72 × 0.0060 = **−2.832%**.
Loss = 2.832% × market value = 0.02832 × 9,44,337 = **Rs 26,744**.
Note the loss is measured on the **market value** of Rs 9,44,337, not on face value.

- **B) Rs 27,877** - wrong; this applies the Macaulay duration of 4.92 rather than the modified duration of 4.72.
- **C) Rs 28,320** - wrong; this applies the correct 2.832% to the Rs 10,00,000 face value instead of to the market value.
- **D) Rs 2,67,436** - wrong; this reads "60 basis points" as 6%, overstating the yield shift tenfold.

</details>

**Q134.** How many Nifty futures contracts must be sold to hedge the equity sleeve on a beta-adjusted basis, rounded to the nearest whole contract?
A) 8  B) 9  C) 188  D) 216
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) 9**

Value to be hedged = Portfolio value × beta = 46,00,000 × 1.15 = **Rs 52,90,000**.
Value of one futures contract = 24,500 × 25 = **Rs 6,12,500**.
Contracts = 52,90,000 ÷ 6,12,500 = 8.64, rounded to **9 contracts**.

- **A) 8** - wrong; this is the unadjusted hedge, 46,00,000 ÷ 6,12,500 = 7.51 rounded up, which leaves the 1.15 beta unhedged.
- **C) 188** - wrong; this is 46,00,000 ÷ 24,500 — the number of index *units*, with neither the beta nor the lot size applied.
- **D) 216** - wrong; this is 52,90,000 ÷ 24,500 — beta-adjusted index units, but still not divided by the lot size of 25.

</details>

**Q135.** What is the net profit on the long put position at expiry?
A) Rs 14,200  B) Rs 50,800  C) Rs 65,000  D) Rs 79,200
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Rs 50,800**

Total units = 4 lots × 25 = **100 units**.
Intrinsic value at expiry = max(Strike − Spot, 0) = 24,300 − 23,650 = **Rs 650 per unit**.
Net gain per unit = 650 − 142 = **Rs 508**.
Net profit = 508 × 100 = **Rs 50,800**. (The break-even was 24,300 − 142 = 24,158, and the Nifty settled well below it.)

- **A) Rs 14,200** - wrong; this is the premium paid (142 × 100), which is the *maximum loss* on a long put, not the profit.
- **C) Rs 65,000** - wrong; this is the gross intrinsic value with no deduction for the premium already paid.
- **D) Rs 79,200** - wrong; this adds the premium to the intrinsic value instead of subtracting it. The premium is an outflow.

</details>
