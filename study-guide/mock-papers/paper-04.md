**Q1.** In the six-step financial planning process, which step immediately follows "defining client needs and financial goals"?
A) Establishing the client–planner relationship  B) Gathering data on the client's financial situation  C) Risk profiling and asset allocation  D) Reviewing and rebalancing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Risk profiling and asset allocation**

The order is: (1) establish the client–planner relationship, (2) gather data on the client's financial situation, (3) define client needs and financial goals, (4) **risk profiling and asset allocation**, (5) portfolio construction, (6) reviewing and rebalancing. Step 4 follows step 3.

- **A) Establishing the client–planner relationship** - wrong; that is step 1, the very first step.
- **B) Gathering data on the client's financial situation** - wrong; that is step 2, which precedes goal definition.
- **D) Reviewing and rebalancing** - wrong; that is the last step, step 6.

</details>

**Q2.** Dr Aravind Rajagopal is a 29-year-old radiologist in Chennai with a stable hospital income, no dependants and a 25-year investment horizon, but he redeemed his entire equity fund holding after a 10% market fall last year. How should his risk profile be described?
A) High risk capacity and high risk tolerance  B) High risk capacity but low risk tolerance  C) Low risk capacity but high risk tolerance  D) Low risk capacity and low risk tolerance
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) High risk capacity but low risk tolerance**

**Risk capacity (ability)** is objective — long horizon, stable income, no dependants and a young age all point to **high** capacity. **Risk tolerance (willingness)** is subjective, and panic-selling after a modest 10% fall reveals **low** tolerance. Where the two conflict, the adviser plans to the **lower** of the two.

- **A) High risk capacity and high risk tolerance** - wrong; the panic sale contradicts high tolerance.
- **C) Low risk capacity but high risk tolerance** - wrong; it reverses both readings of the evidence.
- **D) Low risk capacity and low risk tolerance** - wrong; his horizon, income stability and lack of dependants give him high capacity.

</details>

Choosing the mix of asset classes to match each goal's horizon and the client's risk profile — and then selecting products within that mix — is precisely **investment planning and asset allocation**.

**Q5.** Which statement best describes SEBI's approach to the conflict between advice and distribution?
A) An individual investment adviser may both advise and distribute provided commissions are disclosed  B) An individual investment adviser must keep advisory activity segregated from distribution activity  C) Distribution is prohibited for all entities registered with SEBI  D) The conflict is resolved entirely by applying a suitability standard
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) An individual investment adviser must keep advisory activity segregated from distribution activity**

SEBI's answer to the commission-driven conflict is **segregation of advice and distribution**. A registered Investment Adviser owes a **fiduciary** duty — the client's interest ahead of the adviser's own — and an individual IA cannot carry on both activities.

- **A) An individual investment adviser may both advise and distribute provided commissions are disclosed** - wrong; disclosure alone does not permit an individual IA to do both.
- **C) Distribution is prohibited for all entities registered with SEBI** - wrong; distributors are a legitimate, separately regulated category.
- **D) The conflict is resolved entirely by applying a suitability standard** - wrong; an IA is held to a fiduciary standard, which is higher than suitability.

</details>

**Q6.** Dr Aravind invests a lump sum of Rs 3,40,000 in an instrument earning 10.5% p.a. compounded annually. What will it be worth after 9 years?
A) Rs 6,61,300  B) Rs 7,55,748  C) Rs 8,35,102  D) Rs 9,22,760
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Rs 8,35,102**

FV = PV × (1 + r)^n = 3,40,000 × (1.105)^9. (1.105)^9 = 2.456182. FV = 3,40,000 × 2.456182 = **Rs 8,35,102**.

- **A) Rs 6,61,300** - wrong; this applies **simple** interest: 3,40,000 × (1 + 0.105 × 9).
- **B) Rs 7,55,748** - wrong; this compounds for only 8 years instead of 9.
- **D) Rs 9,22,760** - wrong; this over-compounds, using 10 years instead of 9.

</details>

**Q7.** A portfolio Dr Aravind inherited was worth Rs 2,75,000 seven years ago and is worth Rs 6,60,000 today. What is the CAGR?
A) 13.32%  B) 15.60%  C) 20.00%  D) 140.00%
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: A) 13.32%**

CAGR = (FV ÷ PV)^(1/n) − 1 = (6,60,000 ÷ 2,75,000)^(1/7) − 1 = (2.4)^(1/7) − 1 = 1.13322 − 1 = **13.32% p.a.**

- **B) 15.60%** - wrong; no valid computation of the compounded rate produces this figure.
- **C) 20.00%** - wrong; this is simple-interest thinking — the 140% total gain divided by 7 years.
- **D) 140.00%** - wrong; this is the **total** percentage gain over the whole seven years, not an annual rate.

</details>

FV = 18,000 × [(3.720979 − 1) ÷ 0.0091667] = 18,000 × 296.834 = **Rs 53,43,013**.

- **B) Rs 49,06,048** - wrong; this treats the SIP as a single annual contribution of Rs 2,16,000 compounded annually.
- **D) Rs 53,91,990** - wrong; this is the **annuity due** value (× 1.0091667), i.e. payments at the beginning of each month.

- **C) Rs 22,000** - wrong; no valid computation produces this figure.
- **D) Rs 33,854** - wrong; this is simply 65,00,000 ÷ 192, ignoring all compounding.

**Q12.** A charitable trust Dr Aravind supports will receive Rs 96,000 one year from now, and the payment will grow at 3% a year for ever. At a discount rate of 9% p.a., what is the present value of this stream?
A) Rs 8,00,000  B) Rs 10,66,667  C) Rs 16,00,000  D) Rs 32,00,000
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: C) Rs 16,00,000**

Growing perpetuity: PV = PMT₁ ÷ (r − g) = 96,000 ÷ (0.09 − 0.03) = 96,000 ÷ 0.06 = **Rs 16,00,000**.

- **A) Rs 8,00,000** - wrong; this **adds** g to r instead of subtracting it: 96,000 ÷ 0.12.
- **B) Rs 10,66,667** - wrong; this is a level perpetuity, 96,000 ÷ 0.09, ignoring growth entirely.
- **D) Rs 32,00,000** - wrong; this divides by the growth rate alone (96,000 ÷ 0.03).

</details>

**Q17.** Dr Aravind's monthly budget balances comfortably all year, yet every February he has to use his credit card because his annual professional indemnity premium falls due. Which category of outflow has his budget failed to provide for?

**Correct: C) Periodic (lumpy) outflows**

Annual premiums, festivals, vacations and servicing costs are **periodic outflows** — predictable but infrequent, and the most commonly forgotten item in a household budget. The fix is to divide the annual amount by 12 and set that aside every month.

</details>

**Q22.** A borrower with four loans decides to clear the smallest outstanding balance first, paying only the minimum on the others. Which repayment strategy is this?
A) Avalanche  B) Snowball  C) Blizzard  D) Refinancing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct: B) Snowball**

The **snowball** method attacks the **smallest balance** first, regardless of interest rate. It is psychologically powerful because of the quick wins, but it costs more in total interest than the avalanche method.

- **A) Avalanche** - wrong; the avalanche targets the **highest interest rate** first and is mathematically optimal.
- **C) Blizzard** - wrong; the blizzard is a hybrid — clear one or two small balances first, then switch to the highest rate.
- **D) Refinancing** - wrong; refinancing replaces an existing loan with a cheaper one; it is not a repayment ordering method.

</details>

**Q28.** Which statement correctly distinguishes a rights issue from a bonus issue?

**Q31.** Under the ASBA facility, what happens to an IPO applicant's money?
A) It is debited in full on application and refunded after allotment  B) It is blocked in the applicant's own bank account and debited only to the extent of the allotment  C) It is transferred to the registrar's escrow account on application  D) It is blocked in the merchant banker's account until listing
<details><summary>Answer & Explanation</summary>
