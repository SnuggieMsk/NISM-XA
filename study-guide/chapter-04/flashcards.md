# Chapter 4: Debt Management and Loans — Flashcards

Cover the answer, recall it, then check. **The security-type trio (mortgage / pledge / hypothecation) and the three repayment strategies are near-certain exam marks.**

**Q1.** State the EMI formula and define each term.
> **A:** **EMI = P × r(1+r)ⁿ ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1]**, where **P** = principal, **r** = **monthly** rate (annual ÷ 12), **n** = number of **months**.

**Q2.** How do you compute total interest on a loan?
> **A:** **Total Interest = (EMI × n) − P.**

**Q3.** In an amortisation schedule, how is each month's interest and principal found?
> **A:** **Interest = outstanding balance × monthly rate.** **Principal = EMI − interest.** New balance = old balance − principal repaid.

**Q4.** Why do early EMIs barely reduce the loan?
> **A:** Interest is charged on the (large) outstanding balance, so early EMIs are **mostly interest**. On a 20-year 9% loan, month 1 is about **83% interest**. The principal share only crosses 50% around the two-thirds point.

**Q5.** What is the practical consequence of that?
> **A:** **Prepayments made early save dramatically more interest** than the same amount paid later — and borrowers have repaid far less principal partway through than they assume.

**Q6.** Distinguish mortgage, pledge and hypothecation.
> **A:** **Mortgage** = security over **immovable** property (home loan). **Pledge** = **movable**, and **possession passes to the lender** (gold loan). **Hypothecation** = **movable**, but **possession stays with the borrower** (car loan).

**Q7.** What is the difference between hire purchase and leasing?
> **A:** In **hire purchase**, ownership transfers to the user on the **final instalment**. In **leasing**, ownership **never** transfers — the user only pays for the right to use.

**Q8.** What is pre-EMI interest?
> **A:** On a **partly disbursed** loan (e.g. an under-construction property), the borrower pays **interest only on the amount disbursed**. Full principal repayment starts after complete disbursement.

**Q9.** What is a moratorium, and what is the catch?
> **A:** An agreed period of **no repayment** (or interest-only). The catch: **interest usually continues to accrue**, so the total repaid increases.

**Q10.** Distinguish consumption from investment expenditure, and give the test.
> **A:** **Investment expenditure** creates an appreciating asset or future income (home, education, business); **consumption expenditure** leaves no lasting value (holiday, gadgets). Test: *will the thing still be worth something when the loan ends?*

**Q11.** What is the debt-to-income ratio and its ceiling?
> **A:** **Total EMIs ÷ Gross income**; keep it below about **35–40%**. Lenders use it directly for eligibility.

**Q12.** Rank common Indian loans from most to least expensive.
> **A:** **Credit card revolving (36–48%)** → personal loan (11–18%) → gold loan / LAS (9–14%) → vehicle loan (9–12%) → home loan (8–10%) → education loan (8–12%).

**Q13.** What are the four questions of debt counselling?
> **A:** The **purpose**, the **cost**, the **maturity** of the debt, and whether **rescheduling** is needed.

**Q14.** Name the credit information companies in India and their regulator.
> **A:** **CIBIL (TransUnion CIBIL), Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark** — regulated by the **RBI** under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, **2005**.

**Q15.** What is the credit score range, and what counts as good?
> **A:** Typically **300–900**; **750+** is generally considered good.

**Q16.** What drives a credit score?
> **A:** **Repayment history** (the largest factor), **credit utilisation**, length of credit history, credit mix, and the number of recent **hard enquiries**.

**Q17.** What is the RBI rule on free credit reports?
> **A:** Every individual is entitled to **one free full credit report per calendar year from each CIC**.

**Q18.** Does checking your own credit score hurt it?
> **A:** **No** — that is a **soft enquiry**. Only a **hard enquiry** (a lender checking when you apply) can affect the score.

**Q19.** Compare secured and unsecured loans.
> **A:** **Secured** = collateral pledged, **lower rate**, larger amounts, lender can seize the security on default. **Unsecured** = no collateral, **higher rate**, smaller amounts, lender must pursue legal recovery.

**Q20.** What is an overdraft's key advantage?
> **A:** Interest is charged **only on the amount actually used, only for the days used** — not on the whole sanctioned limit.

**Q21.** What is a loan against securities, and its distinctive risk?
> **A:** Borrowing against pledged shares or mutual fund units, giving liquidity without selling. The distinctive risk is a **margin call** if the pledged securities fall in value.

**Q22.** How does credit card interest actually work?
> **A:** Paying the **"minimum due" avoids a late fee but not interest** — the balance revolves at roughly **3–4% per month**. Once a balance revolves, the **interest-free period on new purchases is typically lost**.

**Q23.** State the three debt-repayment strategies and the memory hook.
> **A:** **Avalanche** = highest **rate** first (**A for APR**) — mathematically optimal. **Snowball** = **smallest balance** first (**S for smallest**) — psychological momentum. **Blizzard** = a **hybrid** of the two.

**Q24.** Which repayment strategy minimises total interest, and which builds motivation?
> **A:** **Avalanche** minimises total interest. **Snowball** builds motivation through quick wins but costs more overall.

**Q25.** When a floating rate rises, what two things can the lender adjust?
> **A:** **Keep the EMI and extend the tenure**, or **keep the tenure and raise the EMI**. Indian practice usually defaults to extending the tenure.

**Q26.** When rates FALL and the client can afford it, what is the best move?
> **A:** **Keep paying the same EMI** and let the tenure shorten. The entire extra amount goes to principal — the single most powerful interest saver.

**Q27.** What is the rule for deciding between prepaying a loan and investing?
> **A:** Compare the **loan rate** with the **expected POST-TAX return** on the investment. Repaying is a **risk-free, tax-free return equal to the interest rate**.

**Q28.** A client has spare cash, a 14% personal loan and an investment expected to return 11%. What should they do?
> **A:** **Repay the loan.** 14% certain and tax-free beats 11% expected, uncertain and taxable.

**Q29.** Name two caveats to the prepay-vs-invest rule.
> **A:** 1) **Never deplete the emergency fund** to prepay. 2) A **certain** return beats an *expected* one for a risk-averse client or a short horizon.

**Q30.** What is the "EMI illusion"?
> **A:** A **lower EMI usually means a longer tenure and MORE total interest**. Always compare **total interest outgo**, not the monthly figure.

**Q31.** How is a loan's outstanding balance calculated at any point?
> **A:** It is the **present value of all remaining EMIs** discounted at the loan rate: **Outstanding = EMI × [(1 − (1+r)⁻ᵐ) ÷ r]**, where m = remaining months.

**Q32.** What is the RBI position on prepayment charges for home loans?
> **A:** **Floating-rate home loans to individual borrowers carry no prepayment/foreclosure penalty.** Fixed-rate loans may still carry one.

**Q33.** What is refinancing (balance transfer)?
> **A:** Replacing an existing loan with a **new one at a lower rate**, usually with another lender. Weigh the interest saved against processing fees and any foreclosure charges.

**Q34.** List the principles of responsible borrowing.
> **A:** Borrow for **investment not consumption**; keep servicing under **35–40%** of income; **match tenure to asset life**; compare **effective** cost including fees; keep an **emergency fund**; **never borrow to service existing debt**; and **insure large loans** with term cover.

**Q35.** Why should a large home loan be paired with term insurance?
> **A:** So that if the borrower dies, **dependants do not inherit the liability** — and the family home is not lost.

**Q36.** What does persistent reliance on credit cards to reach month-end indicate?
> **A:** A **structural budget deficit**, not a temporary one. More debt cannot fix it — only raising income or cutting expenses can.

**Q37.** What should be done with a windfall (bonus, inheritance)?
> **A:** **Pre-allocate it**: clear the costliest debt, top up the emergency fund, then fund goals. Unallocated windfalls get absorbed into consumption.

**Q38.** What is leverage, in one sentence?
> **A:** Using **borrowed money to control a larger asset** — which magnifies **both gains and losses**.

**Q39.** A ₹40,00,000 loan at 9% for 20 years — what is the EMI and total interest?
> **A:** r = 0.0075, n = 240, (1.0075)²⁴⁰ = 6.00915. **EMI ≈ ₹35,989**; total interest = (35,989 × 240) − 40,00,000 = **₹46,37,360** — more than the loan itself.

**Q40.** What is the typical education loan repayment structure?
> **A:** A **moratorium during the course plus 6–12 months** thereafter, with repayment beginning after that. Often unsecured up to a limit, with collateral required above it.
